The best Advent books for kids are the ones structured as a daily read: *The Advent Storybook* by Laura Richie for ages 4-10, *Unwrapping the Greatest Gift* by Ann Voskamp for mixed-age families, and *Jotham's Journey* by Arnold Ytreeide for kids 8 and up who want a cliffhanger every night. Below are 15 real, in-print titles, split between true day-by-day devotionals and picture books for the December read-aloud pile.

The distinction matters more than most gift guides admit. A gorgeous Christmas picture book is a lovely 10-minute read on December 12; it is not an Advent devotional. If you want a tradition your kids ask for, you need something with 24 or 25 numbered pieces. That's the first section.

## What Are the Best Daily Advent Devotionals for Kids?

A true Advent devotional gives you one short reading per day from December 1 to Christmas, so the habit runs itself -- open the book, read tonight's number, done.

### 1. The Advent Storybook: 25 Bible Stories Showing Why Jesus Came -- Laura Richie
Twenty-five numbered Bible stories that walk from Creation to Bethlehem, so kids see the whole rescue story building toward the manger. Warm illustrations by Ian Dale, and each story ends with a short prayer. Ages 4-10, and it works for a wide sibling spread. **How to use it:** read one chapter a night, December 1-25, in order. This is the easiest first Advent book for most families.

### 2. Unwrapping the Greatest Gift: A Family Celebration of Christmas -- Ann Voskamp
The most popular family Advent book of the last decade, and effectively a Jesse Tree in book form: 25 daily readings tracing Christ's family line from Creation forward, each with a Scripture passage, a devotion, and a matching ornament to print and hang. Whole-family, with the reading pitched at parents reading aloud. **How to use it:** read the day's devotion at dinner, then let a child hang the ornament on a branch or small tree. **Honest note:** Voskamp's prose is lyrical and dense -- some parents love it, others find it more poetic than a six-year-old can follow.

### 3. The Wonder of the Greatest Gift -- Ann Voskamp
Voskamp's same 25 devotions in a pop-up format, with a physical 3D Jesse Tree that stands up out of the book and 24 ornaments plus a star. Ages 3 and up, because even the youngest kids can participate in the opening and hanging. **How to use it:** open one numbered door per day, hang the ornament on the pop-up tree, read the devotion. **Honest note:** it's a near-duplicate of the book above in content -- buy one, not both.

### 4. Jotham's Journey: A Storybook for Advent -- Arnold Ytreeide
A gripping read-aloud novel about a ten-year-old shepherd boy searching for his family across first-century Israel, divided into a chapter for each day of Advent and ending at the manger. Ages 8-12, younger with a parent narrating. **How to use it:** read one chapter aloud each night; every chapter ends on a cliffhanger, which is why kids nag you for it. Ytreeide wrote three more in the same style -- *Bartholomew's Passage*, *Tabitha's Travels*, and *Amon's Adventure* -- so a family can run four Advents without repeating. **Honest note:** genuinely tense in places, with peril and violence appropriate to the setting; preview if you have a sensitive listener.

### 5. Prepare Him Room: Celebrating the Birth of Jesus -- Marty Machowski
A family Advent devotional built around the four Sundays of Advent rather than 25 days: 13 devotions total, three per week plus Christmas Day, woven together with an original Christmas story and paired with carols and craft ideas. Whole-family. **How to use it:** three sit-down devotions a week instead of a nightly reading -- the better structure for families whose weeknights are unpredictable. **Honest note:** if you specifically want a December 1-25 countdown, this isn't it.

### 6. The Jesus Storybook Bible: A Christmas Collection -- Sally Lloyd-Jones
A Christmas edition of the beloved storybook Bible, with sound buttons, hymns, activities, and an Advent reading map suggesting daily Scripture passages through Christmas and into Epiphany. Ages 3-8. **How to use it:** follow the included reading map day by day, using the storybook pages as the anchor. **Honest note:** it's a separate purchase from the standard Jesus Storybook Bible, which many families already own -- check before buying twice.

### 7. God With Us: A Family Advent Celebration -- Katie Pawlak
A Jesse Tree devotional with 25 daily readings tied to 25 symbols, short prayers, and extension activities, designed to pair with a companion ornament set. Ages 4-14, which is a rare range. **How to use it:** one symbol, one reading, one ornament per day from December 1. **Honest note:** small-press and mostly sold direct, so it takes a little more effort to find than the big-publisher titles.

### 8. Truth in the Tinsel: An Advent Experience for Little Hands -- Amanda White
Not a bound book but a 24-day downloadable guide: each day has a short Scripture reading and a simple ornament craft using materials you already own. Preschool through early elementary. **How to use it:** read the passage, make the ornament, hang it, repeat for 24 days. **Honest note:** it's a PDF, and it's craft-heavy -- brilliant if your kids need their hands busy, exhausting if you don't want 24 craft sessions in December.

### 9. The Jesse Tree -- Geraldine McCaughrean
A literary retelling in which a woodcarver carves a Jesse Tree in a church while a curious boy watches, with each carving prompting an Old Testament story. Ages 6-10. **How to use it:** read one story per day alongside a separate ornament set. **Honest note:** it's a storybook, not a devotional -- there are no numbered days, no prayers, and no ornaments included, so it works best as the reading half of a Jesse Tree you build yourself. Our full [Jesse Tree guide for kids](/blog/jesse-tree-for-kids) lists all 25 symbols and readings if you want to assemble it.

### 10. The Christmas Promise -- Alison Mitchell
A short, bright storybook explaining that God's promise of a new King, made centuries before, was kept in Bethlehem -- the clearest gospel framing of Christmas for the youngest kids. Ages 3-6, with a board book edition for toddlers. **How to use it:** the storybook is a single sitting, but the publisher also sells it bundled with an Advent calendar and a family devotional booklet, which turns it into a real December-long tradition for ages 5-11.

## What Are the Best Christmas Picture Books for Christian Kids?

Picture books aren't Advent devotionals, but they're what makes December feel like December -- put five in a basket by the tree and read one a night alongside your devotional.

### 11. Song of the Stars: A Christmas Story -- Sally Lloyd-Jones
The nativity told from the perspective of all creation waiting and whispering that the King is coming, with luminous Alison Jay illustrations. Ages 3-7. **How to use it:** the Christmas Eve read. It's short, reverent, and it lands.

### 12. The Crippled Lamb -- Max Lucado
A lamb who can't keep up with the flock is left behind in a stable on the night Jesus is born, and discovers that God had a purpose in the very thing that made him different. Ages 3-7. **How to use it:** the book to read to a child who feels left out of something. It has sold over a million copies for a reason.

### 13. The Legend of the Candy Cane -- Lori Walburg
A stranger opens a shop in a small frontier town and, through a candy cane, explains the whole story of Jesus to a curious girl. Ages 4-8. **How to use it:** pair it with the first candy canes of the season -- one of the easiest traditions to start, and kids retell the symbolism for years.

### 14. Room for a Little One: A Christmas Tale -- Martin Waddell
A gentle, rhythmic story in which animals make room for one another in a stable until there's room for a little one born that night. Ages 2-6, board book edition available. **How to use it:** the bedtime pick for the youngest child in a mixed-age household, while older siblings do a longer devotional.

### 15. The Christmas Miracle of Jonathan Toomey -- Susan Wojciechowski
A grieving, solitary woodcarver is asked by a widow and her son to carve a nativity set, and is slowly thawed by the work and the boy. Ages 5-10. **How to use it:** a single long read on a quiet evening, and a genuinely good conversation starter about grief at Christmas. **Honest note:** it's melancholy, and the themes of loss are real -- best for older kids or with a parent reading alongside.

## How Do You Actually Run an Advent Reading With Kids?

Attach the reading to something that already happens every night -- dinner or bedtime -- and keep it under ten minutes. Advent traditions die from ambition, not from disinterest.

A few things that make it work:

- **Pick one devotional, not three.** One numbered book, read at the same time each day. The picture books are extra, not a second obligation.
- **Start on December 1, but start late if you have to.** If you find this on December 8, read three days' worth the first night and catch up. Nobody has ever regretted an imperfect Advent.
- **Give a child the job.** One kid opens the door, one hangs the ornament, one reads the verse. Rotate. Ownership does more for consistency than any reminder app.
- **Let the youngest wander.** A three-year-old who plays on the floor during the reading is still hearing it.
- **Keep it on the table.** The book on the dinner table gets read; the book in a bedroom doesn't.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Advent book for kids overall?
*The Advent Storybook* by Laura Richie is the best all-around pick, with 25 numbered Bible stories that build from Creation to the manger for ages 4-10. For a mixed-age family that wants an ornament tradition attached, *Unwrapping the Greatest Gift* by Ann Voskamp is the stronger choice, and for kids 8 and up, *Jotham's Journey* is the one they'll beg you to keep reading.

### When should we start reading an Advent book?
Most Advent books for kids are built for December 1 through 25, so start on the first. Traditional church Advent begins on the fourth Sunday before Christmas, which is why devotionals like *Prepare Him Room* use a four-week structure instead. If you're starting late, double up for a few nights rather than skipping content.

### What's the difference between an Advent book and a Jesse Tree?
A Jesse Tree is a specific Advent tradition where you read one Bible story per day and hang a matching ornament, tracing the family line from Creation to Jesus. Several Advent books -- *Unwrapping the Greatest Gift*, *The Wonder of the Greatest Gift*, and *God With Us* -- are Jesse Trees in book form. Our [Jesse Tree for kids](/blog/jesse-tree-for-kids) guide covers all 25 symbols if you want to build one without buying a kit.

### What Advent book works for a wide age range?
*Unwrapping the Greatest Gift* and *God With Us* are the two designed for mixed ages, because the parent reads aloud and the younger kids participate through the ornaments rather than the text. *Prepare Him Room* also flexes well. For a household with a toddler and a ten-year-old, run one shared devotional at dinner and give the little one a board book like *Room for a Little One* at bedtime.

### Are Advent books only for liturgical churches?
No. Advent simply means "coming," and a countdown to Christmas built on Scripture works in any tradition -- most of the books on this list come from broadly evangelical, non-liturgical publishers. If the word itself is unfamiliar in your church, our explainer on [what Advent is for kids](/blog/what-is-advent-for-kids) gives you language to use with your children.

### What Advent book makes a good gift?
*Unwrapping the Greatest Gift* and *The Wonder of the Greatest Gift* are the two that feel like gifts, because they arrive as a complete tradition in a box rather than as a book to read. For a family with older kids, the four-book Ytreeide set is a gift that lasts four Christmases.
