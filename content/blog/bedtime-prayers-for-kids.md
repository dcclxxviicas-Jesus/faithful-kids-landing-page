The best bedtime prayers for kids are short, warm, and easy to remember -- a rhyming prayer for little ones, a simple honest prayer for older kids, and a calming prayer for scared nights. Below you'll find 18 original bedtime prayers organized by age and situation, so you can find the right one tonight without searching any further.

Bedtime is the single easiest moment of the day to pray with your kids. They're already still (mostly), the lights are low, and their hearts are open in a way they rarely are at 4 p.m. A two-minute prayer at bedtime, repeated night after night, quietly teaches a child that God is the last person they talk to before they sleep -- and that He's still there in the dark after you leave the room.

Use these prayers word-for-word, or treat them as training wheels until your child starts praying in their own words.

## Rhyming Bedtime Prayers for Little Kids (Ages 2-6)

Rhyming prayers work best for toddlers and preschoolers because rhythm makes them easy to memorize -- most kids can say these on their own within a week or two. Say each line and have your child repeat it after you until they know it by heart.

### 1. The Day Is Done
Dear God, the day is done,
Thank You for the moon and sun.
Watch me while I sleep tonight,
And wake me with the morning light. Amen.

### 2. Time to Rest
Thank You, God, for today,
For time to learn and time to play.
Bless my family, big and small --
Goodnight, God, You made us all. Amen.

### 3. Little Stars
Little stars are shining bright,
God is with me through the night.
I close my eyes and rest my head,
Safe and cozy in my bed. Amen.

### 4. My Goodnight Thank-You
Thank You for my comfy bed,
For the stories that we read.
Thank You, God, for loving me --
Goodnight from our whole family. Amen.

### 5. You Never Sleep
God, You never fall asleep,
So it's You my heart will keep.
I can close my eyes and rest --
Being loved by You is best. Amen.

## Simple Bedtime Prayers for Older Kids (Ages 7-12)

Older kids are ready for prayers that sound like real conversation instead of a nursery rhyme. These prose prayers model the honest, everyday way we want kids to talk to God for the rest of their lives.

### 6. The Whole Day Prayer
Dear God, thank You for today -- the good parts and even the hard parts, because You were with me in all of them. Forgive me for the ways I messed up, and help me start fresh tomorrow. I trust You with everything I can't control. Amen.

### 7. Quiet My Mind
Father, as I lie down, please quiet my mind. Take the thoughts that are still spinning and hold them for me until morning. The Bible says You give sleep to the ones You love, so I'm going to rest now. Amen.

### 8. For Other Kids Tonight
God, thank You for my family, my friends, and my home. Please be close to kids tonight who don't have those things. Show me a way to be kind to someone tomorrow. Amen.

### 9. Tomorrow Belongs to You
Lord, there are things about tomorrow that make me nervous. But You are already there waiting for me. Help me sleep well tonight and be brave in the morning. Amen.

### 10. Sorry, Thank You, Please
Dear God -- I'm sorry for the wrong things I did today. Thank You for the good things You gave me today. Please watch over everyone I love tonight. Amen.

## What Should Kids Pray When They're Scared at Night?

When a child is scared at night, the best prayer names the fear out loud and then names the truth that God is bigger than it -- kids calm down faster when both halves are spoken. These prayers are built for monsters, shadows, storms, and worried thoughts. Pair them with a memorized verse like Psalm 4:8 -- "In peace I will lie down and sleep, for you alone, Lord, make me dwell in safety" -- and you've given your child a tool they can use even when you're not in the room. (For more verses like that one, see our list of [Bible verses for anxious kids](/blog/bible-verses-for-anxious-kids).)

### 11. You Are Watching
God, I feel scared right now. But the Bible says You never sleep, so You are watching over me all night long. When scary thoughts come, help me remember that You are bigger than anything in the dark. Amen.

### 12. Be Still
Jesus, You told the storm to be still, and it obeyed You. Please tell my worried heart to be still too. I am safe because You are near. Amen.

### 13. Right Here in This Room
Dear God, when I hear noises or see shadows, remind me that You are right here in this room with me. You said, "Do not fear, for I am with you." Help me believe it all the way down to my toes. Amen.

### 14. Brave in the Dark (rhyming, for little ones)
When my room is dark and deep,
You stay close and watch my sleep.
You are bigger than my fear,
I am safe when You are near. Amen.

## Bedtime Prayers of Thanks

A thankfulness prayer at bedtime trains kids to end the day counting what went right instead of replaying what went wrong. These work especially well as call-and-response, where your child fills in the blanks.

### 15. Best Thing Today
Thank You, God, for this whole day. Thank You especially for ______ (let your child name the best thing that happened today). You are so good to me. Goodnight. Amen.

### 16. The People Who Love Me
Dear God, thank You for the people who love me -- for my family, my friends, and everyone who takes care of me. And thank You that You love me most of all. Amen.

### 17. The Ten-Second Thank You
Father, thank You for food today, a bed tonight, and Your love forever. Amen.

### 18. Everything Around Me
God, thank You for things I forget to notice -- warm blankets, funny jokes, my favorite songs, and one more day with my family. Help me wake up thankful too. Amen.

## How Do You Build a Bedtime Prayer Habit That Sticks?

Attach prayer to something you already do every night -- habits stick when they're anchored to an existing routine, not added as one more task. For most families that anchor is the bedtime story: book closes, lights dim, prayer happens. Same order, every night.

A few things that make the habit last:

1. **Keep it under two minutes.** A short prayer every night beats a long prayer twice a month. If your child is yawning through it, it's too long.
2. **Let them pick.** Once your child knows two or three prayers from this list, let them choose which one you'll say. Choice creates ownership.
3. **Take turns with sentences.** You pray one sentence, they pray one sentence. This is the gentlest on-ramp to praying in their own words -- we walk through the full progression in [how to teach kids to pray](/blog/how-to-pray-with-kids).
4. **Pair it with Scripture.** One short verse before the prayer gives the prayer something to stand on. Our list of [goodnight Bible verses for kids](/blog/goodnight-bible-verses-for-kids) has ten that work perfectly.
5. **Don't skip on bad nights.** The nights after tantrums, tears, and discipline are the nights bedtime prayer matters most. A prayer that begins "God, today was hard" teaches a child that they can come to God exactly as they are.
6. **Expect seasons.** A four-year-old who loved rhyming prayers may roll her eyes at them at nine. That's not backsliding -- it's a cue to graduate her to the prose prayers above, or to her own words.

Many families find that stacking a story, a verse, and a prayer works best -- read one of these [Bible stories for bedtime](/blog/bible-stories-for-bedtime), say one verse, pray one prayer, lights out. ## What Age Should Kids Start Saying Bedtime Prayers?

Kids can start participating in bedtime prayer as soon as they can talk -- around age two, they can repeat short lines after you, and even babies benefit from hearing you pray over them. There's no age that's "too early," because in the early years the goal isn't comprehension. It's atmosphere: your child growing up unable to remember a time when the family didn't talk to God at night.

By age three or four, most kids can recite a short rhyming prayer from memory. By six or seven, they can pray a sentence or two of their own. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a good short bedtime prayer for kids?
A good short bedtime prayer thanks God for the day and asks Him to watch over the child through the night. A simple original example: "Dear God, the day is done, thank You for the moon and sun. Watch me while I sleep tonight, and wake me with the morning light. Amen." Anything your child can memorize in a week is the right length.

### Should kids memorize bedtime prayers or pray in their own words?
Both, in that order. Memorized prayers give young kids words before they have their own, the way nursery rhymes come before conversation. Around age six or seven, start blending in free prayer -- "you say one thing you're thankful for, then we'll say our prayer" -- until their own words carry most of the weight.

### What should my child pray when they're scared at night?
Have them name the fear and then name the truth: "God, I'm scared of the dark, but You are with me and You never sleep." Pairing the prayer with a memorized verse like Psalm 4:8 or Isaiah 41:10 gives kids something concrete to hold onto when they wake up afraid and you're not in the room.

### How long should bedtime prayer take with kids?
One to two minutes is plenty for kids under ten. The power of bedtime prayer comes from repetition over years, not length on any given night. If your child wants to keep talking to God longer, let them -- but never stretch it so long that prayer starts to feel like a delay tactic or a chore.

### Is it okay to say the same bedtime prayer every night?
Yes -- repetition is a feature, not a failure. A prayer your child knows by heart becomes a comfort they can reach for on their own, at a sleepover or a scary night, without any help. To keep things from going stale, keep one familiar prayer as the anchor and add one fresh sentence of thanks or asking each night.

### What if my child refuses to pray at bedtime?
Don't force it -- pray over them instead, briefly and warmly, and let them just listen. A child who hears "God, thank You for this kid I love so much" every night is still learning to pray, even silently. Most refusals are about tiredness or testing limits, not God, and they usually pass within a few weeks if prayer stays pleasant and pressure-free.
