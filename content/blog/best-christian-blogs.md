Most "best Christian blogs" lists have a dirty secret: nobody checked whether the blogs still exist. The niche churns hard, and lists keep recommending sites whose last post shipped years ago, whose domains now sell quilting patterns, or whose pages no longer load at all. We know because we checked: every blog below was verified live and maintained in November 2026, on its own pages, and several famous names are missing from this list for exactly that reason.

One disclosure before the list, because it is our house rule: Faithful Kids runs its own parenting section, it appears under a clear "this is ours" label, and no one on this list paid anything or traded anything to be here.

At a glance, this guide covers 26 verified-active resources grouped by what you actually need: gospel-centered mom blogs (Risen Motherhood, The Better Mom, Raising Arrows), everyday parenting help (Christian Parenting, Focus on the Family, Connected Families, FamilyLife, the Parent Cue app), parents of teens (Rooted Ministry), teens and technology (Axis, Plugged In, Protect Young Eyes, Fuller Youth Institute), Christian homeschool blogs (The Unlikely Homeschool, Hip Homeschool Moms, Simple Homeschool), dads (All Pro Dad, Dad Tired), Catholic and Orthodox families (CatholicMom, Messy Family Project, Ancient Faith), kids' ministry (Ministry-to-Children), and deeper theological reading with real parenting coverage (The Gospel Coalition, Desiring God, Christianity Today, Plough), plus our own Raising Faithful Kids, disclosed as ours.

## For Christian Moms

**[Risen Motherhood](https://risenmotherhood.com)** remains the standard for gospel-centered motherhood writing: short articles that start in the mess of a real morning and end in Scripture, built on a stated formula of roughly 30% story and 70% gospel application. If you read one motherhood site, this is the one, and if you write, they take submissions (details in our [Christian writing opportunities](/blog/christian-writing-opportunities) guide).

**[The Better Mom](https://thebettermom.com)**, led by Ruth Schwenk, is the long-running encouragement-first site: faith, family, marriage, meal plans, and health for mothers, with a steady stream of devotionals and study resources alongside the articles.

**[Raising Arrows](https://www.raisingarrows.net)** is Amy's large-family homeschool and homemaking blog ("mom to 10 arrows"), explicitly Christian, publishing through fall 2026 with a weekly large-family homeschool diary and a podcast; the realest picture on this list of what a big homeschooling household actually looks like.

## Parenting Every Age

**[Christian Parenting](https://www.christianparenting.org)** is a contributor-driven site whose whole editorial filter is that articles rely on Scripture rather than vibes: many voices, one standard, organized around the actual questions parents ask.

**[Focus on the Family's parenting section](https://www.focusonthefamily.com/parenting/)** is the institutional heavyweight, and the reason it earns its slot is the age-stage organization: guidance sorted from 0-3 up through 19+, so you can read for the kid you have now instead of parenting in general.

**[Connected Families](https://connectedfamilies.org/blog/)** publishes biblical, research-informed parenting articles built around one coherent framework (connection before correction), posting weekly as of this month; the strongest single site on this list for discipline, anger, and sibling conflict.

**[FamilyLife](https://www.familylife.com)** is the marriage-and-parenting ministry: podcasts, courses, and articles on the premise that the state of the marriage is parenting infrastructure. Strongest on blended families, where it has a dedicated track.

**[Parent Cue](https://theparentcue.org)**, Orange's parenting brand, now lives primarily in its app rather than its blog: weekly age-based faith and character cues, actively maintained (the app shipped updates in 2026) while the site's visible articles date from several years back. Recommended as an app; just don't go looking for a current blog.

## Parents of Teens

**[Rooted Ministry](https://rootedministry.com)** is the best thing published for parents of teenagers in the faith, full stop: gospel-centered articles on anxiety, social media, and discipleship at home, publishing multiple times a week as of this month, plus a weekly "Rooted Parent Top 10" roundup worth subscribing to by itself.

## Teens, Tech and Media

The question behind half of Christian parenting search traffic is really "what do I do about the phone," and four sites own it.

**[Axis](https://axis.org)** equips parents to disciple their teens through culture: guides and videos on social media, identity, and whatever your teenager is actually watching, plus the weekly Culture Translator email that decodes teen culture for parents. If you subscribe to one thing on this list, make it that.

**[Plugged In](https://www.pluggedin.com)**, Focus on the Family's review site, covers movies, TV, music, games, books, and YouTube channels from a family perspective, publishing new reviews nearly daily (current within days when we checked).

**[Protect Young Eyes](https://protectyoungeyes.com)** does digital safety: device guides, app reviews written for parents, and a Christian digital discipleship curriculum, serving families, schools, and churches.

**[Fuller Youth Institute](https://fulleryouthinstitute.org)** is the research arm: Sticky Faith, Growing Young, and Gen Alpha studies on why kids keep or leave faith, translated into a practical parent library. Heavier reading, highest payoff per page.

## Christian Homeschool Blogs

**[The Unlikely Homeschool](https://www.theunlikelyhomeschool.com)**: Jamie Erickson (mother of five, author, co-host of the Mom to Mom podcast) writing practical Christian homeschooling: curriculum choices, read-alouds, and the honest texture of the homeschool day.

**[Hip Homeschool Moms](https://hiphomeschoolmoms.com)** is the big-tent community site, faith-friendly with a dedicated Bible section, fresh printables (they had November 2026 material up when we checked), and the widest range of practical how-tos.

**[Simple Homeschool](https://simplehomeschool.net)** carries the "seasoned guidance for a simpler homeschool" banner: curriculum, routines, and the emotional side of home education from a rotating cast of veteran contributors.

## For Christian Dads

**[All Pro Dad](https://www.allprodad.com)**, Family First's fatherhood program, pairs short practical articles with a weekly podcast; it is the most consistent dad-focused publishing operation in the space.

**[Dad Tired](https://dadtired.com)** is podcast-first rather than blog-first: Jerrad Lopes' ministry for Christian men on fatherhood, marriage, and discipleship, with weekly episodes, books, and an annual retreat. If your husband will listen but won't read, start here.

## Catholic and Orthodox Families

**[CatholicMom.com](https://www.catholicmom.com)** runs daily articles from a large volunteer roster of practicing Catholic writers: faith at home, prayer, and family life, with an editorial policy (updated April 2026) that keeps it human-written.

**[Messy Family Project](https://messyfamilyproject.org)** is Catholic marriage and family formation from Mike and Alicia Hernon: a long-running podcast (400+ episodes), courses, and parenting resources, active through late 2026.

**[Ancient Faith](https://www.ancientfaith.com)** is the Orthodox Christian hub: podcasts, publishing, daily devotional content, plus an Ancient Faith Kids line and a dedicated parenting topic, with new shows as recent as this week when we checked.

## Deeper Reading: Theology and Culture for Parents

Not parenting blogs, but the models and the mature readers both reach for them, and their family coverage is real.

**[The Gospel Coalition](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org)** publishes steady parenting-adjacent pieces inside its broader theology and culture output (recent examples run from AI-era proactive parenting to teen tech habits); there is no dedicated parenting section, so arrive via search or its podcasts.

**[Desiring God](https://www.desiringgod.org/topics/parenting)** keeps a deep parenting topic archive in the John Piper tradition, still adding articles through 2026: theological ballast for the whys underneath the daily hows.

**[Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com)** is the newsroom of the list: reporting and commentary across church life and culture, useful to parents as the place to understand the debates the other sites assume.

**[Plough](https://www.plough.com)** publishes narrative essays on faith, family, and society in print and online; the most literary writing in this roundup, and it takes outside essay pitches.

**[Ministry-to-Children](https://ministry-to-children.com)** publishes free Bible lessons, crafts, and teaching ideas for anyone who teaches kids, which in practice makes it as useful at the kitchen table as in a Sunday school classroom, and everything on it is genuinely free.

## The Ones We Could Not Verify

Several names that still circulate on older lists did not survive our November 2026 check. **Catholic Sprouts**, whose daily kids' podcast many families love, had no dated content newer than 2024 visible on its site (the podcast may well be current; the site does not show it). **(in)courage** and the **MomCo (formerly MOPS) blog** were unreachable or showed no confirmable 2026 publishing, and at least one much-recommended homeschool blog has visibly pivoted to other topics. None of that means these are gone for good; it means a list you are trusting for current recommendations should tell you when it last checked. This one will be re-verified when we update it.

### What are the best Christian mom blogs?

From our verified-active list: Risen Motherhood for gospel-centered depth and The Better Mom for broad encouragement, with Christian Parenting as the strongest multi-contributor site. All three were confirmed live and maintained in November 2026.

### Are there good Christian dad blogs?

Fewer than mom blogs, but yes: All Pro Dad for consistent practical articles and Dad Tired for a podcast-first ministry aimed squarely at Christian fathers. The dads' side of this niche is underserved, which is also why dad-voiced writing is in demand (see our [Christian writing opportunities](/blog/christian-writing-opportunities) guide if that is you).

### What are the best Christian homeschool blogs?

The Unlikely Homeschool, Hip Homeschool Moms, and Simple Homeschool, in that order for depth, breadth, and simplicity respectively. For choosing curriculum specifically, our [homeschool Bible curriculum comparison](/blog/homeschool-bible-curriculum-comparison) covers the Bible side in detail.

### Do homeschool bloggers earn from their blogs?

The established ones do, through curriculum affiliate programs and review partnerships; the verified current programs and rates are in our guide to [brands that work with homeschool bloggers](/blog/brands-that-work-with-homeschool-bloggers).

### What about blogs for parents of teenagers?

Rooted Ministry is the clear answer: actively publishing gospel-centered material on teens, anxiety, and social media, with a weekly parent roundup. Pair it with our own essays on [screen time and faith](/parenting) for the home front.

### What should Christian parents read about teens and technology?

Four sites split the job: Axis for discipling teens through culture (its weekly Culture Translator email is the single best subscription), Plugged In for reviews of specific movies, games, and channels, Protect Young Eyes for device and app safety, and Fuller Youth Institute for the research on why kids keep or lose faith.

### Are there good Catholic and Orthodox family sites?

Yes: CatholicMom.com for daily Catholic family writing, Messy Family Project for Catholic marriage and parenting formation, and Ancient Faith for Orthodox families, including its kids' line and parenting topic.
