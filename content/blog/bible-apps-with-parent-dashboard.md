If you want to see what your child watched and how they did, two Bible apps for kids offer a real parent dashboard: **Faithful Kids** (PIN-protected; shows each lesson watched, the quiz score, and the reflection answer, for up to five kid profiles) and **BibleBuddy Kids** ("parent dashboard with weekly progress" plus a weekly summary email). **Superbook** gives parents controls — child profiles, content you can hide, sharing you can switch off — but no progress view. **Guardians of Ancora** and **TruPlay** have profiles without a dashboard. **YouVersion's Bible App for Kids** and **Minno** have neither. Checked September 16, 2026.

Three different things get called "parental controls," and the difference decides whether an app answers the question you are actually asking:

- **Profiles** — each child has their own space, so a nine-year-old is not seeing a five-year-old's progress, and vice versa.
- **Controls** — what a parent can restrict: content, sharing, purchases, time.
- **A dashboard** — what a parent can *see*: which stories, when, and what the child got right.

Only the third one tells you whether anything is being learned. Here is who offers what.

## The two with a dashboard

**Faithful Kids** (ours). Each child gets a profile from a Netflix-style picker; the parent area sits behind an optional PIN. The dashboard shows, per child, every lesson watched, the score on the quiz that follows it, and what they answered on the reflection question. That last one is the part parents tell us they did not expect to care about: a nine-year-old's answer to "when have you felt like Jonah?" is a conversation starter you would not otherwise have. Up to five profiles on one family account. No ads; the subscription is bought by the parent and sits behind a birth-year gate in the iOS app, so there is nothing a child can trigger. Web and iOS. $12.99 a month or $97 a year on the web (7-day trial on annual), $8.99 a month or $79.99 a year in the iOS app (3-day trial). Free for churches.

**BibleBuddy Kids.** "Parent dashboard with weekly progress" and a "weekly parent summary email," alongside "3-question quizzes after every story." Made for ages 6 to 8, 82 stories, iOS only, $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year, 14-day trial. If a weekly email is the level of detail you want, and your child is six or seven, it is the cheaper option and does it well.

## Controls without a dashboard

**Superbook Kids Bible App** has the most thorough parental *controls* of any free app here: only adults can register, parents create the child profiles, and they can hide sensitive content, use parent gates, control who can view a child's profile and activity, and turn sharing and contact features off. What it does not have is a progress view — you can see what your child is allowed to do, not what they did.

**Guardians of Ancora** lets several children share one device with their own profiles, enforces usernames that are not real names, and has a report button. A parent account is optional and exists for updates, not oversight.

**TruPlay** offers "up to 10 profiles so members of your household can enjoy their own personalized experience," with "no chatrooms." No dashboard is described.

## Neither

**Bible App for Kids** (YouVersion) is free and ad-free and does not have profiles or a parent view; it is designed for ages 4 to 8, where the parent is usually sitting right there.

**Minno** lets a child pick an avatar and background colour, which functions as a profile, but there is no parent dashboard and no controls are described — it is a streaming catalog, and it behaves like one.

## What to look for, whichever you choose

- **Ask whether "controls" means restrict or see.** Most apps mean restrict. Only two here mean see.
- **Profiles matter more with two or more children.** A single shared progress bar is meaningless once a five-year-old and a ten-year-old both use it.
- **Check where the subscription screen lives.** On a child's device, the difference between a subscription the parent buys and one the child can reach is worth more than any dashboard. On Faithful Kids it is behind a parental gate; on the others, use the device's purchase restrictions.
- **A PIN on the parent area is not the same as a PIN on the app.** You want the first: the child can open the app alone, but cannot change settings or see other profiles.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Bible apps for kids have a parent dashboard?

Faithful Kids (per-child lessons watched, quiz scores, and reflection answers, behind a PIN) and BibleBuddy Kids (weekly progress and a weekly summary email). Superbook has parental controls but no progress view; the others have neither.

### Which Bible apps let me set up separate profiles for each child?

Faithful Kids (up to five), Superbook (parent creates child profiles), Guardians of Ancora (multiple on one device), and TruPlay (up to ten). Minno has avatars. Bible App for Kids does not describe profiles.

### Can I limit what my child sees?

Superbook has the most granular content controls of any app here. Faithful Kids does not need them in the same way — every lesson is a Bible story in order, with no feed, no comments and no external links — but the parent area is PIN-protected and there are no ads.

### Is there a free Bible app with a parent dashboard?

Not that we found. Superbook (free) has controls and profiles; Bible App for Kids (free) has neither. Faithful Kids is free for churches and kids' ministries.

### Does the dashboard show reflection answers or just scores?

Faithful Kids shows both: the quiz score and the tapped reflection answer for each lesson. BibleBuddy Kids reports weekly progress. No other app on this list reports what a child answered.
