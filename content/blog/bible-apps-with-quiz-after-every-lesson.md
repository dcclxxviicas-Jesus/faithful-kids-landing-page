Only two Bible apps for kids check whether a child understood the story they just watched: **Faithful Kids** (a quiz (three to five questions on most lessons) and a reflection question after every one of its 300+ video lessons, ages 5 to 15) and **BibleBuddy Kids** ("3-question quizzes after every story," 82 stories, ages 6 to 8). Superbook, YouVersion's Bible App for Kids, Minno and SunScool all have games or trivia somewhere in the app, but none of them asks a question at the end of each story, so a child can watch for an hour and you cannot tell what stuck.

That distinction is the whole reason this page exists. "Has quizzes" appears in the description of almost every Bible app. "Asks a few questions after every story and shows you the score" is a different product, and only two apps do it. Checked September 16, 2026.

## What "quiz after every story" means, and why it matters

A quiz in a separate games tab tests whether a child feels like playing a trivia game. A quiz that appears the moment a story ends tests whether they were paying attention to *that* story — and it works because it is short, it is expected, and it closes the loop before the next thing starts. Teachers call this a comprehension check; the rest of us call it "so what happened to Daniel?" at the dinner table, except the app asks so you do not have to.

Two things follow from it. The child learns to watch differently, because they know a question is coming. And the parent gets a number — this story, this score — instead of "did you like it?"

## The apps that have quizzes somewhere, but not after each story

**Superbook Kids Bible App** (free, ages 5 to 12) has "more than 20 fun Bible games," including trivia and multiple-choice quizzes inside its Daily Bible Quest activities, alongside 68 animated episodes. The quizzes are not attached to the episodes; a child can watch every episode without answering a question. Parents get profiles and content controls, but not a per-story score.

**Bible App for Kids** (YouVersion, free, ages 4 to 8) has trivia questions among its activities — Story Memory Match, Sticker Story Time, Story Mixup — as games a child chooses to play. No quiz follows a story, and there is no parent view.

**Minno** ($10.99 a month or $69.99 a year, ages 2 to 10) is a streaming service: shows and screen-free audio, no quizzes at all. It is the right pick for a family that wants a Christian TV catalog, not for checking what was learned.

**SunScool** (free, ages 3 and up) is built around puzzles — word searches, crosswords, unscrambles, coloring — with 600+ lessons. The activities *are* the lesson, which is a good format for a young child, but they are not a comprehension check on a story.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Bible apps for kids have a quiz after every story?

Two: Faithful Kids (a quiz of three to five questions on most lessons plus a reflection, after every video lesson, ages 5 to 15) and BibleBuddy Kids (three questions after every story, ages 6 to 8). Superbook, Bible App for Kids, Minno and SunScool have games or trivia in a separate area, not after each story.

### Do the quizzes make it feel like school?

They are three to five questions on most lessons, take about a minute, and give the same kind of feedback a game does — a streak, a score, a cheer. In our experience children start expecting the quiz and watch more carefully because of it. If a child dislikes it, Superbook's approach — watch freely, play trivia separately — is the alternative.

### Can I see my child's quiz scores?

On Faithful Kids, yes: the parent dashboard shows each lesson watched, the quiz score, and the reflection answer, per child. BibleBuddy Kids shows weekly progress and emails a weekly summary. No other app on this list reports scores to a parent.

### Is there a free Bible app with quizzes after each story?

Not that we found. The free apps — Bible App for Kids, Superbook, SunScool, Guardians of Ancora — put quizzes or puzzles in a games area rather than after each story. Faithful Kids is free for churches and kids' ministries, and its web library of 200 story pages with discussion questions is free for everyone.

### Are the quiz questions the same for a 6-year-old and a 12-year-old?

On Faithful Kids the questions are per lesson, not per age; younger children usually do them with a parent, and confident readers do them alone. BibleBuddy Kids writes for ages 6 to 8 throughout.
