Bible charades is played exactly like regular charades -- one player silently acts out a Bible character, story, or action while everyone else guesses -- and this page gives you the rules plus 70 ready-to-act prompts sorted by difficulty. Write the prompts on slips of paper, drop them in a bowl, and you have 30 minutes of family entertainment that doubles as Bible review, for zero dollars and zero prep beyond scissors.

It's the single best Bible game for wiggly kids, because the "distraction" (moving, performing, being silly) *is* the game. If your crew has energy to burn, this beats any worksheet ever printed.

## How Do You Play Bible Charades?

One player draws a prompt and acts it out with no words or sounds while their team guesses within a time limit -- usually 60 seconds. Full rules for family play:

1. **Cut and fold the prompts** below into a bowl (or three bowls by difficulty).
2. **Split into two teams** -- or skip teams entirely with little kids and just take turns, everyone guessing.
3. **The actor draws a slip**, silently reads it (read it quietly to pre-readers), and acts. No talking, no mouthing words, no pointing at objects in the room.
4. **Set a timer** -- 60 seconds standard, 90 for young kids.
5. **Score** one point per correct guess. First team to 10 wins, or just play until the bowl is empty.

Standard charades gestures kids pick up fast: hold up fingers for the number of words, tap your arm for which word you're acting, pinch fingers for "small word," tug your ear for "sounds like." With kids under 6, drop scoring entirely and let them act in pairs.

## What Variations Keep Bible Charades Fresh?

Three variations cover every group size and energy level -- rotate them so the game never gets stale.

**Bible Pictionary.** Same prompt slips, but the player draws on paper or a whiteboard instead of acting -- no letters or numbers allowed. Better for shy kids who'd rather hold a marker than perform, and stick-figure Goliaths are their own reward. Use the easy lists; drawing is harder than acting.

**Reverse charades.** The whole team acts *one* prompt together while a single player guesses. This is the best version for big groups and [Sunday school classes](/blog/sunday-school-games-for-kids) -- nobody stands alone, everyone moves at once, and six kids being a collapsing wall of Jericho is genuinely funny.

**Story chain.** For stories (35-52), each teammate acts one scene in sequence -- builder one does the ark, builder two the animals, builder three the rain -- and the other team guesses the full story. It quietly teaches narrative order, which is half of Bible literacy.

Charades also works best as *review*: kids act out stories they know, so if a prompt draws blank stares, that's tomorrow's story time -- families using [Faithful Kids](https://faithfulkids.app/quiz) often pull charades prompts straight from whichever video series their kids just finished. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do you play Bible charades with kids?
One player silently acts out a Bible character, story, or action while everyone else guesses within 60 seconds -- no words, sounds, or pointing at objects allowed. Write prompts on slips, split into teams for kids 7+, or skip scoring entirely with younger kids and simply take turns acting and guessing.

### What are good Bible charades words for young kids?
Characters with famous physical trademarks work best for ages 4-7: Noah building the ark, David slinging a stone, Goliath stomping, Jonah being swallowed, Moses parting the sea, and Zacchaeus climbing his tree. Stick to stories your kids already know -- charades reviews knowledge rather than teaching it.

### How long should a game of Bible charades last?
Twenty to thirty minutes is the sweet spot -- roughly 15-20 prompts with a 60-second timer each. End while kids still want more rather than playing the bowl empty; leftover slips seed next week's game. For parties, run it as one 20-minute station among several games.

### Can you play Bible charades with only two or three people?
Yes -- drop teams and play cooperatively: one person acts, the others guess, and you count total correct guesses as a family score to beat next time. With exactly two players, alternate actor and guesser and race the clock together. The reverse charades variation needs at least four.

### Is Bible charades good for Sunday school classes?
Yes -- it's one of the best zero-cost Sunday school games, especially as review of the day's lesson. Use reverse charades for classes of eight or more so the whole team acts while one child guesses, keep prompts limited to stories the class has covered, and let pre-readers have prompts whispered to them.

### What's the difference between Bible charades and Bible Pictionary?
Same prompts, different medium: charades is acted silently with the body, Pictionary is drawn on paper or a whiteboard with no letters or numbers. Charades favors energetic kids and needs zero supplies; Pictionary suits shy kids and quieter settings. Many families alternate rounds of each in one game night.
