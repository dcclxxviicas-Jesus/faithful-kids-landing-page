A Bible scavenger hunt turns Scripture into a game of searching and finding -- and below are 11 complete hunts with ready-to-use clue lists, so you can run one in the next fifteen minutes with paper, tape, and a Bible. There are indoor verse hunts for rainy Tuesdays, a creation hunt for the backyard, an Easter story hunt, a books-of-the-Bible hunt, and two designed for church buildings and Sunday school groups.

Scavenger hunts work because seeking is the whole point -- and that's not an accident. "You will seek me and find me when you seek me with all your heart" (Jeremiah 29:13). Kids who hunt for a verse remember it far longer than kids who are handed it.

## How Do You Run a Bible Scavenger Hunt?

Write clues on slips of paper, hide each clue where the previous one points, and put a small prize or a final verse at the end -- that's the whole mechanic. Three tips: keep it to 6-10 clues (attention dies around clue 12), read clues aloud for pre-readers, and for mixed ages let the youngest child grab each found clue while an older one reads it. Most hunts below take 15-25 minutes.

### 1. The Indoor Verse Hunt (Ages 4-10)
Write one word of a memory verse on each card and hide the cards around one room. Kids find all the cards, then race to arrange them in order -- use the Bible to check. Try "Your word is a lamp for my feet, a light on my path" (Psalm 119:105) -- 13 cards. Rhyming location clues if you want a trail version:
1. "Where you rest your sleepy head, the first clue waits upon your ___." (bed)
2. "Clue two is chilling, wait and see -- it's where the milk and butter be." (fridge)
3. "Splish and splash, then dry off up -- clue three hides beneath a bathroom cup." (bathroom)
4. "Where dirty clothes go round and round, the next clue's waiting to be found." (washing machine)
5. "Under the place where the family eats, you'll find the last of all your sheets." (kitchen table)

### 2. The Creation Nature Hunt (Ages 3-10)
Head to the backyard or park with this find-it list based on the days of creation in Genesis 1. Kids check off each item and shout which day it was made:
- Something the **light** touches (Day 1)
- Something the **sky** holds -- a cloud, the blue itself (Day 2)
- A **plant**, a **flower**, and something a **seed** made (Day 3)
- Evidence of the **sun** -- a shadow counts (Day 4)
- A **bird** or something that **swims** (Day 5)
- An **animal** or its footprint, and a **person** -- hug them (Day 6)
- Somewhere quiet to **rest** for ten seconds (Day 7)
Finish by reading Genesis 1:31 together: "God saw all that he had made, and it was very good."

### 3. The Easter Story Hunt (Ages 4-12)
Hide 8 objects, each standing for part of Holy Week, with a numbered card telling kids what to find next. When all 8 are found, line them up and retell the story:
1. A **leaf** -- palm branches on Palm Sunday (Matthew 21:8-9)
2. A **piece of bread** -- the Last Supper (Luke 22:19)
3. **Three coins** -- Judas's betrayal (Matthew 26:14-15)
4. A **cross** shape (two taped sticks) -- Good Friday (John 19:17-18)
5. A **strip of cloth** -- the burial linens (John 19:40)
6. A **rock** -- the stone rolled away (Matthew 28:2)
7. An **empty container** with a note inside: "He is not here; he has risen!" (Matthew 28:6)
8. A **candy or treat** -- the joy of resurrection morning
Empty plastic eggs work perfectly as containers if you have them from Easter.

### 4. The Books-of-the-Bible Hunt (Ages 6-12)
Write 10-15 book names on cards and hide them. Kids collect all the cards, then race a timer to put them in biblical order using the Bible's table of contents as the answer key. Start easy (Genesis, Exodus, Psalms, Matthew, Mark, Luke, John, Acts, Revelation) and add trickier books (Obadiah, Philemon, Habakkuk) as they improve. Beat the timer three weeks in a row and they've painlessly memorized the order -- it pairs well with the games in [Sunday school games for kids](/blog/sunday-school-games-for-kids).

### 5. The Church Building Hunt (Ages 5-12)
For Sunday school classes or family church tours -- kids find each spot and answer the question there:
1. Find the **baptistery or baptism spot**: what does baptism show? (Romans 6:4)
2. Find the **communion table**: what do the bread and cup remind us of? (Luke 22:19-20)
3. Find a **Bible in a pew or chair**: open to Psalm 100 and read verse 1 loudly
4. Find the **place where people give**: why do we give? (2 Corinthians 9:7)
5. Find a **cross** anywhere in the building: say one sentence about why it's here
6. Find the **pastor or a leader**: ask them their favorite Bible verse and write it down
7. Find the **main doors**: who are we supposed to go tell? (Matthew 28:19)
Get permission first, set boundaries on where kids can roam, and pair young kids with older ones.

### 6. The Sword Drill Verse Hunt (Ages 7-14)
The classic, competition-style: everyone holds a closed Bible ("swords up!"), you call a reference ("Philippians 4:13!"), and the first to find it stands and reads it aloud. Turn it into a hunt by hiding 8 reference cards around the room -- kids must find a card, then look up and copy the verse before hunting the next. Great references to use: Genesis 1:1, Psalm 23:1, Proverbs 3:5, Isaiah 41:10, Matthew 5:14, John 3:16, Romans 8:28, Philippians 4:13.

### 7. The Armor of God Hunt (Ages 5-10)
Hide six household items, each with a card naming its piece of armor from Ephesians 6:14-17: a belt (truth), a coat or apron (breastplate of righteousness), shoes (readiness of the gospel of peace), an oven mitt or pot lid (shield of faith), a hat (helmet of salvation), and a wrapping-paper tube (sword of the Spirit). Kids suit up in each piece as they find it -- fully armored kid at the end makes the photo of the week. ### 8. The Fruit of the Spirit Hunt (Ages 4-9)
Hide nine paper fruits (or real ones), each labeled with one fruit of the Spirit from Galatians 5:22-23: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control. As kids find each one, they must name one way to show it this week ("Kindness -- I'll let my sister pick the show"). All nine found and named, everyone gets a fruit snack.

### 9. The Christmas Nativity Hunt (Ages 3-10)
Hide the pieces of your nativity set around the house in early December, one clue leading to the next: find the shepherd near the sheep (stuffed animals), the wise men "in the east" (east side of the house), the angel up high, Mary and Joseph near a door ("no room at the inn"), and the manger last -- empty. Baby Jesus doesn't get hidden until Christmas Eve. Read Luke 2:1-20 as you place each found figure.

### 10. The Psalm 23 Picture Hunt (Ages 4-8)
Hide six pictures (draw stick figures -- artistry not required): a shepherd, green grass, still water, a dark valley, a table with food, a house. As kids find each picture, read the matching line of Psalm 23 and tape the picture to the wall in order. By the end, the whole psalm hangs on the wall in pictures -- leave it up for a week and littles will "read" it back to you.

### 11. The Kindness Hunt (Ages 5-12)
The only hunt where kids hide things instead of finding them: give each child five sticky notes with encouraging verses (like "Be kind and compassionate to one another" -- Ephesians 4:32) and have them secretly plant the notes where family members will find them all week -- lunchboxes, mirrors, pillows, Dad's laptop. The "win" is catching someone smiling at a note they found. This one flips seeking into serving.

## What Do You Need to Prepare?

Almost nothing -- paper, a pen, tape, a Bible, and ten minutes of hiding. A small prize at the end (a treat, picking the next family movie, staying up 15 minutes late) raises the energy, but for most kids the finding *is* the prize. Rotate hunts rather than repeating one -- variety is why this beats a worksheet, though on quieter days a [Bible word search](/blog/bible-word-search-for-kids) scratches a similar itch. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many clues should a Bible scavenger hunt have?
Six to ten clues is the sweet spot for kids ages 4-12. Fewer than five feels over too fast, and past twelve, younger kids lose steam. For mixed ages, use 8 clues and assign roles: youngest kids grab the found clues, older kids read them, everyone hunts.

### What is a Bible verse scavenger hunt?
A Bible verse scavenger hunt hides the words of one verse -- one word per card -- around a room, and kids find all the cards and reassemble the verse in order. It's the fastest hunt to set up and one of the most effective memory-verse tools, because kids handle and arrange every word.

### How do you do a Bible scavenger hunt indoors?
Hide word cards or clue slips in one or two rooms, using rhyming clues that point from one hiding spot to the next -- bed, fridge, couch cushions, washing machine. Keep hiding spots at kid height, cap it at 8 clues, and put a treat or the final verse at the end of the trail.

### What ages are Bible scavenger hunts good for?
Ages 3-12, with adjustments: pre-readers need picture clues or an adult reading aloud, ages 5-9 are the sweet spot for classic clue trails, and tweens prefer competitive formats like sword drills, timed books-of-the-Bible ordering, or team-versus-team hunts.

### Can I run a Bible scavenger hunt for a large church group?
Yes -- split kids into teams of 3-5 with an adult per team, and either run identical parallel clue trails or use station-based hunts like the church building hunt where teams visit stations in a different order. Get staff permission, mark off-limits areas clearly, and set a time limit of 20-30 minutes.

### What prizes work for Bible scavenger hunts?
Small and immediate beats big and rare: a treat at the trail's end, choosing the next family movie, a later bedtime, or stickers for younger kids. For church groups, a group prize (popsicles for everyone when all teams finish) avoids tears and keeps the focus on the fun of finding.
