## Hunt 1: The Bible Verse Scavenger Hunt (Ages 7+)

This is a sword drill wearing a party hat. Each clue names a verse; the answer is *in* the verse, so finding it means actually reading Scripture. First player or team to answer all twelve wins.

1. What did God put in the sky as a promise? — **Genesis 9:13**
2. How many smooth stones did David pick up? — **1 Samuel 17:40**
3. What was Jonah inside for three days and nights? — **Jonah 1:17**
4. What did the dove bring back to Noah? — **Genesis 8:11**
5. Where was Jesus born? — **Luke 2:4-7**
6. What fell from heaven for Israel to eat each morning? — **Exodus 16:14-15**
7. What did Jesus use to feed five thousand people? — **John 6:9**
8. Who climbed a tree to see Jesus? — **Luke 19:2-4**
9. What shut the lions' mouths in the den? — **Daniel 6:22**
10. What did the shepherds find lying in a manger? — **Luke 2:16**
11. How many days did it rain on Noah's ark? — **Genesis 7:12**
12. What did Moses see burning that was not burned up? — **Exodus 3:2**

**How to run it:** one Bible per player or team, clues face down, flip and go. For kids still learning the books, tape a [books of the Bible chart](/blog/books-of-the-bible-printable) to the table -- half the skill being built here is just *finding* 1 Samuel. (The answers are in the PDF's answer key, on its own page.)

## Hunt 2: The Around-the-House Bible Hunt (Ages 3-7)

Little kids can't look up verses, but they can absolutely find "something that floats, like Noah's ark." Each object connects to a story, which makes the debrief the best part: when everything's gathered, pick one object and tell its story together.

- Something that **floats** — like Noah's ark
- Something that **gives light** — like the star over Bethlehem
- A **small smooth stone** — like David's
- Something **made of wood** — like the manger
- A piece of **bread or a cracker** — like the loaves Jesus shared
- Something with **all the rainbow's colors** — like God's promise
- A **toy animal** that was on the ark — two is better
- Something a **shepherd** could use — a stick, a blanket, a bell
- A **coin** — like the lost coin in Jesus' story
- Something that **holds water** — like the jars at the wedding in Cana
- A **seed**, or something a seed becomes — like the mustard seed
- **Your Bible** — bring it back and pick tonight's story

## Hunt 3: The Church Photo Scavenger Hunt (Youth Groups)

One phone per team, twenty minutes, and every photo must include the whole team. This is the loud one -- schedule it *before* the lesson, not after, because nobody lands a plane after Jericho jumping photos.

1. The whole team by the church sign (or front door)
2. Someone holding a Bible open to **Psalms** — the exact middle
3. A **cross** anywhere in the building — the less obvious, the better
4. The team acting out **David and Goliath**, frozen mid-scene
5. Something that holds water (bonus points for the baptistery)
6. A team member shaking hands with **someone over 60**
7. The verse **John 3:16** — printed, on a wall, or handwritten
8. The team recreating the **fiery furnace**: three standing, one "angel"
9. Something with a **dove, lamb, or fish** on it
10. A hymnal or songbook opened to a song about **grace**
11. The whole team pointing at a picture or window of **Jesus**
12. Everyone **jumping at once** out front — the walls of Jericho coming down

**Judging:** most photos wins; tiebreaker is the most creative Goliath face. Review the photos together on a screen afterwards -- that's ten minutes of guaranteed laughing, and it's also your attendance record.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you do a Bible scavenger hunt?**
Pick the format that fits your kids: a verse hunt (clues answered by looking up Scripture) for confident readers, an object hunt for younger children, or a photo hunt for teens. Set a time limit, play in teams of two or three, and end by connecting what was found back to the stories.

**What is a good Bible scavenger hunt for youth group?**
The photo hunt above is built for it: twelve photo challenges around the church building, twenty minutes, whole team in every shot. It burns energy, builds teams, and produces photos the group will want to see -- which conveniently gathers everyone back for the lesson.

**Can you do a Bible scavenger hunt indoors?**
Yes -- the verse hunt happens entirely at a table, and the object hunt works in any house or classroom. Only the photo hunt needs room to roam, and even that stays inside one building.
