Bible scripture printables are verses set in large type and laid out for a wall, a door, a lunchbox, or a card ring -- and the trick to making good ones is picking verses short enough to read from across a room. Below are 25 verses that work, sorted by where they belong in a house, each with a described layout you can build in any word processor in about five minutes.

Not every great verse makes great wall art. Romans 8 is magnificent and unprintable at 5 inches wide. What follows are the verses that survive the size constraint, plus the layout that fits each one.

## What Makes a Verse Work as a Printable?

Three rules cover almost everything:

- **Under 20 words.** Anything longer shrinks the font past the point of being read in passing, which defeats the purpose of hanging it.
- **One idea, not two.** A verse with a "therefore" in the middle needs the whole context to make sense and belongs in a study, not on a wall.
- **Always cite it.** Reference in smaller type below the verse, every time. A quote without a citation teaches kids that Scripture is a slogan.

For sizing: 8x10 inches for a frame, 5x7 for a shelf or nightstand, 4x6 for a door or a light switch, and 2.5x3.5 (playing card size) for a card ring or a lunchbox. Print wall pieces on card stock or matte photo paper; card stock is essential for anything a child will handle daily.

## Verses for Bedrooms and Bedtime (1-6)

These go on a nightstand, a door, or the wall a child sees from bed. Keep them calm -- soft type, lots of white space, no exclamation points.

1. **"When I am afraid, I put my trust in you."** (Psalm 56:3) The single best verse for a scared child's wall. Layout: centered, one line per phrase, "I put my trust in you" in the largest weight. Works beautifully at 5x7 on a nightstand.

2. **"In peace I will lie down and sleep."** (Psalm 4:8) Layout: horizontal 5x7, the words spread across the bottom third with the top two thirds left as open sky or a simple line-drawn moon. The empty space is the design.

3. **"The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."** (Psalm 23:1) Layout: 8x10 portrait, verse in the center, and if you want an illustration, a single line-art sheep small and low. Resist filling the page.

4. **"I praise you because I am fearfully and wonderfully made."** (Psalm 139:14) Layout: put "fearfully and wonderfully made" on its own oversized line and the rest in smaller type above it. This one belongs at a child's eye level, not adult eye level.

5. **"The LORD your God is with you."** (Zephaniah 3:17) Layout: 4x6 for a door or a light switch plate. Five words, huge, one line if it fits. The full verse is longer and lovely, but this phrase is the printable.

6. **"Come to me, all you who are weary."** (Matthew 11:28) Layout: 5x7, verse across two lines, reference in small caps below. A good one for a teenager's room, where "weary" lands differently than it does at age 6.

## Verses for Courage and Hard Days (7-12)

These go where a kid is about to do something difficult: the back of a bedroom door, inside a locker, taped inside a binder.

7. **"Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid."** (Joshua 1:9) The most-printed verse in Christian homes for good reason. Layout: 8x10, "BE STRONG AND COURAGEOUS" in heavy caps filling the top half, the rest in light type below.

8. **"Do not fear, for I am with you."** (Isaiah 41:10) Layout: 4x6 card for a backpack pocket. Six words, bold, no illustration.

9. **"I can do all this through him who gives me strength."** (Philippians 4:13) Layout: 8x10 for a wall or 2.5x3.5 for a card. Set "through him" in a contrasting weight -- that phrase is the whole verse and is the part people drop.

10. **"God is our refuge and strength, an ever-present help in trouble."** (Psalm 46:1) Layout: 5x7 landscape, three stacked lines broken at the commas. Good for a hallway or a family entryway.

11. **"They will soar on wings like eagles."** (Isaiah 40:31) Layout: 8x10 with the text in the lower third and a single wide line-art wing or horizon above. The most illustratable verse on this list.

12. **"The Spirit God gave us does not make us timid."** (2 Timothy 1:7) Layout: 4x6, one sentence, no decoration. Aimed squarely at middle schoolers, who need this one on the inside of a locker door.

## Verses About Love and Kindness (13-18)

These go in shared spaces: the kitchen, the hallway, the bathroom mirror, the spot where siblings argue.

13. **"Love is patient, love is kind."** (1 Corinthians 13:4) Layout: 8x10, the two phrases stacked and centered, huge. The most reprinted verse in the English-speaking world and still a good printable.

14. **"Be kind and compassionate to one another."** (Ephesians 4:32) Layout: 5x7 landscape for a kitchen wall or the back of a bathroom door. Consider printing it twice and putting one in each kid's room.

15. **"We love because he first loved us."** (1 John 4:19) Layout: 5x7, one line if your paper is wide enough, with "he first" emphasized. Short enough for a light switch plate at 3x3.

16. **"A friend loves at all times."** (Proverbs 17:17) Layout: 2.5x3.5 card. This is a card, not wall art -- perfect for a lunchbox on a week when friendship is hard.

17. **"For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son."** (John 3:16) Layout: 8x10, five short lines with the line breaks at natural pauses. At 20 words this is the maximum length that still works framed.

18. **"Let the little children come to me."** (Matthew 19:14) Layout: 5x7 for a nursery, playroom, or Sunday school classroom door. Warm type, wide margins.

## Verses for Gratitude, Joy, and Mornings (19-22)

These go in the kitchen and near the breakfast table, where the day gets set.

19. **"This is the day the LORD has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it."** (Psalm 118:24) Layout: 5x7 landscape above the breakfast table. Break the line at the semicolon.

20. **"Give thanks in all circumstances."** (1 Thessalonians 5:18) Layout: 4x6, four words, maximum size. Excellent as a November piece and worth leaving up in December.

21. **"His mercies are new every morning."** (Lamentations 3:22-23) Layout: 5x7, text lower third, plain horizon line above. A rare case where a two-verse citation is worth it because the phrase everyone knows spans both.

22. **"Rejoice always, pray continually."** (1 Thessalonians 5:16-17) Layout: 2.5x3.5 card or a 4x6 for a mirror. Two commands, two lines, no ornament.

## Verses for a Family Wall or Entryway (23-25)

These are the statement pieces, the ones that go at the front door or over a mantel where guests see them.

23. **"As for me and my household, we will serve the LORD."** (Joshua 24:15) Layout: 8x10 or larger, portrait, four lines with "we will serve the LORD" as the anchor line. The classic entryway verse.

24. **"Trust in the LORD with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding."** (Proverbs 3:5) Layout: 8x10 portrait, three lines, reference at the bottom. If you want the full thought, add verse 6 in smaller type beneath.

25. **"The LORD bless you and keep you."** (Numbers 6:24) Layout: 5x7 or 8x10, one line, wide letter spacing, generous margins. Traditionally the last word spoken over a family -- a fitting one for a door people leave through.

## How Do You Turn These Into Cards Instead of Wall Art?

Set the same verses at 2.5 by 3.5 inches, print eight to a sheet of card stock, cut, punch one corner, and put them on a binder ring. A card ring is the highest-value scripture printable there is, because a framed verse gets absorbed into the wallpaper of a house within a month while a card ring keeps getting picked up.

Three ways families actually use the ring:

- **Lunchbox rotation.** One card a week, tucked in the lunchbox. Kids never mention it and always notice.
- **Car cards.** Keep the ring in the seat pocket; a red light is a review.
- **The bedtime card.** One verse read aloud at lights out, same card all week. Ours lives with the [Bedtime Bible Kit](/printables/bedtime-bible-kit), a free seven-night set of short readings designed for winding down, and pairing the two means the verse on the card is the verse from the story.

## Design Notes That Make a Printable Look Made, Not Printed

- **Two fonts, maximum.** One for the verse, one for the reference. Three fonts is where homemade starts to look homemade.
- **Left-align or center, never both.** Pick one per piece and hold it.
- **Grayscale is fine, and often better.** A black-on-white verse in a cheap frame looks intentional; a five-color one usually does not.
- **Margins wider than you think.** A half inch of white space on every side is the difference between a poster and a page.
- **Print the reference smaller, never smaller than 10pt.** It has to stay readable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Bible verses work best as printable wall art?

Short ones -- under 20 words, expressing a single idea. Psalm 56:3, Joshua 1:9, Philippians 4:13, and Proverbs 3:5 are the reliable workhorses because each fits on one line of large type and makes sense without surrounding context. Long passages like Romans 8:28-39 or the full Beatitudes are wonderful to read and nearly impossible to hang legibly.

### What size should scripture printables be?

Use 8x10 inches for framed wall art, 5x7 for a shelf or nightstand, 4x6 for a door or light switch, and 2.5x3.5 for pocket and lunchbox cards. Standard frame sizes matter more than they sound -- an 8x10 fits a $4 frame from any store, while a custom size means a custom frame. Print cards eight to a sheet of card stock to save paper.

### Do I need special paper or a special printer?

No. A home inkjet and 65lb white card stock handle every piece on this list. Matte photo paper looks noticeably better framed and costs a few dollars more for a pack, and plain copy paper is fine for anything you will replace seasonally. What actually matters is card stock for cards, because plain paper cards go soft within a week of handling.

### Which Bible translation should I print?

Whichever translation your family and church actually read, so the printed version matches what your kids hear. Consistency helps memorization more than the choice between the NIV, ESV, CSB, or NKJV does. Whichever you pick, note it if you are giving printables away, and always include the book, chapter, and verse.

### Can I sell scripture printables I make?

Most major translations allow limited quotation for non-commercial use but require a license for commercial products, so check the copyright terms of your specific translation before selling anything. Public-domain translations such as the KJV and ASV carry no such restriction. Printing for your own home, church, or classroom is not the issue -- selling is.

### How often should I change scripture printables on the wall?

Every one to three months for verses in kids' rooms, and leave the entryway or family-wall pieces up long-term. Verses become invisible with familiarity, and swapping a bedroom piece each season restores the attention that made you hang it. An easy rhythm: rotate the child's room verse whenever they finish memorizing it, so the wall and the card ring move together.
