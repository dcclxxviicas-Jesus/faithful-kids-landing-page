The best Bible stories about friendship for kids are David and Jonathan, Ruth and Naomi, and Jesus calling His disciples friends -- stories where loyalty costs something and someone pays it gladly. Below are 8 of these friendships retold simply for ages 5-12, each with the friendship lesson drawn out and a conversation question to ask your child.

## Why Teach Kids About Friendship Through Bible Stories?

Friendship is the arena where kids live out their faith first. Long before a child faces a career decision or a crisis of belief, they will face a friend who betrays a secret, a lunch table that excludes someone, and the pull to drop an old friend for a more popular one. Stories prepare them for those moments better than rules do, because stories show what loyalty actually looks like when it's expensive. The Bible's friendships are strikingly costly ones -- Jonathan giving up a throne, Ruth giving up a homeland -- and that's exactly the standard worth planting early: a real friend is someone who stays when staying costs something.

### 1. David and Jonathan (1 Samuel 18-20)

Jonathan was the crown prince, next in line to be king -- and David was the boy God had chosen to be king instead. By every rule of palaces, they should have been enemies. Instead, Jonathan loved David "as his own soul," gave him his royal robe and sword, and when his father King Saul plotted to kill David, Jonathan warned him with a secret arrow signal and helped him escape. Jonathan chose his friend over his own throne.

**The friendship lesson:** Real friends want what's best for you even when it costs them. Jonathan is the Bible's picture of a friend without jealousy.

**Ask your child:** "Jonathan gave up being king for his friend. What's the biggest thing you'd give up for yours?" (Full retelling: [David and Jonathan for kids](/blog/david-and-jonathan-for-kids).)

### 2. Ruth and Naomi (Ruth 1-4)

After famine and death left Naomi a widow with no sons, she told her widowed daughters-in-law to go home and rebuild their lives. Orpah went. Ruth stayed, with the most famous loyalty speech in Scripture: "Where you go I will go, and where you stay I will stay. Your people will be my people and your God my God." Ruth followed Naomi to a foreign country, gleaned grain in the fields to feed them both, and God wove her into the family line of King David -- and of Jesus.

**The friendship lesson:** Friendship isn't only for people your own age. Ruth's loyalty to a grieving older woman changed history.

**Ask your child:** "Orpah wasn't bad -- she just left. What made Ruth's choice different?" (More: [Ruth, a story of loyalty](/blog/ruth-a-story-of-loyalty-for-kids).)

### 3. Jesus and His Disciples (John 15:12-15)

At the last supper, Jesus told the twelve men who had followed Him for three years: "I no longer call you servants... I have called you friends." Then He defined friendship forever: "Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one's life for one's friends." The next day, He did exactly that.

**The friendship lesson:** The God of the universe wanted friends, not just followers. Kids who know Jesus called ordinary fishermen "friends" understand that He offers them the same.

**Ask your child:** "What's the difference between a servant and a friend?" (Meet all twelve in [the twelve apostles for kids](/blog/the-twelve-apostles-for-kids).)

### 4. The Four Friends and the Paralyzed Man (Mark 2:1-12)

A paralyzed man had four friends who believed Jesus could heal him -- but the house where Jesus taught was packed to the doorway. So they climbed onto the roof, dug a hole through it, and lowered their friend down on his mat in front of everyone. Mark records the detail that matters: "When Jesus saw *their* faith," He healed the man.

**The friendship lesson:** Sometimes a friend can't get to Jesus on their own, and your job is to carry them. This is the Bible's best story about friends who refuse to give up.

**Ask your child:** "Whose faith did Jesus notice? What does that tell you about what friends can do for each other?"

### 5. Moses and Aaron (Exodus 4-17)

When God called Moses at the burning bush, Moses was terrified of public speaking -- so God sent his brother Aaron to stand beside him and be his voice. Aaron spoke to Pharaoh when Moses couldn't. Later, when Israel battled the Amalekites, Moses' arms grew too tired to hold up the staff of God -- so Aaron and Hur held his arms up until sunset, and the battle was won.

**The friendship lesson:** Good friends cover each other's weaknesses. Nobody is meant to hold their arms up alone.

**Ask your child:** "What's something a friend is good at that you're not -- and the other way around?"

### 6. Elijah and Elisha (2 Kings 2)

Elisha was Elijah's student and closest companion. On Elijah's final day on earth, the old prophet three times told Elisha to stay behind, and three times Elisha refused: "As surely as the Lord lives, I will not leave you." He stayed until the chariot of fire carried Elijah into heaven -- and he received the double portion of Elijah's spirit he had asked for.

**The friendship lesson:** Some friendships pass something on. An older friend or mentor can hand you things no classmate can.

**Ask your child:** "Who's an older person in your life who teaches you the way Elijah taught Elisha?"

### 7. Mary and Elizabeth (Luke 1:39-56)

When the angel told teenage Mary she would carry God's Son, the first thing she did was hurry to the hill country to see her older cousin Elizabeth -- the one other woman on earth carrying a miracle baby. Elizabeth greeted her with a blessing instead of doubt, the baby in her womb leapt for joy, and Mary burst into song. Mary stayed three months.

**The friendship lesson:** When something huge happens -- wonderful or frightening -- you need a friend who believes you. Elizabeth believed Mary before anyone else did. (The full story starts at [an angel visits Mary](/blog/an-angel-visits-mary-for-kids).)

**Ask your child:** "Why do you think Mary went to Elizabeth first instead of anyone else?"

### 8. Paul and Barnabas (Acts 9-15)

When the ex-persecutor Saul tried to join the Christians in Jerusalem, everyone was afraid of him -- except Barnabas, whose name means "son of encouragement." Barnabas vouched for him, tracked him down years later in Tarsus, and brought him into ministry in Antioch. They traveled and preached together across the ancient world. They even had a sharp disagreement later and split up -- and God used both of them anyway.

**The friendship lesson:** Barnabas believed in someone everyone else had written off. And even good friends sometimes disagree -- the story of [when Paul and Barnabas split](/blog/paul-and-barnabas-split-for-kids) shows kids that conflict doesn't erase what a friendship built.

**Ask your child:** "Is there a kid other people avoid who might just need one Barnabas?"

## How Do You Teach These Friendship Stories at Home?

1. **Read or watch one story a week.** Short and repeated beats long and once.
2. **Ask "What did the friendship cost?"** Jonathan's throne, Ruth's homeland, a stranger's roof. The cost is where the lesson lives.
3. **Flip it to their world.** "Who's your Jonathan?" is a better question than "Are you a good friend?" -- and "Who are you a Jonathan to?" is better still.
4. **Use the stories at friendship flashpoints.** A falling-out at school is the moment to revisit Paul and Barnabas; a lonely lunch season is the moment for Barnabas vouching for Saul.
5. **Anchor with a verse.** Proverbs 17:17 -- "A friend loves at all times" -- fits every story here, and our list of [Bible verses about friendship for kids](/blog/bible-verses-about-friendship-for-kids) has a dozen more worth memorizing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Bible story about friendship for kids?

David and Jonathan. It has everything kids need to see: loyalty under pressure, zero jealousy, a secret signal, and a friend who protects rather than competes. It also raises the question every child eventually faces -- will you stay loyal to a friend when it costs you something?

### Who were best friends in the Bible?

The clearest pairs are David and Jonathan, Ruth and Naomi, Elijah and Elisha, Paul and Barnabas, and Mary and Elizabeth. Jesus also had an inner circle -- Peter, James, and John -- and John is called "the disciple whom Jesus loved," which suggests even Jesus had an especially close friend.

### What does the Bible say a good friend is?

Three verses give the core: "A friend loves at all times" (Proverbs 17:17), "there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother" (Proverbs 18:24), and "greater love has no one than this: to lay down one's life for one's friends" (John 15:13). In short -- constant, closer than family, and sacrificial.

### How do I help my child choose good friends using the Bible?

Teach the pattern, not a checklist: a good friend makes you braver and kinder (Jonathan), stays in hard seasons (Ruth), and tells you the truth (Nathan with David). Then ask the honest question 1 Corinthians 15:33 raises -- "bad company corrupts good character" -- gently: "After you spend time with them, are you more like Jesus or less?"

### What Bible story helps a child who is lonely or left out?

Barnabas and Saul. Everyone avoided Saul, and one person's welcome changed his life. It comforts a lonely child (God sends Barnabases) and commissions a kind one (you can be someone's Barnabas). The four friends and the roof also reassures kids that being carried by others in a hard season is part of God's design.

### What age are these friendship stories appropriate for?

Ages 5-12. Younger kids (5-7) connect fastest with the four friends on the roof and Ruth's "where you go I will go." Older kids (8-12) are ready for the complicated ones -- Jonathan choosing a friend over his father's wishes, and Paul and Barnabas disagreeing without becoming enemies.
