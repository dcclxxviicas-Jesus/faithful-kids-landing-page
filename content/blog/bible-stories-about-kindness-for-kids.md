The best Bible stories about kindness for kids are the Good Samaritan, Ruth and Boaz, David and Mephibosheth, and Jesus washing His disciples' feet -- stories where someone shows costly, unexpected kindness to a person who couldn't pay it back. Below are 10 of these stories retold simply for ages 5-12, each with the kindness lesson made clear and a conversation question to ask your child.

## Why Teach Kids About Kindness Through Bible Stories?

Every parent says "be kind," and every kid nods and forgets it by recess. Stories work where reminders don't, because they show what kindness looks like when it's inconvenient -- which is the only time kindness really counts. Notice the pattern below: biblical kindness almost always crosses a line somebody else drew. The Samaritan helps a man from the "wrong" group. Jesus touches the untouchable. David honors his enemy's grandson. Teach a child that pattern and you've taught them something sturdier than politeness: kindness is doing something for someone who can't repay you.

### 1. The Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25-37)

A traveler was beaten by robbers and left half-dead on the Jericho road. A priest saw him and crossed to the other side. A temple worker did the same. Then a Samaritan -- a man from a group the audience despised -- stopped, bandaged the stranger's wounds, carried him on his own donkey to an inn, and paid the bill with a promise to cover more. Jesus ended the story with a command that still stings: "Go and do likewise."

**The kindness lesson:** Kindness doesn't check whether someone is in your group first. The hero is the person who stops.

**Ask your child:** "The priest probably had good excuses. Do excuses ever make unkindness okay?" (Full retelling: [the Good Samaritan for kids](/blog/the-good-samaritan-for-kids).)

### 2. Ruth and Naomi (Ruth 1)

When Naomi lost her husband and both sons, she was an old widow with nothing to offer anyone. Ruth, her daughter-in-law, refused to leave her side -- moving to a foreign land and gleaning leftover grain in the hot fields every day so an old woman she owed nothing would eat.

**The kindness lesson:** The truest kindness goes to people who can do nothing for you in return. Ruth got no applause -- just a hungry mother-in-law fed, day after day.

**Ask your child:** "Ruth's kindness was mostly chores. Can chores be kindness?"

### 3. Boaz and Ruth (Ruth 2-4)

Boaz, the wealthy owner of the field where Ruth gleaned, noticed the foreign widow gathering leftovers. The law required him to leave the edges of his field for the poor -- but Boaz went far past the law: he told his workers to drop extra grain on purpose, invited Ruth to eat with them, protected her, and eventually married her and redeemed Naomi's whole family line.

**The kindness lesson:** Kindness does more than the minimum. Anyone can follow the rule; Boaz dropped extra grain on purpose.

**Ask your child:** "What's a 'minimum' you could beat this week -- doing more than what's required?" (More: [Ruth and Boaz](/blog/ruth-and-boaz-for-kids).)

### 4. Jesus Heals the Man with Leprosy (Mark 1:40-42)

Lepers in Jesus' day were untouchable -- required by law to live outside town and shout "Unclean!" so no one came near. One knelt before Jesus: "If you are willing, you can make me clean." Jesus could have healed him with a word from a distance. Instead, Mark records, He "reached out his hand and touched him." It was likely the first touch the man had felt in years.

**The kindness lesson:** Jesus' kindness was specific -- He gave the man exactly what he'd been starved of. Kindness pays attention to what a person actually needs.

**Ask your child:** "Why do you think Jesus touched him instead of just speaking?"

### 5. The Boy Who Shared His Lunch (John 6:1-13)

Five thousand hungry people, one remote hillside, and the disciples' math said feeding them was impossible. The only food anyone could find was one boy's lunch: five small barley loaves and two fish. He handed it over. Jesus gave thanks, multiplied it, and fed the entire crowd -- with twelve baskets left over.

**The kindness lesson:** No act of kindness is too small for God to multiply. The boy's lunch is every kid's invitation: bring what you have.

**Ask your child:** "What's in your 'lunch' -- something small you could offer someone this week?" (Full story: [feeding the five thousand](/blog/feeding-the-five-thousand-for-kids).)

### 6. David and Mephibosheth (2 Samuel 9)

After King Saul spent years trying to kill David, David finally took the throne. New kings usually eliminated the old king's family. David did the opposite -- he asked, "Is there anyone left of Saul's house to whom I can show kindness?" There was: Jonathan's son Mephibosheth, disabled in both feet and hiding in fear. David gave him back his grandfather's land and a permanent seat at the king's own table.

**The kindness lesson:** Kindness to an enemy's family, kept as a promise to a friend -- this is kindness as strength, not weakness.

**Ask your child:** "David went looking for someone to be kind to. What would happen if you started your day that way?" (More: [David and Mephibosheth](/blog/david-and-mephibosheth-for-kids).)

### 7. Jesus Welcomes the Children (Mark 10:13-16)

Parents brought their little kids to Jesus for a blessing, and the disciples shooed them away. Jesus was indignant: "Let the little children come to me, and do not hinder them, for the kingdom of God belongs to such as these." Then He took the kids in His arms and blessed them one by one.

**The kindness lesson:** Kindness treats "unimportant" people as important. Jesus had time for the people everyone else waved off -- including kids.

**Ask your child:** "Who gets treated as unimportant at your school?"

### 8. Elisha and the Widow's Oil (2 Kings 4:1-7)

A widow came to Elisha desperate: her husband was dead, the debts were due, and the creditor was coming to take her two sons as slaves. All she had was one small jar of oil. Elisha told her to borrow every empty jar in the neighborhood and start pouring -- and the oil kept flowing until every jar was full. She sold it, paid the debt, and kept her sons.

**The kindness lesson:** Kindness moves toward desperate people instead of away from them. Elisha involved the whole neighborhood's empty jars -- kindness often recruits helpers. (Full retelling: [the widow's oil](/blog/the-widows-oil-for-kids).)

**Ask your child:** "The neighbors' only job was lending jars. Can lending something be kindness?"

### 9. Barnabas the Encourager (Acts 4, 9, 11)

His real name was Joseph, but the apostles renamed him Barnabas -- "son of encouragement" -- because encouraging people is simply what he did. He sold a field and gave the money to believers in need, and he vouched for Saul when every Christian in Jerusalem was afraid of him.

**The kindness lesson:** Words are kindness too. Barnabas got a whole new name just for building people up.

**Ask your child:** "If people renamed you for the thing you do most, what would your name be?"

### 10. Jesus Washes the Disciples' Feet (John 13:1-17)

Foot-washing was the lowest servant's job. At the last supper, with no servant present, none of the disciples volunteered. So the King of kings wrapped a towel around His waist and washed all twenty-four feet -- including the feet of Judas, who would betray Him within hours. "I have set you an example," He said, "that you should do as I have done for you."

**The kindness lesson:** No act of kindness is beneath you. If Jesus can do the grossest job in the room, so can we.

**Ask your child:** "What's the 'foot-washing job' at our house -- and who usually gets stuck with it?"

## How Do You Teach These Kindness Stories at Home?

1. **One story a week, five minutes.** Read or watch it together, then talk.
2. **Ask "What did it cost?"** The Samaritan's money, Ruth's sweat, Jesus' dignity at the basin. Free kindness is rare in the Bible; noticing the cost is the lesson.
3. **Ask "Who was surprised?"** Biblical kindness almost always lands on someone who didn't expect it. Train your child to look for the person nobody else is being kind to.
4. **Assign a secret mission.** After each story, challenge your child to one matching act -- share a "lunch," write a Barnabas note, do the foot-washing job unasked -- and report back at dinner.
5. **Memorize one verse.** Ephesians 4:32 ("Be kind and compassionate to one another") covers the whole list, and our collection of [Bible verses about kindness for kids](/blog/bible-verses-about-kindness-for-kids) has more to grow on.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Bible story about kindness for kids?

The Good Samaritan. Jesus designed it as the definitive answer to "Who is my neighbor?", and it works at every age -- a five-year-old sees a stranger helping a hurt man, while a preteen can wrestle with why the religious professionals walked past and the outsider stopped.

### What does the Bible actually say about kindness?

Kindness is a fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22), commanded directly ("Be kind and compassionate to one another" -- Ephesians 4:32), and modeled by God Himself (Psalm 103:8). In Scripture, kindness isn't a personality trait; it's evidence of God's Spirit at work in anyone.

### How do I teach kindness to a child who struggles with it?

Shrink the target. "Be kind" is vague; "drop extra grain on purpose like Boaz" is a mission. Give one concrete, story-linked challenge per week and celebrate it at dinner. Kids repeat what gets noticed, and small wins compound faster than lectures.

### What's the difference between kindness and being nice?

Niceness is pleasant and costs nothing; kindness acts and usually costs something. The priest in the Good Samaritan story may have been perfectly nice -- he just didn't stop. Teaching kids this difference matters, because the Bible never commands niceness, but it commands kindness constantly.

### Which kindness story is best for preschoolers?

The boy who shared his lunch. It stars a child, involves food (preschoolers relate), and the lesson is concrete: share what you have and watch God do big things with it.

### What age are these kindness stories appropriate for?

Ages 5-12. Younger kids (5-7) will connect with the shared lunch, the welcomed children, and the Good Samaritan. Older kids (8-12) are ready for the layered ones -- David honoring his enemy's grandson, and Jesus washing the feet of the man about to betray Him.
