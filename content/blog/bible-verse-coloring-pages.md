A Bible verse coloring page is a single verse hand-lettered in outline form, surrounded by a simple motif a child can fill in -- and the reason it works better than a plain memory card is that a kid who spends fifteen minutes coloring the words of Psalm 56:3 has read that verse thirty times without being asked to once. This guide gives you 30 verses that are genuinely well-suited to lettering and coloring, grouped by theme, each with the verse text and a described visual motif so you can draw it yourself, hand the description to an older child as an art project, or search for a version that already exists.

## What Makes a Verse Good for a Coloring Page?

Short verses with one concrete image work best -- roughly 8 to 20 words, with a noun a child can draw. "The Lord is my shepherd" gives you a sheep, a staff, and a hillside. A long doctrinal sentence gives you nothing to color and a page full of tiny letters no crayon can survive. Three quick tests: can a 7-year-old say it in one breath, is there an image inside the words, and does this child need it this week?

## Comfort Verses (Pages 1-8)

These are the pages to have ready for hard days -- a sick week, a move, a bad night. Letter them large and leave big open shapes, because an anxious child colors slowly.

**1. Psalm 23:1** -- "The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want." *Motif:* a bubble-letter banner across the top, with a shepherd's crook forming the left border and three round sheep along the bottom. The classic, and still the best first verse page for young kids.

**2. Psalm 56:3** -- "When I am afraid, I put my trust in you." *Motif:* the words inside a large open heart, with a small nightlight or lamp glowing in the corner. Great for a bedside wall.

**3. Matthew 11:28** -- "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." *Motif:* the verse tucked inside an outlined pillow or a quilt square with a repeating patchwork border kids can color block by block.

**4. 1 Peter 5:7** -- "Cast all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you." *Motif:* a fishing scene -- a line arcing from a small boat, the verse riding the arc of the cast. The pun is intentional and kids love it.

**5. Psalm 34:18** -- "The Lord is near to the brokenhearted." *Motif:* two heart halves being joined, with a soft radiating sunburst behind them. Leave the sunburst rays wide so a child can alternate colors.

**6. Isaiah 41:10** -- "Fear not, for I am with you." *Motif:* a single large open hand with the words lettered across the palm, small stars filling the background.

**7. John 14:27** -- "Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you." *Motif:* a dove in flight over calm water, the verse curving along the horizon line.

**8. Psalm 121:1-2** -- "I lift up my eyes to the hills. From where does my help come? My help comes from the Lord." *Motif:* layered mountain ranges, each range an open band to color a different shade, with the verse across the sky.

## Courage Verses (Pages 9-15)

Courage pages are the ones kids ask to hang on their door. Letter these bolder and blockier -- thick outlines, shield shapes, strong diagonals.

**9. Joshua 1:9** -- "Be strong and courageous... for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go." *Motif:* a shield with the verse inside and a simple decorative border of chevrons. The single most-requested courage page.

**10. Philippians 4:13** -- "I can do all things through him who strengthens me." *Motif:* a mountain peak with a small climbing figure near the summit, the verse lettered in the sky above.

**11. 2 Timothy 1:7** -- "God gave us a spirit not of fear but of power and love and self-control." *Motif:* three linked circles labeled power, love, and self-control, with the verse arched over the top.

**12. Deuteronomy 31:6** -- "Be strong and courageous... he will not leave you or forsake you." *Motif:* a pair of footprints walking a winding path that runs from the bottom corner up to the horizon.

**13. Psalm 27:1** -- "The Lord is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear?" *Motif:* a lantern with rays radiating outward, the verse filling the rays. Ask kids to leave the rays yellow and color everything outside them dark.

**14. Isaiah 40:31** -- "They who wait for the Lord shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings like eagles." *Motif:* a wide-winged eagle with feather outlines large enough to color individually, verse along the wingspan.

**15. 1 Samuel 16:7** -- "The Lord looks on the heart." *Motif:* a heart made of overlapping outlined shapes, surrounded by small mirror frames. Pair this one with the story of David's anointing.

## Praise and Thankfulness Verses (Pages 16-22)

Praise pages want busy, joyful borders -- sunbursts, musical notes, flowers, confetti. These are the ones to use when you need a page that survives a whole classroom of energy.

**16. Psalm 100:1** -- "Make a joyful noise to the Lord, all the earth!" *Motif:* the verse ringed by outlined instruments -- tambourine, horn, drum -- plus a globe at the center.

**17. Psalm 150:6** -- "Let everything that has breath praise the Lord!" *Motif:* a border of animals, birds, and children all facing inward toward the lettered verse.

**18. Psalm 118:24** -- "This is the day that the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it." *Motif:* a rising sun over rooftops, verse in the sky. Perfect as a breakfast-table page.

**19. Psalm 19:1** -- "The heavens declare the glory of God." *Motif:* a night sky with constellations, a crescent moon, and the verse across the middle. Add star stickers after coloring.

**20. Psalm 139:14** -- "I praise you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made." *Motif:* a mirror frame with the verse inside, and space beneath for the child to draw their own face. One of the most meaningful pages on this list.

**21. Psalm 136:1** -- "Give thanks to the Lord, for he is good; for his steadfast love endures forever." *Motif:* a harvest wreath of leaves and fruit, each leaf blank so kids can write one thing they're thankful for before coloring.

**22. Nehemiah 8:10** -- "The joy of the Lord is your strength." *Motif:* bold bubble letters with a starburst behind them, no other detail. Good for kids who want to finish fast.

## Love and Kindness Verses (Pages 23-30)

These are the pages that end up as gifts. Leave a small blank corner on each so a child can sign it.

**23. John 3:16** -- "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son." *Motif:* a globe cradled in two open hands, with a simple cross on the horizon behind it.

**24. 1 John 4:19** -- "We love because he first loved us." *Motif:* a chain of hearts looping around the page like a paper garland.

**25. 1 Corinthians 13:4** -- "Love is patient and kind." *Motif:* the verse in tall thin letters down the center, with a repeating flower border. Older kids can letter the rest of the chapter's list in the margins.

**26. John 13:34** -- "A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another." *Motif:* a circle of linked paper-doll figures holding hands around the lettered verse.

**27. Romans 8:38-39** -- "Neither death nor life... will be able to separate us from the love of God." *Motif:* a long unbroken rope or ribbon winding the whole page, verse riding along it, nothing cutting it.

**28. Ephesians 4:32** -- "Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another." *Motif:* two hands passing a flower, with a soft rounded frame. The best page for a sibling-conflict week.

**29. Mark 12:30-31** -- "Love the Lord your God with all your heart... Love your neighbor as yourself." *Motif:* two panels side by side, one with a heart pointing upward and one with a heart pointing toward a neighbor's house.

**30. Zephaniah 3:17** -- "The Lord your God is in your midst... he will rejoice over you with gladness." *Motif:* musical notes rising from a small house, verse arched above like a lullaby. A lovely bedtime page and a verse most kids have never heard.

## How to Use a Verse Coloring Page to Actually Teach the Verse

The page is the excuse; the repetition is the lesson. Use this four-step rhythm and a coloring page becomes a memorization session that never feels like one.

1. **Read it aloud together before a single crayon moves.** Twice. Once by you, once by the child.
2. **Ask one question about a single word.** "What does a shepherd actually do?" "What's the opposite of courageous?" One word, not the whole verse -- depth beats coverage.
3. **Let them color while you talk.** Retell the story the verse comes from, or tell them about a time you needed it. Hands busy, ears open.
4. **Ask for it back at the end.** "Can you say it without looking?" Most kids can, after fifteen minutes of staring at their own coloring. That's the whole trick.

Then post it somewhere they walk past. A verse on the bedroom door gets reviewed a hundred times a week for free. ## Printing Tips That Save Ink and Frustration

**Use 24 lb paper or light card stock for anything with markers.** Standard 20 lb copy paper bleeds through the moment a marker touches it. Card stock at 65 lb is worth it for pages you plan to frame or gift.

**Print in grayscale, and turn on draft or economy mode.** Coloring pages are outlines. There is no reason to spend color ink on black lines, and draft mode cuts consumption roughly in half with no visible difference on line art.

**Set "fit to page" and check the margins.** Hand-lettered designs often run edge to edge; without scaling you'll lose the bottom of the citation.

**Batch-print ten at once, monthly.** A folder of ready pages gets used. "I should go print something" does not.

**Slip finished pages into a page protector in a three-ring binder.** By the end of a year your child has a personal illustrated verse book in their own colors, which is a keepsake no store sells.

**For reusable pages, laminate one copy and hand over dry-erase markers.** One print, unlimited colorings, ideal for the verse of the month.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are Bible verse coloring pages?

They're coloring sheets where the artwork *is* a verse -- the words are drawn in outline lettering, usually surrounded by a simple illustration related to the verse's meaning. Unlike a scene coloring page, which shows a Bible story, a verse page puts Scripture itself in front of the child for the entire coloring session, which is why they work so well for memorization.

### What age are scripture coloring pages best for?

Ages 4 to 12, with the design shifting as they grow. Under 6, use very few words in very fat letters with one big shape to fill. Ages 6 to 9 handle a full short verse with a moderate border. Ages 10 and up often prefer intricate, adult-style lettering and pattern work, and many will happily letter the verse themselves rather than color someone else's.

### How do I make my own Bible verse coloring page?

Letter the verse lightly in pencil on plain paper, trace over it with a black fine-tip marker, add one simple motif from the descriptions above, then erase the pencil and photocopy it. It takes about five minutes, and homemade pages have an advantage: your child recognizes your handwriting. For a classroom, letter it once on a whiteboard, photograph it, and print copies.

### Which Bible translation should I use on coloring pages?

Use whatever translation your family reads together, so the coloring page matches what the child hears at home and church. If you're choosing fresh, a modern readable translation makes memorization easier for young kids. Whatever you pick, letter the reference underneath in smaller type -- learning "Joshua 1:9" alongside the words is what lets a child find it again in a decade.

One at a time, for about a week each. A single verse colored, posted, and reviewed for seven days sticks far better than four verses rushed in a month. Roughly fifty verses a year is an ambitious, achievable pace for an elementary-age child, and this list alone gives you more than half of that.
