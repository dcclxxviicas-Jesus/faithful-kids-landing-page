The best Bible verses about courage for kids are Joshua 1:9 ("Be strong and courageous... for the Lord your God will be with you wherever you go") and Psalm 56:3 ("When I am afraid, I put my trust in you"). Below are 23 courage verses, each paired with the story it comes from -- because a verse a child can attach to Joshua, David, Esther, or Daniel sticks ten times better than a verse floating on its own.

Notice what biblical courage actually is: not the absence of fear, but trusting God while afraid. David was a kid facing a giant. Esther's hands were surely shaking. That's the version of courage a scared 7-year-old can actually use.

## What Courage Verses Come From Joshua's Story?

Joshua 1:9 is the most famous courage verse in the Bible, and it was spoken to a man taking over the biggest job of his life. Moses had died, and Joshua had to lead a whole nation into an unknown land.

**1. Joshua 1:9** -- "Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid... for the Lord your God will be with you wherever you go."
God says "be strong and courageous" to Joshua three times in one chapter -- because once is rarely enough, for Joshua or for kids. The reason attached is the whole point: God goes wherever you go.

**2. Deuteronomy 31:6** -- "Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid... for the Lord your God goes with you; he will never leave you nor forsake you."
Moses' farewell speech to the people -- and to Joshua -- before the handoff. Courage gets passed down like an heirloom: Moses to Joshua, parent to child.

**3. Numbers 13:30** -- "We should go up and take possession of the land, for we can certainly do it."
Caleb's minority report. Twelve spies saw the same giants; ten saw grasshoppers in the mirror, but Caleb and Joshua saw God's promise. Courage is often about which report you believe.

## What Courage Verses Come From David's Story?

David's verses are the gold standard for kids because David faced Goliath as a young person -- probably a teenager -- with no armor and full confidence in God. If your child knows one Bible story, it's probably this one; the full story is in our retelling of [David and Goliath for kids](/blog/david-and-goliath-for-kids).

**4. 1 Samuel 17:37** -- "The Lord who rescued me from the paw of the lion... will rescue me from the hand of this Philistine."
David's courage had a track record. He remembered past rescues (the lion, the bear) to face the present giant -- a strategy kids can copy: "Remember the last scary thing God got you through?"

**5. 1 Samuel 17:45** -- "I come against you in the name of the Lord Almighty."
The shepherd boy's answer to a nine-foot warrior. David's confidence wasn't in his sling; it was in whose name he showed up under.

**6. Psalm 27:1** -- "The Lord is my light and my salvation -- whom shall I fear?"
David wrote this as a grown man who had spent years being hunted. The question format is powerful for kids: with God as your light, what exactly is left to fear?

**7. Psalm 56:3** -- "When I am afraid, I put my trust in you."
David wrote this psalm while captured by enemies -- genuinely afraid. Seven words, completely honest, and probably the best first courage verse for any child to memorize.

## What Courage Verses Come From Esther's Story?

Esther's courage was quiet and costly: she risked her life by walking into the king's throne room uninvited to save her people. Her story (told fully in [Who Was Esther? for kids](/blog/who-was-esther-for-kids)) gives kids two of the bravest lines in Scripture.

**8. Esther 4:14** -- "And who knows but that you have come to your royal position for such a time as this?"
Mordecai's challenge to Esther. Kids need to hear this framing: maybe you're in that class, on that team, in that neighborhood on purpose -- for such a time as this.

**9. Esther 4:16** -- "And if I perish, I perish."
Esther's answer. Real courage counts the cost and goes anyway. She also asked everyone to fast and pray first -- brave people ask for backup.

## What Courage Verses Come From Daniel and His Friends?

Daniel's story proves that courage for kids isn't usually about fighting -- it's about quietly doing right when everyone else doesn't. Daniel and his three friends were young exiles, far from home, under enormous pressure to conform.

**10. Daniel 3:17-18** -- "The God we serve is able to deliver us... But even if he does not..."
Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, facing the furnace. "But even if he does not" might be the bravest phrase in the Bible: they trusted God's power and accepted his choice. That's mature courage, and kids can grasp it.

**11. Daniel 6:22** -- "My God sent his angel, and he shut the mouths of the lions."
Daniel's report from the pit the morning after. He'd kept praying when prayer was outlawed, knowing the cost -- the full story is in our guide to [Daniel in the lion's den for kids](/blog/daniel-in-the-lions-den-for-kids).

**12. Daniel 1:8** -- "But Daniel resolved not to defile himself."
The quietest courage verse on this list. Long before the lions, Daniel decided in advance what he would and wouldn't do -- which is exactly how kids beat peer pressure: decide before the moment arrives.

## What Courage Verses Come From Moses and the Prophets?

The Exodus gives kids the picture of courage as standing still while God fights. Trapped between Pharaoh's army and the sea, Israel got this instruction:

**13. Exodus 14:13** -- "Do not be afraid. Stand firm and you will see the deliverance the Lord will bring you today."
Moses, to a panicking crowd. Sometimes courage is charging; sometimes it's standing still and watching God work.

**14. Exodus 14:14** -- "The Lord will fight for you; you need only to be still."
The follow-up line, and a favorite for anxious kids: the fight isn't actually yours.

**15. Judges 6:12** -- "The Lord is with you, mighty warrior."
Said to Gideon while he was hiding in a winepress. God names people by their potential, not their fear -- your scared kid is a "mighty warrior" in progress too.

**16. 2 Kings 6:16** -- "Don't be afraid... Those who are with us are more than those who are with them."
Elisha to his terrified servant, surrounded by an enemy army. Then God opened the servant's eyes to see hills full of angel armies. Courage is often just seeing the whole picture.

**17. Nehemiah 4:14** -- "Don't be afraid of them. Remember the Lord, who is great and awesome."
Nehemiah to wall-builders facing threats. His formula works at any age: stop staring at the scary thing, start remembering how big God is.

## What Courage Verses Come From Jesus and Peter?

Jesus' most repeated command is some version of "don't be afraid" -- he says it to sinking disciples, grieving fathers, and his own frightened friends. Peter's story wraps around several of these.

**18. Matthew 14:27** -- "Take courage! It is I. Don't be afraid."
Spoken across a storm, at night, to disciples who thought they were seeing a ghost. Peter answered by stepping onto the water -- and when he sank, Jesus caught him immediately. Both halves of that story matter to kids.

**19. Mark 5:36** -- "Don't be afraid; just believe."
Jesus to Jairus, a dad who'd just gotten the worst possible news about his daughter. Five words that fit any scary moment a child faces.

**20. John 16:33** -- "In this world you will have trouble. But take heart! I have overcome the world."
Jesus is honest with kids where we're sometimes not: trouble will come. The courage isn't in pretending otherwise -- it's in knowing who already won.

**21. Acts 4:29** -- "Enable your servants to speak your word with great boldness."
The early church's prayer after being threatened -- notice they didn't pray for the danger to disappear, but for courage inside it. Earlier in that chapter, observers realized ordinary, unschooled Peter and John were bold because "these men had been with Jesus" (Acts 4:13). Courage is contagious from Jesus.

**22. 2 Timothy 1:7** -- "For the Spirit God gave us does not make us timid, but gives us power, love and self-discipline."
Paul, writing from prison to young, timid Timothy. Fear whispers "that's just how I am"; this verse answers that timidity isn't a kid's God-given identity.

**23. 1 Corinthians 16:13** -- "Be on your guard; stand firm in the faith; be courageous; be strong."
Four short commands -- practically a locker-room chant. Great as a family motto or a sports-season memory verse.

## How Do You Use Courage Verses When Your Child Is Scared?

Use a three-step pattern: name the fear, say the verse together, then recall the story behind it. In practice:

1. **Name it first.** "You're scared about the recital. That makes sense." A verse offered before the fear is acknowledged feels like a brush-off.
2. **Say one verse out loud, together.** Pick ONE -- Psalm 56:3 for younger kids, Joshua 1:9 for older -- and say it slowly, twice. Under stress, short beats comprehensive.
3. **Attach the story.** "Remember David? He was scared of nothing? No -- he faced a giant, and he remembered the lion and the bear." Stories give the verse a face. This is the core idea behind our list of [Bible stories about courage for kids](/blog/bible-stories-about-courage-for-kids), and it's how our video lessons at [Faithful Kids](https://faithfulkids.app/quiz) work too -- each courage story comes with a quiz, so kids remember Joshua and Esther when they need them.
4. **Build a track record like David.** Keep a simple list of "scary things God got us through." Before the next hard thing, read it. That's 1 Samuel 17:37 in action.
5. **Pre-decide like Daniel.** For peer-pressure fears, help your child decide their answer in advance, the way Daniel "resolved" before the test came.

One more distinction worth teaching: courage verses are for doing scary-but-right things; for the fears that just need soothing -- dark rooms, thunderstorms, bad dreams -- lean on our companion list of [Bible verses about fear for kids](/blog/bible-verses-about-fear-for-kids).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Bible verse about courage for a child?
Joshua 1:9 is the best courage verse for kids: "Be strong and courageous... for the Lord your God will be with you wherever you go." It's memorable, it comes with a great story, and its logic works for a child -- you can be brave because you're never alone. For children under 6, start with the shorter Psalm 56:3.

### What does the Bible say courage actually is?
Biblical courage is trusting God while you're still afraid -- not the absence of fear. David admitted fear (Psalm 56:3), Esther risked her life trembling ("If I perish, I perish"), and Jesus told the truth that "in this world you will have trouble." Teach kids that feeling scared isn't failing; doing the right thing scared is what the Bible calls courage.

### How many times does the Bible say "do not be afraid"?
Commands like "do not be afraid" and "fear not" appear well over a hundred times across Scripture -- it's one of the most repeated instructions in the Bible. The exact count varies by translation, but the pattern is unmistakable: nearly every time God or an angel shows up, the first words are some form of "don't be afraid."

### Which Bible story about courage should I teach a scared child first?
Start with David and Goliath -- a young person, a giant problem, and courage built on remembering past rescues (1 Samuel 17:37). It's the most relatable courage story for kids because David wins with trust, not size. Follow it with Daniel in the lions' den for quiet courage and Esther for courage that helps other people.

### What courage verse works for a child afraid of trying new things?
Deuteronomy 31:8 and Joshua 1:9 are the best fits, because both were spoken at the edge of something brand new -- entering an unknown land. Say the verse the night before and again on the way there, and remind your child that God "goes before you" into the new classroom or tryout, arriving ahead of them.

### At what age can kids memorize courage verses?
By age 3 or 4, most kids can memorize Psalm 56:3 ("When I am afraid, I put my trust in you") with repetition and hand motions. Around 6 to 8, add Joshua 1:9 and 1 Corinthians 16:13, and by the preteen years kids can handle Daniel 3:17-18 with its "but even if he does not" nuance. Always pair the verse with its story -- kids remember heroes better than references.
