The best Bible verses about forgiveness for kids are Ephesians 4:32, Colossians 3:13, and Matthew 18:21-22 -- short, concrete verses that explain why we forgive, how often, and how God forgives us first. Below are 22 verses organized by the moments parents actually face: sibling fights, hurt feelings, stubborn grudges, and the child who needs to know God still loves them after a mistake.

Forgiveness is one of the hardest things the Bible asks of anyone, and kids feel that keenly. "But she started it!" is a real theological objection, and it deserves a real answer. These verses give you one. Each verse below includes a quick note on how to actually use it with your child -- because a verse on the fridge only matters if it comes up on a Tuesday afternoon when someone slams a door.

## Which Verses Teach Kids Why We Forgive?

We forgive because God forgave us first -- that is the entire logic of Christian forgiveness, and these five verses say it plainly.

### 1. Ephesians 4:32
*"Be kind and compassionate to one another, forgiving each other, just as in Christ God forgave you."*

This is the single best forgiveness verse to memorize as a family. When a conflict cools down, ask your child: "How did God forgive you -- quickly or slowly? Fully or halfway?" Then invite them to forgive the same way.

### 2. Colossians 3:13
*"Bear with each other and forgive one another if any of you has a grievance against someone. Forgive as the Lord forgave you."*

"Bear with each other" is a phrase kids understand once you translate it: put up with each other's annoying moments. Use this verse to name the difference between small irritations (bear with it) and real hurts (forgive it).

### 3. Matthew 6:14
*"For if you forgive other people when they sin against you, your heavenly Father will also forgive you."*

Jesus links our forgiving to our being forgiven -- receiving and giving go together. When your child wants God's forgiveness but withholds their own, this verse gently names the mismatch.

### 4. Matthew 6:12
*"And forgive us our debts, as we also have forgiven our debtors."*

This line from the Lord's Prayer means kids pray about forgiveness every time they pray it. Pause on this line some night and ask, "Is there anyone you're still holding a debt against?"

### 5. Luke 6:37
*"Forgive, and you will be forgiven."*

Six words -- short enough for a four-year-old. Make it the family's quick-draw verse: say the first half and let your child finish it.

## What Verses Help During Sibling Fights?

For everyday conflict, reach for verses about how often to forgive, making peace quickly, and not keeping score.

### 6. Matthew 18:21-22
*"Lord, how many times shall I forgive my brother or sister who sins against me? Up to seven times?" Jesus answered, "I tell you, not seven times, but seventy-seven times."*

Peter thought seven was generous; Jesus blew the number up on purpose. Ask your child to do the math out loud -- then explain that Jesus means we stop counting altogether. The story Jesus tells right after this, [the unforgiving servant](/blog/the-unforgiving-servant-for-kids), makes the point unforgettable.

### 7. Mark 11:25
*"And when you stand praying, if you hold anything against anyone, forgive them, so that your Father in heaven may forgive you your sins."*

This verse makes bedtime prayers a natural checkpoint. Before praying, ask: "Anything you're holding against anybody?"

### 8. Matthew 5:23-24
*"Leave your gift there in front of the altar. First go and be reconciled to them; then come and offer your gift."*

Jesus says making things right with a person comes before religious activity. In family terms: apologies before dessert, reconciliation before screen time.

### 9. Ephesians 4:26
*"Do not let the sun go down while you are still angry."*

A built-in family rule: fights get resolved before bed. Kids like the concreteness -- the sun literally sets, and the grudge has a deadline.

### 10. Proverbs 17:9
*"Whoever would foster love covers over an offense, but whoever repeats the matter separates close friends."*

This one targets the child who keeps bringing up what their sibling did last week. Teach the phrase "covered means covered" -- once it's forgiven, we don't dig it back up.

### 11. Proverbs 19:11
*"A person's wisdom yields patience; it is to one's glory to overlook an offense."*

Not every bump needs a trial. Use this verse to teach the skill of letting small things go -- and praise your child specifically when they do it ("That was your glory moment").

### 12. Romans 12:17-18
*"Do not repay anyone evil for evil... If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone."*

"As far as it depends on you" is honest -- your child can't control the other kid, only their own response. That honesty makes this verse believable for kids who've been genuinely wronged.

## Which Verses Help When Forgiving Feels Too Hard?

When the hurt is real and forgiveness feels impossible, show kids two people who forgave the unforgivable.

### 13. Genesis 50:20
*"You intended to harm me, but God intended it for good."*

Joseph said this to the brothers who sold him into slavery. When your child says "I can't forgive them," Joseph's story proves God can bring good even out of real betrayal -- forgiveness doesn't mean pretending the hurt wasn't real.

### 14. Luke 23:34
*"Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing."*

Jesus prayed this from the cross, for the people hurting him. When forgiving feels too big, kids can borrow Jesus' words as their own prayer: "God, help me forgive them."

## What Verses Show Kids How God Forgives Us?

God's forgiveness is complete, fast, and permanent -- and kids who mess up need to hear that most.

### 15. 1 John 1:9
*"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins and purify us from all unrighteousness."*

This is the verse for the child hiding a mistake. The deal is simple: tell God the truth, and forgiveness is guaranteed -- every time, no exceptions.

### 16. Psalm 32:5
*"Then I acknowledged my sin to you and did not cover up my iniquity... and you forgave the guilt of my sin."*

David hid his sin and felt awful; he confessed and felt free. Use it when your child has clearly been carrying something -- confession is the exit door from guilt.

### 17. Psalm 103:12
*"As far as the east is from the west, so far has he removed our transgressions from us."*

Ask your child: "If you fly east, when do you start going west?" Never -- east and west never meet. That's how far God moves forgiven sin away from us.

### 18. Isaiah 1:18
*"Though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be as white as snow."*

A vivid picture kids can draw: a red stain turned snow-white. Great for a craft night -- and it pairs well with a story like [the prodigal son](/blog/the-prodigal-son-for-kids), where the father restores everything.

### 19. Micah 7:19
*"You will again have compassion on us; you will tread our sins underfoot and hurl all our iniquities into the depths of the sea."*

God hurls our sins into the deepest ocean. Some families add the classic line: "and He puts up a No Fishing sign" -- once forgiven, we don't go dredging it back up.

### 20. Psalm 86:5
*"You, Lord, are forgiving and good, abounding in love to all who call to you."*

"Abounding" means overflowing -- God doesn't ration forgiveness. A good verse to pray back to God after your child apologizes for something.

### 21. Daniel 9:9
*"The Lord our God is merciful and forgiving, even though we have rebelled against him."*

Even though. God's forgiveness doesn't wait for us to deserve it. Use this when your child feels they've messed up too badly to come back.

### 22. Acts 3:19
*"Repent, then, and turn to God, so that your sins may be wiped out."*

"Wiped out" is language every kid who's erased a whiteboard understands. Forgiveness isn't sin crossed out and still visible -- it's gone.

## How Do You Actually Use These Verses With Kids?

Pick one verse per situation, not twenty-two at once -- the goal is that the right verse surfaces at the right moment.

| The moment | Start with |
|---|---|
| Sibling fight, round three | Matthew 18:21-22 |
| "But they started it!" | Romans 12:17-18 |
| Grudge that won't die | Proverbs 17:9 |
| Bedtime, still angry | Ephesians 4:26 |
| Your child hid a mistake | 1 John 1:9 |
| "God can't forgive that" | Psalm 103:12 |

A few habits that make these verses stick:

**Memorize one a month.** Ephesians 4:32 first -- it's the whole gospel of forgiveness in one sentence. Write it on a card by the breakfast table.

**Pair verses with stories.** Abstract commands land better inside a narrative, so read the [Bible stories about forgiveness](/blog/bible-stories-about-forgiveness-for-kids) -- Joseph, the prodigal son, the unforgiving servant -- alongside these verses. Our video lessons at [Faithful Kids](https://faithfulkids.app/quiz) pair each of those stories with a quiz and a reflection, which helps the verse and the story reinforce each other.

**Model it out loud.** When you apologize to your child -- and parents should -- say the verse: "Ephesians says be kind and forgiving, so I'm asking you to forgive me." Nothing teaches forgiveness like receiving a parent's apology.

**Separate forgiving from feeling.** Teach kids that forgiveness is a decision, not a mood. The angry feeling may take days to fade, and that's okay -- the choice to forgive can happen tonight. Forgiveness is ultimately an act of love, and the [Bible's verses about love](/blog/bible-verses-about-love-for-kids) make a natural next step.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Bible verse about forgiveness for a young child?
Ephesians 4:32 is the best starting verse: "Be kind and compassionate to one another, forgiving each other, just as in Christ God forgave you." It's short, concrete, and contains both the command and the reason. For very young kids, Luke 6:37 -- "Forgive, and you will be forgiven" -- is only six words.

### How do I explain forgiveness to a child?
Forgiveness means choosing not to make someone pay for what they did wrong, the way God chose not to make us pay. A helpful kid-sized definition: "Forgiving means I stop holding it against you." Make clear it does not mean saying the hurt didn't matter, and it doesn't always mean trusting the person again right away.

### Does forgiving mean my child has to keep playing with a kid who hurts them?
No. The Bible commands forgiveness, not unlimited access -- Romans 12:18 says to live at peace "as far as it depends on you." A child can fully forgive a classmate and still keep a healthy distance, and parents should still address bullying with teachers. Forgiveness releases the grudge; boundaries manage the relationship.

### How many times does the Bible say to forgive?
Jesus said "seventy-seven times" in Matthew 18:22 (some translations render it "seventy times seven"). Either way, the point isn't the number -- Jesus picked a figure too big to track so we'd stop keeping count altogether.

### What Bible story teaches forgiveness best for kids?
The parable of the unforgiving servant (Matthew 18:23-35) is the clearest, because Jesus told it specifically to answer the "how many times" question. The prodigal son (Luke 15) shows a father's forgiveness, and Joseph's story (Genesis 37-50) shows forgiveness after years of real harm. Reading the story and its key verse together doubles retention.

### At what age can kids understand forgiveness?
Toddlers can practice the mechanics -- saying sorry, offering a hug -- from around age 2-3, but genuine understanding of releasing a grudge typically develops between ages 5 and 8. Don't force instant reconciliation on a still-angry child; give a short cool-down, then walk through the verse and the apology.
