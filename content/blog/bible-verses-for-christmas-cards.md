The best Bible verses for Christmas cards are short, well-known, and matched to the tone you want -- joyful for celebrations, peaceful for weary friends, hopeful for new seasons, and gentle for families walking through grief. Below are 20 verses organized by those four tones, each paired with a short original message you can copy straight into a card.

A quick rule that makes every card better: pick the verse for the recipient, not for yourself. "Joy to the world" lands differently in a house that just lost a grandparent than it does at a college roommate's first married Christmas. That's why the grouping below is by tone -- skim the category that fits the family you're writing to, and the verse plus a two-line message will take you under a minute per card.

## What Makes a Good Christmas Card Verse?

A good card verse is short enough to read in one breath, familiar enough to feel warm, and specific enough to actually say something about Christmas. Aim for one or two sentences of Scripture -- long passages get skimmed. Always include the reference (book chapter:verse) so the verse can be found again, and let your handwritten message do one job: connect the verse to that family's year.

## Joyful Verses (For Celebrating Families)

These fit families in a season of good news -- new babies, new homes, engagements, or just a house full of kids who love Christmas morning.

**1. Luke 2:10 -- "I bring you good news that will cause great joy for all the people."**
*Message:* "Wishing your house the same great joy the angels announced -- may it fill every corner this Christmas!"

**2. Luke 2:11 -- "Today in the town of David a Savior has been born to you; he is the Messiah, the Lord."**
*Message:* "A Savior, born for you -- that's still the best news in the world. Merry Christmas from our family to yours!"

**3. Psalm 98:4 -- "Shout for joy to the Lord, all the earth, burst into jubilant song with music."**
*Message:* "May your home be loud with carols, laughter, and joy this season. Sing one extra verse for us!"

**4. Isaiah 9:6 -- "For to us a child is born, to us a son is given."**
*Message:* "Celebrating the greatest gift ever given -- and grateful for the gift of friends like you."

**5. Luke 1:46-47 -- "My soul glorifies the Lord and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior."**
*Message:* "Rejoicing with you this Christmas -- what a year God has given your family!" (Perfect for a family with news worth celebrating; nod to it by name.)

## Peaceful Verses (For Weary or Busy Families)

These fit friends in exhausting seasons -- new parents, caregivers, people in demanding jobs, anyone whose year ran them ragged.

**6. Luke 2:14 -- "Glory to God in the highest heaven, and on earth peace to those on whom his favor rests."**
*Message:* "Amid all the December rush, we're praying some real 'on earth peace' finds its way to your house this year."

**7. Isaiah 9:6 -- "And he will be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace."**
*Message:* "May the Prince of Peace rule your home this Christmas -- and give you a quiet moment or two to enjoy it."

**8. John 14:27 -- "Peace I leave with you; my peace I give you. I do not give to you as the world gives."**
*Message:* "Wishing you the kind of peace no schedule can shake. You've earned a slow Christmas morning -- take it!"

**9. Micah 5:2 -- "But you, Bethlehem... out of you will come for me one who will be ruler over Israel."**
*Message:* "God did His biggest work in a small, quiet town. Praying your Christmas is full of small, quiet, wonderful moments."

**10. Philippians 4:7 -- "And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus."**
*Message:* "After the year you've had, we're praying this verse over your family all December long. Merry Christmas, friends."

## Hopeful Verses (For New Seasons and Fresh Starts)

These fit graduates, new households, career changers, families who moved, and anyone standing at the edge of something new -- including cards you send around New Year's.

**11. Isaiah 9:2 -- "The people walking in darkness have seen a great light."**
*Message:* "Whatever this next year holds, the Light has already come -- and He goes ahead of you. Merry Christmas!"

**12. Matthew 1:23 -- "They will call him Immanuel (which means 'God with us')."**
*Message:* "New city, new chapter -- same Immanuel. He's with you there just as He was here. We miss you and celebrate you!"

**13. Jeremiah 29:11 -- "'For I know the plans I have for you,' declares the Lord, 'plans to prosper you and not to harm you, plans to give you hope and a future.'"**
*Message:* "As you step into this new season, we're praying this promise over you. Excited to watch what God does next!"

**14. Lamentations 3:22-23 -- "His compassions never fail. They are new every morning; great is your faithfulness."**
*Message:* "Here's to a Christmas full of mercy and a new year full of new mornings. Great is His faithfulness -- we've watched it in your life!"

**15. Numbers 6:24-25 -- "The Lord bless you and keep you; the Lord make his face shine on you."**
*Message:* "Our Christmas prayer for your family, word for word. Blessed, kept, and shined upon -- all year long."

## Gentle Verses (For Grief-Touched Families)

These fit families facing their first Christmas after a loss, or carrying illness, infertility, estrangement, or a hard diagnosis. The rule here: name the comfort, don't explain the pain. Avoid "everything happens for a reason" energy entirely -- these verses say "God is near," not "cheer up."

**16. Matthew 1:23 -- "God with us."**
*Message:* "Two words we're praying over you this Christmas: God with us. With you in the missing, and with you in the memories. We love you."

**17. Psalm 34:18 -- "The Lord is close to the brokenhearted and saves those who are crushed in spirit."**
*Message:* "We know this Christmas looks different. We're not far, and neither is He. No need to write back -- just know you're loved."

**18. John 1:5 -- "The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it."**
*Message:* "Holding your family up this season. Even this darkness doesn't get the last word -- the Light has come, and He stays."

**19. Isaiah 40:11 -- "He tends his flock like a shepherd: He gathers the lambs in his arms and carries them close to his heart."**
*Message:* "Praying you feel carried this Christmas -- close to His heart, right where you belong. Thinking of [name] with so much love."

**20. Revelation 21:4 -- "He will wipe every tear from their eyes. There will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain."**
*Message:* "Christmas is the down payment on this promise. Until the day it's all made new, we're beside you. Merry Christmas, dear friends."

Use verse 20 thoughtfully -- it fits families grieving a believer's death, where the promise of reunion comforts rather than stings.

## How Do You Write the Message Part of a Religious Christmas Card?

Write two sentences: one that connects the verse to their year, and one that's plain human warmth. The verse carries the theology; your note carries the relationship. A simple formula:

| Line | Job | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| The verse | Says the true thing | "The light shines in the darkness..." (John 1:5) |
| Sentence 1 | Connects it to them | "We've watched you carry so much this year." |
| Sentence 2 | Plain warmth | "We love your family and we're cheering for you." |
| Sign-off | Keep it simple | "Merry Christmas -- The Muellers" |

Three quick don'ts: don't preach (one verse, not three), don't explain the verse (trust it), and don't recycle the identical message into every card (the verse can repeat; the middle sentence shouldn't).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the most popular Bible verse for Christmas cards?
Luke 2:10-11 -- "I bring you good news that will cause great joy... a Savior has been born to you" -- is the most-used Christmas card verse, with Isaiah 9:6 ("For to us a child is born") a close second. Both are short, instantly recognizable, and tonally safe for nearly any recipient, which is exactly why they endure.

### Should I put the Bible reference on the card or just the quote?
Always include the reference (for example, "Luke 2:14"). It takes four characters, lets the recipient look up the full passage, and signals care rather than a generic greeting. Standard format is the quote followed by the reference, with no need to name the translation on a card.

### Is it okay to send Scripture cards to friends who aren't religious?
Yes, if the verse is warm and the message is about them rather than an altar call. Peace and light verses (Luke 2:14, John 1:5) read as graceful to almost anyone, while heavy doctrinal picks can feel pointed. One verse plus a genuinely personal note communicates love; three verses and a mini-sermon communicates agenda.

### What verse should I use for a family that lost someone this year?
Psalm 34:18 -- "The Lord is close to the brokenhearted" -- is the safest and most comforting choice, with Matthew 1:23's "God with us" a beautiful two-word alternative. Name their person if you can, acknowledge that this Christmas is different, and skip every impulse to explain or silver-line the loss. Presence comforts; explanations wound.

### Can kids help choose and write Christmas card verses?
Absolutely -- assign each child two or three families and let them pick the verse and add one line or a drawing. Matching a verse's tone to a real person is one of the best applied-Scripture exercises a kid can do, and grandparents keep kid-written cards forever. Have younger kids copy just the reference while you write the quote.

### How long should a Christmas card message be?
Two to four sentences beyond the verse is plenty -- roughly 25 to 50 words. Cards are read standing up next to a refrigerator; a short specific message ("We loved watching Ella start kindergarten this year") beats a long generic one every time. Save the full family newsletter for the insert, if you must.
