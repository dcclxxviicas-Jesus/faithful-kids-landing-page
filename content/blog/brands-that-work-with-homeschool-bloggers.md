## Curriculum Affiliate Programs That Pay Cash

**All About Learning Press** (All About Reading and All About Spelling) has the best published terms in the niche: **15% commission on all purchases** through your link, a **180-day cookie**, and monthly PayPal payouts once you reach $25. Two catches from their own terms: paid search advertising on their brand is banned, and enrollment runs through allaboutlearningpress.net, their separate affiliate domain.

**Notgrass History** pays **12% on Notgrass.com purchases** (excluding shipping and tax), attributed first-click, paid via PayPal or Stripe about 15 days after each month closes. Applications are reviewed by a person, and they may ask for a W-9 once you cross $600 a year.

**Sonlight** pays **6% with a 90-day first-click cookie**, $50 payout threshold, monthly PayPal payments, and reviews applications within two business days. First-click matters here: if your post is the first place a family ever clicked, you get the credit even if they buy through someone else's link later in the window. Its secular sibling **BookShark** runs an identical program (6%, 90-day first-click, $50 threshold) if your audience spans both.

**Master Books** pays **8-10% on a referred customer's first qualified sale** and 2% on their next orders, with a 60-day cookie, but the program is currently **invitation-only** per their own page, which also warns that affiliates who go six months without a sale can be dropped and that balances under $50 are forfeited if you leave. Apply and see; just know the terms before you count on it.

**Time4Learning** runs an open-application program aimed explicitly at homeschooling families, parent creators, and co-op leaders, and says a large following is not required. The commission rate is only shared after approval, so treat any number you see elsewhere as a guess.

## Review-for-Product Programs

**The Homeschool Review Crew still exists**, rebranded. It now operates as the **Homeschool Influencer Network** under The Old Schoolhouse (the old homeschoolreviewcrew.com redirects there). Three roles: evaluators who write reviews, content creators who deliver images and testimonials, and reel makers for Instagram. Their page says members "will be compensated for their work" without stating amounts, and the brands supply the products. For a newer blog, this is the classic on-ramp: a steady stream of curriculum to review builds the portfolio that cash programs want to see.

**Timberdoodle's Ambassador Team** trades free or discounted products for honest reviews. Their requirements are specific: established blogs or YouTube channels only (an Instagram account alone does not qualify), US mailing addresses only, and you review everything you receive, usually within two to three months, using their tracking links. Applications are open year-round and the 2026-2027 team page confirms the program is active.

## Subscription Boxes and Learning Apps

**KiwiCo** pays content sites **up to $20 on renewing subscription sales**, up to $10 on non-renewing orders, and 10% on KiwiCo Store purchases, with a 30-day cookie, through CJ. Note the two-tier structure: coupon sites get capped at $2 per subscription, so a real content blog earns ten times what a deals page does. They also run separate sponsored-content tracks for Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube.

**Little Passports** invites "bloggers of all sizes" (their words), pays **$20 per subscription box sale and 10% on shop sales** through Impact.com, names homeschooling blogs as a fit on the page itself, and takes custom-rate conversations by email for bigger audiences.

**Reading Eggs** has a real program on Impact.com aimed at bloggers with their own websites, but the commission rate is not public; an old "up to 35%" figure still circulates from a previous network era, and you should not plan around it.

## The Brands With No Program (Stop Pitching These)

Verified on their own sites in November 2026, these homeschool staples have **no affiliate, ambassador, or blogger program**:

- **Abeka**: nothing; the closest thing is a 10%-off email signup for customers.
- **Teaching Textbooks**: no program; a refer-a-friend scheme claimed by coupon sites does not appear anywhere on their own site.
- **The Good and the Beautiful**: no partner program of any kind on the site.
- **Gather Round Homeschool**: employment and speaking-request pages only.
- **Classical Conversations**: no affiliate program; what exists is paid advertising and exhibiting, which is you paying them.

One structural oddity worth knowing: **HSLDA** pays $20 per new member referred, but their own FAQ says individuals do not qualify; you need a business or organization with an EIN. If you have an LLC for your blog, it counts.

## How to Actually Get Accepted

1. **Apply with a real site, not just social.** Timberdoodle says it outright (established blogs or channels only), and the cash programs all review applications by hand.
2. **Match the audience before the rate.** All About Learning's 15% beats Sonlight's 6% on paper, but only if your readers are teaching early readers. A mismatched program earns zero at any rate.
3. **Mind the cookie type.** First-click programs (Sonlight, Notgrass, BookShark) reward being the blog that introduces a curriculum. Write the comparison post nobody else has written, not the tenth review.
4. **Disclose properly.** FTC rules require clear disclosure on affiliate links and gifted products. Every program above also requires it in their own terms.
5. **Keep income streams separate in your head.** Review-for-product programs build credibility and content; affiliate programs build income; sponsorships come to blogs that already have both. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which homeschool curriculum companies have affiliate programs?

Verified in November 2026: All About Learning Press (15%), Notgrass History (12%), Sonlight (6%), BookShark (6%), Master Books (8-10%, invitation-only), and Time4Learning (rate shared on approval). Abeka, Teaching Textbooks, The Good and the Beautiful, Gather Round, and Classical Conversations have no affiliate program.

### Does Abeka have an affiliate program?

No. As of November 2026 there is no affiliate, ambassador, or blogger program anywhere on abeka.com. The closest thing is a 10% discount offer for joining their customer email list.

### Is the Homeschool Review Crew still active?

Yes, under a new name. It now operates as the Homeschool Influencer Network at schoolhousereviewcrew.com, run by The Old Schoolhouse, and was actively recruiting evaluators, content creators, and reel makers as of November 2026.

### How much do homeschool bloggers make from Amazon links?

Amazon Associates currently pays 4.5% on physical books and 3% on toys, per their published rate card. Those are among the lower rates on this page, which is why most established homeschool bloggers link direct-to-publisher programs first and use Amazon as the fallback.
