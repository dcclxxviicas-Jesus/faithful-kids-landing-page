The best Christian board games for families are Outburst Bible Edition for loud group fun, Apples to Apples Bible Edition for mixed ages, Bibleopoly for Monopoly lovers, and The Settlers of Canaan for strategy fans. Below are 10 real, buy-able games plus 4 games you can play tonight with a regular deck of cards and a pair of dice -- no purchase required.

One honest note up front: Christian game shelves have their share of duds -- games that are basically pop quizzes in a box. Every game below was chosen because the *game* part is genuinely fun. A game night that feels like a test teaches kids that the Bible is homework; a game night full of laughing teaches them the opposite.

## What Makes a Good Christian Family Game?

A good Christian family game is fun first and educational second -- if your kids would refuse to play the secular version, the Bible version won't save it. Look for three things: playable by mixed ages (a 6-year-old and a 13-year-old at the same table), rounds short enough for weeknights, and Bible content that rewards familiarity without punishing beginners. Several of these pair perfectly with a [family Bible trivia night](/blog/family-bible-trivia-night) if you want to build a whole evening around them.

## Best Christian Board Games You Can Buy

These 10 games are real, widely available titles -- here's who each one fits.

### 1. Outburst Bible Edition
The classic shout-fest: a topic card like "Books of the New Testament" or "Things on Noah's Ark" lists 10 answers, and your team yells guesses against the timer. It's loud, fast, and works with big groups, which makes it the single best pick for family reunions and youth groups. Kids 8+ can contribute; younger ones pair with a parent. If your family likes it, our roundup of [Bible games for kids](/blog/bible-games-for-kids) has similar high-energy picks.

### 2. Apples to Apples Bible Edition
Same beloved mechanic as the original -- a judge flips a description card, everyone plays the noun card from their hand that best matches, funniest comparison usually wins. The Bible edition uses biblical people, places, and things, so kids absorb names and stories while laughing. Ages 7+, and it's the easiest game on this list for guests who don't know the Bible well.

### 3. Bibleopoly
Monopoly with biblical cities -- Jerusalem, Jericho, Bethlehem -- where instead of bankrupting opponents, you win by building a church. It fixes Monopoly's meanest edge (you help other players in "cooperation" spaces), though it's still a long game. Best for families who already love Monopoly marathons. Ages 8+.

### 4. The Settlers of Canaan
A licensed Catan variant set in the land of Israel: you place settlements among the twelve tribes, trade resources, and race to build toward Jerusalem. If your family has outgrown roll-and-move games, this is the strategy pick. Ages 10+, about 60-90 minutes.

### 5. Bible Blurt!
The reader gives a rapid-fire definition ("Blurt out: the giant David defeated!") and the first player to shout the right word advances. Almost no setup, rounds take seconds, and it plays fine in the car with just the cards. Great for ages 7-12.

### 6. Redemption
A collectible trading card game (think battling card games your kids already know, with Heroes and Evil Characters from Scripture) that's been around since 1995 with organized tournaments. Best for kids 10+ who love deck-building games -- it has real depth, and kids end up knowing obscure Bible characters cold.

### 7. Commissioned
A cooperative board game where players are apostles growing the early church through Acts and the epistles while facing persecution and trials -- everyone wins or loses together. Cooperative play is fantastic for families with a sore-loser stage (every family has one). Ages 10+, and it sparks genuinely good conversations about Acts.

### 8. Kings of Israel
A cooperative strategy game where players are prophets working to remove sin and build altars across Israel before the timeline of kings runs out. It follows the actual sweep of 1-2 Kings, so kids painlessly absorb the divided-kingdom era most adults find confusing. Ages 12+ or younger with help.

### 9. Journeys of Paul
Players are apostles building churches across the Roman Empire while managing travel, funds, and setbacks drawn from Acts. Slower-paced and best for ages 10+, but it makes Paul's missionary journeys -- usually a blur of maps in Sunday school -- feel like a real adventure.

### 10. The Ungame (Christian Version)
Not a competition at all -- it's a conversation game where cards prompt players to share ("Describe a time God answered a prayer"). No winning, no losing, no wrong answers. It sounds too simple to work, but it reliably gets quiet kids and teens talking. All ages. ## Bible Games You Can Play With a Regular Deck of Cards or Dice

You don't need to buy anything to have Bible game night -- a standard deck and two dice cover these four games.

### 11. Bible Books War
Deal a standard deck evenly. Each flip, both players convert their card to a book of the Bible by counting from Genesis (Ace = Genesis, 2 = Exodus... up the line, with face cards as 11-13). Say your book aloud to claim the trick; highest card wins it, but *only if you named your book correctly*. Kids memorize Bible book order shockingly fast this way.

### 12. Verse Dice Race
Write six memory verses on a paper numbered 1-6. Players take turns rolling a die and reciting the matching verse -- stumble and your turn ends, nail it and roll again. First to recite all six from memory wins. Rotate in new verses each week and you have a free memory-verse system.

### 13. Character 21 (Bible Blackjack)
Play blackjack rules, but before you can "hit," you must name a Bible character nobody has said this round. Run out of characters and you must stand. Simple, but by round three kids are digging deep past David and Moses into Bartimaeus and Lydia territory.

### 14. Story Suits
Assign each suit a category: hearts = name a Bible character, diamonds = name a Bible place, clubs = act out a Bible story (like our [Bible charades prompts](/blog/bible-charades-for-kids)), spades = answer a trivia question from the reader. Flip cards from a shuffled deck; complete your challenge to keep the card. Most cards after 20 flips wins. This one game covers review for whatever your kids learned that week -- and it costs nothing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Christian board game for families?
Outburst Bible Edition is the best all-around pick because it's fast, loud, team-based, and works for groups of four to fourteen. For families with younger kids or guests new to the Bible, Apples to Apples Bible Edition is the easier entry point since there are no wrong answers.

### Are there Christian board games for kids under 7?
Most boxed Bible games target 7-8 and up because they lean on reading and Bible knowledge. For younger kids, pair them with a parent on a team, or use the DIY games above -- Story Suits and acting-out games work from about age 4. Simple Bible memory-match card sets also exist for preschoolers.

### What Christian games work for a big group or youth night?
Outburst Bible Edition and Bible Blurt! scale best because they're team-based and loud -- ten or more players is no problem. Reverse charades and trivia tournaments also handle crowds well; see our guide to running a family Bible trivia night for a full format.

### Is there a Christian version of Catan?
Yes -- The Settlers of Canaan is a licensed Catan variant set in biblical Israel, where players settle among the twelve tribes and build toward Jerusalem. It plays like classic Catan with a few unique rules, and it's the best strategy-weight Christian game for families with kids 10 and up.

### Are cooperative Bible games worth it for competitive families?
Yes, especially if game nights keep ending in tears or gloating. Commissioned and Kings of Israel put the whole family on one team against the game, which keeps a bad loser from wrecking the evening and teaches kids to strategize together. Many families alternate: competitive game one week, co-op the next.

### Do these games actually teach the Bible or just quiz it?
Both, depending on the game. Trivia-style games (Outburst, Bible Blurt!) review what kids already know, while narrative games like Commissioned, Kings of Israel, and Journeys of Paul teach the shape of biblical history as you play. For real learning, pair games with regular story time during the week -- games are the review, not the lesson.
