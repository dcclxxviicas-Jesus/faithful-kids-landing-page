Ask the internet which Christian publishers accept submissions and you'll get lists that haven't been checked in years, recommending portals that no longer exist and policies that quietly flipped. We checked every publisher below on its own live guidelines page on November 16, 2026, and the honest headline is this: **the genuinely open, free, unagented doors in Christian publishing today number four.** Everything else runs through agents, conferences, or your wallet, and this guide tells you which is which, with each publisher's own words.

One rule to carry through everything below: **a traditional publisher pays you and charges nothing to submit or publish.** The moment any offer involves a "package," "co-publishing contribution," or reading fee, you are the customer of a paid self-publishing service, whatever the letterhead says. Both things can be legitimate; confusing them is how writers get hurt.

## The Four Genuinely Open Doors

**Eerdmans** (and its children's imprint, Eerdmans Books for Young Readers) is the standout. The academic and theological side takes proposals by email with a stated eight-week response window, and states plainly that "Eerdmans does not undertake subsidy publishing for individuals." EBYR accepts unagented children's submissions outright: picture books and board books as full manuscripts, novels as query plus three chapters, with a four-months-silence-means-no policy and a stated refusal of AI-generated text or art. Guidelines: [eerdmans.com/submissions](https://www.eerdmans.com/submissions/) and [the EBYR page](https://www.eerdmans.com/youngreaders/submission-guidelines/).

**Judson Press** (American Baptist) runs two annual acquisition cycles, with proposals due January 31 for the March cycle and July 31 for September, which makes the January deadline the next real date on a Christian writer's calendar. They publish practical church and leadership resources (explicitly not fiction, memoir, or gift books), describe themselves as "a traditional royalty press" and "NOT a subsidy press," and quote a three-to-six-month review. One eyes-open note: the same page promotes their separate "Inspirit" co-publishing arm, which is the paid track; confirm which track any offer is on. [Guidelines](https://www.judsonpress.com/Pages/Info/For-Authors.aspx).

**CLC Publications** accepts unsolicited proposals for "deeper life" Christian nonfiction only (no fiction, poetry, or children's books), requires their proposal form, asks authors to accept their statement of faith, and states a six-to-eight-week response. [Guidelines](https://www.clcpublications.com/about/prospective-authors-submissions/).

**Ambassador International** is "now accepting submissions for our 2027-2028 catalogues" across devotional, fiction, biography, and children's work, via an online form taking your first three chapters and the last. Their pages are silent on contract terms, so confirm there's no author-paid component before signing anything. [Submit here](https://ambassador-international.com/?p=3042).

## The Partial Doors

- **Zondervan** accepts unsolicited *proposals* for exactly two imprints, Zondervan Reflective and Zondervan Academic, by email with a stated six-week interested-or-assume-no window. Everything else at Zondervan, fiction and children's included, is agent-only.
- **Baker Publishing Group**: trade divisions (Bethany House, Revell) are agent-or-conference only, but **Baker Academic** takes direct proposals and Brazos Press posts its own guidelines.
- **InterVarsity Press** reviews proposals only from writers who've had direct contact with one of its editors, which in practice means conferences; there is no open portal.

## Agent-Only (Their Own Words)

Thomas Nelson: "does not accept unsolicited manuscripts." B&H (including B&H Kids): "we only accept proposals from literary agents." Tyndale: "reviews only manuscripts submitted by professional literary agents" and industry contacts (their policy page blocks robots; we verified the wording from an October 2026 archived copy of their own FAQ). Harvest House, Kregel, WaterBrook & Multnomah, Paraclete: all agent-or-invitation, per their own pages. Iron Stream Media (which absorbed New Hope Publishers) takes agented proposals only, for 2026-2028 releases.

## The Agent Path, Verified

Since most of the industry is agent-gated, the agencies themselves, checked the same day:

- **The Steve Laube Agency**: represents Christian fiction and nonfiction broadly (no poetry, screenplays, or family histories); query one agent at a time with a full proposal; interested-response window roughly ninety days. Note: their page does not currently post per-agent open/closed status, whatever third-party lists claim.
- **Books & Such**: email queries only, one page, no attachments, for adult, YA, and middle-grade Christian-market work, with a 2026-era policy worth quoting: no "query letters, proposals, or sample writing originated, written, or edited by artificial intelligence."
- **WordServe Literary**: email queries with your first five pages pasted in (attachments are refused); states a four-to-six-week response. Their page also carries a warning every writer should read: scammers impersonate their agents, and real agents "will never solicit for payment." Query via their [submissions page](https://www.wordserveliterary.com/submissions/).
- **Gardner Literary** posts live, dated per-agent status (currently: Rachelle closed, Sherri open for nonfiction) and closes agency-wide from Thanksgiving to New Year's, which means the window is closing within days of this post's publication; queries reopen in January.

The other real path is conferences, which five publishers above name as their only non-agent door: **Write to Publish** (June 2027, Wheaton; two editor/agent meetings included in registration) and the **Blue Ridge Mountains Christian Writers Conference** (May 2027, North Carolina) are the established ones.

## The Paid Traps, Named Precisely

- **WestBow Press** is the self-publishing arm of Thomas Nelson and Zondervan, run with Author Solutions, with packages posted from $2,699 to $8,299. Its pitch that top-performing titles are "provided to the acquisition teams... for consideration" promises exactly nothing about traditional publication. It is a purchase, not an acceptance.
- **Christian Faith Publishing** sells author-funded packages (its own blog cites roughly $4,000-$8,000); the watchdog site Writer Beware has documented its sales practices for years, including salespeople calling themselves "literary agents." Again: a purchased service.
- Publishers' own referral pages point writers to paid screening (The Writers Edge) and paid self-publishing (Xulon, Essence); those referrals are conveniences for the publisher, not endorsements of a path to a royalty contract.

Paid self-publishing can be a fine, clear-eyed choice. Just never mistake it for being published in the sense this guide covers, and never pay anyone who calls the payment a submission.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Christian publishers accept unsolicited manuscripts?

As of November 16, 2026, verified on their own pages: Eerdmans (academic and children's), Judson Press (church resources, two annual cycles), CLC Publications (deeper-life nonfiction), and Ambassador International (broad list, 2027-2028 catalogues), plus proposal-only doors at Zondervan Reflective/Academic and Baker Academic. Moody and Chalice Press, on many older lists, both currently state they are not accepting submissions.

### Do any Christian publishers accept children's book submissions without an agent?

One: Eerdmans Books for Young Readers, with full guidelines on its own page. Beaming Books, the other name old lists give, has gone agent-only and retired its portal. For everything else in Christian children's publishing, the path is an agent or a writers conference.

### How long do Christian publishers take to respond?

The stated windows, from their own pages: Zondervan six weeks (silence means no), CLC six to eight weeks, Eerdmans about eight weeks (children's: four months), WordServe four to six weeks, Steve Laube about ninety days, Judson three to six months. Treat silence past the stated window as a pass and move on; every one of these publishers says some version of exactly that.

### Should I pay a Christian publisher to publish my book?

Only if you knowingly want self-publishing services and have compared prices, because that's what you'd be buying; WestBow's packages run $2,699-$8,299 and similar outfits cluster in that range.
