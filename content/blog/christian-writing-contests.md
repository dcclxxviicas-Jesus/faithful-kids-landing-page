Free-to-enter contests lead, fees are never buried, and the vanity-contest pattern gets named at the end so you can recognize it anywhere.

## The Flagship: Plough's Two Prizes

**Plough's Short Fiction Contest** pays **$2,000 plus publication** in Plough Quarterly for an unpublished story up to 5,000 words, with up to three finalists earning $250 and publication each. Entry is free for Plough subscribers and $10 otherwise, judging is blind, and AI-written work is barred. The 2026 round just closed with results due this month; the cycle runs annually with a late-summer deadline.

**The Rhina Espaillat Poetry Award** mirrors it for poets: **$2,000 plus publication** for the winner, $250 for two finalists, up to three unpublished poems of 50 lines or less, free for subscribers and $7 per poem otherwise. The 2027 deadline is already posted: **April 30, 2027**.

These two are the highest-prestige, cleanest-terms contests in the niche, which is why they also anchor our guide to [Christian writing jobs that pay](/blog/christian-writing-jobs).

## Free to Enter, Real Money

**The Banner's Young Adults Writing Contest** pays the biggest free-entry prize we verified: **$1,000 first, $500 second, $250 third** for a personal story on sustaining faith, up to 1,000 words. The catch is eligibility: writers 16-23 who are members or regular attenders of the Christian Reformed Church. If that is you, this is the best-odds contest on this page. Deadline May 31.

**The Gospel Coalition's annual essay contest** pays **$500** for first place plus publication on TGC, open to writers 16-25, on prompts the editors set each year (the 2026 round included "staying human in the age of AI"). Free to enter; entries run June 1 to July 1. Note their terms: entrants grant TGC permission to publish.

**CBE International's writing contest** (annual, free) awards publication in their magazine plus a subscription rather than cash, across six categories including poetry and song lyrics. Know the lane before entering: CBE is Christians for Biblical Equality and entries must support biblical mutuality, their theological distinctive.

**Today's Christian Living** runs a free annual contest on a set theme (2026: "Sweet Hour of Prayer"), with winners printed in the January issue. Their page does not state a cash prize, and they purchase first rights, so enter it for the byline. The annual deadline falls around August 15.

For students, **The Word Guild's Fresh Ink category is now free for students**, within its Canadian awards program below.

## Conference and Association Contests (Fees Apply)

**The Cascade Writing Contest**, run by Cascade Christian Writers (formerly Oregon Christian Writers; same organization, new name), takes unpublished work in 13 categories including young adult, middle grade, children's chapter books, picture books, poetry, and devotionals. Fees are **$45 per entry for members, $65 for non-members**, entries run January 15 to February 15, and winners are announced at their June conference. The value here is category breadth: it is one of few places a children's picture book manuscript can compete in a Christian contest.

**The Selah Awards** (Blue Ridge Mountains Christian Writers Conference) honor **published** work across 30+ categories at **$55 per entry**, with author deadlines in early January. Its sibling **Foundation Awards** for unpublished work charge $35 per entry but require paid registration for the whole conference, so count the real cost as the conference, with the contest as a bonus.

**The Word Awards** (The Word Guild) are Canada's Christian writing awards: published work across many categories at CAD $30-$75 per entry depending on category and membership, with cash attached to several special awards (up to $750) and a publishing contract for the Best New Canadian Manuscript stream. Canadian writers only.

**The CALEB Award** (Omega Writers) serves Australia, New Zealand, and the South Pacific, alternating published books in even years and unpublished manuscripts, with judge feedback, in odd years; the next unpublished round is 2027. Entry fees apply, with member discounts.

**American Christian Fiction Writers (ACFW)** also runs long-established contests for its members; their site blocks automated checking, so verify current cycles at acfw.com directly.

## The Vanity-Contest Pattern: Learn It Once

Some heavily advertised "Christian writing contests" are marketing funnels for paid self-publishing services. The pattern: the sponsor is a vanity press, and the grand prize is a "publishing package" of the sponsor's own services, historically valued at thousands of dollars, which costs the sponsor almost nothing and recruits you as a customer. Xulon Press's long-running contest was the famous example. Before entering anything, ask three questions: Is the prize cash or publication in a real magazine, or the sponsor's own service? Who keeps the rights to your entry? And would this sponsor make money if you lose? A real contest passes all three.

Two names still circulating on stale lists that we could not verify as operating: the Athanatos Christian Ministries literature contest (the organization has refocused; no contest exists on its site) and Guideposts' famous biannual Writers Workshop contest, which shows no current cycle on their site. Do not plan around either.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Christian writing contests are free to enter?

Verified in November 2026: The Banner's Young Adults contest ($1,000 top prize, CRC members 16-23), The Gospel Coalition's essay contest ($500, ages 16-25), CBE International's contest (publication prize), Today's Christian Living's annual theme contest, The Word Guild's Fresh Ink student category, and both Plough contests if you subscribe to Plough Quarterly.

### What is the biggest prize in Christian writing contests?

Among verified contests, Plough's twin $2,000 awards (short fiction and poetry) are the largest open-to-everyone cash prizes. The Banner's $1,000 first prize is the largest free-entry prize, within its denominational eligibility.

### Are writing contest entry fees normal?

Yes, within reason: association and conference contests in this niche charge $30-$75 per entry to fund judging. The flag is not a fee; it is a "prize" consisting of the sponsor's own paid publishing services, which marks a vanity-press funnel.

### Can kids and teens enter Christian writing contests?

Teens have the best options: TGC takes writers from 16, The Banner from 16, and The Word Guild's Fresh Ink category is free for students. For younger kids, contests are scarce; Scripture-memory competition is the richer path, topping out at the National Bible Bee's $50,000 senior prize, covered in our guide to [Christian scholarships](/blog/christian-scholarships).
