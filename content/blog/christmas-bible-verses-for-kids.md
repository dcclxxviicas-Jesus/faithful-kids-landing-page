The best Christmas Bible verses for kids are the ones that tell the actual Christmas story -- and here are 25 of them, arranged in order from the first ancient promise to the arrival of the magi. Read one per day as an Advent countdown, and by Christmas your kids will have walked through the whole nativity story straight from Scripture.

Each verse below comes with a one-line explanation written for kids, so you can read the verse, read the line, and be done in under a minute. Verses are in NIV-style wording -- read them from your own Bible in whatever translation your family uses.

## How Should Families Use These Verses?

The simplest approach: read one verse a day from December 1-25, in order, at breakfast or bedtime. Because they're arranged chronologically, the daily verses unfold like a serialized story -- kids genuinely start asking "what happens next?"

Other ways families use this list:

- **Advent calendar filler.** Print the verses on slips and tuck one behind each door -- our [Advent calendar ideas for kids](/blog/advent-calendar-ideas-for-kids) guide has several setups built for exactly this.
- **Memory verses.** Pick 3-4 favorites (we flag the easiest ones below) and practice all month. Our tips for [Bible verses for kids to memorize](/blog/bible-verses-for-kids-to-memorize) work perfectly here.
- **Christmas Eve reading.** Read verses 14-23 in one sitting for the full birth narrative.

## Part 1: The Promises -- Prophecy Verses (700+ Years Before Bethlehem)

The Christmas story doesn't start in Bethlehem -- it starts with promises God made centuries earlier. These verses show kids that Jesus' birth was God's plan all along.

**1. Genesis 3:15** -- God promises that one of Eve's descendants will crush the serpent.
*For kids: On the very day sin entered the world, God already announced His rescue plan -- and Christmas is that plan arriving.*

**2. Isaiah 7:14** -- "The virgin will conceive and give birth to a son, and will call him Immanuel."
*For kids: About 700 years early, God said exactly how the Savior would arrive -- born to a virgin, and named "God with us."*

**3. Isaiah 9:2** -- "The people walking in darkness have seen a great light."
*For kids: Jesus coming into the world is like someone flipping on the lights in a dark room.*

**4. Isaiah 9:6** -- "For to us a child is born, to us a son is given... And he will be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace."
*For kids: This baby would have the most amazing names ever given, because He would be God Himself. (Great memory verse -- kids love listing the four names.)*

**5. Micah 5:2** -- "But you, Bethlehem... out of you will come for me one who will be ruler over Israel."
*For kids: God even named the exact small town where the Savior would be born -- hundreds of years ahead of time.*

## Part 2: The Announcement -- Annunciation Verses

An angel brings the news to Mary, and then to Joseph. If your kids want the full scene, our retelling of [the angel's visit to Mary](/blog/an-angel-visits-mary-for-kids) walks through it story-style.

**6. Luke 1:26-27** -- God sends the angel Gabriel to Nazareth, to a virgin named Mary.
*For kids: God picked an ordinary young woman in a small town for the most important job in history.*

**7. Luke 1:30-31** -- "Do not be afraid, Mary; you have found favor with God. You will conceive and give birth to a son, and you are to call him Jesus."
*For kids: Mary was scared at first -- but the angel told her God had chosen her, and even told her the baby's name.*

**8. Luke 1:32** -- "He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High."
*For kids: This wouldn't be an ordinary baby -- He would be God's own Son.*

**9. Luke 1:37** -- "For no word from God will ever fail."
*For kids: If God says it, it will happen -- period. (One of the shortest, best memory verses on this list.)*

**10. Luke 1:38** -- "I am the Lord's servant... May your word to me be fulfilled."
*For kids: Mary's answer was brave and simple: "Yes, God -- I'll do it."*

**11. Luke 1:46-47** -- "My soul glorifies the Lord and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior."
*For kids: Mary was so amazed she burst into a song of praise -- the first Christmas carol, in a way.*

**12. Matthew 1:20-21** -- An angel tells Joseph in a dream: "give him the name Jesus, because he will save his people from their sins."
*For kids: The name Jesus means "God saves" -- His name is His mission.*

**13. Matthew 1:23** -- "They will call him Immanuel (which means 'God with us')."
*For kids: Christmas in three words: God with us. Not far away -- right here.*

## Part 3: The Birth -- Bethlehem Verses

The journey, the full guest rooms, and the manger. For a read-aloud version of this whole section, see [the Christmas story for kids](/blog/the-christmas-story-for-kids).

**14. Luke 2:1** -- Caesar Augustus orders a census of the whole Roman world.
*For kids: A powerful emperor made a rule about counting people -- and without knowing it, he was helping God's Bethlehem promise come true.*

**15. Luke 2:4-5** -- Joseph travels from Nazareth to Bethlehem with Mary, who is expecting a baby.
*For kids: Mary and Joseph made a long, hard trip of about 90 miles -- with no cars, right before the baby came.*

**16. Luke 2:6-7** -- "She gave birth to her firstborn, a son. She wrapped him in cloths and placed him in a manger, because there was no guest room available for them."
*For kids: The King of the universe was born where animals eat -- God chose the humblest spot in town. (This is THE Christmas verse -- worth memorizing.)*

## Part 4: The Shepherds -- Good News Verses

God announces the birth first to ordinary shepherds working the night shift.

**17. Luke 2:8-9** -- Shepherds are watching their flocks at night when an angel appears and God's glory shines around them.
*For kids: The biggest announcement in history went first to ordinary workers in a field, not to kings in palaces.*

**18. Luke 2:10** -- "Do not be afraid. I bring you good news that will cause great joy for all the people."
*For kids: Christmas is good news for everyone -- including you.*

**19. Luke 2:11** -- "Today in the town of David a Savior has been born to you; he is the Messiah, the Lord."
*For kids: The angel gave the baby three titles -- Savior, Messiah, Lord -- because this baby is the Promised One.*

**20. Luke 2:12** -- "You will find a baby wrapped in cloths and lying in a manger."
*For kids: The sign of the Savior wasn't a crown or a castle -- it was a baby in a feeding trough.*

**21. Luke 2:14** -- "Glory to God in the highest heaven, and on earth peace to those on whom his favor rests."
*For kids: The sky filled with angels singing -- heaven threw the first Christmas celebration. (Wonderful memory verse for singers.)*

**22. Luke 2:16-17** -- The shepherds hurry to Bethlehem, find Mary, Joseph, and the baby, and then spread the word.
*For kids: The shepherds became the first people ever to tell others about Jesus.*

**23. Luke 2:19** -- "But Mary treasured up all these things and pondered them in her heart."
*For kids: Mary kept every moment of that night like treasure -- some things are so special you think about them forever.*

## Part 5: The Magi -- Worship Verses

Wise men from far away follow a star to worship the new King.

**24. Matthew 2:1-2** -- Magi from the east arrive asking, "Where is the one who has been born king of the Jews? We saw his star when it rose and have come to worship him."
*For kids: God put a sign in the sky so that even people from faraway lands could find Jesus.*

**25. Matthew 2:10-11** -- The magi are overjoyed, bow down and worship the child, and open gifts of gold, frankincense, and myrrh.
*For kids: The first Christmas gifts were given TO Jesus -- the wise men's example is why we give gifts at all.*

## Which Verses Should Kids Memorize First?

Start with Luke 2:11 -- it's short, it's the heart of Christmas, and most kids can learn it in a week. Here's a quick age guide:

| Age | Best Starter Verses | Why |
|-----|--------------------|----|
| 3-5 | Matthew 1:23; Luke 1:37 | Very short, huge meaning |
| 6-8 | Luke 2:11; Luke 2:14 | Story-central, singable |
| 9-11 | Luke 2:10-11; Isaiah 9:6 | Longer but rhythmic |
| 12+ | Isaiah 9:6; Luke 2:8-14 | Full passages, deeper discussion |

Make it stick with motions (kids invent great ones for "wrapped him in cloths"), by singing the verse to a familiar carol tune, or with a nightly "fill in the blank" game at dinner. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the most famous Christmas Bible verse?
Luke 2:11 -- "Today in the town of David a Savior has been born to you; he is the Messiah, the Lord" -- is the most famous verse from the Christmas story itself. Isaiah 9:6 ("For to us a child is born") and Luke 2:14 ("Glory to God in the highest") are close behind, and John 3:16 is often added because it explains why Jesus came.

### Where is the Christmas story in the Bible?
The Christmas story is told in Matthew 1-2 and Luke 1-2. Luke gives the angel's visit to Mary, the journey to Bethlehem, the manger, and the shepherds; Matthew gives Joseph's side, the magi, and the star. The prophecies about Jesus' birth appear centuries earlier in Isaiah 7:14, Isaiah 9:6, and Micah 5:2.

### What is a good short Christmas verse for a young child to memorize?
Matthew 1:23's phrase "Immanuel -- God with us" is the shortest meaningful option, followed by Luke 1:37, "No word from God will ever fail." For a slightly longer verse, Luke 2:11 is the classic first Christmas memory verse. Keep it to one verse for ages 3-5 and practice a little every day rather than a lot once.

### How do I use these verses as an Advent countdown?
Read one verse per day from December 1-25 in the order listed -- the sequence follows the story from prophecy to the magi, so each day continues the narrative. Print them on numbered slips for an Advent calendar, or simply bookmark this list and read from your Bible each morning. If you miss a day, just read two the next day or skip ahead.

### Were the wise men really at the manger?
Probably not -- Matthew 2:11 says the magi came to a "house" and saw the "child," suggesting they arrived weeks or months after the birth night. It's a fun fact kids love catching in nativity scenes. It doesn't change the meaning: the magi still followed the star to worship the young King.

### What order do the Christmas story events happen in?
The order is: prophecies (centuries earlier), the angel Gabriel visits Mary, an angel reassures Joseph in a dream, the journey to Bethlehem for the census, Jesus' birth and the manger, the angels' announcement to the shepherds, the shepherds' visit, and finally the magi following the star. The 25 verses above follow exactly this sequence, so reading them in order tells the complete story.
