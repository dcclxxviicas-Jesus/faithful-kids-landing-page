Here are 25 fall festival games for church that pass the three tests every festival game must pass: a seven-year-old can play it, a volunteer can run it with ninety seconds of instruction, and it doesn't fall apart when forty kids arrive at once. They're organized the way festivals actually run -- booth games, trunk or treat games, big group games, and quiet printable stations -- with the cheap-and-cheerful supply list for each.

A fall festival or trunk or treat is most churches' single biggest outreach night of the year: more first-time families walk onto the property on that one evening than in any month of Sundays. The games below are designed with that in mind -- fun first, zero awkwardness for visiting families, and a natural touchpoint or two for the ones who want more.

## Booth Games (The Backbone)

Classic carnival booths, Bible-flavored where it helps and plain fun where it doesn't need to be.

1. **Jericho Wall Knockdown** — stack cardboard-brick walls, three bean bags to bring them down. The toddler version: they walk up and push it over. (Everyone shouts.)
2. **David's Sling Range** — pool noodles make the "sling": launch ping-pong "stones" at a Goliath drawn on cardboard. Hit the forehead, win big.
3. **Fishers of Men Pond** — the classic fishing booth: clothespin on a string over a sheet, volunteer behind it clips on the prize.
4. **Ark Animal Toss** — toss pairs of small stuffed animals through a plywood ark's window. Pairs only -- that's the joke, and kids get it.
5. **Pumpkin Ring Toss** — glow rings over pumpkin stems. Five pumpkins, graduated distances.
6. **Donut on a String** — no hands. Eternal. Budget: one box of donuts per hour.
7. **Candy Corn Guess Jar** — one jar, one clipboard, closest guess at closing time takes the jar.
8. **Cake Walk** — numbered circles, music, a table of donated cakes. The seniors run this one and defend it fiercely.
9. **Manna Drop** — kids catch "manna" (large marshmallows) in a basket while a volunteer rains them down from a step stool.
10. **Feats of Samson** — a kid-sized strongman challenge: carry two water jugs across a line, hold a broomstick out straight for 20 seconds.

## Trunk or Treat Games

If your festival is a trunk or treat, the best trunks aren't the most decorated -- they're the ones with a 15-second game, because a game turns a candy hand-off into a conversation.

11. **Noah's Ark Trunk** — stuffed animals everywhere; kids guess how many pairs are in the trunk before they treat.
12. **Jonah's Whale Toss** — a whale mouth cut from a foam board at the tailgate; one bean bag through the mouth, double candy.
13. **Lost Sheep Hunt** — 99 cotton-ball sheep glued around the trunk, one black sheep hidden; find it for the bonus.
14. **Red Sea Crossing** — two blue tarps the kids "part" and run through to reach the candy.
15. **Armor of God Dress-Up Photo Trunk** — helmet, shield, foam sword, instant photo. Parents love this one more than the kids do.
16. **Mini Sword Drill** — for the older kids: first to find John 3:16 in the trunk's Bible wins the king-size bar.

(For the decorating side, keep it simple: a theme, a tablecloth, and string lights beat four hours of Pinterest engineering. Spend the saved time on the game.)

## Big Group Games (Scheduled Twice a Night)

17. **Walls of Jericho March** — everyone marches around the "city" seven times with kazoos and tambourines, then the leaders collapse the box wall. Chaos. Perfection.
18. **Gideon's Torch Relay** — glow sticks in cups ("torches hidden in jars"): relay to the far cone, reveal the glow, run home.
19. **Giant Bible Charades** — two teams, prompts from our [70 Bible charades cards](/blog/bible-charades-for-kids) ([printable deck](https://d3g07v1w0lehiv.cloudfront.net/printables/bible-charades.pdf)).

## Running the Night Well: Five Hard-Won Rules

- **One ticket system, not cash at booths.** A ticket table at the entrance is also your headcount and your welcome point.
- **Volunteer-proof every game**: instructions taped to the booth, prizes pre-bagged, a "too hard? they win anyway" policy stated out loud. The festival's goal is not fair competition.
- **Candy math**: plan on 2-3 pieces per booth visit, 15-20 per child total, and buy 25% more than that. Running out at 7:40 is the only true festival disaster.
- **Mark the quiet corner on the map.** Overwhelmed kids exist; give their parents somewhere to aim.
- **The follow-up is the point.** A card in every candy bag -- service times, kids' program, something free -- is the difference between a fun night and an outreach. ## What About Halloween Itself?

Churches land differently on Halloween, and a fall festival works for every position: it's an *alternative* for families who skip the holiday and simply a *great night* for families who don't. Keep the framing on what you're celebrating -- harvest, community, light -- rather than what you're against, and visiting families from both camps will feel welcome. If your church leans into the alternative framing, scheduling on October 31 itself serves those families best; if not, the weekend before wins on attendance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What games should a church have at a fall festival?**
Aim for a mix: six to ten booth games (ring toss, knockdown walls, fishing pond), one or two scheduled big-group games, and a quiet table with bingo, coloring, or puzzles. The list above covers all three tiers with supply notes.

**What are good trunk or treat games?**
The best trunk games take under 20 seconds per child: a single bean-bag toss, a find-the-hidden-object challenge, or a guess-the-count jar. Six ready-to-use trunk games are in the list above -- each one themed to a Bible story the trunk can decorate around.

**How do you do a fall festival on a small budget?**
Borrow, don't buy: bean bags, tarps, and cardboard cover half the games above. Ask the congregation for candy donations three weeks out, use donated cakes for the cake walk, and print the free game stations ([bingo](https://d3g07v1w0lehiv.cloudfront.net/printables/bible-bingo.pdf), [charades](https://d3g07v1w0lehiv.cloudfront.net/printables/bible-charades.pdf), [coloring pages](/printables/bible-coloring-pages)) instead of renting anything.
