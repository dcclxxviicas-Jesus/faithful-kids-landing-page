Here are 20 Easter crafts for Sunday school, each listed with its materials, prep time, age range, and the exact Bible lesson it reinforces -- so you can match a craft to whatever you're teaching that Sunday instead of hunting for one that fits. They run from Palm Sunday branches through cross crafts to empty tomb dioramas and butterfly new-life projects.

Everything here uses supply-closet basics: paper plates, construction paper, craft sticks, tissue paper, playdough, cotton balls. Nothing needs advance ordering, and most finish inside 20 minutes -- which matters, because Easter Sunday is the one morning of the year when your class doubles in size and your clock shrinks.

A craft earns its place when it hands you a question. A paper-plate tomb lets you ask "what did the women find when they got here?" while the glue dries. Every craft below names the moment it teaches, so you never have to invent that question on the spot. For the lesson plans these pair with, see our [Easter Sunday school lessons](/blog/easter-sunday-school-lessons).

## Crafts 1-5: Palm Sunday and Holy Week

### 1. Paper Palm Branches

**Teaches:** The crowds welcome Jesus into Jerusalem waving palm branches (John 12:13).
**Materials:** Green construction paper, scissors, a paper straw or dowel, tape.
**Prep:** 5 minutes. **Ages:** 3-10.
**Steps:** Fold green paper lengthwise, cut a long leaf shape, then snip fringe along both edges toward the fold. Open it and tape the base to a straw. Then use them -- a palm parade around the room, shouting "Hosanna!" is half the craft.

### 2. Paper Plate Donkey

**Teaches:** Jesus rode a borrowed donkey, not a warhorse (Matthew 21:5-7).
**Materials:** Paper plate, gray or brown paint, construction paper, yarn, glue.
**Prep:** 10 minutes to pre-cut ears and a muzzle. **Ages:** 3-7.
**Steps:** Paint the plate gray and let it dry. Glue on paper ears, a muzzle, and a yarn mane and tail. Ask while they work: kings rode horses -- why did Jesus pick the humblest animal in town?

### 3. Hosanna Handprint Banner

**Teaches:** "Hosanna to the Son of David!" (Matthew 21:9) -- hosanna means "save us."
**Materials:** Butcher paper or a fabric strip, washable paint, marker.
**Prep:** 5 minutes. **Ages:** 2-8.
**Steps:** Write HOSANNA in big bubble letters across the banner, then let every child add green handprints around the words as palm leaves. Hang it in the hallway for Holy Week. One banner, whole class, zero cleanup per child beyond a wet wipe.

### 4. Last Supper Table Setting

**Teaches:** "This is my body given for you; do this in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19).
**Materials:** Small paper plate, paper cup, tan paper or a real cracker, markers.
**Prep:** 5 minutes. **Ages:** 5-11.
**Steps:** Kids decorate the plate and cup, then glue a torn piece of "bread" on the plate and write "Remember me" around the rim. Talk about why Jesus gave His friends something to do rather than only something to believe.

### 5. Servant Towel

**Teaches:** Jesus washed His disciples' feet and told them to do the same (John 13:14).
**Materials:** White washcloth or a strip of white fabric, fabric markers.
**Prep:** 2 minutes. **Ages:** 5-12.
**Steps:** Each child writes "Love one another" on the towel plus one household job they'll do this week without being asked. It goes home on a doorknob as a reminder. Older kids can list three jobs and check them off.

## Crafts 6-10: The Cross

### 6. Craft Stick Cross with Twine

**Teaches:** Jesus died in our place (Romans 5:8).
**Materials:** Two craft sticks per child, twine or jute, glue.
**Prep:** 2 minutes. **Ages:** 5-12.
**Steps:** Cross the sticks and wrap twine around the joint in a figure eight until it holds, then knot and add a hanging loop. It's the fastest cross craft that still looks good on a wall, and the wrapping is genuinely calming for wound-up classes.

### 7. Nail Cross Ornament

**Teaches:** Jesus took what we deserved (Isaiah 53:5).
**Materials:** One nail (or a nail-shaped paper cutout), red paper hearts, twine, cardstock.
**Prep:** 10 minutes to cut hearts. **Ages:** 7-12.
**Steps:** Tie or glue a nail to a cardstock cross, then let each child cover the nail with a red paper heart glued at the top so it lifts like a flap. The image does the teaching: the nail is real, and love covers it.

### 8. Stained-Glass Cross Sun-Catcher

**Teaches:** The cross is the center of the story (1 Corinthians 1:18).
**Materials:** Black construction paper, tissue paper scraps, glue stick or contact paper.
**Prep:** 15 minutes to cut cross frames. **Ages:** 4-10.
**Steps:** Cut a cross-shaped window from black paper. Kids fill the opening with overlapping tissue scraps, then tape it to a window so the light comes through. Purple and red scraps make the most striking version.

### 9. Heart-Covered Cross Mosaic

**Teaches:** "God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8).
**Materials:** Cardstock cross, hole punch or pre-cut paper hearts, glue.
**Prep:** 10 minutes. **Ages:** 3-8.
**Steps:** Kids glue small hearts all over a cross shape until no white shows. Simple enough for a three-year-old, and the finished pieces make a striking class display when you tape twenty of them together on a wall.

### 10. Pretzel Crown of Thorns

**Teaches:** The soldiers twisted a crown of thorns and put it on His head (Matthew 27:29).
**Materials:** Thin pretzel sticks, a small paper plate, glue -- or edible: pretzels plus melted chocolate.
**Prep:** 5 minutes. **Ages:** 7-12.
**Steps:** Arrange pretzel sticks into a ring, overlapping at sharp angles, and glue or bind them. Handle this one carefully with young classes; it is the most sober craft on the list and belongs with older kids who can hold what it means.

## Crafts 11-15: The Empty Tomb

### 11. Paper Plate Empty Tomb

**Teaches:** "The stone, which was very large, had been rolled away" (Mark 16:4).
**Materials:** Two paper plates, gray and brown paint or paper, a brad fastener.
**Prep:** 10 minutes. **Ages:** 4-10.
**Steps:** Color one plate as a hillside tomb with a dark doorway. Cut a circle "stone" from the second plate and attach it beside the door with a brad so it rolls open and shut. Kids will roll it a hundred times, saying the line every time: "He is not here!"

### 12. Shoebox Tomb Diorama

**Teaches:** The whole resurrection scene -- garden, tomb, rolled stone, folded cloths (John 20:1-9).
**Materials:** Shoebox, brown paper or paint, a rock or crumpled foil ball, moss or green paper, a scrap of white cloth.
**Prep:** 20 minutes. **Ages:** 8-12.
**Steps:** Line the box as a hillside cave, add a garden outside, set the round stone rolled to one side, and place the folded linen cloth inside -- empty. Best done in pairs over two class sessions. Pair it with [The empty tomb for kids](/blog/the-empty-tomb-for-kids) as your story reading.

### 13. Resurrection Eggs

**Teaches:** The whole Holy Week arc in twelve objects (Matthew 21 through 28).
**Materials:** A dozen plastic eggs, an egg carton, small objects: a leaf, a cracker, a coin, a small cup, praying hands cutout, a piece of rope, a thorn or toothpick, a nail, a die, a small cross, a spice packet, a stone -- and one empty egg.
**Prep:** 30 minutes the first year, 2 minutes forever after. **Ages:** 4-12.
**Steps:** Kids open one egg at a time in order and retell the story from the object. The last egg is empty on purpose, and every year some child says "you forgot one." That's the moment.

### 14. Resurrection Rolls

**Teaches:** The tomb was empty on the third day (Luke 24:3).
**Materials:** Refrigerated crescent roll dough, large marshmallows, melted butter, cinnamon sugar, an oven.
**Prep:** 10 minutes plus 12 minutes baking. **Ages:** 5-12.
**Steps:** Roll a marshmallow (Jesus' body) in butter and cinnamon sugar (the burial spices), wrap it fully in dough (the tomb), and bake at 375 degrees for 11-13 minutes. The marshmallow melts away. Kids open a hollow roll and find it empty. Check your class allergy list first.

### 15. Salt Dough Tomb and Stone

**Teaches:** Saturday's waiting and Sunday's surprise (Matthew 27:60, 28:2).
**Materials:** Salt dough or air-dry clay (2 cups flour, 1 cup salt, 1 cup water), a small figure or slip of paper.
**Prep:** 10 minutes to mix dough. **Ages:** 4-10.
**Steps:** Each child molds a cave tomb with a hollow opening and a separate round stone that seals it. Close it at the end of one class and don't open it until the next -- the week of waiting is the lesson, and elementary kids feel it more than you'd expect.

## Crafts 16-20: New Life

### 16. Coffee Filter Butterfly

**Teaches:** "If anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come" (2 Corinthians 5:17).
**Materials:** Coffee filters, washable markers, a spray bottle of water, pipe cleaners, clothespin (optional).
**Prep:** 5 minutes. **Ages:** 3-10.
**Steps:** Kids color a flattened filter heavily with markers, spritz it with water and watch the colors bleed and blend, then dry it and pinch the middle with a pipe cleaner twisted into antennae. Nothing on this list gets a bigger reaction than the spray bottle moment.

### 17. Caterpillar-to-Butterfly Flip Card

**Teaches:** Death is not the end of the story -- new life follows (John 12:24).
**Materials:** Cardstock, markers, glue, a small piece of yarn or a bean.
**Prep:** 5 minutes. **Ages:** 5-11.
**Steps:** Fold cardstock in half. On the front, draw a caterpillar and a cocoon; inside, a butterfly with the words "He is risen." Kids open and close it while retelling the story. Simple, cheap, and it survives a backpack better than a diorama.

### 18. Grass Seed Tomb Garden

**Teaches:** Mary mistook the risen Jesus for the gardener (John 20:15) -- resurrection happened in a garden.
**Materials:** A shallow cup or foil pan, potting soil, grass seed, small stones, craft sticks.
**Prep:** 15 minutes. **Ages:** 4-12.
**Steps:** Fill the container with soil, press in a small stone-lined tomb opening, add three craft-stick crosses on the hill, and sprinkle grass seed over the soil. Water lightly. Send it home -- it sprouts within a week, which turns Easter into an eight-day lesson.

### 19. Flowering Cross

**Teaches:** "Christ has indeed been raised from the dead" (1 Corinthians 15:20).
**Materials:** A cardboard or wire-mesh cross, tissue paper or real flowers, tape.
**Prep:** 20 minutes to build the cross (reusable every year). **Ages:** all.
**Steps:** Set a bare cross in your room on Good Friday. On Easter Sunday, every child adds one paper or fresh flower until the whole thing is covered. It is the best whole-class craft on this list, and photographs beautifully for your church newsletter.

### 20. "He Is Risen" Good News Card

**Teaches:** "He is not here; he has risen, just as he said" (Matthew 28:6) -- and the news was meant to be carried.
**Materials:** Cardstock, markers, stickers, envelopes.
**Prep:** 5 minutes. **Ages:** 5-12.
**Steps:** Each child makes a card for one specific person -- a neighbor, a grandparent, a friend who doesn't go to church -- with "He is risen!" on the front and their own sentence inside. Address it before they leave, or it never gets delivered. That last step is the whole craft.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the easiest Easter craft for a Sunday school class?

The paper plate empty tomb (#11) is the easiest craft that still teaches the central story -- two plates, one brad, ten minutes of prep, and it works from age four to ten. For the very youngest classes, the heart-covered cross mosaic (#9) needs no scissors at all. Both survive a busy Easter morning with a doubled class size.

### Which Easter craft best explains the resurrection?

Resurrection eggs (#13) explain the most, because the twelve objects walk kids through the entire Holy Week sequence and end on an empty egg. If you have only one class session, the paper plate tomb with a rolling stone (#11) gets the single most important image into every child's hands. For a full week-by-week walkthrough to teach alongside it, use our [Holy Week day by day for kids](/blog/holy-week-day-by-day-for-kids).

### Are these Easter crafts printable or free?

Every craft here is free to use and needs no purchased curriculum -- the instructions above are the whole thing, and you can print this page for your teacher binder. Total supply cost for a class of fifteen is roughly $25-35 if you're buying everything from scratch, and most churches already have the paper plates, glue, and construction paper on hand.

### How do I handle cross crafts with preschoolers?

Keep cross crafts simple and symbolic for ages 3-5: coloring, gluing hearts, and twine wrapping are all fine, but skip nails, thorns, and anything depicting suffering. Say one sentence -- "Jesus died on the cross because He loves us, and three days later He came back alive" -- and let the craft be about love, not detail. The nail cross (#7) and pretzel crown of thorns (#10) belong with kids seven and up.

### How much prep time do these crafts really take?

Most take 5-15 minutes of prep, and the longest is the shoebox diorama at about 20. The real time sink is pre-cutting shapes for large classes, so recruit one volunteer to cut crosses and palm leaves the week before -- it turns a 15-minute prep into a 2-minute one. The flowering cross (#19) takes 20 minutes to build once and then serves you every Easter after that.

### Can I do these crafts at home instead of in a classroom?

Yes -- every craft on this list works for one child at a kitchen table, and several are better at home because there's no clock. The grass seed tomb garden (#18) and the resurrection rolls (#14) are especially good family projects, since both unfold over days rather than minutes. Read the Scripture line aloud before you start and ask the "talk about it" question while hands are busy.
