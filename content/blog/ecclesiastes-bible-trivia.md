These 22 Ecclesiastes Bible trivia questions cover the Bible's most surprising book -- "a time for everything," a cord of three strands, and the Teacher's famous cry of "Meaningless!" -- sorted into easy, medium, and hard rounds. Answers with verse references follow every question.

Ecclesiastes is short (just 12 chapters), but it punches above its weight in trivia: it hides the source of a #1 pop song, one of the Bible's strangest word pictures (dead flies in perfume), and a final answer to the biggest question a kid can ask -- what is life actually for?

## What Is the Book of Ecclesiastes About?

Ecclesiastes is the record of a wise king's search for meaning -- he tries pleasure, money, work, and wisdom itself, finds that none of them satisfy on their own, and lands on this conclusion: "Fear God and keep his commandments" (Ecclesiastes 12:13). The writer calls himself "the Teacher, son of David, king in Jerusalem" (Ecclesiastes 1:1), and he has traditionally been identified as Solomon, though the book never uses Solomon's name.

It's set in Jerusalem at the height of Israel's wealth, which is what makes the experiment believable -- this is a man who really did try everything. Ecclesiastes matters because it's the Bible's most honest book about disappointment: it says out loud what every teenager eventually feels, and then points past the feeling to God.

## Easy Ecclesiastes Trivia Questions (Ages 5-8)

1. What does the writer of Ecclesiastes call himself in the very first verse? **The Teacher** (Ecclesiastes 1:1)
2. Finish the famous line: "There is a time for everything, and a ___ for every activity under the heavens." **Season** (Ecclesiastes 3:1)
3. According to chapter 3, there is "a time to weep and a time to ___." **Laugh** (Ecclesiastes 3:4)
4. "Two are better than ___." **One** (Ecclesiastes 4:9)
5. A cord of how many strands is not quickly broken? **Three** (Ecclesiastes 4:12)
6. Ecclesiastes 12:1 says to remember whom in the days of your youth? **Your Creator** (Ecclesiastes 12:1)
7. According to Ecclesiastes 1:9, there is nothing new under the what? **The sun** (Ecclesiastes 1:9)

## Medium Ecclesiastes Trivia Questions (Ages 9-12)

8. Which king is traditionally believed to be the Teacher? **Solomon** -- the book calls him "son of David, king in Jerusalem" (Ecclesiastes 1:1)
9. What word does the Teacher famously repeat in verse 2 -- "___! ___! Everything is ___"? **Meaningless** -- "Vanity of vanities" in the King James Version (Ecclesiastes 1:2)
10. What does hevel, the Hebrew word behind "meaningless," literally mean? **A breath or vapor** -- something you can't hold on to
11. How many chapters does Ecclesiastes have? **12**
12. What does Ecclesiastes 3:11 say God has set in the human heart? **Eternity** (Ecclesiastes 3:11)
13. According to Ecclesiastes 5:10, whoever loves money never has what? **Enough** (Ecclesiastes 5:10)
14. "Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with all your ___." **Might** (Ecclesiastes 9:10)
15. What is the Teacher's final conclusion at the very end of the book? **Fear God and keep his commandments** (Ecclesiastes 12:13)

## Hard Ecclesiastes Trivia Questions (Teens and Adults)

16. In chapter 2, name two of the big projects the Teacher built while searching for meaning. **Houses, vineyards, gardens, parks, and reservoirs** (Ecclesiastes 2:4-6)
17. What tiny pests "give perfume a bad smell," just as a little folly outweighs wisdom? **Dead flies** (Ecclesiastes 10:1)
18. Ecclesiastes 9:4 says a live dog is better than a dead what? **Lion** (Ecclesiastes 9:4)
19. What does Ecclesiastes 11:1 famously say to cast upon the waters? **Your bread** (Ecclesiastes 11:1, KJV -- the NIV reads "ship your grain across the sea")
20. Finish the warning every student loves: "Of making many ___ there is no end." **Books** -- "and much study wearies the body" (Ecclesiastes 12:12)
21. The poem in chapter 3 pairs opposite "times." How many pairs are there? **Fourteen** -- twenty-eight "times" in all (Ecclesiastes 3:2-8)
22. Which 1965 #1 hit song took its lyrics almost word-for-word from Ecclesiastes 3? **"Turn! Turn! Turn!" by The Byrds**

## What Does "Meaningless" Really Mean?

It means "vapor," not "worthless" -- and that one word unlocks the whole book for kids. The Hebrew word hevel pictures a puff of breath on a cold morning: real, visible, and gone before you can grab it. The Teacher isn't saying life is bad; he's saying that money, fame, and fun evaporate when you try to build a life on them.

That's why Ecclesiastes is secretly a great book for older kids and teens. It takes their "what's the point?" mood seriously instead of shushing it, lets a king who had everything confirm that everything isn't enough, and then hands them the real answer in the last two verses. ## Why Should Kids Learn Ecclesiastes?

Because it's the Bible's own answer to boredom and disappointment -- two things every kid meets early. A child who learns "a time to weep and a time to laugh" (Ecclesiastes 3:4) gets language for hard days; a teen who learns that even Solomon's pleasures felt like chasing the wind (Ecclesiastes 2:11) gets a head start on the emptiness our culture usually discovers at thirty.

## Which Ecclesiastes Passages Should You Read Aloud First?

Start with the three passages that carry most of the trivia above: the "time for everything" poem (Ecclesiastes 3:1-8), the friendship passage (4:9-12), and the closing charge to the young (12:1, 13). All three read beautifully out loud, and together they take under five minutes.

The friendship passage is the sleeper hit with kids. "Two are better than one... if either of them falls down, one can help the other up" (Ecclesiastes 4:9-10) is concrete enough for a six-year-old, and the "cord of three strands" (4:12) gives you a physical object lesson -- let kids try to snap one string, then a braid of three. A trivia answer they've tested with their own hands never gets forgotten.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who wrote Ecclesiastes?

The book names its author only as "the Teacher, son of David, king in Jerusalem" (Ecclesiastes 1:1), and he has traditionally been identified as Solomon. Some scholars think a later writer presented his wisdom in Solomon's voice; either way, the book presents the view from the top -- a king who tried everything.

### How many chapters does Ecclesiastes have?

Ecclesiastes has 12 chapters and sits between Proverbs and Song of Solomon in the Old Testament. It belongs to the Bible's wisdom books, alongside Job and Proverbs.

### What is the main message of Ecclesiastes?

That nothing "under the sun" -- money, pleasure, work, or even wisdom -- can carry the weight of a human life, and the conclusion of the matter is to "fear God and keep his commandments" (Ecclesiastes 12:13). The book clears away every false answer first so the real one lands harder.

### What does "vanity of vanities" mean?

It's the King James translation of the Hebrew hevel hevelim -- literally "vapor of vapors," meaning utterly fleeting, like breath on a cold morning (Ecclesiastes 1:2). Modern translations render it "Meaningless! Meaningless!" -- not that life is worthless, but that life apart from God can't be gripped or kept.

### Is Ecclesiastes too depressing for kids?

No -- but it lands best from about age 9 up, when kids start asking "what's the point?" themselves. Younger kids can enjoy the chapter 3 poem and the "two are better than one" passage, while the book's darker honesty is a genuine gift to teens.

### What is the most famous passage in Ecclesiastes?

The "time for everything" poem in Ecclesiastes 3:1-8 -- "a time to be born and a time to die... a time to weep and a time to laugh." It's so famous it became a #1 hit song ("Turn! Turn! Turn!") in 1965, nearly three thousand years after it was written.
