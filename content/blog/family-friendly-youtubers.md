The hard part about "family-friendly YouTubers" isn't finding lists; it's that the lists rot. Channels retire without announcing it, kid-famous creators grow up and change lanes, and the name your child knows from three years ago may not describe the channel that exists today. The clearest example from our verification pass: DanTDM, the beloved kid-Minecraft name of the 2010s, now makes no family-friendly claim on his own page at all, and his current flagship series is a game rated Mature 17+ for blood, gore, and strong language. The channel your kid knew from Minecraft may not be that channel anymore.

So we checked every channel below on its own YouTube page in November 2026: whether it's actually uploading, what it says about itself in its own words, and what the major parent-review outlets add. One honesty rule throughout: when a channel claims family-friendliness itself, we quote it; when it doesn't, we say the clean reputation belongs to reviewers, not to the channel's own promise. And one disclosure: we make [Faithful Kids](https://faithfulkids.app/quiz), a Bible video app for kids, which exists precisely because handing kids an algorithm, even a filtered one, is a different thing from handing them a library you trust.

## 10 Kid-Friendly YouTubers Still Active in 2026

### 1. Thinknoodles: the most explicit promise on the list

About 13.2M subscribers. Roblox (DOORS), Poppy Playtime, and indie games, with his dogs as recurring characters. His own about page says it plainly: "Here, you can find all kinds of family-friendly gaming videos and shorts." The honest caveat is built into his game choices; his staples are horror-themed kid games, so sensitive younger kids may find them scary. Roughly 8 and up. Uploading multiple times a week as of November 16, 2026.

### 2. EYstreem: Minecraft at mainstream scale, Scripture in the bio

About 13.9M subscribers, Minecraft challenges and story videos, and the first line of his channel description is Psalm 89:9. Plugged In notes he makes his Christian faith clear to anyone visiting the channel, and also flags a creepy-themed Minecraft series parents may want to preview. Active same-week at our check. The strongest "your kid will watch it anyway, and the creator is a believer" option.

### 3. Preston (and PrestonPlayz): fueled by fun, family, and faith

About 32.4M subscribers on the main channel and 18M on the gaming channel, both carrying the same self-description: "Fueled by fun, family, and faith! We're building something special here: a community where we can game, grow, and enjoy life." Reviewers call the main channel the tamest of his set, while the gaming side includes jump-scare horror games that can rattle elementary-age kids. Both channels uploaded within days of our check.

### 4. Dude Perfect: the all-ages default

About 62.9M subscribers. Their own words since 2009: "the absolute best family-friendly sports and comedy entertainment we can," signed off with "Go Big and God Bless!" The newer Dude Perfect Gaming channel (about 436K subscribers) carries the same promise with a controller. Trick shots, battles, and stereotypes that genuinely play for every age in the room; both channels were uploading the week we checked.

### 5. Shiloh & Bros: clean family skits (not faith-based)

About 11.6M subscribers. "Our channel is clean family fun where we love to make skits about video games," by their own description, with current output heavy on Five Nights at Freddy's in-real-life skits, which sets the spookiness level. Clean, not Christian; worth knowing which promise you're getting.

### 6. SSundee: tame at scale, by reputation rather than promise

About 25.5M subscribers, near-daily Minecraft, Roblox, and Among Us mods. His own description promises nothing about family-friendliness (it mostly warns that he yells), and the clean-leaning reputation comes from reviewers, who note his faith while flagging mild language and horror-game content in places. A fine default for 10+, previewed.

### 7. FGTeeV: the family that games together

About 25.7M subscribers; dad, mom, and four kids making gaming comedy, currently Roblox-heavy, though the upload pace has slowed to roughly monthly. Worth knowing both numbers: Common Sense Media's editorial review rates it 13+ (finding the dad loud and some game picks mature), while CSM's own parent and kid community reviews average 9+. Watch one episode and you'll know which camp you're in.

### 8. Ryan's World: for the preschool set

About 40.4M subscribers of pretend play, science experiments, and toy content, uploading constantly. Two things parents should know: the audience skews preschool, and the channel's history includes a 2019 watchdog complaint about how clearly sponsored content was disclosed to very young viewers (a complaint, not an enforcement action). Treat it as what it is, cheerful commercial kids' TV.

### 9. Unspeakable: big stunts, clean by reputation

About 20.2M subscribers of large-scale real-life challenges. His entire self-description is "eat pizza for breakfast," so the family-friendly label here is the reviewers' (Bark suggests 10+), not his own promise. Active days before our check.

### 10. Aphmau: scripted Minecraft stories, no kid-content promise

About 25.4M subscribers, near-daily scripted Minecraft roleplay. She makes no family-friendly claim herself, and current titles lean on mild peril, romance, and supernatural themes; popular and clean-reputation, but preview a current episode rather than trusting the 2019 memory of the channel.

## The Platform Matters as Much as the Channel

A vetted channel list is half the job, because on regular YouTube the sidebar, autoplay, and comments come with it. The relevant facts from Google's own help pages:

- **YouTube Kids** has three parent-chosen content levels (Preschool for 4 and under, Younger for 5-8, Older for 9-12), plus an Approved Content Only mode where kids can only watch videos and channels you hand-pick.
- **Supervised accounts on main YouTube** offer 9+, 13+, and 16+ content settings that only a linked parent account can change, and parents can block specific channels, pause history, and disable autoplay.
- Google itself says the quiet part: YouTube Kids filtering uses algorithms, user input, and human review, but "no algorithm is perfect," and your child may find content you wouldn't choose. That sentence, from YouTube's own parental guide, is the whole reason channel lists plus platform settings beat either alone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who are the best family-friendly YouTubers for kids?

From our November 2026 verification: Dude Perfect and Thinknoodles make the family-friendly promise in their own channel descriptions and upload actively; EYstreem and Preston add a faith-forward angle at Minecraft scale; Ryan's World owns the preschool tier. The channels to stop recommending are the dormant ones (LankyBox, SIS vs BRO, EthanGamer) and the changed ones (DanTDM).

### Are there family-friendly streamers on Twitch?

Far fewer than on YouTube, and "family friendly" on Twitch is a self-applied tag with no enforcement, so vet live channels harder than videos; live content can't be pre-screened by anyone. A practical path for families is watching a trusted YouTuber's uploads rather than live streams, or exploring the explicitly faith-based live scene, which we cover in our [Christian streamers guide](/blog/christian-twitch-streamers).

### Which family-friendly YouTubers are Christian?

Verified from their own channels in November 2026: EYstreem opens his channel description with Scripture, Preston's channels describe themselves as fueled by fun, family, and faith, and Dude Perfect signs off with "God Bless" and has built VBS curriculum. Shiloh & Bros is clean but makes no faith claim, and several channels with Christian reputations never state it themselves, which is worth knowing before you treat a channel as discipleship. For creators whose content is actually about the faith, see our [Christian YouTubers roundup](/blog/christian-youtubers).

### How do I make YouTube safe for my kids?

Use the platform's controls, not just a channel list: YouTube Kids with the right age level (or Approved Content Only mode), or a supervised account with autoplay off and specific channels blocked. Then remember Google's own caveat that no algorithm is perfect, keep screens in shared spaces, and give part of the day to closed libraries where the catalog is finite and chosen.
