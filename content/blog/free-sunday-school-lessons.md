The most reliably free Sunday school lessons come from six sources: Ministry-to-Children, Sermons4Kids, Life.Church Open Network, Children's Ministry Deals' free collection, DLTK-Bible, and Answers in Genesis sample lessons. Below is what each one is actually good at and where it falls short -- followed by 12 complete lesson outlines of our own, each with a craft, that you can teach this Sunday without downloading anything.

"Free" means different things on different sites. Some sites mean free with an email signup, some mean a free sample of a paid curriculum, and a few mean genuinely, permanently free. We have sorted them below so you know which is which before you spend an evening clicking.

## What Are the Best Free Sunday School Lesson Websites?

### Ministry-to-Children (ministry-to-children.com)

The largest genuinely free library online, with thousands of lessons, crafts, and coloring pages, plus weekly print-friendly PDF bundles.

- **Good at:** breadth. Nearly every Bible story has a lesson, and most include a craft and printable.
- **Weak at:** consistency. Because lessons come from many contributors over many years, quality and format vary week to week, so a sequential year takes assembling.
- **Truly free?** Yes. They also sell a paid curriculum through The Sunday School Store, but the free library is real and does not require an account.

### Sermons4Kids (sermons4kids.com)

Over 1,500 children's sermons and object lessons, in English and Spanish, following the Revised Common Lectionary.

- **Good at:** the five-minute children's message during a worship service, and object lessons you can prep on Saturday night.
- **Weak at:** full-hour classroom lessons. These are short talks, not complete lesson plans.
- **Truly free?** Yes for the core sermons and activities. A paid "Plus" tier adds video and full elementary and preschool lessons.

### Life.Church Open Network (open.life.church)

Free professionally produced curriculum from a very large church, including Bible App for Kids curriculum for ages 2-5 and Konnect for grades 1-4.

- **Good at:** production quality. Videos, teacher guides, artwork, and activities are the most polished free material available anywhere.
- **Weak at:** flexibility. It is a system, not a menu, so it works best if you adopt it wholesale rather than pulling one lesson.
- **Truly free?** Yes, including the videos. Downloads are large, so grab them on church wifi.

### Children's Ministry Deals (childrens-ministry-deals.com)

A paid curriculum publisher with a large free collection -- their site advertises 150-plus free lessons, which is roughly three years of weekly teaching.

- **Good at:** modern, game-heavy elementary lessons with skits, discussion questions, and parent take-home sheets.
- **Weak at:** it is a sampler. The free lessons are pulled from different series, so they do not connect into a sequence.
- **Truly free?** Yes, but you create an account and they will email you offers.

### DLTK-Bible (dltk-bible.com)

The old-school craft site. Printable templates, coloring pages, and simple lesson guides organized by Bible book.

- **Good at:** crafts for ages 3-7 with printable templates you can run off on a black-and-white printer.
- **Weak at:** teaching content. The lessons are thin; use it for the craft and bring your own story.
- **Truly free?** Yes. The site is ad-supported and dated, but the printables work.

### Answers in Genesis (answersingenesis.org)

Free sample lessons from Answers Bible Curriculum, including a set of free Christmas Sunday school lessons for pre-K through middle school.

- **Good at:** a chronological, creation-focused approach with the same topic taught at every age level, which is useful if you want the whole family on one lesson.
- **Weak at:** volume. Only samples are free; the full four-year curriculum is paid.
- **Truly free?** The samples are, yes.

**One caution for all six:** preview anything before you teach it. Free curriculum comes from many theological traditions, and a lesson that fits one church perfectly can land badly in another. Read the whole lesson, not the title.

## 12 Free Sunday School Lessons With Crafts

These are ours, complete and ready to teach. Each runs 35-45 minutes for ages 5-10. The crafts use supply-closet materials -- paper plates, construction paper, cotton balls, craft sticks -- and nothing needs ordering.

### Lesson 1: God Made Everything (Creation)

**Objective:** Kids will say that God made everything, including them, and called it good.
**Scripture:** Genesis 1:1-2:3.
**Story:** There was nothing, and then God spoke. Light. Sky. Sea and dry land. Plants, sun and moon, fish and birds, animals -- and finally people, made in His image. Six days of speaking, one day of rest, and after every step: "It was good." Read the [full story for kids](/blog/in-the-beginning-creation-for-kids).
**Discussion:** Which day would you most like to have watched? What does it change to know God called you "very good"?
**Craft:** Creation wheel. Two paper circles pinned with a brad; the top circle has a window cut out, the bottom is divided into seven wedges kids draw one day into. Turning the wheel retells the whole story.
**Memory verse:** "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth." -- Genesis 1:1

### Lesson 2: Noah and the Rainbow Promise

**Objective:** Kids will explain that God keeps His promises even when we cannot see how.
**Scripture:** Genesis 6:9-22; 9:8-17.
**Story:** God tells Noah to build a boat longer than a football field in a place with no sea. Noah builds it anyway. The animals come, the rain falls forty days, and when the water dries God hangs a rainbow in the sky as a promise. Read the [full story for kids](/blog/noah-and-the-great-flood-for-kids).
**Discussion:** What do you think the neighbors said while Noah built? What is something you have to trust God about before you can see it?
**Craft:** Paper plate rainbow. Cut a plate in half, glue tissue-paper or crayon arcs across it, and staple cotton-ball clouds at each end. Write the verse along the bottom edge.
**Memory verse:** "I have set my rainbow in the clouds." -- Genesis 9:13

### Lesson 3: Joseph's Colorful Coat

**Objective:** Kids will name a time they were treated unfairly and connect it to God still being present.
**Scripture:** Genesis 37:1-28.
**Story:** Jacob loves Joseph best and gives him a beautiful coat. Joseph's brothers, sick of the favoritism and the dreams, throw him in a pit and sell him to traders headed for Egypt. It is a hard chapter, and God is not finished.
**Discussion:** Why were the brothers so angry? Can something terrible happen and God still be at work?
**Craft:** Woven coat. Kids cut slits in a coat-shaped paper and weave colored strips through it. Every strip gets one word of something God has given them.
**Memory verse:** "The Lord was with Joseph so that he prospered." -- Genesis 39:2

### Lesson 4: Baby Moses in the Basket

**Objective:** Kids will say that God protects His people in ways nobody expects.
**Scripture:** Exodus 1:22-2:10.
**Story:** Pharaoh orders Hebrew baby boys thrown into the Nile. One mother waterproofs a basket, hides her son in the reeds, and posts his big sister to watch. Pharaoh's own daughter finds him -- and pays his real mother to nurse him. Read the [full story for kids](/blog/a-baby-in-a-basket-for-kids).
**Discussion:** How brave was Miriam, standing there watching? Where do you see God working in this story even though He is never mentioned?
**Craft:** Reed basket. A small paper cup or half an egg carton, wrapped in strips of brown paper, with a cotton-ball baby inside. Blue crepe paper on the table makes the river.
**Memory verse:** "The Lord watches over you." -- Psalm 121:5

### Lesson 5: David and Goliath

**Objective:** Kids will identify one way God can use them at their current age.
**Scripture:** 1 Samuel 17:32-50.
**Story:** A nine-foot champion taunts Israel's army for forty days and nobody moves. A shepherd boy delivering lunch to his brothers volunteers, refuses the king's armor, picks up five stones, and runs toward the giant "in the name of the Lord Almighty." Read the [full story for kids](/blog/david-and-goliath-for-kids).
**Discussion:** Why did David refuse Saul's armor? What is a "giant" you are facing right now?
**Craft:** Five smooth stones. Kids paint or decorate five paper or river stones, writing on each one something they can already do for God: help, share, pray, invite, encourage. Send them home in a snack bag.
**Memory verse:** "Don't let anyone look down on you because you are young." -- 1 Timothy 4:12

### Lesson 6: Daniel and the Lions' Den

**Objective:** Kids will name a fear and connect it to God's presence in it.
**Scripture:** Daniel 6:1-23.
**Story:** Jealous officials trick the king into outlawing prayer. Daniel goes home, opens his windows, and prays exactly as always. He spends the night with lions and walks out in the morning untouched, "because he had trusted in his God." Read the [full story for kids](/blog/daniel-in-the-lions-den-for-kids).
**Discussion:** Daniel knew about the law before he prayed. Why keep the windows open? What is your lions' den?
**Craft:** Paper plate lion. A plate face with a yarn or paper-strip mane. On the back, kids write one fear and the words "God is with me here."
**Memory verse:** "When I am afraid, I put my trust in you." -- Psalm 56:3

### Lesson 7: Jonah and the Big Fish

**Objective:** Kids will explain that you cannot outrun God, and that He forgives people we would not.
**Scripture:** Jonah 1-3.
**Story:** God sends Jonah to warn Nineveh; Jonah buys a ticket in the opposite direction. A storm, a fish, three days, and a prayer later, Jonah preaches -- and the whole city repents, which is exactly what he was afraid of.
**Discussion:** Why did Jonah run? Is there anyone you would rather God did not forgive?
**Craft:** Paper bag whale puppet. Kids decorate a lunch bag as a fish, then tuck a small paper Jonah inside the flap so the puppet can "swallow" and release him while you retell the story.
**Memory verse:** "Salvation comes from the Lord." -- Jonah 2:9

### Lesson 8: Jesus Is Born

**Objective:** Kids will explain that Jesus came in the humblest way possible, on purpose.
**Scripture:** Luke 2:1-20.
**Story:** A census forces Mary and Joseph to Bethlehem, where there is no room. The Son of God is born in a stable and laid in a feed trough -- and the birth announcement goes to night-shift shepherds, not kings. Read the [full story for kids](/blog/born-in-a-manger-for-kids).
**Discussion:** Why do you think God told shepherds first? If Jesus is a king, why be born like this?
**Craft:** Craft-stick manger. Glue five or six sticks into a small trough, add raffia or yellow paper straw, and lay a clothespin baby wrapped in a scrap of cloth inside.
**Memory verse:** "Today in the town of David a Savior has been born to you." -- Luke 2:11

### Lesson 9: The Lost Sheep

**Objective:** Kids will say that God comes looking for people who wander.
**Scripture:** Luke 15:3-7.
**Story:** A shepherd with a hundred sheep loses one. He leaves the ninety-nine, searches until he finds it, carries it home on his shoulders, and throws a party. Jesus says heaven does the same thing over one person coming home. Read the [full story for kids](/blog/the-lost-sheep-for-kids).
**Discussion:** Was the shepherd being reckless leaving ninety-nine? What does the party at the end tell you about God?
**Craft:** Cotton-ball sheep. A clothespin body with cotton glued on and a paper face. Hide one sheep per child around the room before class and let them hunt -- nobody sits down until every sheep is found.
**Memory verse:** "He calls his own sheep by name." -- John 10:3

### Lesson 10: The Good Samaritan

**Objective:** Kids will define "neighbor" the way Jesus did: anyone who needs my help.
**Scripture:** Luke 10:25-37.
**Story:** A man is robbed and left on the road. A priest passes. A temple assistant passes. The one who stops, bandages him, and pays for his care is the foreigner nobody in the audience liked. Read the [full story for kids](/blog/the-good-samaritan-for-kids).
**Discussion:** What might the first two men have told themselves? Helping cost the Samaritan time and money -- what would it cost you?
**Craft:** Helping-hands bandage book. Kids trace their hand on paper and write one specific way to help on each finger, then attach a real adhesive bandage to the palm as the cover.
**Memory verse:** "Love your neighbor as yourself." -- Mark 12:31

### Lesson 11: The Empty Tomb

**Objective:** Kids will state the central Christian claim in their own words.
**Scripture:** Luke 23:50-24:12.
**Story:** Jesus is buried in a borrowed tomb with a stone across the door. On Sunday morning the women come with spices, find the stone rolled away and the tomb empty, and two angels ask, "Why do you look for the living among the dead? He is not here; he has risen!" Read the [full story for kids](/blog/the-empty-tomb-for-kids).
**Discussion:** The women came expecting a body. What changed everything? Why is the resurrection the most important part of the whole Bible?
**Craft:** Rolling-stone tomb. Half a paper plate folded into a cave shape glued to a base, with a circle of gray cardstock that really rolls aside. Inside, the strips of cloth -- and nothing else.
**Memory verse:** "He is not here; he has risen!" -- Luke 24:6

### Lesson 12: The Armor of God

**Objective:** Kids will name each piece of armor and what it stands for.
**Scripture:** Ephesians 6:10-18.
**Story:** Paul, writing from prison and probably looking at a Roman guard, tells Christians to put on God's armor: the belt of truth, breastplate of righteousness, shoes of peace, shield of faith, helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is God's word. Read the [full story for kids](/blog/the-armor-of-god-for-kids).
**Discussion:** Which piece do you most need this week? Why is the sword the only piece you attack with -- and why is it Scripture?
**Craft:** Wearable paper armor. Cut a breastplate and shield from grocery bags or cardstock, a helmet band, and a foil-wrapped cardboard sword. Kids label each piece with its meaning before wearing it.
**Memory verse:** "Put on the full armor of God." -- Ephesians 6:11

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are free Sunday school lessons actually good enough to use?

Yes, with one condition: read the whole lesson before you teach it. Free material from Ministry-to-Children, Life.Church, and Children's Ministry Deals is written by working children's pastors and is genuinely classroom-ready. The risk is not quality but fit -- theology, age level, and cultural assumptions vary, so preview rather than print-and-go.

### What is the catch with "free" curriculum sites?

Most free curriculum is either ad-supported, a sample of a paid product, or gated behind an email signup. Ministry-to-Children, Sermons4Kids, and DLTK-Bible are ad-supported and require nothing. Life.Church Open Network is free with no upsell at all. Children's Ministry Deals and Answers in Genesis give away samples to introduce a paid curriculum, which is a fair trade as long as you know that is what is happening.

### Can I use free lessons for a whole year without paying anything?

Yes. Between Life.Church's Konnect curriculum, the 150-plus free lessons at Children's Ministry Deals, and our own [52-week plan](/blog/sunday-school-lessons-for-kids), a small church can run a complete year for zero dollars beyond craft supplies. The trade is your time: free curriculum requires more assembly than a purchased box.

### What crafts work when I have almost no supplies?

Paper plates, construction paper, and glue cover nine of the twelve crafts above. If you have literally nothing, use drawing: fold a page in half and have kids draw the "before" and "after" of the story. It costs one sheet of paper per child and produces better retelling than most kits.

### How much prep time do these 12 lessons take?

Roughly 15 minutes each -- read the passage, gather the craft supplies, and pick two discussion questions. The crafts are deliberately built from items most church closets already hold, so the only real prep is cutting paper shapes in advance for classes under age six.

### Can I copy these lessons for my volunteers?

Yes. Print them, email them, or hand out copies to your teaching team for use in your church. We ask only that you do not resell them or republish the text on another website. If your children's ministry director wants the full year, send them the [52-week outline](/blog/sunday-school-lessons-for-kids).
