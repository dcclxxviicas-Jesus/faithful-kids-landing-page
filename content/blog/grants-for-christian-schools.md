Search for Christian school grants and you get grant-database aggregators promising thousands of funders behind a signup wall, plus lists that name the biggest Christian foundations without mentioning one crucial fact: several of them do not accept applications at all.

So this guide was built the other way around. We loaded every funder's own pages in November 2026 and sorted them into two piles: programs where a Christian school, church children's ministry, or teacher can actually apply, and famous names that will waste your time. Where a funder does not publish award amounts, this guide says so rather than inventing a number.

## First, the Time-Savers: Big Names You Cannot Apply To

**The Herzog Foundation** has given millions to Christian schools, and their own page says plainly: "The Herzog Foundation does not accept unsolicited grant inquiries." Grants are foundation-initiated. What you CAN get from them is free training: their events for Christian school educators are offered at no charge, and through their partnership with ACSI they have covered registration, hotel, and food for educators attending the Flourishing Schools Institute. Go for the training, not the grant.

**The National Christian Foundation** is not a grantmaker you apply to; it is a donor-advised fund provider. Donors recommend grants from their NCF funds. The practical move for a school is making sure you are a 501(c)(3) in good standing and cultivating the donors in your community who give through NCF, not hunting for an application that does not exist.

**Lilly Endowment** funds Christian life generously, with special attention to children and youth, but through invited initiative rounds aimed at institutions and intermediary organizations. An individual Sunday school will not apply directly; a denomination or seminary might.

**The Mustard Seed Foundation closed** after 40 years. It still appears on grant lists; strike it from yours.

**ACSI** itself runs no open grant program; treat it as a conduit to funded training (see Herzog above) and to state tax-credit scholarship programs for students.

## Grants for Churches and Children's Ministries

**Brotherhood Mutual's Kingdom Advancing Grant** is the one to circle. It funds "innovative Christian church programs that are transforming local communities through ministry," awards a **$150,000 pool annually** across multiple churches, pairs winners with a ministry mentor, and wants programs that are replicable. The 2027 cycle opens **January 4, 2027** and closes **March 31, 2027**. A children's ministry doing something creative that reaches beyond the congregation is exactly the profile.

**The WMU Foundation** (Woman's Missionary Union, in the Southern Baptist orbit) runs the closest thing we found to a dedicated children's ministry grant: the **Barbara Massey Children's Network Grant**, plus curriculum grants for children's and youth missions programs. Applications open **January 1 and close the last day of February**, with notifications by May 15.

**United Methodist churches** have Discipleship Ministries' Grants for Ministries with Young People, funding projects designed and implemented primarily by young people, through a two-stage application that begins each January. Texas UMC applicants can also request up to $15,000 from the Sam Taylor Trust Fund for adult Christian education.

**Walmart's Local Community Grants** ($250-$5,000) name churches and faith-based organizations as eligible, with one condition from their own page: the project must benefit "the community at large," like food pantries or kids' programs open to the neighborhood. Supplies for your own Sunday school classroom will not qualify; a free community kids' event will.

## Grants for Christian School Teachers

Start with the eligibility traps, because they run in both directions:

- **DonorsChoose is public-school only.** Their own site limits signup to teachers employed at US public or public charter schools. Christian school teachers should not spend an evening on it.
- **AdoptAClassroom.org takes you.** Their eligibility covers licensed teachers at "a public, charter, or accredited private PreK-12 school." The operative word is accredited: if your school holds ACSI or regional accreditation, you can register a classroom page, crowdfund supplies, and become eligible for their periodic grants and surprise donations.
- **Toshiba America Foundation** says it "proudly supports public and private schools" with math and science project grants: grades 6-12 requests up to $5,000 are reviewed quarterly (due March 1, June 1, September 1, December 1), larger requests twice a year, and the K-5 application opens each October 1.
- **Dollar General Literacy Foundation** funds any IRS-recognized nonprofit or school providing direct literacy services within 15 miles of a Dollar General store: up to $10,000 for adult and family literacy (opens January 8), $5,000 for youth literacy (opens March 5), $3,000 for summer reading. Religious organizations are neither named nor excluded in their criteria, so a 501(c)(3) Christian school fits the stated terms; confirm with the foundation before building plans on it.

## The Library Grant With a Deadline You Should Not Miss

**The Laura Bush Foundation for America's Libraries** explicitly welcomes private and parochial schools where at least 50% of students qualify for financial aid, and funds books, audiobooks, e-books, and magazines for school libraries. The current application is due **December 31, 2026**, and their own overview page states that **2027 marks the final round of grants the foundation will ever make**. If your Christian school has a library and a qualifying student body, this is the most time-sensitive item on this page.

## For Bigger Projects: The Murdock Trust

Christian schools in Alaska, Idaho, Montana, Oregon, and Washington (plus faith-based organizations in British Columbia) can apply to the **M.J. Murdock Charitable Trust**, a major faith-friendly funder, but read the floor before you dream: minimum total project budget of $200,000 and minimum request of $100,000, accreditation required, church-affiliated schools must draw at least 30% of students from outside a single church, and their early childhood and elementary programs are currently paused. This is for the capital campaign, not the classroom.

**Tyndale House Foundation** grants to US 501(c)(3) Christian ministries on two annual cycles (applications due January 2 and July 2), through a deliberate process: confirm eligibility, get a portal account approved, meet with staff, then receive permission to submit a full proposal. They publish no award ranges, so plan the relationship, not the amount.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the Herzog Foundation give grants to Christian schools?

Yes, but you cannot apply. Their site states they do not accept unsolicited grant inquiries; grants are foundation-initiated. Their free training events and funded conference attendance (through the ACSI partnership) are the parts open to any Christian school.

### Can Christian school teachers use DonorsChoose?

No. DonorsChoose eligibility is limited to teachers at US public and public charter schools. The equivalent that does accept accredited private Christian school teachers is AdoptAClassroom.org.

### What grants can a Sunday school or children's ministry apply for?

Verified options as of November 2026: Brotherhood Mutual's Kingdom Advancing Grant (church programs, $150,000 pool, opens January 4, 2027), the WMU Foundation's children's ministry and curriculum grants (January 1 to end of February), UMC Discipleship Ministries grants for Methodist congregations, and Walmart Local Community Grants when the program serves the wider community.

### Are there grants for Christian school libraries?

Yes, briefly: the Laura Bush Foundation welcomes private and parochial schools where at least half of students qualify for financial aid, with applications due December 31, 2026. Their 2027 round is the final one the foundation plans to make.
