Most "grants for churches" lists share a quiet problem: half their entries are funds you cannot actually apply to, either because the program is invitation-only, because it funds seminaries rather than congregations, or because it shut down years ago and nobody checked. With roughly 350,000 to 400,000 congregations in the US competing for the real programs, a pastor's grant-writing hours are too scarce to spend on closed doors.

So we verified every program below on the funder's own live page on November 16, 2026, sorted by the only question that matters: can your church actually apply, and when? One disclosure: we're not a funder. We make Faithful Kids, a Bible video app for kids, and our entry in this ecosystem is the free-resources section at the end, labeled as exactly that.

## Mark the First Week of January

The calendar quirk of this niche: three of the best programs all open within days of each other in early January 2027.

**[Lilly Endowment Clergy Renewal Programs](https://www.cts.edu/clergy-renewal/)** (administered by Christian Theological Seminary): up to **$60,000** for a pastor's renewal leave, designed jointly by congregation and pastor. Congregations in all 50 states apply with their pastor; part-time and bi-vocational pastors qualify. The 2027 portal opens **January 4, 2027**, proposals due March 17. Last cycle funded 255 congregations. Note the current figure is $60,000; the "$50,000" in older lists is the 2020 number.

**[Brotherhood Mutual's Kingdom Advancing Grant](https://kag.brotherhoodmutual.com/)**: funds innovative church outreach programs from a **$150,000 annual pool**. The 2027 application opens **January 4, 2027**, due March 31. Their own newsroom publishes the honesty stat most funders hide: roughly 400 ministries applied for 5 grants in 2023, so write a genuinely distinctive proposal or spend the hours elsewhere. (Also worth knowing: despite what some lists claim, Brotherhood Mutual has no security grant; this outreach grant is the program.)

**[National Fund for Sacred Places](https://fundforsacredplaces.org/)**: the real building money, if your building qualifies. Matching grants of **$50,000 to $500,000** for capital projects at community-serving *historic* houses of worship, any tradition, funded by Lilly through Partners for Sacred Places. Next cycle opens **January 12, 2027**, letters of intent due March 3.

## Open Cycles Running Now

- **[Calvin Institute's Vital Worship Grants](https://worship.calvin.edu/grants/worshiping-communities/)**: **$8,000-$25,000** for congregations connecting worship with faith formation, US and Canada. It runs twice-yearly deadlines (the fall 2026 one just passed; 2027 dates post on their timeline page, typically spring and fall).
- **[United Thank Offering grants](https://www.episcopalchurch.org/ministries/united-thank-offering/grants/)** (Episcopal): applications open now for 2027, due February 5, for projects connecting churches with local community needs.
- **[Foundation for Evangelism Local Church Cohorts](https://foundationforevangelism.org/grants/open-grant-cycles/)** (Wesleyan-tradition churches): open now; a cohort-training path that leads to grant eligibility, with sessions starting January 2027.
- **Lilly's [Serving Neighbors initiative](https://lillyendowment.org/for-grantseekers/open-initiatives/)**: the one congregation-facing Lilly initiative currently listed open, for charitable organizations coordinating collaborations with congregations. Aggregators report planning grants to $50,000 and concept papers due mid-January 2027; confirm the figures on Lilly's own initiative page, since they aren't restated there.
- **Denominational channels**: most major denominations run grant programs their own churches can reach, like the Presbyterian Foundation's **[Church Technology Grant](https://www.presbyterianfoundation.org/resources/church-financial-literacy-and-leadership-program/tech-grant/)** (up to $5,000, priority to churches of 75 members or fewer; the 2026 round closed in July, next cycle posts on that page). Ask your judicatory office what exists before looking outside; it's the least competitive money in this list.

## Security Money: the FEMA Program Every Church Asks About

The **[Nonprofit Security Grant Program](https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/nonprofit-security)** is the big one: a **$300 million** federal pool (FY2026) for physical security at nonprofits including houses of worship: cameras, locks, lighting, training. Three facts that save churches from wasted effort: you apply through your **State Administrative Agency**, never FEMA directly, and state deadlines fall weeks before the federal one; per-site awards cap around $200,000 per the FY26 state notices; and the FY2026 cycle is closed (awards went out in September), with the next expected in spring 2027. The move to make now: find your SAA, tell them you intend to apply, and join FEMA's NSGP mailing list so the 2027 notice doesn't pass you. The DOJ also ran a separate houses-of-worship security solicitation in 2026; watch for a 2027 round.

## Invitation-Only: Don't Spend an Application Here

Named because old lists keep sending pastors at them:

- **Lilly Endowment's general religion grantmaking** has no open door for congregations; its money flows through funded intermediary organizations (the Thriving Congregations rounds are restricted to organizations already inside the initiative).
- **Kern Family Foundation** states it plainly on its own site: "The Foundation does not accept unsolicited proposals." Its old pastors program is history.
- **GHR Foundation** funds Catholic church vitality through chosen partners and publishes no application process.

## Dead or Miscited: Stop Chasing These

- **Mustard Seed Foundation**: closed at the end of 2023 after a planned wind-down. Its church planting and building grants no longer exist, whatever the roundups say.
- **"Lilly Pathways"**: funds theological schools, not congregations; a church cannot apply.
- **Palmer Trust**: flows through American Baptist channels to that denomination's bodies; there is no open public application.

## Not Grants, But Real Money

- **[Thrivent Action Teams](https://www.thrivent.com/about-us/membership/thrivent-action-teams)**: not a church grant, but up to **$250 in seed money** per project for volunteer-led service projects, available to Thrivent clients (up to twice a year each). A congregation with ten Thrivent members can quietly fund a year of service projects this way.
- **[Google for Nonprofits](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869)**: free Workspace and up to **$10,000 a month in Ad Grants**. The eligibility nuance for churches: Google requires IRS 501(c)(3) recognition, directly or through a denominational group exemption, and its page no longer names houses of worship explicitly either way, so a church with a determination letter (or covered by its denomination's) qualifies on the 501(c)(3) ground.
- **Microsoft**: a correction most lists haven't caught. The famous free Microsoft 365 Business Premium nonprofit grant was **discontinued in 2025**; what remains is up to 300 free Business Basic (web-only) licenses plus deep discounts, per [Microsoft's eligibility page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/nonprofits/eligibility).
- **[TechSoup](https://support.techsoup.org/hc/en-us/articles/360030982713)**: discounted software and hardware, with its own page stating churches need actual 501(c)(3) recognition, "no exceptions," plus an EIN.
- **Buildings, honestly**: outside the historic-buildings fund above, church construction is financed, not granted. The established lenders are **[AGFinancial](https://www.agfinancial.org/church-loans)** (construction and permanent loans, terms to 25 years) and **[Thrivent Church Financing](https://www.thrivent.com/churchfinancing/)** (church mortgages since 1905). Budgeting a build around hoped-for grants is the most common planning mistake in this niche.
- **Community foundations**: nearly every US county sits under one, and most fund church *community programs* (food pantries, after-school care, ESL) rather than worship or buildings. Search your county's foundation plus "grant guidelines."

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How can a small church get grant money?

Start with the least competitive channels: your own denomination's programs, your county's community foundation for community-serving programs, and Thrivent Action Teams for project seed money. Then calendar the January openers (Lilly clergy renewal, Brotherhood Mutual) and the spring FEMA security cycle via your state agency. Small churches get specific advantages in several programs; the Presbyterian tech grant explicitly prioritizes churches of 75 or fewer.

### Are there grants for church buildings?

Almost none, with one real exception: the National Fund for Sacred Places offers $50,000-$500,000 matching grants for community-serving historic buildings, opening again January 2027. Non-historic construction is financed through church lenders, not granted; plan accordingly.

### What security grants exist for churches?

FEMA's Nonprofit Security Grant Program is the main one ($300M in FY2026, houses of worship eligible, applied through your State Administrative Agency). The FY2026 round is closed; prepare now for the expected spring 2027 notice, because state deadlines arrive fast and early applicants with a completed vulnerability assessment score better.

### Is there free money for churches with no application?

No, and treat any pitch that says otherwise as a scam. The closest real things are free-resource programs with light signups: Google for Nonprofits ($10K/month in ad credit for 501(c)(3) churches), TechSoup discounts, free ministry curriculum, and free church accounts for tools like ours. Every legitimate grant above has an application and real competition.
