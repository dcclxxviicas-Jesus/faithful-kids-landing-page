These 22 Habakkuk Bible trivia questions cover all three chapters -- the prophet's two complaints, God's surprising answers, and the "yet I will rejoice" ending -- with answers and verse references after every question. They're sorted into easy (ages 5-8), medium (ages 9-12), and hard (teens and adults) rounds.

Habakkuk is the prophet for every kid who has ever asked "Why does God let bad things happen?" -- because that's literally what the whole book is: a prophet asking God hard questions out loud, and God answering.

## What Is the Book of Habakkuk About?

The book of Habakkuk is a conversation between a frustrated prophet and God, written in Judah shortly before the Babylonian invasion, around 610-605 BC. Unlike most prophets, Habakkuk doesn't speak to the people for God -- he speaks to God about the people. He complains that violence and injustice go unpunished (Habakkuk 1:2-4), and God's answer floors him: judgment is coming through the Babylonians, a nation even more wicked (Habakkuk 1:6). That triggers complaint number two.

We know nothing about Habakkuk personally except his title, "the prophet" (Habakkuk 1:1). But his book holds one of the most influential sentences in Scripture -- "the righteous person will live by his faithfulness" (Habakkuk 2:4) -- and it ends not with answers, but with trust: even if every crop fails and every pen is empty, "yet I will rejoice in the LORD" (Habakkuk 3:18).

## Why Does Habakkuk Matter So Much?

Habakkuk matters because Habakkuk 2:4 -- "the righteous will live by faith" -- became a cornerstone of the New Testament, quoted three separate times (Romans 1:17, Galatians 3:11, Hebrews 10:38). Centuries later, that same verse in Romans helped ignite the Protestant Reformation. Not bad for a three-chapter book most people can't find without the table of contents.

It also matters for parenting: Habakkuk gives kids biblical permission to bring honest questions to God. The prophet asks "How long?" and "Why?" -- and God doesn't scold him for asking. Then history unfolds exactly as God said, when [Judah fell to Babylon](/blog/judah-falls-to-babylon-for-kids) in 586 BC. For a kid wrestling with why bad things happen, Habakkuk models the move from "why?" to "yet I will rejoice" -- honest doubt turning into sturdy [joy that doesn't depend on circumstances](/blog/bible-verses-about-joy-for-kids).

## Easy Habakkuk Trivia Questions (Ages 5-8)

1. Who is talking to God through the whole book of Habakkuk? **The prophet Habakkuk** (Habakkuk 1:1)

2. What did Habakkuk do that most prophets didn't -- complain to the people, or ask God hard questions? **He asked God hard questions** (Habakkuk 1:2)

3. What two-word question opens Habakkuk's first complaint? **"How long?"** (Habakkuk 1:2)

4. Did God get angry at Habakkuk for asking questions? **No -- God answered him** (Habakkuk 1:5, 2:2)

5. What animal's feet does Habakkuk say God gives him at the very end of the book? **A deer's** (Habakkuk 3:19)

6. Finish the verse: "The LORD is in his holy temple; let all the earth be ___ before him." **Silent** (Habakkuk 2:20)

7. Even if the fig tree doesn't bud and the fields grow no food, what does Habakkuk say he will still do? **Rejoice in the LORD** (Habakkuk 3:17-18)

## Medium Habakkuk Trivia Questions (Ages 9-12)

8. What was Habakkuk's first complaint about? **Violence and injustice in Judah going unpunished** (Habakkuk 1:2-4)

9. Which nation did God say he was raising up to bring judgment? **The Babylonians (also called the Chaldeans)** (Habakkuk 1:6)

10. Why did God's answer upset Habakkuk even more? **Because the Babylonians were even more wicked than Judah** (Habakkuk 1:13)

11. Where did Habakkuk station himself to wait for God's second answer? **At his watch, on the ramparts -- like a guard on a watchtower** (Habakkuk 2:1)

12. What did God tell Habakkuk to do with the revelation? **Write it down and make it plain on tablets, so a herald could run with it** (Habakkuk 2:2)

13. What is Habakkuk 2:4's famous line about how the righteous person will live? **By his faithfulness -- "the righteous will live by faith"** (Habakkuk 2:4)

14. Habakkuk 2:14 says the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD -- as what covers what? **As the waters cover the sea** (Habakkuk 2:14)

15. What kind of writing is Habakkuk chapter 3 -- a letter, a prayer-song, or a list of laws? **A prayer-song, complete with musical directions** (Habakkuk 3:1, 3:19)

## Hard Habakkuk Trivia Questions (Teens and Adults)

16. Name one of the three New Testament books that quote Habakkuk 2:4. **Romans, Galatians, or Hebrews** (Romans 1:17, Galatians 3:11, Hebrews 10:38)

17. What Hebrew musical word -- also common in Psalms -- appears three times in Habakkuk 3? **Selah** (Habakkuk 3:3, 3:9, 3:13)

18. What instruction closes the book, showing chapter 3 was meant to be performed? **"For the director of music. On my stringed instruments."** (Habakkuk 3:19)

19. In God's first answer, he says he is doing something in Habakkuk's days that the prophet wouldn't believe even if -- what? **Even if he were told** (Habakkuk 1:5)

20. Habakkuk 2 pronounces five "woes." Name one target. **Those who pile up stolen goods, gain by unjust means, build with bloodshed, get neighbors drunk, or worship idols** (Habakkuk 2:6-19)

21. List two of the six failed harvests and flocks in Habakkuk's "yet I will rejoice" vow. **Fig tree, grapes/vines, olive crop, fields with no food, no sheep in the pen, no cattle in the stalls** (Habakkuk 3:17)

22. What does God tell Habakkuk to do if the revelation seems slow in coming? **Wait for it -- "though it linger, wait for it; it will certainly come and will not delay"** (Habakkuk 2:3)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who wrote the book of Habakkuk?

The book was written by the prophet Habakkuk, who is identified only as "Habakkuk the prophet" (Habakkuk 1:1) -- the Bible gives no hometown, family, or tribe. The musical notes in chapter 3 suggest he may have been involved in temple worship, but that's inference, not stated fact. He prophesied in Judah shortly before Babylon's invasion, around 610-605 BC.

### How many chapters does Habakkuk have?

Habakkuk has 3 chapters and 56 verses. Chapters 1-2 are a back-and-forth dialogue -- two complaints from Habakkuk, two answers from God -- and chapter 3 is a closing prayer-song with musical directions.

### What is the most famous verse in Habakkuk?

Habakkuk 2:4 -- "the righteous person will live by his faithfulness" -- is the book's most famous verse. It's quoted in Romans 1:17, Galatians 3:11, and Hebrews 10:38, and it later became a key verse of the Protestant Reformation. Habakkuk 3:17-18, the "yet I will rejoice" passage, is the family favorite.

### What is the main message of Habakkuk?

The main message is that faith means trusting God's justice even when you can't see it yet. Habakkuk asks why evil goes unpunished, learns that judgment is coming in God's timing, and lands on a resolve to rejoice in God even if everything else fails (Habakkuk 3:17-19).

### Why did Habakkuk question God?

Habakkuk questioned God because he saw violence and injustice all around him in Judah and God seemed to be doing nothing (Habakkuk 1:2-4). When God answered that Babylon would bring the judgment, Habakkuk questioned again -- how could a holy God use an even more wicked nation? The book never treats those questions as sinful; it treats them as the doorway to deeper faith.

### What age can kids start learning the book of Habakkuk?

Kids around 9 and up get the most from Habakkuk, since the book is a dialogue about justice rather than an action story. Younger kids can still learn the "yet I will rejoice" verses and the deer-feet picture in Habakkuk 3:17-19, which work beautifully as memory verses.
