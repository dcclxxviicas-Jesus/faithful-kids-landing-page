## Display Ads: The Thresholds Have Changed

The entry bar is far lower than old advice says, and one famous rule is gone.

**Journey by Mediavine** is the realistic first network for a growing faith blog: their requirement is **1,000 sessions in 30 days** from Tier 1 countries (US, Canada, UK, Australia), verified on Mediavine's own requirements page. That is a new blog's third or fourth month, not its third year.

**Mediavine proper no longer uses the 50,000-sessions rule** you will see quoted everywhere. Their current published requirement is **a minimum of $5,000 in annual ad revenue** plus clean, human, brand-safe traffic. If a post tells you "50k sessions for Mediavine," it is out of date.

**Raptive** (formerly AdThrive) now asks for **at least 25,000 pageviews a month**, down from the old 100k threshold, plus Google Analytics, original content, and majority traffic from US/CA/UK/AU/NZ.

**Monumetric** starts its Propel tier at **10,000 to 80,000 pageviews a month**, and **SHE Media** asks for 50k+ pageviews while saying outright that they review applications case by case and sometimes take smaller sites.

None of these networks charges to apply. The practical path: Journey at 1,000 sessions, then graduate upward as traffic grows.

## Affiliate Programs: What Actually Pays

**Amazon Associates** pays **4.5% on physical books and 3% on toys** per its current published rate card. Those rates are low, which is exactly why the direct programs below matter.

**Christianbook.com** runs its program through CJ Affiliate. Their own page promises commissions on qualified purchases but publishes no rate, so ignore the conflicting percentages floating around third-party lists; you learn your rate on acceptance.

**Logos (Faithlife)** has a real partner program for Bible study content, applied for at logos.com, with roughly a 30-day cookie per their terms. Rates are set individually per partner, so again: no number until you are in. They do say they want "a fairly sizeable audience" and mission alignment.

**Bibles.com** (American Bible Society) pays commissions on store sales through the Awin network; their page lists a direct contact for questions, which is rare and useful.

**Two programs people keep recommending that do not exist:**

- **DaySpring's affiliate program is gone.** The old affiliate URL redirects to Hallmark, which absorbed the brand. Any list still including it has not been checked recently.
- **Crossway has no affiliate program and never paid commissions.** What it has is the **Crossway Review Program**: a free book each month in exchange for an honest review on your blog or social channels, open to US residents, with a required disclosure line. It is a great program; it is just a product-for-review program, not an income stream.

**Lifeway** has no affiliate program either (though its imprint B&H sends review copies to bloggers through NetGalley), and **Minno's** affiliate terms page has not been revised since 2015 with no live signup, so treat that one as "contact them directly."

## Ministry and Brand Partnerships

This is the route every ranking post misses, and for a Christian blogger it is often the most natural fit.

**World Vision** runs a real partnerships program, powered by Impact, where you **earn ad revenue when readers click through your links** to World Vision pages: the Gift Catalog, child sponsorship, clean water campaigns. For a blog whose readers already care about those causes, this is sponsorship without the awkwardness.

**Compassion International** has run blogger and influencer partnerships for years and is actively staffing that team; their program pages block automated checking, so go to compassion.com and pitch directly.

**Angel Studios** runs show-specific partner programs, verified live for The Tuttle Twins and the Wingfeather Saga: an affiliate track with commissions on referred purchases and subscriptions, and an influencer track with rewards and early access. If you write about Christian kids' media, these are built for you.

**Museum of the Bible** runs free influencer preview events for exhibitions (we verified a recent one offering early access and curator meet-and-greets). There is no standing program; watch their site and pitch their press contact.

## Selling Your Own Products

The math, from the platforms' own pages: **Etsy** charges $0.20 per listing plus a 6.5% transaction fee on the order total, which still leaves healthy margin on digital printables that cost nothing to reproduce. **Amazon KDP** pays 35% or 70% royalties on ebooks depending on pricing choices, and **50% or 60% on paperbacks** depending on list price, with printing costs deducted (the old flat "60%" claim is stale).

For a Christian parenting blogger, the proven product ladder is printables first (Bible activity packs, Scripture cards), then a low-priced devotional or study guide, then courses. Our own [free printables library](/printables) is an example of the format that works; yours can be the paid version for your audience.

## The Honest Sequencing

1. **Months 1-3: affiliate links and one product.** Amazon pays from day one; a $4 printable pack can sell from week one. Neither needs traffic thresholds.
2. **Month 3-6: apply to Journey** at 1,000 sessions. Display ads then grow on autopilot while you write.
3. **From month 6: pitch partnerships.** Ministries and Christian media brands care more about audience trust than audience size, and a Christian parenting blog with 5,000 engaged readers is exactly what World Vision's program exists for.
4. **Always: disclose.** Every program above requires clear FTC disclosure of affiliate links and gifted products, and your readers' trust is the whole asset.
5. **Track what actually pays.** Most established faith bloggers report that two or three income streams carry everything; find yours and cut the rest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much traffic do you need to monetize a Christian blog?

Less than old advice claims. Journey by Mediavine accepts blogs at 1,000 monthly sessions from Tier 1 countries, Raptive now takes sites at 25,000 pageviews a month, and affiliate programs plus your own products need no minimum at all. The famous "50,000 sessions for Mediavine" rule no longer exists; their current requirement is $5,000 in annual ad revenue.

### Does DaySpring have an affiliate program?

No, not anymore. DaySpring's affiliate page now redirects to Hallmark, which absorbed the brand, and no affiliate program survives as of November 2026. Lists still recommending it are out of date.

### Does Crossway have an affiliate program?

No. Crossway runs a Review Program: a free book each month in exchange for an honest, disclosed review, open to US residents. It never pays commissions.
