These 33 Job Bible trivia questions cover the man who lost everything and refused to curse God -- his tests, his unhelpful friends, and God's famous speech from the storm. Answers with verse references follow every question, sorted into easy, medium, and hard rounds by age.

Job is deeper water than most trivia books, but kids handle it better than we expect -- the story has a clear hero, a real villain, dramatic disasters, two mystery monsters (Behemoth and Leviathan), and a double-everything happy ending. The hard round even settles what "the patience of Job" actually looked like.

## What Is the Book of Job About?

The book of Job tells the story of a righteous man who loses his wealth, his children, and his health in a test he never learns about -- and asks the question every generation asks: why do good people suffer? The author is unknown and the date is debated; the story is set in the land of Uz in the age of the patriarchs, and many consider Job among the oldest stories in the Bible.

The structure is simple: two chapters of story set up the test, then roughly 39 chapters of poetry as Job debates three friends who insist his suffering must be punishment, until God himself answers from the whirlwind -- not with reasons, but with questions. The book matters because it refuses easy answers: God calls Job's simplistic friends wrong (Job 42:7), honors Job's honest wrestling, and shows that trusting God is possible even without understanding him. Its most famous line is Job's: "I know that my redeemer lives" (Job 19:25).

## Easy Job Trivia Questions (Ages 5-8)

1. What kind of man was Job before his troubles began? **Blameless and upright -- he feared God and shunned evil** (Job 1:1)

2. Was Job rich or poor at the start of the story? **Very rich -- he was the greatest man among all the people of the East** (Job 1:3)

3. How many sons and daughters did Job have at the beginning? **Seven sons and three daughters** (Job 1:2)

4. Who asked God for permission to test Job? **Satan** (Job 1:9-12)

5. When Job lost everything in one day, did he curse God? **No -- he worshiped and said, "The LORD gave and the LORD has taken away; may the name of the LORD be praised"** (Job 1:20-21)

6. What happened to Job's body in the second test? **He was covered with painful sores from head to toe** (Job 2:7)

7. How many friends came to sit with Job? **Three -- Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar** (Job 2:11)

8. Who finally spoke to Job out of a mighty storm? **God (the LORD)** (Job 38:1)

9. Did God make Job rich and happy again at the end? **Yes -- God gave him twice as much as he had before, plus seven sons and three daughters** (Job 42:10-13)

10. What does Job's story teach kids to do when life is hard? **Keep trusting God even when we don't understand -- Job never stopped talking to God** (Job 1:21; 42:1-6)

## Medium Job Trivia Questions (Ages 9-12)

11. In what land did Job live? **The land of Uz** (Job 1:1)

12. How many sheep, camels, oxen, and donkeys did Job own at the start? **7,000 sheep, 3,000 camels, 500 yoke of oxen, and 500 donkeys** (Job 1:3)

13. What did Satan claim was the only reason Job served God? **Because God had blessed and protected him -- "put a hedge around him" -- and Job would curse God if it were all taken away** (Job 1:9-11)

14. What four disasters struck Job in a single day? **Sabeans stole his oxen and donkeys, fire from heaven burned his sheep, Chaldeans stole his camels, and a great wind collapsed the house, killing his children** (Job 1:13-19)

15. What terrible advice did Job's wife give him? **"Curse God and die!"** (Job 2:9)

16. How did Job answer his wife? **"Shall we accept good from God, and not trouble?" -- and Job did not sin in what he said** (Job 2:10)

17. What did Job's three friends do for the first seven days and nights? **They sat on the ground with him in silence, because they saw how great his suffering was** (Job 2:13)

18. What did Job's friends keep insisting was the reason for his suffering? **That Job must have sinned -- suffering, they argued, is always punishment (they were wrong)** (Job 4:7-8; 42:7)

19. What did Job use to scrape his sores, and where did he sit? **A piece of broken pottery, sitting among the ashes** (Job 2:8)

20. What was the first thing Job said when he finally broke his silence in chapter 3? **He cursed the day of his birth -- not God, but his own birthday** (Job 3:1-3)

21. Complete Job's most famous declaration of hope: "I know that my redeemer..." **"...lives, and that in the end he will stand on the earth"** (Job 19:25)

22. What was the first question God asked Job from the storm? **"Where were you when I laid the earth's foundation?"** (Job 38:4)

23. How did Job respond after God finished speaking? **He humbly took back his words and repented -- "Surely I spoke of things I did not understand... my ears had heard of you but now my eyes have seen you"** (Job 42:3-6)

24. What did God tell Job to do for his three friends? **Pray for them, after they offered a sacrifice -- and God accepted Job's prayer** (Job 42:8-9)

## Hard Job Trivia Questions (Teens and Adults)

25. A fourth, younger man spoke after the three friends fell silent. What was his name? **Elihu son of Barakel the Buzite** (Job 32:2-6)

26. Where was Satan when he asked to test Job? **Presenting himself before the LORD when the angels ("sons of God") came, having been "roaming throughout the earth"** (Job 1:6-7)

27. What limit did God set on Satan in each of the two tests? **First, Satan could touch all Job had but not Job himself; second, he could strike Job's body but must spare his life** (Job 1:12; 2:6)

28. Name the two great creatures God describes in his speech to Job. **Behemoth and Leviathan** (Job 40:15; 41:1)

29. What did God say to Eliphaz about the friends' speeches? **"I am angry with you and your two friends, because you have not spoken the truth about me, as my servant Job has"** (Job 42:7)

30. What sacrifice did God require of the three friends? **Seven bulls and seven rams, with Job praying for them** (Job 42:8)

31. What were Job's herds numbered at after his restoration? **14,000 sheep, 6,000 camels, 1,000 yoke of oxen, and 1,000 donkeys -- exactly double** (Job 42:12)

32. What were the names of Job's three daughters born after his trial, and what was unusual about their inheritance? **Jemimah, Keziah, and Keren-Happuch -- and Job granted them an inheritance along with their brothers, rare in the ancient world** (Job 42:14-15)

33. How long did Job live after his trial, and what did he see? **140 more years -- he saw his children and their children to the fourth generation** (Job 42:16)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who wrote the book of Job?

Nobody knows -- the book never names its author, and suggestions have ranged from Job himself to Moses to Solomon. The story is set in the patriarchal era (Job lived over 140 years and offered sacrifices as head of his family), and many consider it among the oldest material in the Bible.

### How many chapters does Job have?

Job has 42 chapters: two chapters of narrative setup, about 39 chapters of poetic dialogue between Job, his friends, Elihu, and God, and a closing narrative of restoration.

### Is Job the oldest book in the Bible?

Possibly -- its patriarchal setting (family sacrifices, wealth measured in livestock, no mention of Israel or the Law) has led many to consider it among the oldest, but the date of writing is genuinely unknown and scholars disagree. It's safest to say the STORY is set very early, even if we can't date the book.

### Was Job a real person?

The Bible treats him as real: Ezekiel lists Job alongside Noah and Daniel as a righteous man (Ezekiel 14:14), and James points to "Job's perseverance" as a model for believers (James 5:11). His homeland of Uz lay somewhere east of Israel.

### What is the main lesson of the book of Job?

That God can be trusted even when suffering makes no sense -- and that "suffering is always punishment" is false, since God himself calls Job blameless and rebukes the friends who argued otherwise (Job 1:8; 42:7). Job never learns why he suffered; he learns WHO holds the world, and that turns out to be enough.

### What age can kids start learning the book of Job?

Ages 5-8 can handle the simple arc -- Job lost everything, kept trusting God, and God restored him -- especially with the happy ending in view. The harder questions about unexplained suffering fit ages 10 and up, and Job is one of the best books in the Bible for honest conversations with teens.
