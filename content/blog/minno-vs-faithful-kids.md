Minno and Faithful Kids are both Christian video apps for kids, but they are built for different jobs: Minno is Christian entertainment -- a streaming library of full-length animated shows -- while Faithful Kids is Bible education, with short structured lessons, quizzes, and progress tracking through Scripture in order. Choose Minno if you want a safe replacement for Netflix; choose Faithful Kids if you want your child to systematically learn the Bible; many families deliberately run both.

This comparison walks through content, Bible coverage, learning features, pricing, and ages so you can match the app to your actual goal -- because "which is better?" has a different answer depending on whether the problem you are solving is screen-time quality or Bible literacy.

## What Is Each App, in One Paragraph?

**Minno** is a streaming service for Christian kids' shows -- think of it as a faith-based Netflix. Its library includes hundreds of episodes of animated series (VeggieTales and What's in the Bible? are its best-known titles) plus movies and music, mostly in the 10-30 minute episode format kids know from mainstream streaming. Kids browse and watch; there is no lesson structure.

**Faithful Kids** is a Bible learning platform disguised as a kids' app. It walks children through the entire Bible -- Genesis to Revelation -- in 20 series of short video lessons, each followed by a comprehension quiz and a reflection question. Progress unlocks like a game (levels, streaks, achievements, Duolingo-style), and parents get a dashboard showing what their child watched and how they scored.

## When Should You Choose Faithful Kids?

Choose Faithful Kids if your goal is that your child actually knows the Bible -- not just recognizes a few famous stories, but can walk from Creation to the early church and tell you how the pieces connect. Three design choices serve that goal:

**Coverage and order.** The 20-series structure moves through Scripture sequentially, so kids build the storyline (promise, rescue, kingdom, exile, Messiah, church) instead of collecting disconnected episodes. Most Christian media, Minno included, concentrates on the same dozen famous stories; a curriculum structure covers Judges, the divided kingdom, the return from exile, and Paul's letters too.

**Accountability.** The quiz after every lesson is the feature parents notice most. A child cannot zone out through a lesson and click next -- comprehension is checked every time, and wrong answers turn into retries. This converts watching from passive to active screen time, which is precisely the distinction pediatric research cares about.

**Visibility.** The parent dashboard answers the question every Christian parent quietly asks about faith apps: "Is anything actually going in?" You can see series completed, quiz scores, and streaks rather than guessing.

The trade-off is honest: one-minute lessons plus quizzes are learning, not entertainment. Faithful Kids will not fill a rainy Saturday afternoon, and it is not trying to.

## When Should You Choose Minno?

Choose Minno if the problem you are solving is what your kids watch during entertainment time. If your 4-to-9-year-old currently watches an hour of Netflix or YouTube daily, redirecting that hour to VeggieTales and What's in the Bible? is a real upgrade -- values-aligned, ad-free, and genuinely fun. What's in the Bible? in particular carries more teaching content than most children's shows.

Minno's strengths are exactly where an education app is weak: long-form content that holds attention for a car ride or a movie night, beloved characters, and a browse-and-pick experience that feels like a treat rather than a lesson. Its trade-offs mirror that: no quizzes, no structured path through Scripture, no parent-facing learning data. Kids gravitate to favorites and rewatch them, which is fine for entertainment and insufficient for Bible literacy.

## Can You Use Both Together?

Yes, and this is often the best answer. The apps occupy different slots in a family's day, so they complement rather than compete:

- **Learning slot (daily, 5-10 minutes):** one Faithful Kids lesson plus quiz -- after breakfast or before bed, same time every day. Short enough to survive busy seasons.
- **Entertainment slot (a few times a week):** Minno instead of mainstream streaming -- family movie night, rainy afternoons, road trips.

Framed this way, the combined cost replaces both "curriculum" and "Netflix for the kids" line items. Families who try to make one app do both jobs usually end up disappointed in the direction they stretched it -- Minno will not produce Bible literacy on its own, and Faithful Kids will not carry a movie night.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Minno or Faithful Kids better overall?
Neither is better at the other's job. For systematic Bible education with quizzes, progress tracking, and full Genesis-to-Revelation coverage, Faithful Kids is the stronger app. For long-form Christian entertainment that replaces Netflix or YouTube, Minno is the stronger app. Decide which problem you are actually solving, and the winner picks itself.

### Does Minno have quizzes or learning features?
No. Minno is a streaming service -- kids browse shows and watch, like Netflix. There are no comprehension quizzes, structured lesson paths, or parent-facing progress reports. That is a design choice, not an oversight: Minno is built for entertainment, and it does that well.

### What ages is each app best for?
Minno lands best with ages 3-10, since most of its animated library targets younger kids. Faithful Kids is designed for ages 7-15 -- old enough to read quiz questions and follow a structured path. For a 7-to-10-year-old, both fit, which is the age band where families most often run the two together.

### Which app is cheaper?
It depends on the plan. Month to month, Faithful Kids is cheaper -- $8.88/mo versus $10.99/mo for Minno. On annual plans, Minno edges ahead -- $69.99/yr (about $5.83/mo) versus $77.77/yr (about $6.48/mo) for Faithful Kids. Comparing prices directly is slightly misleading, though, since one is priced as entertainment and the other as a learning program; the fairer comparison is against what each replaces.

### Does either app work for family devotions or homeschool?
Faithful Kids does -- its lesson-plus-quiz format drops directly into a homeschool Bible block or a nightly devotion slot, and the sequential structure functions as a scope and sequence. Minno can supplement (a What's in the Bible? episode makes a good discussion springboard), but it has no structure to build a devotional habit or curriculum around.
