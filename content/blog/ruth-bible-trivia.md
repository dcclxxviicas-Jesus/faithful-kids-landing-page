These 22 Ruth Bible trivia questions cover all four chapters of the book -- Naomi's heartbreak, Ruth's famous promise, the barley fields of Bethlehem, and the wedding that put a Moabite widow in the family tree of Jesus. Answers with verse references follow every question, sorted into easy, medium, and hard rounds.

Ruth is only four chapters long, which makes it the perfect first book for a family trivia night: kids can read the whole thing in one sitting and then actually compete with the adults. Don't let the length fool you, though -- the hard round has details that most grown-ups miss.

## What Is the Book of Ruth About?

The book of Ruth tells how a widowed foreigner from Moab chose loyalty to her Israelite mother-in-law Naomi, gleaned leftover grain to keep them both alive, and married a kind landowner named Boaz -- becoming the great-grandmother of King David. The author is anonymous; Jewish tradition attributes the book to the prophet Samuel, and the story is set "in the days when the judges ruled" (Ruth 1:1), roughly 1100 BC.

It matters far beyond its size. Ruth is a quiet story -- no plagues, no battles, no miracles -- just ordinary people being faithful in hard times, and God working through every ordinary choice. And its last word is a genealogy that lands on David (Ruth 4:22), which the New Testament extends all the way to Jesus (Matthew 1:5). A famine, two funerals, and a barley field turn out to be part of the road to Bethlehem's most famous birth.

## Easy Ruth Trivia Questions (Ages 5-8)

1. Who was the mother-in-law that Ruth refused to leave? **Naomi** (Ruth 1:14-16)
2. What country was Ruth from? **Moab** (Ruth 1:4)
3. What famous promise did Ruth make to Naomi? **"Where you go I will go... your people will be my people and your God my God"** (Ruth 1:16)
4. What town did Ruth and Naomi travel to together? **Bethlehem** (Ruth 1:19)
5. What did Ruth do in the fields so she and Naomi would have food? **She gleaned -- picked up the leftover grain behind the harvesters** (Ruth 2:2-3)
6. Who owned the field where Ruth gleaned? **Boaz** (Ruth 2:3)
7. Whom did Ruth marry at the end of the book? **Boaz** (Ruth 4:13)
8. What was the name of Ruth and Boaz's baby boy? **Obed** (Ruth 4:17)

## Medium Ruth Trivia Questions (Ages 9-12)

9. Why did Naomi's family leave Bethlehem for Moab in the first place? **There was a famine in the land** (Ruth 1:1)
10. What were the names of Naomi's two sons? **Mahlon and Kilion** (Ruth 1:2)
11. Who was Ruth's sister-in-law who kissed Naomi goodbye and went back to Moab? **Orpah** (Ruth 1:14)
12. What new name did Naomi ask to be called, and what does it mean? **Mara, meaning "bitter," because she felt God had made her life bitter** (Ruth 1:20)
13. Which harvest was just beginning when Ruth and Naomi arrived in Bethlehem? **The barley harvest** (Ruth 1:22)
14. What secret kindness did Boaz order his harvesters to do for Ruth? **Pull out stalks from the bundles on purpose and leave them for her to pick up** (Ruth 2:15-16)
15. What was Boaz to Naomi's family, which explains why he could help them? **A close relative -- one of their guardian-redeemers (kinsman-redeemers)** (Ruth 2:20)

## Hard Ruth Trivia Questions (Teens and Adults)

16. What did Ruth ask Boaz to do at the threshing floor, and what did it mean? **"Spread the corner of your garment over me" -- a request that he redeem and marry her** (Ruth 3:9)
17. What old custom made the redemption deal official at the town gate? **The nearer redeemer took off his sandal and handed it to Boaz** (Ruth 4:7-8)
18. Which of Naomi's sons had been Ruth's first husband? **Mahlon** (Ruth 4:10)
19. What did the women of Bethlehem tell Naomi that Ruth was worth to her? **More than seven sons** (Ruth 4:15)
20. Who was Obed's famous grandson? **King David -- Obed was the father of Jesse, the father of David** (Ruth 4:17)
21. In what era of Israel's history is the book of Ruth set? **"In the days when the judges ruled"** (Ruth 1:1)
22. Where does Ruth show up in the New Testament? **In the genealogy of Jesus** (Matthew 1:5)

## Why Is Ruth a Great Book for Kids to Learn?

Ruth is one of the best entry points to the whole Bible for kids because it is short, gentle, and complete -- a real beginning, middle, and happy ending in four chapters. There are no armies or judgments to explain, just questions kids actually connect with: What do you do when your family is sad? What does loyalty look like? Why does kindness to a stranger matter?

It also teaches two ideas kids will meet again and again in Scripture. The first is gleaning -- God's law told landowners to leave the edges of their fields unharvested so the poor and the foreigner could gather food (Leviticus 19:9-10), and Boaz obeys that law generously. The second is the guardian-redeemer: a close relative who steps in to rescue family members who have lost everything. When kids later hear that Jesus is called our Redeemer, Boaz is the picture that makes it click -- someone who had the right to help, the power to help, and the love to actually do it.

One more connection worth showing your kids: Boaz's own mother was Rahab, the woman who hid the Israelite spies in Jericho (Matthew 1:5). If they know [Rahab and the spies](/blog/rahab-and-the-spies-for-kids), they'll love discovering that the man who welcomed a foreign widow was himself the son of a foreign woman God welcomed first.

Finally, Ruth is a timing story, and kids enjoy tracking it. The book opens at the start of the barley harvest (Ruth 1:22), which falls around Passover in early spring, and the wheat harvest that follows (Ruth 2:23) runs to early summer -- so the whole romance unfolds over roughly one growing season. Jewish families still read Ruth aloud every year at Shavuot, the harvest festival, which means this little book has been a family read-aloud for well over two thousand years. Your dinner table is in good company.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who wrote the book of Ruth?

The book of Ruth is anonymous -- the text never names its author. Jewish tradition attributes it to the prophet Samuel, though the closing genealogy that mentions David (Ruth 4:17-22) suggests it reached its final form during or after David's rise.

### How many chapters does Ruth have?

Ruth has just 4 chapters, making it one of the shortest books in the Old Testament. Each chapter is one scene: the journey back to Bethlehem, the barley field, the threshing floor, and the town gate where Boaz redeems Ruth.

### What is the most famous verse in Ruth?

Ruth 1:16 is the book's most famous verse: "Where you go I will go, and where you stay I will stay. Your people will be my people and your God my God." Ruth says it to her mother-in-law Naomi, and it's one of the greatest statements of loyalty in the whole Bible.

### What does a kinsman-redeemer mean in Ruth?

A kinsman-redeemer (or guardian-redeemer) was a close relative responsible for rescuing family members in crisis -- buying back their land, providing for widows, and keeping the family line alive. Boaz fills that role for Ruth and Naomi (Ruth 2:20, 4:9-10), and many Christians see him as a picture of what Jesus later does for us.

### How is Ruth connected to Jesus?

Ruth was the great-grandmother of King David (Ruth 4:17), and Matthew's genealogy lists her by name in the family line of Jesus (Matthew 1:5). She is one of only a few women named in that genealogy -- remarkable for a foreign-born widow from Moab.

### What age can kids start learning the book of Ruth?

Ages 5 and up can follow Ruth easily -- it's a gentle story about loyalty and kindness with no scary content. The threshing-floor scene in chapter 3 is simply an old betrothal custom, and for young kids you can accurately describe it as Ruth asking Boaz to marry and protect their family.
