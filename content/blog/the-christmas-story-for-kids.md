The Christmas story is the true account of how God's Son, Jesus, was born as a baby in Bethlehem -- announced by angels, visited by shepherds, and worshiped by wise men from far away. Below is the full story retold in warm, simple words for reading aloud to kids, split into five short scenes with a question after each one.

The whole retelling takes about eight to ten minutes to read -- perfect for Christmas Eve, a bedtime in December, or spread across five nights of Advent week. It's drawn from Luke 1-2 and Matthew 1-2, and after the story you'll find a simple guide for turning this reading into a Christmas Eve tradition your kids will ask for every year.

## The Christmas Story: A Read-Aloud Retelling in 5 Scenes

### Scene 1: The Angel's Surprise

A long time ago, in a small town called Nazareth, there lived a young woman named Mary. She wasn't rich. She wasn't famous. She was ordinary -- like you and me.

One day, an angel named Gabriel appeared to her. Just imagine it: one moment Mary is going about her day, and the next, an angel of God is standing there, shining.

"Do not be afraid, Mary," the angel said. "God is very pleased with you. You are going to have a baby boy, and you will name Him Jesus. He will be great -- He will be called the Son of the Most High God, and His kingdom will never end."

Mary's heart pounded. How could this be? But the angel said, "Nothing is impossible with God."

And Mary, brave and trusting, gave her answer: "I am the Lord's servant. Let it happen just as you said."

An angel came to Joseph, too, in a dream, and told him not to be afraid to take Mary as his wife. "Name the baby Jesus," the angel said, "because He will save His people from their sins."

**Ask your kids:** Mary was scared, but she still said yes to God. Can you think of a time when you did the right thing even though it felt scary?

### Scene 2: The Long Journey

Months passed, and Mary's baby was almost ready to be born. But then came an announcement from the emperor, far away in Rome: *Everyone must travel to their family's hometown to be counted.*

Joseph's family came from Bethlehem -- the town of King David -- nearly ninety miles away. So Joseph and Mary packed up and set off. No cars. No trains. Just dusty roads, sore feet, and step after step after step, day after day, with the baby coming soon.

Here's a secret the emperor didn't know: hundreds of years earlier, God had promised through His prophets that the Savior would be born in Bethlehem -- that exact little town. The emperor thought he was counting people. Really, God was keeping a promise.

When Mary and Joseph finally reached Bethlehem, the town was packed with travelers. Joseph knocked on doors, but every guest room was full. Full, full, full. The only place left for them was where the animals stayed.

**Ask your kids:** God used a long, hard journey to bring Mary and Joseph exactly where He promised. Have you ever had something hard turn out to be part of a good plan?

### Scene 3: The Baby in the Manger

And there -- that very night, in that humble place that smelled of hay and animals -- Mary's baby was born.

The Son of God. The King above all kings. The One the whole world had been waiting for.

Mary wrapped Him snugly in strips of cloth, the way mothers did back then, and laid Him gently in a manger -- a feeding box for animals -- because there was no crib, no cradle, no royal nursery.

Think about that. God's own Son could have been born in the grandest palace on earth, with golden blankets and a hundred servants. Instead, He was born in the lowliest spot in a little town, to an ordinary family, and His first bed was a box of hay.

Why? Because Jesus didn't come only for kings and important people. He came for everyone -- shepherds and school kids, grandmas and babies, you and me. He started His life in the humblest place so that no one would ever feel too small, too poor, or too ordinary to come to Him.

**Ask your kids:** Why do you think God chose a manger instead of a palace for His Son?

### Scene 4: Angels and Shepherds

That same night, on the hills outside Bethlehem, shepherds were watching their sheep in the dark. It was a night like any other night -- until it wasn't.

Suddenly an angel stood before them, and the glory of the Lord blazed around them like the sun at midnight. The shepherds were terrified!

"Do not be afraid," the angel said. "I bring you good news that will cause great joy for all the people. Today, in the town of David, a Savior has been born to you. He is the Messiah, the Lord! And here is how you'll know Him: you will find a baby wrapped in cloths and lying in a manger."

Then -- as if one angel wasn't amazing enough -- the whole sky filled with angels, an army of them, praising God: "Glory to God in the highest heaven, and on earth peace to those on whom His favor rests!"

When the angels left, the shepherds looked at each other and said, "Let's go! Right now!" They hurried into Bethlehem, found Mary and Joseph, and there He was -- the baby, lying in the manger, exactly as the angel had said. And the shepherds ran off telling everyone they met what they had seen. They were the very first people to spread the news about Jesus.

**Ask your kids:** God sent the biggest announcement in history to ordinary shepherds first. What does that tell you about who matters to God?

### Scene 5: The Star and the Wise Men

Far, far away in the east, wise men who studied the sky noticed something new: a star. A special star. They knew it meant a great King had been born, so they loaded their camels and followed it -- for weeks and weeks, across deserts and mountains.

When they finally arrived, the star stopped over the place where the child was. The wise men were filled with joy. They went in, and when they saw Jesus with His mother Mary, these important, learned men got down on their knees and worshiped a little child.

Then they opened their treasures and gave Him gifts: gold, a gift for a king; frankincense, a sweet-smelling offering for God; and myrrh, a precious spice. The first Christmas gifts ever given -- and they were given to Jesus.

And that is the Christmas story. God kept His ancient promise. The Savior was born. And the good news the angel announced that night is still true today: great joy, for all the people. That includes you.

**Ask your kids:** The wise men traveled a very long way and gave Jesus their best gifts. What could you give Jesus this Christmas? (Hint: it doesn't have to be something you buy.)

## How Do You Make This a Christmas Eve Tradition?

Read the story aloud on Christmas Eve, in the same spot, the same way, every year -- that's the entire secret. Repetition is what turns a nice reading into a tradition kids treasure into adulthood. Here's a simple format that works:

1. **Set the scene (2 minutes).** Dim the lights, turn on the tree, light a candle -- if you keep an Advent wreath, tonight is the night to light the white Christ candle (our [Advent wreath guide](/blog/advent-wreath-meaning-for-kids) includes a Christmas Eve devotion that pairs with this reading).
2. **Assign parts.** Pre-readers hold nativity figures and place each one -- baby Jesus, shepherds, star -- as it appears in the story. Readers take a scene each. Grandparents on video calls make great Scene 4 narrators.
3. **Read all five scenes (8-10 minutes).** Use the questions or skip them -- on Christmas Eve, one question at the end is plenty.
4. **Read the "real words" (1 minute).** After the retelling, read Luke 2:11 straight from the Bible: "Today in the town of David a Savior has been born to you; he is the Messiah, the Lord." Kids should hear that the story comes from Scripture -- and if they want more of the actual verses, our list of [Christmas Bible verses for kids](/blog/christmas-bible-verses-for-kids) gives the whole story in 25 verses.
5. **Pray and sing one carol.** "Silent Night" or "Away in a Manger." Off-key is traditional.

Total time: about fifteen minutes -- short enough that even present-crazed kids stay in it, long enough to put Jesus unmistakably at the center of the night.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the Christmas story in simple words?
The Christmas story is how God sent His Son, Jesus, into the world as a baby. An angel told Mary she would be His mother; she and Joseph traveled to Bethlehem, where Jesus was born and laid in a manger; angels announced the news to shepherds; and wise men followed a star to worship Him. Christians celebrate Christmas because Jesus' birth was the arrival of the Savior God had promised.

### Where is the Christmas story found in the Bible?
The Christmas story is found in Luke 1-2 and Matthew 1-2. Luke tells about the angel Gabriel visiting Mary, the journey to Bethlehem, the manger, and the shepherds. Matthew tells about Joseph's dream, the star, and the wise men. The core birth account most families read aloud is Luke 2:1-20.

### How long does it take to read the Christmas story aloud?
This five-scene retelling takes about eight to ten minutes to read aloud, or around fifteen with the discussion questions. Reading Luke 2:1-20 directly from the Bible takes about three minutes. Either fits easily into a Christmas Eve evening, even with excited kids.

### What age should kids hear the Christmas story?
There's no minimum age -- toddlers enjoy the pictures and figures from age 1-2, and kids begin following the plot around age 3-4. Ages 4-10 is the sweet spot for a read-aloud retelling like this one, and pre-teens engage best when reading the Gospel text itself or leading the reading for younger siblings.

### Should I read the retelling or the actual Bible verses?
Do both: read the retelling for understanding, then one or two actual verses (like Luke 2:10-11) so kids connect the story to Scripture itself. For young children, a warm retelling first makes the biblical text meaningful instead of confusing. By ages 8-10, many kids can follow Luke 2 directly in an easy translation like the NLT or NIrV.

### Were the wise men there on the night Jesus was born?
Probably not -- Matthew 2 says the magi found the "child" in a "house," which suggests they arrived some time after the birth, possibly months later. Nativity sets put everyone together for simplicity, and that's fine. Scene 5 above is worded so you can honestly tell kids the wise men "finally arrived" after a long journey.
