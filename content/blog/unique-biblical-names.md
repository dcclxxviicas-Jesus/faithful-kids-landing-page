The Bible names over 3,000 people, and parents use about forty of them. These are 50 of the other ones -- rare biblical names that pass the two tests most "unique names" lists fail: a person could actually live with it, and there's a real story or meaning underneath it. We've skipped the trap names (Maher-shalal-hash-baz is real, and no), and linked each name to its story told for kids wherever we have one.

Want the fuller lists first? Start with our [biblical boy names](/blog/biblical-boy-names) and [biblical girl names](/blog/biblical-girl-names) -- or the [complete guide to biblical names](/blog/biblical-names).

## Unique Biblical Boy Names

- **Asa** — "healer" (1 Kings 15:9). A reforming king, three letters, impossible to misspell.
- **Boaz** — "in him is strength" (Ruth 2:1). [Ruth's redeemer](/blog/ruth-and-boaz-for-kids); nickname Bo comes free.
- **Jude** — "praise" (Jude 1:1). One page of the Bible, one great name.
- **Amos** — "burden-bearer" (Amos 1:1). The farmer God promoted to prophet.
- **Ezra** — "helper" (Ezra 7:6). The scribe who [read the Law to a weeping city](/blog/ezra-reads-the-law-for-kids); quietly climbing the charts and still rare in a classroom.
- **Zeke (Ezekiel)** — "God strengthens" (Ezekiel 1:3). The formal name is grand; the nickname does the daily work.
- **Jericho** — the city of the falling walls (Joshua 6). A place name with trumpet fanfare built in.
- **Canaan** — the promised land itself (Genesis 12:5).
- **Gideon** — "mighty warrior" (Judges 6:12). [The least of the least](/blog/gideon-the-unlikely-hero-for-kids), who won with 300.
- **Ira** — "watchful" (2 Samuel 23:38). One of David's mighty men; two letters.
- **Omar** — from the genealogies of Genesis 36:11. Ancient, short, strong.
- **Obed** — "servant" (Ruth 4:17). Ruth and Boaz's son, David's grandfather.
- **Eliam** — "God is my people's" (2 Samuel 23:34). A mighty-man name nobody has taken.
- **Micaiah** — "who is like the Lord?" (1 Kings 22:8). The prophet who wouldn't flatter a king; Micah's rarer big brother.
- **Zephaniah** — "the Lord has hidden" (Zephaniah 3:17). His book says God sings over his people. Zeph is a great nickname.
- **Obadiah** — "servant of the Lord" (Obadiah 1:1). The shortest Old Testament book; Obie for short.
- **Malachi** — "my messenger" (Malachi 1:1). The Old Testament's final word.
- **Tobias** — "the Lord is good" (from Tobiah, Nehemiah 2:10). Toby is the payoff.
- **Phineas (Phinehas)** — a priest's name from Exodus 6:25, rescued from cartoon-villain status by its swing.
- **Rufus** — "red-haired" (Mark 15:21). Son of the man who carried Jesus' cross -- known by name to the early church.
- **Linus** — greeted in 2 Timothy 4:21. Yes, it's biblical.
- **Justus** — "upright" (Acts 1:23). A virtue name hiding in Acts.
- **Cyrus** — the Persian emperor God used to [send the exiles home](/blog/cyrus-sets-them-free-for-kids) (Ezra 1:1). Sci-fi sound, ancient pedigree.
- **Abner** — "father of light" (1 Samuel 14:50). Due back any decade now.
- **Lemuel** — "devoted to God" (Proverbs 31:1). The king whose mother's advice became Proverbs 31; Lem is pure vintage.

## Unique Biblical Girl Names

- **Keziah** — "cassia, sweet spice" (Job 42:14). One of Job's daughters, born after the storm.
- **Jemimah** — "dove" (Job 42:14). Her sister -- the Bible says no women in the land were as beautiful.
- **Keren** — "ray of light" (short for Keren-happuch, Job 42:14). The third sister; all three received an inheritance, unheard of in that world.
- **Hadassah** — "myrtle tree" (Esther 2:7). [Queen Esther's](/blog/esther-becomes-queen-for-kids) Hebrew name; Haddie for everyday.
- **Tirzah** — "she is my delight" (Song of Solomon 6:4). A city so lovely it became a compliment.
- **Atarah** — "crown" (1 Chronicles 2:26). Waiting in the genealogies for someone to find her.
- **Jerusha** — "inheritance" (2 Kings 15:33). A queen mother of Judah.
- **Zipporah** — "bird" (Exodus 2:21). Moses' quick-acting wife; Zippy is objectively delightful.
- **Noa** — one of the five sisters who argued their inheritance case before Moses and won (Numbers 27:1-7). Israel's top girl name for years; still rare in the U.S.
- **Adah** — "ornament" (Genesis 4:19). Among the first women named in Scripture; gentler than the trending Ada.
- **Selah** — the Psalms' mysterious pause (Psalm 46:7). A name that means *stop and breathe*.
- **Shiloh** — "peace" (Joshua 18:1). Where the tabernacle rested for 300 years.
- **Havilah** — the land of gold (Genesis 2:11). Sounds invented; is older than almost every name in use.
- **Carmel** — "garden" (1 Kings 18:19). The mountain of Elijah's showdown.
- **Abilene** — a region from Luke 3:1 with a country-song lilt.
- **Damaris** — the Athenian woman who believed Paul [on Mars Hill](/blog/paul-in-athens-for-kids) (Acts 17:34).
- **Phoebe** — "radiant" (Romans 16:1). Trusted to carry the letter to the Romans; familiar sound, rare on birth certificates.
- **Persis** — "beloved" (Romans 16:12). A one-verse name with a whole commendation in it.
- **Eunice** — "good victory" (2 Timothy 1:5). Timothy's mother; ripe for the vintage revival treatment.
- **Lois** — Timothy's grandmother (2 Timothy 1:5). Three generations of faith in four letters.
- **Rhoda** — "rose" (Acts 12:13). The girl too overjoyed to open the door.
- **Tamar** — "palm tree" (Genesis 38:6). Strong, rooted, and almost unused.
- **Milcah** — "queen" (Genesis 11:29). Abraham's sister-in-law.
- **Vashti** — the queen who said no (Esther 1:11). A name with a backbone and a conversation attached.
- **Zemirah** — "song" (1 Chronicles 7:8). Listed for a man, used today for girls -- the meaning is the draw.

## The Trap Names (Real, but Please Don't)

Proof we read the whole list so you don't have to: **Maher-shalal-hash-baz** (Isaiah's son, the longest name in the Bible), **Mephibosheth** ([David was kind to him](/blog/david-and-mephibosheth-for-kids); the DMV will not be), **Nimrod** (a mighty hunter whose name modern slang ruined), **Jezebel** and **Delilah** -- lovely sounds whose stories arrive before the child does -- and **Dodo** (a real grandfather in Judges 10:1). Rare should never mean cruel.

## How Rare Is Too Rare?

A good uniqueness test: your child will say this name aloud roughly 40,000 times. **Spell test** -- if you must spell it on every phone call (Zipporah), make sure you love it that much. **Playground test** -- yell it across a park; if it embarrasses *you*, it's out. **Nickname escape hatch** -- the rarer the formal name, the more it needs an easy short form: Hadassah→Haddie, Zephaniah→Zeph, Jemimah→Jem. Every name above passes at least two of the three.

And the real advantage of a rare biblical name over a rare invented one: there's a person underneath it. When your Keziah asks who she's named for, the answer is a real girl, born after her father lost everything and God restored it. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the most unique biblical name that's still usable?**
For boys: Asa, Ira, Obed, and Zephaniah are genuinely rare and wear well. For girls: Keziah, Tirzah, Atarah, and Noa. All are short enough -- or nickname-friendly enough -- for daily life.

**Are there unique biblical names that sound modern?**
Several read as fully contemporary: Jude, Cyrus, Jericho, Noa, Selah, Shiloh, and Eden. Most people won't guess they're ancient.

**What unique biblical names work for either a boy or a girl?**
Shiloh, Eden, Jordan, Zion, and Selah are all used for both today. Noa is a woman in Numbers 27 but sounds like Noah -- a built-in conversation starter.
