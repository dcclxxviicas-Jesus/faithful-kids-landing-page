If you've built a Christian app or website for kids, getting it in front of parents is harder than building it. The app stores bury small faith apps, and most "submit your app" lists online are years out of date, full of sites that closed, went paid or quietly stopped reviewing anything.

So every site below was checked on its own pages on November 17, 2026: whether it still accepts submissions, where the form is, and whether listing is free. We left out every site that charges to be listed or asks for a link back in exchange. At the end you'll find the ones that changed in 2026, so you don't waste an afternoon on them.

## The Short Version

- **Christian app directories:** faith.tools, ChristianPicks, CatholicApps.com, For All Things Bible, and the [Christian Apps for Kids directory](/christian-apps) we run.
- **Homeschool reviewers:** Homeschool Fox, The Curriculum Choice, Cathy Duffy Reviews (for curricula, not standalone apps).
- **Kids' app review sites:** Appysmarts, LearningWorks for Kids, The Best Kids Apps.
- **Church and ministry lists:** the Diocese of London resource database, the UCC children's ministries library, Rotation.org.
- **General tech directories:** AlternativeTo, Product Hunt, SaaSHub.
- **Skip:** Christian App Reviews ($29 to list, $99 to review), Movieguide ($500), Educational App Store (submission fee), and Freedom Homeschooling if your app uses AI-generated media.

## Before You Submit Anywhere

A little preparation decides whether a reviewer bothers to open your app.

- **Have a free reviewer account or promo code ready.** Several sites below (Appysmarts, iGameMom, Cathy Duffy) won't review a paid app they can't use.
- **Publish a statement of faith.** Christian directories increasingly ask what tradition you come from. A short page you can link answers it in one click. ([Ours](/beliefs) is deliberately brief.)
- **Disclose AI honestly.** faith.tools applies extra criteria to AI-made apps, and Freedom Homeschooling no longer accepts resources with AI-generated images, video or audio. If yours uses AI, say so up front.
- **Write one plain description.** Most forms want 50 to 100 words. Lead with who it's for and what a child actually does in it, not adjectives.
- **Never pay for a listing that promises a link.** A paid link can hurt your search rankings more than it helps, and the best directories here say outright that placement can't be bought.

## Christian App Directories

**1. faith.tools.** The best-known curated directory of Christian apps, with a "For Kids" section that lists the big names (Minno, Bible App for Kids, TruPlay). Recommending an app has been **free again since April 2, 2026**, when paid submissions ended. It checks every app against published criteria, including properly licensed Bible translations and separate standards for AI apps, and it says plainly that there's no timeline and no reply. Submit through the "Recommend an app" link at [faith.tools](https://faith.tools/for-kids).

**2. ChristianPicks.** A directory of Christian software, products and Christian-owned businesses, with 500+ listings and homeschool curricula included. The suggest form is reviewed by a person and asks a faith-alignment question and why the product belongs. Paid ad slots exist but aren't needed for a listing. [christianpicks.com/suggest](https://christianpicks.com/suggest)

**3. CatholicApps.com.** A Catholic app review directory posting new reviews daily, with Bible and Kids & Family categories. The form asks for the store links, price, platforms and an optional promo code, and the site says it doesn't sell ratings or rankings. Best fit if Catholic families use your app. [catholicapps.com/submit-your-app](https://catholicapps.com/submit-your-app/)

**4. For All Things Bible.** A showcase of 800+ Bible websites and apps run with Bibles.com and the .BIBLE Registry. Two rules matter: the operator and its faith tradition must be clearly stated, and the showcase is "not for marketing products," so suggest your free resource rather than a paid subscription. Suggestions go through the [Get.Bible contact page](https://get.bible/contact), and they don't reply to everyone.

**5. Christian Apps for Kids (ours).** We run a [directory of Christian apps for kids and families](/christian-apps) that checks each app's price, ages, ads and last update on its store page, with a page per app. Every submission is reviewed by our team, and listings are approved at our discretion. [Submit an app or website](/christian-apps/submit).

**6. Scripture Engagement.** The Forum of Bible Agencies' resource database has a Children topic listing tools like Bible App for Kids videos and Superbook. There's no form, but the contact page invites suggestions for additional content. [scripture-engagement.org/contact](https://scripture-engagement.org/contact/)

**7. awesome-bible.** A small curated list of Bible websites and apps on GitHub, last updated in September 2026. Tiny audience, but a pull request takes five minutes, and closed-source apps qualify. [github.com/awesome-bible](https://github.com/awesome-bible/awesome-bible)

**8. Glorifind.** A Christian family-safe search engine that gives reviewed Christian-owned sites a "Christian Owned" label in its results. It's a label, not a listing page, so treat it as a two-minute bonus. [glorifind.com](https://glorifind.com/pages/christian-owned-label)

Catholic apps are also compared in their own hub, [the best Catholic apps](/best-catholic-apps), which takes submissions through the same form.

## Homeschool Directories and Reviewers

If your app teaches anything, homeschool families are often the most motivated buyers in the faith niche.

**9. Homeschool Fox.** A parent-reviewed directory of homeschool curricula with filters by subject (including Bible), format and worldview (Christian, Catholic). It states listing is free and approval "can't be bought," and each submission is checked by hand. [homeschoolfox.com/curriculum/submit](https://homeschoolfox.com/curriculum/submit)

**10. The Curriculum Choice.** A Christian homeschool review site run by veteran homeschool moms, with a Technology > Apps category and new posts weekly. The standard review has no stated fee (paid ad and giveaway packages are optional), they only write about what they actually use, and they ask you to expect at least six months. [Review inquiries](https://thecurriculumchoice.com/review-inquiries/)

**11. Cathy Duffy Reviews.** The best-known homeschool curriculum reviewer. Publishers never pay to be reviewed, but she generally doesn't review apps unless they're part of a program, so pitch a curriculum, not a standalone app. [Contact page](https://cathyduffyreviews.com/contact-us)

**12. Modulo.** A large homeschool resource catalogue with its own page per product, covering curricula, apps and online programs. The add-a-resource form mentions no fee. [joinmodulo.com/pages/add-a-resource](https://joinmodulo.com/pages/add-a-resource)

**13. Ontario Federation of Teaching Parents.** Ontario's homeschool association keeps a free resource directory and edits descriptions to remove hype, so write it plain. Volunteer-run, so expect a wait. [ontariohomeschool.org/resources](https://ontariohomeschool.org/resources/)

**14. Learning Online Blog.** "The Ultimate Directory of Homeschool Resources" has a form for paid resources sold on your own site, with no submission fee and no affiliate links allowed. [Submission form](https://www.learningonlineblog.com/homeschool-resources-submission-form/)

**15. Homeschool Hub Utah.** A nonprofit directory that adds resources for free, with optional paid featured listings. Regional, but online resources fit. [homeschoolhubutah.org](https://homeschoolhubutah.org/resource-directory.html)

**20. Protect Young Eyes.** A parent-facing app review library used by families, schools and churches. There's no submission form, so use the contact page, and lead with what makes your app safe. [protectyoungeyes.com/contact](https://www.protectyoungeyes.com/contact/)

## Church and Children's Ministry Lists

These rarely bring huge traffic, but a listing on a diocese or denomination site puts you in front of exactly the people who choose resources for a whole church.

**21. Diocese of London (Church of England) Children and Youth Resources.** A curated, filterable database for London parishes, with a "submit one here" form. [Resource page](https://www.london.anglican.org/church-and-parish-support/children-and-youth-ministry/resources/)

**22. United Church of Christ Children's Ministries Resources.** The UCC's national children's ministry library has a "Suggest a Resource" form on the page. A mainline audience, so free printables tend to fit better than doctrine-heavy material. [ucc.org](https://www.ucc.org/childrens-ministries-resources/)

**23. MLC-WELS Bible Lesson Media Resources.** A Lutheran hub of media for teaching Bible lessons, with a "Submit a Media Resource" form. Map your content to specific Bible stories. [Resource hub](https://sites.google.com/mlc-wels.edu/biblelessonmediaresources/home)

**24. Rotation.org.** A Sunday school lesson site with a software and apps section focused on tools kids use to learn Bible stories. The moderator invites app suggestions in its forum thread (free sign-in), or use the contact page. [rotation.org](https://www.rotation.org/topic/apps-and-new-or-coming-software-for-sunday-school-kids)

**25. Catholic-Link.** A Catholic media ministry publishing seasonal roundups, including Advent apps and videos. Its contact form takes suggestions, so pitch ahead of the season. [catholic-link.org/contact](https://catholic-link.org/contact/)

## General Tech and Education Directories

**26. AlternativeTo.** A crowd-sourced "alternatives to X" directory, useful because parents search "apps like Minno" or "Superbook alternatives." Listing is free; its FAQ says app links are nofollow unless editors decide otherwise, and a followed link can't be bought. [alternativeto.net](https://alternativeto.net/)

**27. Product Hunt.** A free tech launch site where several kids' Bible apps launched in 2025 and 2026. The audience is tech founders, not parents, and a launch works best when the maker is active in the comments all day. [producthunt.com](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/new)

**28. SaaSHub.** A software alternatives directory; submission is free once your product is verified. [saashub.com/submit](https://www.saashub.com/submit)

**29. Curlie.** The volunteer-edited successor to the old Open Directory. Its Kids & Teens > Christianity category accepts sites aimed at children but not shopping sites, so submit a free resource for kids, and expect months. [Curlie Christianity for kids](https://curlie.org/en/Kids_and_Teens/People_and_Society/Religion_and_Spirituality/Christianity)

**30. EdTech Impact and the ISTE EdTech Index.** Free profiles in school-buyer directories. Low fit for a faith app, since the audience is district procurement, but both are free to register. [edtechimpact.com](https://edtechimpact.com/providers/) · [ltd.iste.org](https://ltd.iste.org/)

## Write for Them Instead

Some of the strongest Christian family sites don't take listings at all; they take articles, with a short author bio that can mention your app. That's more work, but an article on a respected site does more than a directory row.

- **The Old Schoolhouse Magazine** takes unpaid articles from homeschool experts, with a bio under the piece. [Writer guidelines](https://www.theoldschoolhouse.com/writers-guidelines/how-to-submit-a-query/)
- **Christian Parenting** (Denison Ministries) has a contributor route on its contact page.
- **CatholicMom.com** reviews Catholic apps through its volunteer writers; its contact page links to the writers' application.

## What Changed in 2026

Several sites that older "submit your app" lists still recommend have changed:

- **Christian App Reviews went paid:** $29 to list an app and $99 for a hands-on review.
- **Movieguide** charges a $500 review fee and covers films only. The **Dove Foundation** review form includes a payment step and covers movies and TV, not apps.
- **Educational App Store** charges a one-time submission fee for review and certification, and **Mom's Choice Awards** and **KIDS FIRST!** both charge to evaluate.
- **Common Sense Education** isn't completing new reviews, and its review requests are paused.
- **Freedom Homeschooling** no longer accepts resources with AI-generated images, video or audio, and excludes coloring sheets and puzzles.
- **faith.tools** went the other way: paid submissions ended on April 2, 2026, and recommendations are free again.
- **Risen Motherhood** has ended its ministry, and **ChurchLeaders'** "suggest a resource" page now returns an error.
- **Children's Technology Review** has published nothing since 2017, and **TheHomeSchoolMom** no longer reviews resources.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where can I submit a Christian app for free?

faith.tools (free since April 2026), ChristianPicks, CatholicApps.com, Homeschool Fox, Appysmarts, AlternativeTo and our own [Christian Apps for Kids directory](/christian-apps/submit) all take free submissions as of November 2026.

### Is it worth paying to get a Christian app reviewed?

Rarely. The paid options we found (Christian App Reviews at $29 to $99, Movieguide at $500) have small audiences next to the free directories, and paying for a listing that includes a link can hurt your search rankings. Spend the time on a good free trial and an honest description instead.

### How long do app reviews take?

Directories with a simple form usually list within days to weeks. Editorial reviewers take much longer: The Curriculum Choice asks you to expect six months, and iGameMom quotes 6 to 24 months. faith.tools gives no timeline at all.

### What do Christian directories look for?

A clear statement of who made the app and what they believe, a Bible translation that is properly licensed and credited, a working way to try the app, and honesty about AI. Several also exclude anything that reads like an ad, so describe what a child does in the app, not why it's the best.
