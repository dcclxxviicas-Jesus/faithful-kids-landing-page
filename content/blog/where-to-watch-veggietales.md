The short answer: the fastest free way to watch VeggieTales is the **official VeggieTales YouTube channel**, which the show's own site points people to and which carries full videos at no cost. For a bigger, ad-free library, the two subscription services built around Christian kids' content — **Minno** and **Yippee TV** — both carry VeggieTales, and episodes have also appeared on **Prime Video** and **Netflix**. Which you want depends on whether you're after one episode tonight or a permanent replacement for YouTube.

Streaming rights for VeggieTales have moved around more than most children's properties, so treat any list — including this one — as a starting point and check the service before you subscribe.

## The free option: the official YouTube channel

VeggieTales' own website sends visitors to YouTube. Its watch page tells you to "Watch all VeggieTales videos now" and to "Subscribe to VeggieTales for brand new videos," linking straight to the official channel.

That makes YouTube the honest answer to *where to watch VeggieTales for free*. You get real episodes and Silly Songs at no cost, uploaded by the rights holder rather than by a stranger re-uploading a DVD rip.

The catch is the thing that sends most parents looking in the first place. It is still YouTube: there are ads, there are recommendations down the side, and when an episode ends the algorithm decides what plays next. For a lot of families that is exactly the problem they were trying to solve.

**Use it if:** you want one Bob and Larry episode tonight and you'll be in the room.

## Minno

Minno is the deepest Christian kids' library there is, and it grew directly out of VeggieTales history — it began as JellyTelly, co-founded by VeggieTales creator Phil Vischer, before relaunching under the Minno name.

Alongside classic VeggieTales episodes it carries *What's in the Bible?*, Vischer's follow-up series and still one of the better children's theology resources anyone has made, plus originals and movies. Nothing is ad-supported and nothing autoplays into the wider internet.

At $10.99/month it is the most expensive option here, which is the trade for the largest catalog.

**Use it if:** you want a genuine Netflix replacement for Christian kids' viewing and the breadth is worth the price. We wrote a fuller [Minno review](/blog/minno-review) if you want the detail.

## Yippee TV

Yippee is the other subscription built for this exact job, at $7.99/month or $49/year. It carries VeggieTales too, along with *Superbook*, *Owlegories* and a rotating catalog of faith-based shows, all ad-free.

It is cheaper than Minno and the library is smaller. For a lot of families that is the right trade.

**Use it if:** you want ad-free VeggieTales without paying Minno prices. Our [Yippee TV comparison](/blog/yippee-tv-vs-faithful-kids) goes deeper.

## Prime Video and Netflix

Both mainstream services have carried VeggieTales content. Netflix has run *VeggieTales in the House*, the newer series made for the platform, which is a different animal to the 1990s DVDs many parents remember — same characters, different tone.

Prime Video has offered VeggieTales titles both included with a Prime membership and as individual rentals or purchases, depending on the title.

If you already pay for either service, search there first before adding a subscription. Just be aware that "VeggieTales is on Netflix" and "the VeggieTales I grew up with is on Netflix" are not always the same sentence.

## Which one should you actually pick?

**If you want it free and right now:** the official YouTube channel. It is legitimate, it is free, and it takes ten seconds.

**If YouTube itself is the problem:** Yippee at $7.99 or Minno at $10.99. Both remove the ads, the recommendations and the rabbit hole. That is what you are paying for — not just the episodes, but the absence of everything around them.

**If you already have Prime or Netflix:** check those first. There is no sense adding a fourth subscription for one show.

## A note on what VeggieTales does and doesn't do

VeggieTales is very good at what it is: funny, warm, character-driven stories with a values lesson attached. Generations of Christian kids can still sing the Silly Songs.

What it is not is a structured way through the Bible. Episodes are standalone, the biblical stories are retold loosely for comedy, and there is no way to know what your child actually took in. That is not a criticism — it was never trying to be a curriculum.

It does mean that a lot of families end up wanting two things: something fun, and something that teaches. If you're in that group, it's worth knowing the second half exists.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is VeggieTales free to watch?
Yes, on the official VeggieTales YouTube channel, which the show's own website directs viewers to. It is ad-supported, like all of YouTube. The ad-free options are paid subscriptions such as Minno or Yippee TV.

### Is VeggieTales on Netflix?
Netflix has carried *VeggieTales in the House*, a newer series made for the platform. Availability changes, so check Netflix directly. The classic 1990s episodes are more reliably found on YouTube, Minno or Yippee.

### What is the cheapest way to watch VeggieTales ad-free?
Yippee TV at $7.99/month or $49/year is the cheapest ad-free subscription that carries VeggieTales, versus Minno at $10.99/month.

### Are the old VeggieTales and the new ones the same?
No. The classic Big Idea episodes from the 1990s and 2000s, *VeggieTales in the House* (Netflix), and *The VeggieTales Show* are distinct productions with different animation and tone. If you are chasing nostalgia specifically, look for the classic episodes rather than assuming any VeggieTales listing is the one you remember.
