# Who Was Elisha in the Bible? Story for Kids

Elisha is the Bible's great second act. He spent years as the apprentice of the famous prophet [Elijah](/blog/who-was-elijah-for-kids); the Bible sums up the job as "he used to pour water on the hands of Elijah" (2 Kings 3:11), which means he was the servant; and then carried the ministry further than his master, with miracles that read like previews of Jesus: multiplying bread, cleansing a leper, raising a dead boy.

His story runs through 2 Kings 2-13. If Elijah is fire on the mountain, Elisha is God's power in kitchens, rivers, and borrowed rooms.

## The Double Portion (2 Kings 2)

Elisha was plowing a field with twelve yoke of oxen when Elijah walked past and threw his cloak over him, the prophet's way of saying "you're next." Elisha burned his plowing equipment, cooked the oxen as a farewell feast, and followed (1 Kings 19:19-21). No backup plan.

Years later, when Elijah was about to be taken to heaven, he asked his apprentice what he wanted. Elisha's answer was bold: "Let me inherit a double portion of your spirit" (2 Kings 2:9), the legal share of a firstborn son. He wasn't asking to be twice as great; he was asking to be the true heir.

When the chariot of fire took Elijah up in a whirlwind, Elisha picked up the fallen cloak, walked back to the Jordan, struck the water, and it parted for him exactly as it had for his master. The watching prophets said it out loud: "The spirit of Elijah is resting on Elisha" (2 Kings 2:15). (We tell this handoff in [Elisha's Double Portion](/blog/elisha-double-portion-for-kids).)

**Key lesson for kids:** Elisha's greatness started with years of carrying someone else's water. Faithfulness as a helper is the training ground for everything bigger, nobody skips that step, not even prophets.

## Miracles in Ordinary Houses (2 Kings 4)

What makes Elisha different from every prophet before him is where his miracles happen: in poor people's kitchens and guest rooms.

A widow was about to lose her two sons to debt slavery; all she owned was one small jar of olive oil. Elisha told her to borrow every empty jar in the neighborhood and start pouring, and the oil kept flowing until the last borrowed jar was full. She paid the debt and lived on the rest.

A wealthy woman in Shunem built a little rooftop room so the prophet would have a place to stay. God gave her the son she'd never had, and when that boy died suddenly years later, Elisha stretched himself out over the child and God raised him back to life (2 Kings 4:32-37).

He also purified a poisoned pot of stew and fed a hundred men with twenty barley loaves, with bread left over, eight centuries before Jesus did something very similar with five.

**Key lesson for kids:** God cares about debts, dinners, and guest rooms, not just battles and kings. No problem is too ordinary to pray about.

## Naaman: the General Who Had to Get Small (2 Kings 5)

The most famous Elisha story stars a foreigner. Naaman was the commander of Aram's army, powerful, decorated, and sick with leprosy. A little Israelite servant girl, captured in a raid, told her mistress about the prophet in Samaria. That child's sentence set the whole story moving.

Naaman arrived at Elisha's door with chariots, silver, and gold. Elisha didn't even come out; he sent a message: go wash seven times in the Jordan. Naaman was furious. He expected a dramatic ceremony, and he'd seen better rivers back home. His servants talked him down with wonderful logic: if the prophet had asked something hard, wouldn't you have done it?

Naaman waded into the muddy Jordan seven times "and his flesh was restored and became clean like that of a young boy" (2 Kings 5:14). He went back and confessed: "Now I know that there is no God in all the world except in Israel." (Full story: [Naaman the Leper](/blog/naaman-the-leper-for-kids).)

**Key lesson for kids:** Pride almost cost Naaman his healing. God's instructions don't have to be impressive to be right; sometimes obedience just looks like doing the simple thing you were told.

## Horses of Fire, and Mercy (2 Kings 6)

When the king of Aram sent an army to capture Elisha, his young servant woke to find the city surrounded and panicked. Elisha's answer is one of the great sentences of the Bible: "Don't be afraid. Those who are with us are more than those who are with them" (2 Kings 6:16). He prayed, and the servant's eyes were opened: the hills were full of horses and chariots of fire around them.

Then comes the twist kids never expect: with the enemy army helpless before him, Elisha didn't destroy them. He had the king of Israel feed them a great feast and send them home. "So the bands from Aram stopped raiding Israel's territory" (6:23). Mercy ended the war.

**Key lesson for kids:** Reality is bigger than what your eyes can see, and sometimes the strongest move is kindness to an enemy. Elisha did both in one chapter.

## Why Elisha Matters

- **He's the Bible's best case study in mentoring.** Elijah trained him; he trained the companies of prophets; the work outlived them both.
- **His miracles preview Jesus.** Multiplying bread, cleansing a leper, raising the dead, grace to foreigners, the pattern points forward eight hundred years.
- **He served through four kings and two nations' wars**, steady where kings wavered; quiz the era with our [2 Kings Bible trivia](/blog/2-kings-bible-trivia), and find Samaria, Shunem and the Jordan on the free [map of ancient Israel](/printables/bible-maps/ancient-israel).
- **A child set up his most famous miracle.** The servant girl who pointed Naaman to the prophet is one of Scripture's quiet heroes, proof that kids' words can change powerful people's lives.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What was the "double portion" Elisha asked for?

In Israelite inheritance law, the firstborn son received a double share of the estate (Deuteronomy 21:17). When Elisha asked for "a double portion of your spirit" (2 Kings 2:9), he was asking to be recognized as Elijah's true spiritual heir and successor, not to be twice the prophet. Interestingly, Scripture does record about twice as many miracles through Elisha, which readers have noticed for centuries.

### How was Elisha different from Elijah?

Elijah was a loner of the wilderness, dramatic public showdowns, years in hiding, fire from heaven. Elisha lived among people: he kept a room in Shunem, worked with the companies of prophets, and most of his miracles met household needs like debt, food, and sick children. Same God, very different personalities, which is itself a lesson: God uses both kinds.

### Why did Elisha tell Naaman to wash in the Jordan seven times?

The instruction carried no magic; the Jordan is a modest, muddy river, as Naaman loudly pointed out. That was the point: the healing would plainly be God's doing, not the river's or a ceremony's, and obeying the simple command required Naaman to drop his pride. Jesus later referenced this story (Luke 4:27) to show God's grace reaching beyond Israel: "there were many in Israel with leprosy... yet not one of them was cleansed, only Naaman the Syrian."

### Did Elisha die like a normal person?

Yes, unlike Elijah, Elisha fell sick and died an ordinary death (2 Kings 13:14, 20). But even his story ends with one last surprise: a dead man hastily thrown into Elisha's tomb came back to life on touching the prophet's bones (2 Kings 13:21); Scripture's final note that the power at work in Elisha's life had never been his own.
