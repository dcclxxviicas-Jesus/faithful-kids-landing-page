# Who Was Joshua in the Bible? Story for Kids

Joshua is the Bible's great example of the second man — the apprentice who spent forty years serving Moses and then got the job Moses himself never finished: leading Israel into the Promised Land. His book opens with God's words to a man facing impossibly big shoes, repeated three times in one chapter: "Be strong and courageous."

His story stretches from Exodus 17 all the way through the book that bears his name.

## The Apprentice (Exodus 17, 24, 33)

Joshua shows up early. When Amalek attacked Israel just after the Red Sea, Moses picked young Joshua to lead the fighting while Moses held up his hands on the hill (Exodus 17:9-13). From then on he was Moses' aide: he went partway up Sinai when Moses received the law, and when Moses met with God at the tent of meeting, the text adds a detail kids love — Moses would return to camp, "but his young aide Joshua son of Nun did not leave the tent" (Exodus 33:11).

He lingered where God was. Forty years of that is where leaders come from.

**Key lesson for kids:** Joshua's training wasn't sword practice — it was carrying things for Moses and staying close to God's presence. Serve faithfully in the small role, and don't rush out of the tent.

## The Faithful Spy (Numbers 13-14)

When Israel reached the edge of Canaan, Moses sent twelve spies — one per tribe — to scout the land. All twelve saw the same grapes, the same cities, the same giants. Ten came back saying: we can't. The people are stronger; "we seemed like grasshoppers in our own eyes" (Numbers 13:33).

Only two — Joshua and Caleb — said: the land is good, and if the Lord is pleased with us, He will give it to us. "Do not be afraid of the people of the land... the Lord is with us" (Numbers 14:9).

The crowd talked about stoning them. God's verdict fell instead: that whole unbelieving generation would wander the wilderness forty years, and of all the adults, only Joshua and Caleb would live to enter the land. (We tell it in [The Twelve Spies](/blog/the-twelve-spies-for-kids).)

**Key lesson for kids:** Twelve people saw identical facts and split ten-to-two on what they meant. Faith isn't ignoring the giants — Joshua saw them fine. Faith is doing the math with God included.

## "Be Strong and Courageous" (Joshua 1)

After Moses died, God's commissioning of Joshua is one of the most encouraging chapters in the Bible — because God clearly knows the new leader is scared. Three times in nine verses: be strong and courageous. And with it, the secret of the whole assignment: "Keep this Book of the Law always on your lips; meditate on it day and night... Then you will be prosperous and successful" (Joshua 1:8).

Then Israel crossed the Jordan — at flood stage — with the water piling up in a heap the moment the priests' feet touched it, a Red Sea moment for the new generation (Joshua 3).

**Key lesson for kids:** God doesn't say "be strong" to people who already feel strong. Courage, in the Bible, is doing the assignment scared — with God's word in your mouth and His presence beside you: "the Lord your God will be with you wherever you go" (Joshua 1:9).

## Jericho, Ai, and the Long Campaign (Joshua 2-12)

The conquest began at [Jericho](/blog/the-walls-of-jericho-for-kids), the fortress guarding the way in — taken not by siege engines but by six days of silent marching, a seventh day of seven circuits, trumpets, a shout, and walls falling flat. Inside, one family was spared: [Rahab's](/blog/who-was-rahab-for-kids), because of a scarlet cord and a promise.

Right after the triumph came the humbling: tiny Ai defeated Israel because one man, Achan, had secretly stolen from what was devoted to God. Sin dealt with, the campaign resumed — the clever Gibeonite treaty, the long day of battle when "the sun stood still" (Joshua 10:13), the northern coalition under Hazor broken at the waters of Merom. In all of it, the book's refrain is steady: the Lord fought for Israel. (Trace the whole campaign on our free [map of Canaan](/printables/bible-maps/the-promised-land).)

**Key lesson for kids:** Victory at Jericho didn't make Israel victory-proof at Ai. Yesterday's win doesn't cover today's hidden sin — walking with God is a daily thing, for armies and for kids.

## "Choose This Day" (Joshua 13-24)

The second half of the book is quieter: dividing the land among the [twelve tribes](/printables/bible-maps/twelve-tribes-of-israel) by lot at Shiloh, with old Caleb — eighty-five and still fierce — claiming the hill country God had promised him forty-five years earlier.

Joshua's goodbye, at Shechem, gives the Bible one of its most famous sentences. He reviewed everything God had done, then put the choice to the nation: "Choose for yourselves this day whom you will serve... But as for me and my household, we will serve the Lord" (Joshua 24:15).

He died at a hundred and ten, and Scripture gives him a one-line legacy any leader would take: "Israel served the Lord throughout the lifetime of Joshua" (Joshua 24:31).

**Key lesson for kids:** Joshua's most famous verse is a family decision, made out loud. Faith isn't inherited automatically or absorbed from a crowd — every house, and finally every kid, chooses.

## Why Joshua Matters

- **He's the Bible's model successor.** Apprentice for forty years, faithful when it cost him, and strong enough to follow a legend without imitating him.
- **His book completes the promise.** Land promised to Abraham in Genesis 12 is finally occupied — "Not one of all the Lord's good promises to Israel failed; every one was fulfilled" (Joshua 21:45).
- **His name points forward.** Joshua — Yehoshua, "the Lord saves" — is the same name as Jesus in Hebrew. The first Joshua led God's people into the promised land; Christians see the second leading them into a better one.
- **He pairs courage with the Book.** Joshua 1:8 ties his success to meditating on Scripture day and night — the warrior's secret weapon was a scroll. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Was Joshua related to Moses?

No — Joshua son of Nun was from the tribe of Ephraim (Numbers 13:8), while Moses was a Levite. Their bond was apprenticeship, not family: Joshua served as Moses' aide from his youth (Numbers 11:28), and before Moses died, God had him publicly commission Joshua so all Israel would know the leadership had passed intact (Deuteronomy 31:7-8, 14).

### Did the sun really stand still for Joshua?

Joshua 10:12-14 describes it that way — in the battle to defend Gibeon, Joshua prayed, "Sun, stand still over Gibeon," and "the sun stopped in the middle of the sky and delayed going down about a full day." The text itself marks the day as unrepeatable: "There has never been a day like it before or since." Christians discuss exactly what happened physically; what the passage insists on is the point in verse 14 — "surely the Lord was fighting for Israel," and He listened to a human being's audacious prayer.

### Why is Joshua's name the same as Jesus' name?

Both come from the Hebrew Yehoshua/Yeshua, meaning "the Lord saves." In the Greek of the New Testament, the name Joshua is literally written "Iesous" — Jesus (see Hebrews 4:8, where some translations say "Joshua" and the Greek says "Jesus"). The echo is meaningful: Joshua brought God's people into the promised rest of Canaan, and the New Testament presents Jesus as the one who brings God's people into the true rest Joshua's conquest pointed toward (Hebrews 4:8-9).

### How long did the conquest of Canaan take?

Roughly seven years, by the Bible's own math: Caleb was forty when he spied the land, Israel wandered thirty-eight more years, and he was eighty-five when the major campaigns ended (Joshua 14:7-10). And the book is honest that the work wasn't total — plenty of territory and strongholds remained (Joshua 13:1), which sets up the whole era of the judges that follows, from [Deborah](/blog/who-was-deborah-for-kids) to [Samson](/blog/who-was-samson-for-kids).
