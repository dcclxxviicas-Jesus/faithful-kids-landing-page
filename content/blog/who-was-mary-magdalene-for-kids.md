# Who Was Mary Magdalene in the Bible? For Kids

Mary Magdalene holds an honor no one else in history has: she was the first person to see the risen Jesus, and the first person ever sent to tell the news. Early Christians called her "the apostle to the apostles" — the messenger to the messengers.

Her story is scattered through all four Gospels, and it's worth gathering the pieces, because kids usually know her name without knowing why she matters so much.

## Healed and All-In (Luke 8:1-3)

Mary came from Magdala, a fishing town on the shore of the Sea of Galilee — that's what "Magdalene" means, Mary of Magdala. Luke introduces her with one line that tells us everything about her past and her devotion: Jesus had driven seven demons out of her.

We aren't told what her suffering looked like. We're told what came after: she joined the group of women who traveled with Jesus and the Twelve "and were helping to support them out of their own means" (Luke 8:3). Mary didn't just feel grateful — she funded and served the mission with her own money and her own hands, town after town.

**Key lesson for kids:** Gratitude turns into action. Jesus set Mary free, and her whole life became a thank-you. When God does something for you, the natural next question is: what can I give back?

## Faithful at the Cross (Matthew 27:55-61; John 19:25)

Here is the fact about Mary Magdalene that deserves to be famous. On the worst day — when Jesus was arrested, condemned, and crucified — most of the disciples scattered. Peter denied even knowing him.

Mary stayed.

All four Gospels place her at the crucifixion, standing near the cross with Jesus' mother and a few other women (John 19:25). And when Joseph of Arimathea laid Jesus' body in the tomb, Mary Magdalene was still there, "sitting opposite the tomb" (Matthew 27:61), watching where he was laid so she could come back after the Sabbath to anoint the body properly.

**Key lesson for kids:** Love shows up on the hard days, not just the happy ones. Mary couldn't stop what was happening — so she did what love can always do: she stayed, and she watched, and she planned to serve.

## First at the Empty Tomb (John 20:1-10)

Early on the first day of the week, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene went to the tomb — and found the stone rolled away. She ran to Peter and John with the only explanation that made sense to her: "They have taken the Lord out of the tomb, and we don't know where they have put him!" (John 20:2).

Peter and John raced to the tomb, found the grave clothes lying empty, and went home bewildered. ([The Empty Tomb](/blog/the-empty-tomb-for-kids) tells that morning for younger kids.)

Mary stayed behind, alone, crying outside the tomb.

**Key lesson for kids:** Mary got the first look at the greatest event in history because she showed up earliest, in the dark, to do a sad and loving chore. God often meets people in the middle of their faithfulness, not after their confusion clears.

## "Mary." (John 20:11-18)

What happened next is one of the most beautiful moments in the Bible. Through her tears, Mary saw a man she took for the gardener. "Sir, if you have carried him away, tell me where you have put him, and I will get him."

Jesus said one word: "Mary."

She knew the voice instantly. "Rabboni!" — my Teacher. The first appearance of the risen Jesus, and it was to her: not to Peter, not to the eleven, not to a crowd. And Jesus gave her the first assignment of the new world: "Go... to my brothers and tell them" (John 20:17).

So Mary Magdalene ran and delivered the sentence on which everything since has stood: "I have seen the Lord!" (John 20:18).

**Key lesson for kids:** Jesus knows his people by name — and being known by him is what turned Mary's worst morning into the best one. Also worth noticing: the risen Jesus trusted a woman with the most important message ever carried, in a world that often didn't take women's testimony seriously. God's choices correct the world's.

## Why Mary Magdalene Matters

- **First witness of the resurrection.** All four Gospels put her at the tomb on Easter morning, and John records Jesus appearing to her first — the hinge-moment of the Christian faith, entrusted first to her.
- **A picture of what Jesus does.** Seven demons, then total devotion; her life is the before-and-after the Gospels want readers to see.
- **Steady when it was hardest.** Cross, burial, tomb — she's present at every station where courage was most expensive.
- **She clears up a common mix-up.** She is not the sinful woman of Luke 7, and not Mary of Bethany — see the FAQ below. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Was Mary Magdalene the sinful woman who anointed Jesus' feet?

The Bible never says so. The unnamed woman of Luke 7:36-50 appears at the end of chapter 7, and Mary Magdalene is introduced — as if for the first time — at the start of chapter 8, described only as a woman freed from seven demons. A sixth-century sermon merged the two women (plus Mary of Bethany) into one figure, and the mix-up stuck in art and movies for centuries. Careful readers, Catholic and Protestant alike, now agree they are different people.

### Was Mary Magdalene married to Jesus?

No. That idea comes from novels and much later legends, not from Scripture or any reliable early source. The Gospels describe Mary as a healed follower and supporter of Jesus' ministry and the first witness of the resurrection — an extraordinary role that needs no invented romance to make it important.

### Why is she called "the apostle to the apostles"?

"Apostle" means "sent one." Because the risen Jesus sent Mary to announce his resurrection to the disciples (John 20:17-18), early church writers honored her with the title apostola apostolorum — the sent-one to the sent-ones. The men who would carry the message to the world heard it first from her.

### What were the "seven demons"?

Luke 8:2 and Mark 16:9 say Jesus had driven seven demons out of Mary, but Scripture gives no details about her affliction. The number seven often signals completeness in the Bible — her suffering had been severe and total, and so was her deliverance. When kids ask, it's enough to say: Mary had been deeply tormented in a way doctors couldn't fix, Jesus completely freed her, and she followed him with her whole life from then on.
