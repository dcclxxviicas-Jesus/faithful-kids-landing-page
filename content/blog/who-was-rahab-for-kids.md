# Who Was Rahab in the Bible? Story for Kids

Rahab is one of the most surprising heroes in the Bible. She was a Canaanite, a resident of the enemy city of Jericho, and a woman with a past the Bible doesn't hide. Yet the New Testament names her twice as a model of faith — and places her in the family tree of Jesus himself.

Her story takes up one chapter, Joshua 2, with its ending in Joshua 6. It is short, tense, and perfect for reading aloud.

## Two Spies on a Roof (Joshua 2:1-7)

Israel was camped east of the Jordan River, about to enter the Promised Land after forty years in the wilderness. Joshua secretly sent two spies to look over the land, "especially Jericho" — the walled fortress city guarding the way in.

The spies entered the city and stayed at the house of Rahab, which was built into the city wall. Someone reported them, and the king of Jericho sent soldiers to Rahab's door with an order: bring out the men.

Rahab had already hidden the spies on her roof under stalks of flax. She told the soldiers the men had left at dusk before the gate closed — "Go after them quickly. You may catch up with them" (Joshua 2:5). The soldiers raced off toward the river fords, chasing no one.

**Key lesson for kids:** Rahab risked her life for two strangers because she had decided whose side she was on. Courage usually isn't a feeling — it's a decision you make before the knock on the door.

## "The Lord Your God Is God" (Joshua 2:8-14)

That night on the roof, Rahab told the spies something astonishing. All of Jericho, she said, was melting in fear of Israel. They had heard about the Red Sea drying up forty years earlier, and about Israel's victories east of the Jordan. And then this Canaanite woman said words many Israelites struggled to believe:

"The Lord your God is God in heaven above and on the earth below" (Joshua 2:11).

Everyone in Jericho had heard the same news. Only Rahab responded with faith instead of just fear. She asked the spies to swear that when Israel took the city, they would spare her father, mother, brothers, sisters, and all who belonged to them. The spies agreed: our lives for yours.

**Key lesson for kids:** Hearing about God isn't the same as trusting Him. The whole city had the same information as Rahab; she was the only one who acted on it. Faith is what you do with what you've heard.

## The Scarlet Cord (Joshua 2:15-24)

Rahab let the spies down by a rope through her window — remember, her house was part of the city wall — and told them to hide in the hills for three days until the search parties gave up.

The spies gave her one instruction: tie this scarlet cord in the window, gather your whole family inside the house, and when we take the city, everyone inside will be safe.

The scarlet cord in the window became her family's rescue sign. Many Christians down the centuries have noticed how much it echoes the first Passover, when a mark at the doorway meant life inside ([the plagues and Passover story](/blog/the-burning-bush-for-kids) sets that up) — one visible sign of trust, protecting an entire household.

**Key lesson for kids:** Rahab's faith saved more than herself. Because one person trusted God, a whole family walked out alive. Your choices reach further than you.

## When the Walls Fell (Joshua 6)

You probably know what happened next: Israel crossed the Jordan, marched around Jericho once a day for six days, and on the seventh day circled seven times, blew the trumpets, shouted — and [the walls of Jericho fell down flat](/blog/the-walls-of-jericho-for-kids).

Every wall, that is, except where a scarlet cord hung in a window. Joshua gave the order: "Go into the prostitute's house and bring her out and all who belong to her, in accordance with your oath to her" (Joshua 6:22). The two former spies walked through the rubble and led Rahab and her entire family to safety. Verse 25 adds the quiet ending: "she lives among the Israelites to this day."

**Key lesson for kids:** God keeps promises down to the detail — even a promise made by two spies on a rooftop at midnight. Not one person inside that marked house was lost.

## Why Rahab Matters

- **She's in Jesus' family tree.** Matthew 1:5 lists her by name: Rahab married Salmon, became the mother of Boaz — who married [Ruth](/blog/who-was-ruth-for-kids) — and so became the great-great-grandmother of [King David](/blog/who-was-david-for-kids). A Canaanite woman from Jericho stands in the line of the Messiah.
- **The New Testament praises her twice.** Hebrews 11:31 puts her in the hall of faith; James 2:25 holds her up as faith in action.
- **Her past didn't disqualify her.** The Bible plainly calls her a prostitute and never airbrushes it. God's grace met her where she was — and her story is the proof that nobody starts too far away.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Was Rahab really a prostitute?

Yes — the Hebrew text of Joshua 2:1 and the Greek of Hebrews 11:31 and James 2:25 all use the plain word for it. Some older traditions softened it to "innkeeper," but Scripture doesn't. That's the point of her story: God's grace reached a woman her own city would have looked down on, and He placed her in the ancestry of Jesus. When kids ask, you can say honestly that Rahab had a hard and broken life before she trusted God — and that God welcomed her completely.

### Did Rahab lie, and was that okay?

Rahab did deceive the king's soldiers, and the Bible never pauses to grade that moment — it praises what her actions showed: she sided with God's people at the risk of her own life (James 2:25). Christians have discussed her lie for centuries. What Scripture celebrates is not the deception but the faith behind the courage, and her story is honest the way the Bible always is: real people, in real danger, trusting God imperfectly.

### How is Rahab related to Jesus?

Matthew 1:5 traces it: Rahab married an Israelite named Salmon and became the mother of Boaz. Boaz married Ruth, their son was Obed, Obed's son was Jesse, and Jesse's son was David — and Jesus was born into David's royal line. Two foreign women, Rahab and Ruth, stand side by side in that genealogy.

### What happened to Rahab after Jericho?

Joshua 6:25 says she lived among the Israelites from then on — fully included, not kept at the edge of the camp. The genealogy in Matthew 1 shows her married into the tribe of Judah and raising Boaz, whose own famous kindness to a foreign widow (Ruth) makes a lot of sense when you remember who his mother was.
