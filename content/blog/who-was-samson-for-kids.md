# Who Was Samson? The Samson and Delilah Story for Kids

Samson is the strongest man in the Bible and one of its most heartbreaking characters at the same time. He tore a lion apart with his bare hands, defeated whole companies of Philistines alone, and was finally brought down not by an army, but by a secret whispered to the wrong person.

His story fills four chapters, Judges 13-16, at the end of Israel's dark judges era. It reads like a superhero story, but its real lesson is about where strength actually comes from.

## A Baby With a Vow (Judges 13)

Israel had drifted from God again, and the Philistines had dominated them for forty years. Into that situation, the angel of the Lord appeared to a childless couple, Manoah and his wife, with an announcement: you will have a son, and he will begin to deliver Israel from the Philistines.

There was one condition. The boy would be a Nazirite from birth, set apart for God with special rules from Numbers 6: no wine, no touching dead bodies, and no razor ever touching his head. His long hair would be the visible sign of an invisible dedication.

**Key lesson for kids:** Samson's strength was never really in his hair, it was in his dedication to God, which the hair stood for. Every gift you have is like that: it comes from somewhere, and it stays healthy only while you honor where it came from.

## The Lion and the Riddle (Judges 14)

Young Samson's strength was staggering. On the road to Timnah, a lion attacked him, and "the Spirit of the Lord came powerfully upon him so that he tore the lion apart with his bare hands" (Judges 14:6). Later he found bees had made honey in the carcass, which he turned into a wedding riddle that no one could solve: "Out of the eater, something to eat; out of the strong, something sweet."

But the same chapters show the crack in Samson: he wanted a Philistine wife against his parents' pleading, and when his wedding guests cheated the riddle out of him, his temper exploded. Strength and self-control, it turns out, are different gifts, and Samson only had one of them.

**Key lesson for kids:** Being strong and being wise are not the same thing. Samson could beat anyone except his own impulses, and the Bible shows that honestly so we learn to train both.

## One Man Against the Philistines (Judges 15)

The feud with the Philistines escalated, burned fields, revenge upon revenge, until Samson, armed with nothing but the jawbone of a donkey, struck down a thousand men at Lehi. Israel's judge was a one-man army, and for twenty years he led Israel while the Philistines feared him.

Notice what the text keeps repeating: "the Spirit of the Lord came powerfully upon him." The muscles were Samson's; the power was God's. (We tell this era in full in [Samson: Strongest Man Alive](/blog/samson-strongest-man-alive-for-kids).)

**Key lesson for kids:** When God gives someone a gift, it's for protecting and serving others. The moments Samson used his strength for Israel, it worked mightily. The moments he used it for his own temper, everything went wrong.

## Samson and Delilah (Judges 16:4-22)

Then Samson fell in love with Delilah, and the Philistine rulers each offered her a fortune in silver to discover the secret of his strength.

Three times she asked, and three times Samson fed her a false answer: tie me with seven fresh bowstrings; tie me with new ropes; weave my hair into a loom. Each time, Philistines were waiting in the room when she cried, "Samson, the Philistines are upon you!" and each time he snapped the bindings like thread. Any of those nights, he could have seen exactly what she was doing.

But Delilah pressed him "day after day until he was sick to death of it", and Samson finally told her everything: no razor has ever touched my head; shave it, and my strength will leave me.

She lulled him to sleep on her lap, called a man to shave his head, and this time when she shouted, Samson woke and thought, "I'll go out as before and shake myself free." The saddest sentence in the book follows: "But he did not know that the Lord had left him" (Judges 16:20). The Philistines seized him, blinded him, and set him to grinding grain in a Gaza prison.

**Key lesson for kids:** Samson treated his dedication to God like a game of secrets, and lost it a little at a time, not all at once. Nobody falls in one day. Guard the promises that make you who you are, and be careful who you let keep pushing at them.

## The Final Stand (Judges 16:23-31)

In prison, something the Philistines never thought about began to happen: "the hair on his head began to grow again" (Judges 16:22).

At a great festival to their god Dagon, the Philistine rulers brought blind Samson out to mock him in the packed temple. Standing between the two central pillars, Samson prayed for the first recorded time in his whole story: "Sovereign Lord, remember me. Please, God, strengthen me just once more" (Judges 16:28).

God answered. Samson pushed the pillars apart, the temple came down on the rulers of the Philistines, and Samson died with them, his greatest act of deliverance coming in his final, humbled, praying moment. Hebrews 11:32 still lists him among the heroes of faith. (We tell this ending in [Samson's Fall and Final Stand](/blog/samsons-fall-and-final-stand-for-kids).)

**Key lesson for kids:** It is never too late to turn back to God. Blind, shaved, and chained, Samson prayed, and God heard him. Failure is a chapter, not the ending.

## Why Samson Matters

- **He's the Bible's warning about wasted gifts.** No one in Scripture received more raw power or spent it more carelessly, and the story is told so kids can learn the cheap way what Samson learned the hard way.
- **His strength had a source.** "The Spirit of the Lord came powerfully upon him" is the story's real engine, the hair was only the sign of the vow.
- **His last prayer was answered.** The same God who left when the vow was broken returned the moment Samson humbly asked.
- **He closes the judges cycle** that [Deborah](/blog/who-was-deborah-for-kids) and [Gideon](/blog/who-was-gideon-for-kids) carried before him, after Samson, Israel starts asking for a king, which is [Samuel's story](/blog/who-was-samuel-for-kids). ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Was Samson's strength really in his hair?

No, his strength was from God, and the uncut hair was the last visible sign of his Nazirite dedication (Judges 13:5). The vow had three parts, and Samson had already brushed past the others; the haircut was the final break. Judges 16:20 states the real loss plainly: not "his hair was gone" but "the Lord had left him." When his hair grew back in prison, it marked a heart returning to God, and it was prayer, not hair length, that God answered at the pillars.

### Why did Delilah betray Samson?

Money. The Philistine rulers each offered her 1,100 shekels of silver (Judges 16:5), a staggering fortune, several lifetimes of wages, from each of the five rulers. The Bible never says Delilah loved Samson; it says Samson loved her (Judges 16:4). That one-way devotion is exactly what the Philistines exploited.

### Was Samson a good judge of Israel?

He judged Israel twenty years (Judges 15:20) and single-handedly kept the Philistines in check, which is what God raised him up to do, "he will begin the deliverance of Israel" (Judges 13:5). But unlike Deborah or Gideon, he never led armies or called the nation back to God; his story is personal, impulsive, and tragic. Scripture counts him a hero of faith (Hebrews 11:32) while honestly showing every flaw, both things are true at once.

### How should I explain the ending to my kids?

Honestly but gently: Samson's last act brought down the temple of Dagon on the Philistine rulers, the oppressors of Israel, and Samson died with them, praying to God as he did. For younger children, the emphasis that helps most is the one the text gives: God heard Samson's prayer even after all his failures. It's a story about a second chance arriving in the very last moment of a life.
