# Who Was Solomon in the Bible? Story for Kids

Solomon was the richest and wisest king Israel ever had. He wrote thousands of proverbs, built the temple his father David only dreamed of, and hosted queens who traveled across the world just to test his wisdom. And then — this is the part kids must not miss — the wisest man alive slowly stopped living by his own wisdom.

His story fills 1 Kings 1-11, and his words fill Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, and the Song of Songs.

## "Ask for Whatever You Want" (1 Kings 3:1-15)

Solomon was the son of [King David](/blog/who-was-david-for-kids) and Bathsheba, chosen to succeed his father while still young ([Solomon Is Chosen](/blog/solomon-is-chosen-for-kids) tells that tense story). Early in his reign, God appeared to him in a dream at Gibeon with a staggering offer: "Ask for whatever you want me to give you."

Solomon's answer changed his life. He admitted he felt like "a little child" who didn't know how to carry out his duties, and asked for one thing: "Give your servant a discerning heart to govern your people and to distinguish between right and wrong" (1 Kings 3:9).

The request pleased God so much that He gave Solomon what he didn't ask for too — riches and honor beyond any king of his day — along with the wisdom he did.

**Key lesson for kids:** God's question to Solomon is worth asking yourself: if you could ask for anything, what would it be? Solomon's answer — wisdom to do his job well and know right from wrong — is still the best one anybody's given.

## The Wisest Judgment (1 Kings 3:16-28)

Solomon's wisdom got its famous test almost immediately. Two women came before him with one living baby, each claiming to be the mother. No witnesses, no evidence — just two words against each other.

Solomon called for a sword: divide the living child in two, half for each.

The real mother instantly gave the baby up — "Please, my lord, give her the living baby! Don't kill him!" — while the other coldly accepted the split. Solomon had his answer: give the baby to the first woman; "she is his mother." All Israel heard the verdict "and they held the king in awe, because they saw that he had wisdom from God to administer justice" (1 Kings 3:28). (We retell it in [Solomon's Wise Judgment](/blog/solomons-wise-judgment-for-kids).)

**Key lesson for kids:** Real love gives up its rights to protect the beloved — that's how Solomon spotted the true mother. Wisdom often works like that: it watches what love does under pressure.

## Builder of the Temple (1 Kings 5-8)

Solomon's greatest project took seven years: the temple in Jerusalem, the permanent house for the ark of God that his father David had longed to build. Cedar from Lebanon, stone finished at the quarry so no hammer rang at the site, gold overlaying the inner sanctuary — the Bible gives the dimensions and details like a builder's log ([Building the Temple](/blog/building-the-temple-for-kids) walks kids through it).

At the dedication, the glory of the Lord filled the temple like a cloud, and Solomon's prayer (1 Kings 8) is one of the longest and most beautiful in Scripture — including this honest astonishment: "But will God really dwell on earth? The heavens, even the highest heaven, cannot contain you. How much less this temple I have built!" ([The Temple Is Dedicated](/blog/the-temple-is-dedicated-for-kids) covers that day.)

**Key lesson for kids:** Solomon built God a magnificent house and then said out loud that no building could hold Him. Both were right: we give God our very best, and God is always bigger than our best.

## Proverbs, and a Visiting Queen (1 Kings 4:29-34; 10)

"Solomon spoke three thousand proverbs and his songs numbered a thousand and five" (1 Kings 4:32). Much of the book of Proverbs is his — short, sharp sentences about laziness and diligence, honest scales, kind words, guarded tongues ([Solomon's Proverbs](/blog/solomons-proverbs-for-kids) samples the best for kids).

His fame traveled. The Queen of Sheba crossed the desert with a camel train just to test him with hard questions — and left breathless: "Not even half was told me" (1 Kings 10:7).

**Key lesson for kids:** Wisdom is attractive — people cross deserts for it. A kid who learns a few proverbs by heart is carrying pocket-sized wisdom that outlasts every trend.

## The Slow Turn Away (1 Kings 11)

Then comes the chapter every parent should read with their kids, because it answers the question "how does the wisest man fall?" Answer: slowly, and by the heart.

Solomon married hundreds of foreign wives — political alliances, mostly — in direct defiance of God's warning, "and his wives led him astray. As Solomon grew old... his heart was not fully devoted to the Lord his God" (1 Kings 11:3-4). The man who built the temple built shrines to idols. God told him the kingdom would be torn from his son — and after Solomon's death, it split in two, exactly as said ([Solomon Turns Away](/blog/solomon-turns-away-for-kids) tells it honestly).

Ecclesiastes reads like Solomon's late-life verdict on chasing everything under the sun: meaningless, a chasing after wind — and its last words point home: "Fear God and keep his commandments" (Ecclesiastes 12:13).

**Key lesson for kids:** Knowing what's right and doing it are two different muscles. Solomon's fall wasn't one big decision — it was years of small compromises. Guard your heart a day at a time; even the wisest person can't coast.

## Why Solomon Matters

- **He asked for the right thing.** His prayer for wisdom is the model request — and James 1:5 says God still answers it: "If any of you lacks wisdom, you should ask God, who gives generously."
- **He built the temple** — the center of Israel's worship for four centuries, until [Babylon destroyed it](/blog/judah-falls-to-babylon-for-kids). Find Jerusalem on our free [map of ancient Israel](/printables/bible-maps/ancient-israel).
- **His books still teach daily life.** Proverbs is the Bible's practical-wisdom manual, perfect for reading one chapter a day with kids.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Was Solomon the wisest man who ever lived?

1 Kings 4:30-31 says his wisdom "was greater than the wisdom of all the people of the East... he was wiser than anyone else." Within the Old Testament's frame, yes — though Jesus later said of himself, "now something greater than Solomon is here" (Matthew 12:42). Solomon's own story adds the crucial footnote: wisdom possessed isn't automatically wisdom obeyed.

### Which books of the Bible did Solomon write?

Tradition attributes most of Proverbs to him (the book names him in 1:1, 10:1 and 25:1), along with Ecclesiastes ("the Teacher, son of David, king in Jerusalem") and the Song of Songs. Psalm 72 and 127 also carry his name. Scholars discuss the details of authorship, but the Bible consistently presents Solomon as Israel's great source of wisdom literature.

### How rich was Solomon?

1 Kings 10 details it: 666 talents of gold arriving yearly (roughly 25 tons), a throne of ivory overlaid with gold, silver "as common in Jerusalem as stones," and a fleet of trading ships. The Bible itself draws the conclusion: "King Solomon was greater in riches and wisdom than all the other kings of the earth" (10:23). Jesus still used him as the benchmark of splendor — and noted that the lilies of the field are dressed better (Matthew 6:29).

### Why did the kingdom split after Solomon?

Because of his idolatry, God told Solomon the kingdom would be torn from his line, sparing one tribe for David's sake (1 Kings 11:11-13). After his death, his son Rehoboam answered the people's plea for lighter burdens with threats, and ten northern tribes walked away to follow Jeroboam (1 Kings 12). From then on there were two kingdoms — Israel in the north, Judah in the south — the stage for the whole era of the prophets.
