# Who Was Stephen in the Bible? Story for Kids

Stephen appears in only two chapters of the Bible — Acts 6 and 7 — and changed the history of the church forever. He was the first follower of Jesus to die for his faith, and he died the way his Master did: praying for the people hurting him.

His story needs care when sharing it with kids, but it shouldn't be skipped. It contains one of the Bible's best pictures of courage, and a surprise ending that takes years to pay off.

## Chosen to Wait Tables (Acts 6:1-7)

The first church in Jerusalem was growing fast — and growth brought problems. The Greek-speaking widows were being overlooked in the daily food distribution. The apostles asked the church to choose seven trusted men to take charge of it, "known to be full of the Spirit and wisdom."

Stephen is the first name on the list, described as "a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit" (Acts 6:5).

Notice what the job was: making sure hungry widows got fed. That's the task the church's brightest young man was given, and he took it. But God had more in him — "Stephen, a man full of God's grace and power, performed great wonders and signs among the people" (Acts 6:8).

**Key lesson for kids:** Stephen's path to greatness started with serving food fairly. No task done for God is small — faithfulness in the unglamorous job is exactly what God promotes.

## On Trial With an Angel's Face (Acts 6:8-15)

Stephen's wisdom made enemies. Members of a Jerusalem synagogue debated him and lost — "they could not stand up against the wisdom the Spirit gave him as he spoke" (Acts 6:10). So they did what losing arguments often do: they arranged false witnesses, stirred up the crowd, and dragged him before the Sanhedrin, the same council that had condemned Jesus.

As the false accusations flew, the council stared at the defendant — "and they saw that his face was like the face of an angel" (Acts 6:15). No panic. No hatred. Something shining.

**Key lesson for kids:** You can't control what people say about you; you can control the face you answer with. Stephen's calm came from being full of something — grace — so that when he was squeezed, grace is what came out.

## The Longest Speech in Acts (Acts 7:1-53)

Given the chance to defend himself, Stephen instead told Israel's whole story — the longest speech in the book of Acts. [Abraham](/blog/who-was-abraham-for-kids) called from Ur. [Joseph](/blog/who-was-joseph-for-kids) rejected by his brothers but raised up to save them. [Moses](/blog/who-was-moses-for-kids) rejected by his people but sent back as their rescuer. The tabernacle, [David](/blog/who-was-david-for-kids), [Solomon's temple](/blog/who-was-solomon-for-kids) — and the prophets, persecuted in every generation.

His point landed like a hammer: the pattern was repeating. The rescuers God sends keep getting rejected by the people they're sent to — "and now you have betrayed and murdered him," the Righteous One himself (Acts 7:52).

**Key lesson for kids:** Stephen knew his Bible so well he could tell the whole story from memory under threat of death — and see its pattern pointing to Jesus. That's why we learn the stories now: they become a map you carry when things get hard.

## The First Martyr (Acts 7:54-60)

The council erupted. But Stephen, "full of the Holy Spirit, looked up to heaven and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing at the right hand of God" — and said so. They dragged him out of the city and stoned him.

Luke records his two final prayers carefully, because both echo Jesus' own words from the cross: "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit," and then, on his knees, with his last strength: "Lord, do not hold this sin against them" (Acts 7:59-60). Then, says the Bible — gently — "he fell asleep."

One detail in the scene looks like a footnote and turns out to be a fuse: "the witnesses laid their coats at the feet of a young man named Saul" (Acts 7:58), who approved of the killing.

**Key lesson for kids:** Stephen's last act was forgiveness. He didn't win the argument that day — he showed what Jesus makes a person into, which is a different kind of winning. For younger kids, keep the focus there: he was brave, he trusted Jesus completely, and he forgave.

## The Surprise Ending (Acts 8:1-4; 9)

Stephen's death set off the first great persecution, scattering believers from Jerusalem — and everywhere they scattered, "they preached the word" (Acts 8:4). The attempt to stamp out the message became its delivery system.

And that young man Saul, who guarded the coats? He kept persecuting the church — until Jesus stopped him on the Damascus road ([Saul Meets Jesus](/blog/saul-meets-jesus-for-kids)). Saul became [Paul](/blog/who-was-paul-for-kids), the greatest missionary in history — and years later, Paul himself connected the dots out loud: "when the blood of your martyr Stephen was shed, I stood there giving my approval" (Acts 22:20). Many readers have wondered whether Stephen's dying prayer — "do not hold this sin against them" — got answered in Paul.

**Key lesson for kids:** You don't get to see what your faithfulness will cause. Stephen never knew the man holding the coats would carry the gospel across the world. Plant seeds anyway; God handles the harvest.

## Why Stephen Matters

- **He was the first martyr** — the first of a long line who valued Jesus above life itself, and the word "martyr" itself comes from the Greek for "witness."
- **He died like his Master.** Both of his final prayers mirror Jesus' words from the cross — the clearest proof in Acts that following Jesus actually changes a person.
- **His death spread the gospel.** Persecution scattered the church out of Jerusalem and into the world — exactly the opposite of what his killers intended. Quiz the era with our [Acts Bible trivia](/blog/acts-bible-trivia).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a martyr?

The word comes from the Greek martys, which simply means "witness." Because so many early witnesses to Jesus were killed for refusing to deny him, the word came to mean someone who dies for their faith. Stephen was the first (Acts 7), which is why he's called the protomartyr — the first martyr.

### Why was Stephen killed?

Officially, for "blasphemy" — his accusers claimed he spoke against the temple and the law (Acts 6:13-14). Really, because his argument couldn't be answered: he showed from Israel's own scriptures that rejecting Jesus fit a long pattern of rejecting the rescuers God sends. When he declared that he saw Jesus standing at God's right hand (Acts 7:56), the council treated it as blasphemy deserving death and stoned him without any Roman trial.

### Why does it say Jesus was "standing" at God's right hand?

Everywhere else, Scripture pictures Jesus seated at God's right hand (Psalm 110:1; Hebrews 10:12). Only here does he stand (Acts 7:55-56). Readers across the centuries have loved the detail: the Lord rising — as a witness rises, or as you stand to welcome someone — for the first of his followers to come home this way.

### How do I talk about this story with young children?

Lead with the parts that are actually the point: Stephen was kind (he fed widows), wise (nobody could out-argue him), brave (he told the truth when it was dangerous), and forgiving (his last prayer was for his enemies). You can say "people hurt him very badly and he died, and Jesus welcomed him home" without dwelling on the mechanics. Older kids can handle the full scene — and the astonishing link to Paul.
