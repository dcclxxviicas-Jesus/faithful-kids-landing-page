## What Games Work for Small Youth Groups (Under 10)?

Small groups die on the vine with relay races and thrive on games built around talking, guessing, and knowing each other. These 15 games all work with 4-10 students and give every single person a turn.

### 1. Hot Seat
**Group: 4-10 | Space: circle of chairs | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
One student sits in the hot seat for 90 seconds while the rest of the group fires questions -- favorite food, worst haircut, best day of your life. The rule: no question that would embarrass someone, and the person in the seat may pass twice. Rotate until everyone's had a turn. **Faith tie-in:** it makes real names and real details available for prayer requests later, which is half the point of youth group.

### 2. Two Truths and a Lie
**Group: 4-12 | Space: any | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Each student states three things about themselves, one of which is false. The group votes on the lie, then the student reveals. Push for specific truths ("I've broken my arm twice") over vague ones ("I like pizza"). **Faith tie-in:** a natural on-ramp to talking about how well we actually know each other, and about honesty in Ephesians 4:25.

### 3. Mafia
**Group: 7-15 | Space: circle | Materials: slips of paper or a deck of cards | Prep: 2 min**
Deal one "mafia" card per 4 players and one "detective"; everyone else is a villager. At "night" everyone closes their eyes, the mafia silently pick someone out, and the detective checks one person. At "day" the group debates and votes to eliminate a suspect. Mafia win when they equal the villagers; villagers win when all mafia are out. **Note:** takes 15-20 minutes and gets loud in the fun way.

### 4. Wink Murder
**Group: 6-14 | Space: circle | Materials: slips of paper | Prep: 1 min**
One student is secretly the "winker." They eliminate people by making eye contact and winking; the victim counts to three, then dramatically dies. Anyone who thinks they know the winker says "I accuse" -- but they need a second accuser before the reveal, so a wrong guess costs them. **Faith tie-in:** none needed. This is a five-minute filler and it's excellent.

### 5. Contact
**Group: 4-10 | Space: any, including a van | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
One student picks a word and says only its first letter. Others try to guess it by describing a word that starts with that letter ("Is it something you sleep in?"). If another guesser figures out what you mean, they say "Contact," the two count down from three and say the word together -- if they match, the word-holder must reveal the next letter. **Note:** best game in this list for long car rides to camp.

### 6. Salad Bowl
**Group: 6-16 | Space: any with a bowl in the middle | Materials: paper slips, pens, a bowl | Prep: 3 min**
Everyone writes 3-4 Bible people, places, or objects on slips. Round 1: describe the slip in as many words as you want. Round 2: same slips, one word only. Round 3: same slips, act it out silently. The same slips returning three times is what makes it hilarious by round three. **Faith tie-in:** by the end the group has reviewed 40 Bible references without a single worksheet.

### 7. Psychiatrist
**Group: 6-12 | Space: circle | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
One student leaves. The rest agree on a shared rule -- everyone answers as the person to their left, or everyone lies, or everyone answers as if they're 80 years old. The "psychiatrist" returns and asks questions until they diagnose the rule. **Note:** works best the second time you play it, once students start inventing rules.

### 8. Fortunately / Unfortunately
**Group: 4-10 | Space: circle | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Build a story around the circle, alternating: "Fortunately, Jonah found a boat." "Unfortunately, there was a storm." Try it with an actual Bible narrative and see how close the group can steer it to the real ending. **Faith tie-in:** run it on Joseph (Genesis 37-50) and the game basically tells itself -- students are shocked how well the real story fits the format.

### 9. The Question Game
**Group: 3-8 | Space: any | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Two students face off and may only speak in questions. No repeats, no hesitating, no statements. Whoever breaks is out and the next challenger steps up. Rounds last 20 seconds. **Note:** the fastest game here for filling dead time while you wait on late arrivals.

### 10. Categories
**Group: 5-12 | Space: circle | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Set a clap-clap-snap-snap rhythm. The leader names a category ("books of the New Testament") and each person must name an item on their snap. Miss the beat or repeat an answer and you're out; the rhythm never stops. **Faith tie-in:** rotate in "fruit of the Spirit" (Galatians 5:22-23), "the twelve disciples," and "the plagues of Egypt" for painless review.

### 11. Bippity Bippity Bop
**Group: 6-14 | Space: circle, room to spin | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
The leader stands in the middle, points at someone, and says "Bippity bippity bop" -- that person must say "Bop" before the leader finishes. If the leader just says "Bop," the person must stay silent. Add group poses (three people build an "elephant," three build a "washing machine") on command. Anyone who fails becomes the new middle. **Note:** high energy, no equipment, ends when you say it ends.

### 12. One-Word Story
**Group: 4-10 | Space: circle | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
The group tells a single story one word per person around the circle. Nobody may pause longer than two seconds. Then do it again with a required ending ("and that's how the walls of Jericho fell"). **Faith tie-in:** great warm-up before students retell a Bible story in their own words, which is the real skill you're building.

### 13. Sculptor
**Group: 4-12 | Space: room to stand in pairs | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
In pairs, one student is the sculptor and poses their partner (shoulders, arms, expression) into a scene from a Bible story. The rest guess the scene. Set a house rule up front: sculptors direct with words and never move a person by force. **Faith tie-in:** works beautifully on the crucifixion, the prodigal son's return, or Peter stepping out of the boat (Matthew 14:29).

### 14. Paranoia
**Group: 5-10 | Space: circle | Materials: a coin | Prep: 0 min**
A student whispers a question to their neighbor ("Who here would survive the longest on a desert island?"). The neighbor answers out loud with a name -- but nobody else knows the question. Flip a coin: heads, the question gets revealed; tails, it stays secret forever. **Note:** keep the questions positive. Ban anything about looks, dating, or ranking people, and this stays fun instead of turning into a wound.

### 15. Sword Drill Showdown
**Group: 4-12 | Space: any with seats | Materials: one physical Bible per student | Prep: 0 min**
Students hold closed Bibles overhead. Call a reference, say "Charge," and the first to find it stands and reads it aloud. For teenagers, make it harder: call the verse text instead of the reference and let them hunt with a concordance or index. **Faith tie-in:** "the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God" (Ephesians 6:17). Students who play this a few times stop being scared of a physical Bible, which is worth more than the game.

## What Games Work for Medium Youth Groups (10-25)?

Ten to 25 is the sweet spot for team games, tag variants, and anything with a relay. These 22 games all scale in that range without leaving anyone standing on the sideline.

### 16. Ninja
**Group: 8-25 | Space: open circle, no furniture | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Everyone stands in a circle, bows, and yells "Ninja." Play goes around the circle; on your turn you make one fluid motion to try to strike another player's hand. That player gets one motion to dodge. Hit hand = arm out; both arms out = eliminated. Everyone must freeze in whatever pose their motion ends in. **Note:** the freeze rule is what makes it funny. Clear the chairs first.

### 17. Signs
**Group: 10-20 | Space: circle | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Everyone invents a hand sign and shows it to the group. One person is "it" in the middle. Players pass the turn by doing their own sign then someone else's; if you're caught doing your sign by the person in the middle, or you fumble the pass, you swap in. **Note:** deceptively hard and it keeps 20 students locked in for 10 minutes.

### 18. Mingle
**Group: 12-30 | Space: open floor | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Students mill around saying "mingle, mingle, mingle" until you shout a number. They must form groups of exactly that size; anyone left over is out. Shrink to a winner. **Faith tie-in:** run one round where each group of 3 has to name one thing they're thankful for before the next round starts, and you've turned a game into a gratitude exercise.

### 19. Human Bingo
**Group: 10-40 | Space: open room | Materials: a printed 4x4 grid, pens | Prep: 10 min**
Fill a grid with traits ("has broken a bone," "can name all 12 disciples," "has been to a funeral"). Students hunt for a different person to sign each square. First to a full row shouts bingo. **Note:** the only game here with real prep, and it's the best first-night-of-the-semester game there is.

### 20. Clumps
**Group: 12-40 | Space: open floor | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Like Mingle, but instead of a number you call a category: "group up by month you were born," "by number of siblings," "by favorite Gospel." No elimination, just constant reshuffling. **Faith tie-in:** it physically breaks up cliques for the next activity, which is the actual reason to play it.

### 21. Silent Line-Up
**Group: 8-25 | Space: a wall or hallway | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Without speaking, students line up in order of birthday, height, or house number. Then check. **Faith tie-in:** an easy bridge into James 1:19, "quick to listen, slow to speak," and it works in a room with a ceiling fan.

### 22. Rock Paper Scissors Evolution
**Group: 10-40 | Space: open floor | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Everyone starts as an egg (squat and waddle). Win a round of RPS and evolve: egg to chicken to dinosaur to human. Lose and you devolve one stage. You may only challenge someone at your own stage. **Note:** works from 10 to 100 students and takes about six minutes.

### 23. Amoeba Tag
**Group: 12-30 | Space: gym, fellowship hall, or lawn | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Two taggers hold hands. Anyone tagged joins the chain. The chain may only tag with its two free end hands, and if the chain breaks, they must reconnect before tagging again. Once the blob hits six, it may split into two blobs of three. **Faith tie-in:** an obvious picture of the body of Christ in 1 Corinthians 12 -- the blob only works when it moves together.

### 24. Sardines
**Group: 8-25 | Space: a whole building or floor | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Reverse hide-and-seek. One student hides; everyone else searches. When you find them, you silently squeeze in and hide too. Last person still searching hides next. **Note:** set boundaries out loud -- which rooms are off limits, lights on or off -- before anyone moves.

### 25. Shrinking Islands
**Group: 10-25 | Space: open floor | Materials: newspaper, towels, or paper plates | Prep: 2 min**
Scatter "islands" on the floor. Play music; when it stops, everyone must have a body part on an island. Remove one island each round. Teams get creative fast -- piggybacks, human pyramids, one toe each. **Faith tie-in:** talk about Matthew 7:24-27 and what people stand on when the ground gets smaller. **Supervision:** watch the stacking. Call it before someone gets lifted overhead.

### 26. Chair Basketball
**Group: 10-24 | Space: a room with two ends | Materials: paper wads, two trash cans, chairs | Prep: 3 min**
Two teams, everyone seated, feet may not leave the floor. Pass the paper ball to teammates and shoot at the trash can. No standing, no walking. **Note:** the seated rule is what makes this playable in a carpeted classroom, and it accidentally levels the field for less athletic students.

### 27. Shoe Pile Scramble
**Group: 12-30 | Space: open floor | Materials: students' shoes | Prep: 0 min**
Everyone throws one shoe into a center pile. Mix it thoroughly. On go, students grab a shoe that isn't theirs, find the owner, and put it on that person's foot. Team that gets everyone shod first wins. **Faith tie-in:** John 13 -- Jesus washing feet is not a stretch here, and the connection lands because everyone just handled somebody else's sneaker.

### 28. Cup Stack Relay
**Group: 10-25 | Space: two tables | Materials: 10 disposable cups per team | Prep: 2 min**
Each runner sprints to the table, builds a 4-3-2-1 pyramid, collapses it back into a single stack, and runs back to tag out. **Note:** endlessly repeatable and dead quiet compared to most relays, which matters if another class meets next door.

### 29. Mummy Wrap
**Group: 12-30 | Space: open floor | Materials: 1 roll of toilet paper per team | Prep: 0 min**
Teams of 4-6 pick a mummy and wrap them head to toe. First team to use the whole roll with no gaps wins. **Faith tie-in:** John 11:44 -- Lazarus comes out still wrapped, and Jesus says "take off the grave clothes and let him go." That's a five-minute devotional handed to you.

### 30. Balloon Stomp
**Group: 12-30 | Space: open floor, nothing breakable | Materials: balloons, string | Prep: 10 min inflating**
Tie a balloon to each student's ankle. On go, everyone tries to pop everyone else's while protecting their own. Last balloon standing wins. **Supervision:** no pushing, no grabbing, feet only. Sit out anyone who shoves. Keep it to 60 seconds and it stays fun.

### 31. Telephone Charades
**Group: 10-20 | Space: front of room | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Five students line up facing away. Show #1 an action ("David slinging a stone"). #1 taps #2 and acts it out silently; #2 acts what they saw for #3, and so on. #5 guesses out loud, then you replay the original. The degradation is the whole show. **Faith tie-in:** a great lead-in to why the church wrote things down and why we can trust the manuscripts we have.

### 32. Reverse Charades
**Group: 10-25 | Space: front of room | Materials: paper slips | Prep: 3 min**
Flip the format: the whole team acts and one person guesses. Sixty seconds per round, as many slips as you can burn through. **Note:** shy students hide comfortably inside a group of eight actors, so participation jumps.

### 33. Pterodactyl
**Group: 8-20 | Space: circle | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Pass the word "pterodactyl" around the circle -- but nobody may show teeth. Cover your teeth with your lips. Show a tooth or laugh and you're out. Add a screeching pterodactyl noise to reverse direction. **Note:** two minutes long, unbelievably effective, works on middle schoolers and seniors equally.

### 34. Wax Museum
**Group: 10-25 | Space: any room | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Everyone freezes as a statue. One or two "curators" walk the museum trying to make statues move or laugh -- no touching. Anyone who breaks becomes a curator. **Note:** the rare high-participation game that gets quieter as it goes, so it's a good last game before you teach.

### 35. Speed Sort: Books of the Bible
**Group: 10-25 | Space: two tables or floor space | Materials: index cards or scrap paper | Prep: 8 min writing cards**
Write each book of the Bible on a card. Teams race to lay them out in canonical order. Start with just the New Testament, then the Pentateuch, then go for all 66. **Faith tie-in:** students who can find Habakkuk without the index stop feeling stupid in Bible study, and that is a bigger deal than it sounds.

### 36. Verse Scramble Relay
**Group: 12-25 | Space: a room with two ends | Materials: index cards | Prep: 5 min**
Write a memory verse one word per card, shuffle, and split into two identical sets at the far end of the room. Runners retrieve one card at a time and the team assembles the verse in order. **Faith tie-in:** any verse you're working on. Psalm 119:105 and Romans 12:2 are the right length for this.

### 37. Bigger and Better
**Group: 12-24 | Space: a neighborhood, with drivers | Materials: a paperclip per team | Prep: 15 min lining up adult drivers**
Teams start with a paperclip and knock on doors trading up for something bigger or better. Ninety-minute limit, everyone back by curfew. **Supervision:** an adult with every team, never alone, and teams introduce themselves as being from your church every single time. **Faith tie-in:** debrief on Matthew 13:44 and what people will trade for something worth more.

## What Games Work for Large Youth Groups (25+)?

Large groups need games where the rules are shoutable in 20 seconds and nobody waits in a line. These 18 all handle 25 to 100+ students.

### 38. Ships and Sailors
**Group: 25-100 | Space: gym or large hall | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Teach six commands: "Ships" (run to the left wall), "Sailors" (right wall), "Captain's Coming" (salute and freeze until "At Ease"), "Sick Turtle" (on your back, arms and legs waving), "Man Overboard" (pairs, one kneels, one points with a foot on the knee), "Crow's Nest" (groups of three, arms up in a point). Last group formed each round is out. **Note:** the single best big-group game ever invented. Ten minutes of teaching pays off for years.

### 39. Giants, Wizards, Elves
**Group: 25-80 | Space: gym or lawn with two safe zones | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Full-body rock paper scissors between two teams. Giants beat elves, elves beat wizards, wizards beat giants. Each team huddles, picks one, lines up at midfield, and on three does the motion. The losing team runs for their safe line; anyone tagged switches teams. **Note:** natural end point is when one team absorbs everyone, usually about eight minutes.

### 40. Four Corners
**Group: 25-100 | Space: any room with four corners | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Number the corners. A blindfolded caller counts to ten while everyone picks a corner, then calls a number -- that corner is out. Repeat until one student stands. **Note:** zero athleticism required, works in a carpeted room with a low ceiling, and 60 students can play it.

### 41. Sharks and Minnows
**Group: 25-60 | Space: gym or lawn, two end lines | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
One or two sharks in the middle. Minnows run end to end on "go"; anyone tagged becomes a shark. Continue until one minnow survives. **Supervision:** call two-hand touch only and no grabbing clothes, or you will spend the drive home apologizing to a parent.

### 42. Steal the Bacon
**Group: 24-60 | Space: gym or hall | Materials: one object (a ball, a shoe, a rolled towel) | Prep: 1 min**
Two teams line up facing each other and number off. Put the "bacon" in the middle. Call a number; those two students race for it. Grab it and get home without being tagged for a point; tag the grabber for a point. **Note:** call two numbers at once to double the pace and cut the standing-around time in half.

### 43. Indoor Capture the Flag
**Group: 30-100 | Space: a whole building, lights on | Materials: two bandanas or towels | Prep: 5 min setting boundaries**
Two teams, two halves of the building, one flag each. Cross into enemy territory and you can be tagged into "jail" until a teammate frees you. **Supervision:** no running on stairs, no locked rooms, no lights out, and post an adult at every stairwell. Announce those four rules before anyone moves.

### 44. Rock Paper Scissors Battle Royale
**Group: 25-150 | Space: open floor | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Everyone pairs up and plays RPS. The loser becomes the winner's cheering section and follows them, chanting their name. Winners keep facing winners, and the trains get longer, until two students play the last round with 50 people screaming behind each. **Note:** three minutes, no equipment, and the energy in the room at the end is genuinely absurd.

### 45. Everybody's It Tag
**Group: 25-80 | Space: gym or lawn | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Everyone is it. Tag someone and they kneel; they get back up when the person who tagged them is tagged out. It self-resolves into total chaos in about 90 seconds. **Note:** perfect for burning the first three minutes of a Wednesday night.

### 46. Elbow Tag
**Group: 24-50 | Space: gym or lawn | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
Everyone stands in pairs, arms linked, scattered around the space. One runner, one tagger. The runner escapes by linking elbows with a pair -- the person on the far end of that trio immediately becomes the new runner. **Note:** the rule shift keeps everyone alert even when they're standing still.

### 47. Octopus Tag
**Group: 25-60 | Space: gym or lawn | Materials: none | Prep: 0 min**
One tagger in the middle. Everyone crosses from one line to the other. Anyone tagged plants their feet and becomes seaweed -- they can tag but never move. The lane closes in fast. **Faith tie-in:** none. It's a four-minute energy burner and it doesn't need one.

### 48. Human Foosball
**Group: 30-60 | Space: gym or hall | Materials: a soft ball, tape or rope for lanes | Prep: 10 min**
Students hold a rope with their row and can only move side to side within their lane, exactly like foosball rods. Score in the far goal. **Note:** the rope is what makes it work. Without it, everyone abandons their lane in 30 seconds.

### 49. Doctor Dodgeball
**Group: 26-60 | Space: gym | Materials: soft foam balls | Prep: 2 min**
Standard dodgeball, but each team secretly picks a doctor who can revive teammates with a touch. When a team figures out the other doctor and gets them out, no more revivals. **Supervision:** below the shoulders only, foam balls only, no headshots ever. State it, then enforce it the first time it happens.

### 50. Ultimate Musical Chairs
**Group: 25-60 | Space: open floor, sturdy chairs | Materials: chairs, music | Prep: 5 min**
Nobody gets eliminated -- chairs get removed and students sit on each other. Play until 40 people are stacked on four chairs. **Supervision:** cap the pile height, keep leaders spotting, and stop before anyone's on someone's shoulders.

### 51. Have You Ever
**Group: 25-60 | Space: circle of chairs, one fewer than students | Materials: chairs | Prep: 3 min**
The person in the middle says "Have you ever..." and finishes it truthfully about themselves. Anyone who has must get up and find a new seat at least two chairs away. Whoever's left standing goes to the middle. **Faith tie-in:** works as a game, then flips into something real when you switch to "Have you ever needed God to show up in a week like this one."

### 52. Beach Ball Rally
**Group: 25-100 | Space: any room, standard ceiling | Materials: 1-3 beach balls or balloons | Prep: 2 min**
The whole group keeps the ball off the floor. Nobody may hit it twice in a row. Count out loud and try to beat last week's record. Add a second and third ball. **Note:** the one big-group game that works in a room with an eight-foot ceiling and no cleared floor.

### 53. Team Trivia Showdown
**Group: 25-80 | Space: any room with seating | Materials: paper, pens, a question list | Prep: 10 min**
Teams of 5-8, written answers, no phones, points doubled on the final question with a wagering round. Run four rounds of six questions. **Faith tie-in:** pull your question bank from our [Bible trivia for teens](/blog/bible-trivia-for-teens) list, which is sorted by difficulty already, or the [Bible trivia for youth](/blog/bible-trivia-for-youth) set for a mixed middle-and-high-school room.

### 54. Sheep and Shepherd
**Group: 25-60 | Space: gym or lawn | Materials: blindfolds or bandanas | Prep: 3 min**
Blindfold the "sheep" and scatter them. Each shepherd has a unique call and must gather their flock by voice alone while every other shepherd is calling too. Sheep may only follow their own shepherd's sound. **Faith tie-in:** "My sheep listen to my voice; I know them, and they follow me" (John 10:27). Debrief on what else is shouting at a 15-year-old all week.

### 55. Crossing the Line
**Group: 25-100 | Space: a room with a line of tape down the middle | Materials: tape | Prep: 2 min**
Start with easy statements -- "cross the line if you've been to the beach" -- then walk it gradually toward honest ones: "cross if this has been a hard month," "cross if you've felt alone at this church." No commentary, no laughing, no explaining. **Note:** this is the game you close a retreat with, not the one you open a Wednesday with. Have leaders ready to follow up with students who cross on the hard ones.

## How Do You Pick the Right Game for Your Group?

Pick for the room and the roster before you pick for the theme: count your students, look at your ceiling and your floor, and check whether tonight needs energy raised or energy lowered. A game that's perfect for 40 kids in a gym will feel sad with nine kids in a carpeted classroom, and vice versa. The most common youth ministry mistake is running a game built for a group three times your size.

Second, build the night in an arc: one loud game to shake off the school day, one mid-energy game that mixes the groups, then teaching, then something quiet if you want conversation afterward. Third, end every game one round before students are tired of it. A leader who calls it at the peak gets asked to run it again next week.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the best youth group games for a small group of 10 or fewer?
Contact, Salad Bowl, Hot Seat, Mafia, and Sword Drill Showdown all work with as few as four students and need almost no materials. Small groups do best with games built on talking and guessing rather than teams or relays, because with nine students a relay means most of the room is standing in line. Aim for games where every person gets a turn every 60 seconds.

### What youth group games require no preparation at all?
Ninja, Pterodactyl, Ships and Sailors, Rock Paper Scissors Evolution, Four Corners, Everybody's It Tag, and the Question Game all need zero materials and zero setup. You can teach any of them in under 30 seconds standing in the middle of the room. Memorize three no-prep games at each group size and you will never be caught without something to run.

### How long should a youth group game last?
Five to twelve minutes for most games, and stop one round before students lose interest. Big-group games like Ships and Sailors can hold 25 minutes if you keep adding commands, while circle games like Pterodactyl are best at two or three minutes. The single best rule in youth ministry games is to end at the peak.

### How do I get teenagers to actually participate instead of standing on their phones?
Start with a game where the leaders play first and look ridiculous, keep the rules under 30 seconds, and never call students out by name for not playing. Games with a hiding place for shy students -- Reverse Charades, Clumps, Team Trivia -- pull in far more non-participants than anything requiring a solo performance. A phone basket by the door, framed as fair for leaders too, solves most of the rest.

### Which youth group games connect to Scripture without feeling forced?
Sheep and Shepherd (John 10:27), Amoeba Tag (1 Corinthians 12), Mummy Wrap (John 11:44), Shoe Pile Scramble (John 13), Sword Drill Showdown (Ephesians 6:17), and Shrinking Islands (Matthew 7:24-27) all carry a natural one-sentence connection. Say the connection once, in a single sentence, right after the game ends -- then move on. Explaining it for four minutes is what makes students roll their eyes, not the connection itself.

### What games work when I have both middle schoolers and high schoolers?
Choose games where size and athleticism give no advantage: Four Corners, Salad Bowl, Rock Paper Scissors Battle Royale, Pterodactyl, Wax Museum, and Team Trivia all flatten the age gap. For anything physical, assign mixed teams and give high schoolers a role -- captain, scorekeeper, referee -- so they become hosts instead of steamrollers. Avoid tag games with a big age spread unless you have space for the speed difference.
