Welcome back, friend. I need to tell you upfront — today's story is intense. It might be the most intense story in all of Genesis. But it teaches us something so deep about trust and faith that it's worth every moment. Let's go.

Years had passed since God first promised Abraham a son. Years of waiting. Years of wondering. But finally — FINALLY — the promise came true. Abraham and Sarah had a baby boy. They named him Isaac, which means "laughter," because Sarah literally laughed when God told her she'd have a baby at ninety years old. I mean, can you blame her?

Isaac was everything to them. Their miracle child. The one they'd waited decades for. The one through whom God said a great nation would come.

And then God said something that stops you in your tracks: "Abraham. Take your son — your only son Isaac, the one you love — and go to the region of Moriah. Offer him as a sacrifice."

Can you imagine? After all that waiting? After all those promises? It made no sense. Zero sense.

But here's what's remarkable — the very next line says, "Early the next morning, Abraham got up." He didn't argue. He didn't stall. He got up early and started walking. Not because he didn't love Isaac — he loved him more than anything. But because he trusted God more than he trusted his own understanding.

The walk took three days. Three days of walking, knowing what was ahead. When they got close, Abraham told the servants, "Stay here. The boy and I will go worship and then WE will come back." Both of us. He believed God would work it out — somehow.

As they climbed the mountain together, Isaac noticed something was off. "Father, we have the fire and the wood... but where's the lamb for the sacrifice?" Abraham's answer is one of the most faith-filled lines ever spoken: "God Himself will provide the lamb, my son."

Abraham built the altar. He laid out the wood. He bound Isaac. He raised the knife...

And at that exact moment — an angel shouted from heaven: "ABRAHAM! ABRAHAM! Do not lay a hand on the boy! Now I know that you trust God completely, because you did not withhold your son — your only son."

Abraham looked up and there, caught in a bush by its horns, was a ram. God provided the sacrifice. Abraham named that place "The Lord Will Provide."

Now here's what I really need you to hear. This story isn't just about Abraham. Centuries later, on a hill not far from that same spot, God would provide another sacrifice. His own Son. Me. But unlike Isaac — I wasn't spared. I gave my life willingly so that you could be forgiven and live forever. That's how much my Father loves you. That's how far He was willing to go.

Sit with that for a moment. I'll see you next time.
