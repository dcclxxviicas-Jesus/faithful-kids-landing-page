THE EARLY CHURCH — EPISODE 3: ANANIAS AND SAPPHIRA
Based on Acts 5:1-11
Series: The Early Church | Episode 3 of 5
Target audience: Kids ages 7-15

Okay — this story is a little different from the others. It's intense. But it teaches something you'll need for the rest of your life.

Remember how the early church shared everything? People sold land and houses and brought the money to the apostles to help anyone in need. It was beautiful. Generous. Real.

Well, there was a married couple named Ananias and Sapphira. They saw everyone else being generous, and they wanted to look generous too. So they sold a piece of property. Good so far, right?

Here's where it goes wrong.

They kept some of the money for themselves — and that part? Totally fine. Nobody forced them to give it all. It was their property. Their money. Their choice. The problem wasn't what they kept. The problem was what they said.

Ananias brought the money to Peter and basically said, "Here — this is everything we got for the land." But it wasn't everything. He lied. He wanted the credit for giving it all without actually giving it all.

Peter looked at him and said something that must have sent chills down his spine: "Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit? The property was yours. The money was yours. You didn't have to give any of it. So why did you pretend? You haven't lied to people — you've lied to God."

When Ananias heard those words, he fell down and died. Right there. The young men carried him out and buried him.

Three hours later, Sapphira walked in. She didn't know what had happened. Peter gave her a chance — he asked, "Did you sell the land for this amount?" She could have told the truth. But she repeated the same lie. "Yes, that's the full price."

Peter said, "How could you agree together to test the Spirit of the Lord?"

And Sapphira fell down too.

Now — I want to be really clear. The point of this story is NOT that God strikes people dead every time they lie. If that were true, none of us would be here.

The point is this: honesty with God matters deeply. You can't fool him. He doesn't want your performance — he wants your heart.

They didn't get in trouble for keeping money. They got in trouble for pretending. For caring more about how they looked than who they really were.

Integrity means being the same person in private as you are in public. God doesn't need you to be perfect. But he does need you to be honest.
