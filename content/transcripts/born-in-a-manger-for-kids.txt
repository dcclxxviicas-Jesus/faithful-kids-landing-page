Joseph is knocking on doors. "Please — my wife is about to give birth. Do you have any room?" Door after door after door. No. No. No. Sorry. We're full.

Can you imagine the stress? The fear? The desperation? This wasn't a couple looking for a nice hotel. This was a young woman about to deliver her first child with nowhere to go.

Finally, someone — we don't know who — offered them the only space left. A stable. A place where animals slept. Think about that for a second. Hay on the ground. The smell of donkeys and cattle. Cobwebs. Flies. Not exactly the delivery room you'd pick for any baby — let alone the Son of God.

And that's where it happened. In the middle of the night, in a stable behind some overcrowded inn in a tiny town, the Creator of the universe took His first breath on earth. Mary wrapped me in strips of cloth — swaddling cloths — and laid me in a manger. Do you know what a manger is? It's a feeding trough. A box that animals eat out of. That was my first bed.

Now let me be real with you. I could have been born in a palace. I could have arrived with trumpets and thrones and servants. I'm the King of everything — the one who spoke the stars into existence. I could have chosen anywhere.

I chose a manger.

The people who feel like there's no room for them. I wanted you to know, from my very first moment on earth, that I understand what it's like to start with nothing.

Here's what I need you to hold onto: God doesn't do things the way the world expects. The world says kings need palaces. God says real kings serve. The world says power comes from being on top.
