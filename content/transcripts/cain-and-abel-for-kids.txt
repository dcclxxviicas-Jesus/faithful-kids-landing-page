Hey, welcome back. I have to warn you — today's story is a heavy one. It's about the first two brothers who ever lived. And it shows us how dangerous jealousy can be if we let it take over.

After Adam and Eve left the garden, they had two sons. The older one was Cain, and the younger one was Abel. As they grew up, Cain became a farmer — he worked the soil and grew crops. Abel became a shepherd — he raised flocks of sheep.

One day, both brothers brought an offering to God. Think of it like a gift to say, "God, I appreciate you." Abel brought the very best of his flock — the firstborn, the top pick. He held nothing back. Cain brought some of his crops... but not his best. It was more like leftovers.

God looked at Abel's offering and was pleased. But Cain's offering? Not so much. And here's the important thing — it wasn't about crops versus animals. It was about the heart behind the gift. Abel gave his best. Cain gave whatever was convenient.

Now, instead of taking a step back and thinking about it, Cain got furious. His face went dark with anger. And this is where God does something amazing — He steps in with a warning. God says to Cain, "Why are you so angry? If you do what is right, you'll be accepted. But be careful — sin is crouching at your door like a wild animal. It wants to devour you. You have to master it."

That image — sin crouching like a predator at your door — that's one of the most powerful warnings in the entire Bible. God was basically saying, "You're in danger. But you can still make the right choice."

Cain didn't listen.

He invited Abel out to the field. And there, Cain killed his own brother. The first murder in human history — and it was between family.

God came to Cain and asked, "Where is your brother Abel?" And Cain shot back with one of the most famous lines in the Bible: "Am I my brother's keeper?" But God already knew. He said, "Your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground."

Cain was punished — he was cursed to wander the earth, never able to settle. But even then — and this blows my mind — God put a protective mark on Cain so no one would kill him. Even after the worst thing Cain could possibly do, God showed mercy.

So what's the takeaway? Jealousy is like a tiny flame. If you ignore it, it grows into a fire that destroys everything. But you always have a choice. God warned Cain BEFORE it happened. He gave him an exit. The question is — will we take it?

Don't let jealousy win. Deal with it before it deals with you. I'll see you next time.
