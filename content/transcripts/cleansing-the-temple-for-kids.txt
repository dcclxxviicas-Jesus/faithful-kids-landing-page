You probably think of me as gentle. And I am. But today I need to tell you about the angriest I ever got in public.

The day after I rode into Jerusalem on that donkey, I walked into the temple — my Father's house. The place that was supposed to be the most sacred spot on earth. A place where anyone — rich or poor, Jew or foreigner — could come and meet with God.

And what did I find?

A marketplace. No — worse than a marketplace. A scam.

Merchants had set up tables everywhere, selling doves and lambs for the sacrifices. But they weren't just selling — they were gouging people. A pair of doves that cost a few coins in the regular market? Inside the temple, they charged ten times the price. And the money changers — people had to exchange their Roman coins for temple currency to pay the temple tax, and these guys were skimming off the top, cheating people right in God's house.

The poorest families, the ones who had traveled the farthest, the ones who wanted nothing more than to worship God — they were being exploited. Robbed. And the religious leaders allowed it because they were getting a cut.

Something rose up inside me — not rage for the sake of rage, but a holy fire. This was wrong. And someone had to say so.

I made a whip out of cords. I started flipping tables. Coins exploded across the stone floor, scattering everywhere. Cages broke open and doves flew out. Merchants scrambled. I drove them out — every single one.

And I said it loud enough for everyone to hear: "It is written — my house shall be called a house of prayer. But you have made it a den of robbers!"

The religious leaders were furious. But you know what happened next? The blind and the lame — the people everyone else ignored — they came to me right there in the temple, and I healed them. And children — kids probably your age — started singing "Hosanna to the Son of David!" right in the middle of the chaos.

The leaders were disgusted. "Do you hear what these children are saying?" they demanded. I looked at them and said, "Haven't you read the Scripture? From the lips of children and infants, God has ordained praise."

Listen to me carefully: I am slow to anger — but I am not passive when people are being hurt. When powerful people use God's name to exploit the vulnerable, it makes my blood boil. Righteous anger is real. It's not about losing your temper — it's about standing up because something matters.

Never let anyone tell you that following me means being a pushover. Following me means caring enough to act.

KEY SCRIPTURE: "My house shall be called a house of prayer, but you are making it a den of robbers!" — Matthew 21:13

LESSON: Righteous anger is real. Jesus gets angry at injustice and exploitation — especially when it blocks people from God.
