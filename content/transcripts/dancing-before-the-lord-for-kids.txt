KING DAVID'S REIGN — EPISODE 1: DANCING BEFORE THE LORD
Based on 2 Samuel 6 | Narrated by Jesus

Hey there, friends! Today I want to tell you about one of the wildest, most joyful moments in King David's life — the day he danced before the Lord with everything he had.

So picture this: David is finally king over all of Israel. And there is one thing burning in his heart — he wants to bring the Ark of the Covenant to Jerusalem. The Ark was the most sacred object in all of Israel. It represented the very presence of God. And David wanted God's presence right in the center of his capital city.

So David gathers thirty thousand men, and they load the Ark onto a brand-new cart pulled by oxen. Music is blasting — harps, lyres, tambourines, cymbals — it is a full-on celebration! But then something terrible happens. The oxen stumble. A man named Uzzah reaches out and grabs the Ark to steady it. And instantly — he dies.

Everyone freezes. David is shaken to his core. See, God had given very specific instructions for how the Ark should be carried — on poles, by the Levites. Not on a cart. David had good intentions, but he did it his own way instead of God's way.

David is so afraid that he leaves the Ark at the house of a man named Obed-Edom. And for three months, that household is blessed like crazy! Everything Obed-Edom touches seems to prosper. When David hears about it, something clicks. God's presence is not something to fear — it is something to embrace. But you have to do it with reverence, God's way.

So David tries again. This time, the Levites carry the Ark on their shoulders with poles, exactly as God commanded. And after every six steps, they stop and offer a sacrifice. David is so overwhelmed with joy that he strips off his royal robes and dances before the Lord in a simple linen garment — spinning, leaping, shouting — worshiping with every fiber of his being!

But watching from a palace window is his wife Michal, and she is disgusted. She thinks he looks ridiculous, undignified, embarrassing. When David comes home, she mocks him. But David says, "I was dancing before the LORD. And I will become even more undignified than this!"

Here is the lesson, friends: when you worship God, give Him everything. Do not hold back because someone might laugh. Do not play it cool because you are worried about looking silly. David was a king — and he did not care. He danced for an audience of One.

That kind of worship? That moves My Father's heart.
