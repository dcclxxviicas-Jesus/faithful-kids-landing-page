THE RISE OF KINGS — Episode 6: David and Jonathan
Based on 1 Samuel 18-20 | Narrated by Jesus

Hey! Welcome back. Last time, David knocked out a giant. But today's story? It's about something even more powerful than a slingshot — it's about friendship. REAL friendship. The kind that costs you something.

So right after David defeated Goliath, he met Jonathan — Saul's oldest son. And something incredible happened. The Bible says Jonathan's soul was "knit" to David's. Like, instantly bonded. Not just "hey, you're cool" — we're talking soul brothers. Deep, unshakable loyalty from that very first moment.

And Jonathan did something wild. He took off his own royal robe and gave it to David. Then his armor. Then his sword and his bow. Think about that — Jonathan was the PRINCE. He was next in line for the throne. And he's handing his royal gear to a shepherd kid from Bethlehem. Jonathan was basically saying, "I see that God's hand is on YOU, and I'm all in."

But here's where it gets intense. David became a hero. The women sang in the streets: "Saul has killed his thousands, but David his TEN thousands!" And Saul? He LOST it. Jealousy ate him alive. He started eyeing David like an enemy. He threw a spear at David — TWICE — while David was playing music for him! Then Saul started plotting to have David killed.

Now, Jonathan was stuck in the middle. This was his DAD trying to kill his best friend. What do you do with that? Jonathan went to bat for David. He talked to Saul, he defended David, he put himself at risk. At one point, Saul threw a spear at his OWN SON for defending David!

The hardest moment came when Jonathan realized David had to run for his life. They met in a field, and they wept together. Jonathan told David, "Go in peace. The LORD is between you and me forever." Jonathan KNEW David would become king instead of him — and he was okay with it. He chose his friend's future over his own crown.

That's what real friendship looks like. It doesn't keep score. It doesn't hold back when it costs something. It puts the other person first — even when it hurts.

I told my disciples the same thing: "Greater love has no one than this — to lay down one's life for one's friends." Jonathan lived that out centuries before I said it.

Find a friend worth fighting for. BE a friend worth having. See you next time!
