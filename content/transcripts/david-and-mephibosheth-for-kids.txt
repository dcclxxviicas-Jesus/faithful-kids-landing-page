KING DAVID'S REIGN — EPISODE 3: DAVID AND MEPHIBOSHETH
Based on 2 Samuel 9 | Narrated by Jesus

Friends, get ready — because today's story is one of the most beautiful pictures of grace in the entire Old Testament. It is going to hit you right in the heart.

David is at the height of his power. He is king. His enemies are defeated. His kingdom is secure. But one day, David asks a question that changes everything: "Is there anyone still left from the house of Saul that I can show kindness to — for Jonathan's sake?"

Now, Saul was the previous king who hunted David for years. But Saul's son Jonathan was David's best friend. They had made a covenant — a promise — to always look after each other's families. And David never forgot.

David's servants find a man named Ziba, who used to work for Saul's family. Ziba says, "There is Jonathan's son. His name is Mephibosheth. He is crippled in both feet."

Here is the backstory. When Mephibosheth was just five years old, news came that Saul and Jonathan had been killed in battle. His nurse grabbed him and fled in a panic, but she dropped him. Both his feet were permanently injured. Ever since, he had been living in hiding in a desolate place called Lo-Debar — which literally means "no pasture," a place of nothing.

Now put yourself in Mephibosheth's sandals. He is the grandson of David's enemy. In those days, when a new king took over, he would wipe out the previous royal family. So when David summons him, Mephibosheth is terrified. He falls flat on his face. "I am your servant," he says, trembling.

But listen to what David says: "Do not be afraid. I will show you kindness for the sake of your father Jonathan. I will restore all the land that belonged to your grandfather Saul. And you will always eat at my table."

Mephibosheth is stunned. He says, "What is your servant, that you should look at a dead dog like me?" He went from hiding in a wasteland, crippled, forgotten, afraid — to sitting at the king's table as one of his own sons.

Friends, that is exactly what grace looks like. That is what My Father does for you. You did not earn a seat at His table. You cannot walk your way there on your own strength. But God finds you in your Lo-Debar and says, "Come. Sit with Me. You are Mine."

Mephibosheth did not deserve it. He could not repay it. All he could do was receive it.

And that, friends, is the gospel.
