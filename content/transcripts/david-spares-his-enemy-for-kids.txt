THE RISE OF KINGS — Episode 8: David Spares His Enemy
Based on 1 Samuel 24, 26 | Narrated by Jesus

Welcome back! Today's story is one of the most intense moments in David's life — and it happened not once, but TWICE. It's the story of what David did when he had the perfect chance to destroy his enemy.

Scene one. David and his men are hiding deep inside a cave at En Gedi. It's dark. They're pressed against the walls. And guess who walks into that exact cave to... use the bathroom? SAUL. The king who's been hunting David with three thousand soldiers just strolled in, completely clueless.

David's men are whispering, "This is it! God delivered your enemy right to you! Take him out!" And David crept forward in the darkness. He got SO close he could have ended Saul right there. But instead? He cut off a corner of Saul's robe. That's it. Just a piece of fabric.

And even THAT bothered David's conscience. He told his men, "The LORD forbid that I should do this to my master. He is the LORD's anointed." When Saul left the cave, David called out to him, held up the piece of robe, and said, "I could have killed you, but I didn't. I am not your enemy!"

Saul actually wept. He said, "You are more righteous than I am." But did he change? Not for long.

Scene two. Later, David sneaks into Saul's camp at NIGHT. Saul is sleeping, surrounded by soldiers, with his spear stuck in the ground by his head. David's warrior Abishai whispers, "God has delivered him to you. Let me pin him to the ground with one thrust!" David says, "No. Do NOT destroy him. Who can lay a hand on the LORD's anointed and be guiltless?"

Instead, David grabbed Saul's spear and water jug and walked away. From a safe distance, he shouted to Saul's general, "Look what I have! I could have killed your king!" Once again — mercy instead of murder.

Here's why this matters SO much. David understood something that most people miss: just because you CAN do something doesn't mean you SHOULD. He had the power to take revenge. He had the opportunity. His friends told him it was God's will! But David knew better. Revenge wasn't his to take.

I taught the same thing: "Love your enemies. Pray for those who persecute you." That's not weakness — that's the strongest thing a person can do. Letting God handle justice while you choose mercy? That takes MORE courage than swinging a sword.

Don't grab what isn't yours to take. Trust God with the outcome. See you next time!
