KING DAVID'S REIGN — EPISODE 4: DAVID'S BIG MISTAKE
Based on 2 Samuel 11-12 | Narrated by Jesus

Friends, today's story is hard but important. It teaches us something every person needs to know — even heroes fall. What matters most is what you do after.

It was springtime — the season when kings went to battle. But David stayed behind in Jerusalem. That was his first mistake. He was not where he was supposed to be.

One evening, David was walking on the roof of his palace when he saw a woman named Bathsheba. Instead of turning away, he let his eyes linger. One bad look led to a bad decision. David sent for her, and he did something terribly wrong — something that broke God's heart.

When David realized there would be consequences, he panicked. Instead of confessing, he tried to cover it up. He called Bathsheba's husband Uriah home from battle. But Uriah was so honorable he refused to enjoy comfort while his fellow soldiers fought. The cover-up failed.

So David did something even worse. He sent Uriah back with a sealed letter to Joab: put Uriah on the front lines and pull back so he gets killed. Uriah died in battle. David thought he had gotten away with it.

But God saw everything.

God sent the prophet Nathan. Nathan did not point a finger right away. He told a story: "There were two men. One was rich with huge flocks. The other was poor and had one little lamb he loved like a daughter — it ate from his plate, slept in his arms. The rich man had a guest, and instead of taking from his own flock, he stole the poor man's only lamb."

David was furious! "The man who did this deserves to die!"

Nathan looked him in the eyes and said four words that shook the kingdom: "You are that man."

David's heart shattered. No excuses. No blaming others. He said three words that changed everything: "I have sinned against the Lord."

Here is the lesson. David was a man after God's own heart — not because he was perfect, but because when he was caught, he owned it. He wrote Psalm 51, one of the most raw, honest prayers ever written.

There were real consequences. But God forgave him, because that is what God does when a heart truly repents.

You are going to mess up. The question is not if you fall — it is what you do when you hit the ground. Will you hide? Or say, "I was wrong. God, have mercy on me"?

That kind of honesty? That is the beginning of healing.
