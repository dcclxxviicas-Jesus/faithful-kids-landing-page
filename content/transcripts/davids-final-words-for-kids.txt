KING DAVID'S REIGN — EPISODE 10: DAVID'S FINAL WORDS (Series Finale)
Scripture: 1 Chronicles 28-29
Narrated by Jesus

Friends, we've reached the end. This is the final episode of King David's story — and I have to tell you, it's one of the most beautiful moments in the entire Bible.

David is old. His battles are behind him. His songs have been written. His mistakes have been confessed. And now, one last time, he gathers all of Israel together for a farewell that will echo through eternity.

David stands before them and shares his greatest dream: the temple. All his life, David wanted to build a permanent house for God. But God had told him, "You won't build it, David. You've been a man of war. Your son Solomon will build my house."

David accepted that. He didn't sulk or fight it. Instead, he spent years preparing everything Solomon would need — detailed blueprints that God Himself had given him. The dimensions of every room, the design of every lampstand, the weight of every golden bowl. David couldn't build the temple, but he made sure his son could.

Then David did something that moved the entire nation. He opened his personal treasury and donated his own gold and silver — thousands of talents. This wasn't tax money. This was David's personal fortune, given freely because he loved God. And when the people saw their king's generosity, they gave too — gold, silver, bronze, iron, precious stones. The giving was so joyful that the whole assembly was overwhelmed.

Then David prayed one of the most stunning prayers ever spoken: "Yours, O Lord, is the greatness and the power and the glory. Everything in heaven and earth is yours. Everything comes from you, and we have given you only what comes from your hand."

Did you catch that? David looked at all the gold and gifts and said, "God, none of this was really ours. We're just giving back what you gave us first." That's true humility.

Shortly after, David died — full of years, full of honor, and full of faith. His life wasn't perfect. He stumbled, he fell, he sinned grievously. But he always came back to God. Every single time.

And THAT is why Scripture calls David "a man after God's own heart." Not because he was flawless — but because he never stopped reaching for God.

Friends, a life well lived isn't a perfect life. It's a life that keeps coming back to God. That's the legacy of David. And it can be yours too.

Thank you for walking through this journey with me. I'm so proud of you. Now go — and live a life after God's own heart.
