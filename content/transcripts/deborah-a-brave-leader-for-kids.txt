Deborah is a prophet AND a judge — she's hearing directly from God AND leading the nation. People come from all over to sit under the palm tree where she holds court, seeking her wisdom. She is fierce, she is faithful, and she is fearless.

One day, Deborah summons a warrior named Barak and tells him, "God is commanding you to gather ten thousand men and march against Sisera's army. God will hand him over to you."

Now here's where it gets interesting. Barak looks at Deborah and says, "I'll go — but ONLY if you come with me."

Deborah doesn't flinch. She says, "I'll go with you. But because of this, the honor of defeating Sisera won't go to you — it'll go to a woman."

Barak probably thinks she means herself. He's wrong.

They march into battle. God throws Sisera's entire army into confusion. Chariots are useless. Soldiers are panicking. It's a total rout. Sisera, the fearsome commander, abandons his own chariot and runs for his life on foot.

He stumbles to the tent of a woman named Jael, thinking he's found a safe hiding place. Jael welcomes him in, gives him something to drink, covers him with a blanket. And when he falls asleep? She takes a tent peg and ends the threat of Sisera once and for all. One woman. One tent peg. Battle over.

Deborah and Barak sing a victory song that echoes through history. The land has peace for forty years.
