ELISHA: DOUBLE PORTION
Series: Elijah & Elisha | Episode 7
Scripture: 2 Kings 2:14-25, 4:1-7
Narrated by Jesus

Hey kids — last time, we watched Elijah get swept into heaven in a chariot of fire. But the story didn't end there. It was just getting started. Because now? It's Elisha's turn.

Elisha is standing at the edge of the Jordan River, holding Elijah's cloak. The big question is — did the power transfer? Is the double portion REAL?

Elisha rolls up the cloak, strikes the Jordan, and shouts: "Where now is the Lord, the God of Elijah?" BOOM — the water splits! Right down the middle! He walks across on dry ground. The double portion is alive and active in Elisha.

Prophets watching from a distance saw it and said, "The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha!" They bowed to the ground. A new era had begun.

Elisha wasted no time. The people of Jericho said, "Our water is BAD. It's poisoning the land." Elisha threw salt into the spring and declared, "The Lord has healed this water!" From that day on — pure. Fresh. Life-giving.

But here's my FAVORITE Elisha story from this chapter. A widow came to him, crying her eyes out. She said, "My husband — one of your fellow prophets — has died. And now the creditors are coming to take my two sons as SLAVES to pay off our debts!"

Elisha looked at her and asked one simple question: "What do you have in your house?"

She said, "Nothing. Just a small jar of oil."

That's it. That's all she had. A tiny jar.

But Elisha said, "Go. Borrow empty jars from every neighbor you can find. Don't just get a few — get A LOT. Then go inside, shut the door, and start pouring."

She obeyed. She poured from that tiny jar into the first empty vessel — and the oil KEPT FLOWING. She filled the second jar. The third. The tenth. The twentieth. Jar after jar after jar. The oil did not stop until every single container was full.

She sold the oil, paid off ALL her debts, and had enough left over for her family to live on.

Here's the lesson: God works with what you ALREADY have. You might think your talent is too small, your resources are too little, your gifts are nothing special. But bring your "small jar" to God — and watch Him multiply it beyond anything you imagined. He just needs you to pour.
