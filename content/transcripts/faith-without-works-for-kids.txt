TITLE: Faith Without Works
SERIES: The Ending & the Beginning
EPISODE: 2
SCRIPTURE: James 2:14-26

My brother James didn't hold back. He never did. And when he wrote his letter to the early church, he said something that still cuts right to the heart.

"What good is it if someone claims to have faith but has no deeds? Can such faith save them?"

Then he painted a picture. Imagine a brother or sister comes to you — no coat, no food, shivering and hungry. And you look at them and say, "Go in peace! Stay warm and well fed!" But you don't lift a finger to help. You don't give them a jacket. You don't share your meal. You just... talk.

James says: what's the point of that? Words without action are worthless. And faith without works is dead. Not dying — dead. Like a body without a spirit.

Now, I need you to understand something important. James was not saying you earn your way to God. Salvation is a gift. You can't buy it, work for it, or deserve it. But here's the thing — real faith changes you. If you truly believe, it shows. Not because you're trying to impress anyone, but because faith that's alive naturally moves your hands and your feet.

Think about Abraham. God told him to offer his son Isaac on the altar. That was the hardest thing anyone could ever be asked to do. But Abraham obeyed — because his faith was real. His belief wasn't just in his head. It moved him to action. And God counted that as righteousness.

And Rahab — remember her? She wasn't just a woman who believed the spies' God was real. She hid them. She risked her life. Her faith made her do something.

James put it plainly: "As the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without deeds is dead."

So let me ask you something. What does your faith look like on the outside? Not just what you say you believe — but what you do because you believe it. Do you share when someone has nothing? Do you stand up for the kid everyone ignores? Do you forgive when it's hard? Do you serve when it's inconvenient?

Real faith isn't just something you think. It's something you live. It moves your feet, not just your mouth. If you believe it — really believe it — you'll live like it.

That's not a burden. That's faith coming alive.
