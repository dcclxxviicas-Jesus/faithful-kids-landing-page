Hey kids! Grab a seat — and maybe a snack — because today's story involves the biggest picnic you've EVER heard of.

I had been teaching and healing people all day. A MASSIVE crowd had followed me to a remote hillside near the Sea of Galilee. Over five thousand men — not even counting women and children. Could've been twenty thousand people total.

It was getting late. And all these people? They were HUNGRY. No food trucks. No restaurants. Nothing but grass and hills for miles.

I turned to Philip and asked — already knowing what I was going to do — "Philip, where can we buy bread for all these people?"

Philip's eyes went WIDE. He did the math in his head and said, "It would take more than EIGHT MONTHS' wages to buy enough bread — and even THAT wouldn't give everyone a single bite!"

Then Andrew — Peter's brother — walked up with a kid. A young boy. And this boy had his little lunch: five small barley loaves and two fish. Andrew looked at me and said, "Here's a boy with some food... but what good is THAT with this many people?"

What good is that? Oh, Andrew. Watch and learn.

I told everyone to sit down on the grass. Then I took those five loaves and two fish, looked up to heaven, and gave THANKS. Not for a feast — for a kid's lunch. Then I started breaking the bread and handing it out.

And it just kept COMING.

Piece after piece after piece. Fish after fish. My disciples were distributing food to THOUSANDS of people, and it never ran out. Every single person ate until they were FULL. Not just a taste — STUFFED.

And when everyone was done? I told my disciples, "Gather up the leftovers. Don't waste anything." They collected TWELVE baskets full of leftover bread. TWELVE. They started with five loaves and ended with twelve baskets of extras.

Here's what blows my mind — it all started with one kid. One boy who was willing to hand over his tiny lunch. He didn't say, "This isn't enough." He didn't hold it back. He gave what he had.

And THAT is my message for you: bring what you have to me. Your talent, your time, your kindness — even if it seems too small. I'm in the multiplication business. You bring the little. I'll make it MORE than enough.
