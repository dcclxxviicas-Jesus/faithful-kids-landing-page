Hey friend! Welcome back. Today we meet someone who changes everything — a man named Abram, who God would later rename Abraham. And this story? It's about the moment God asked someone to trust Him with absolutely zero details.

Abram was living in a place called Haran with his wife Sarai and his nephew Lot. He had a good life — comfortable, settled, safe. And then, completely out of nowhere, God spoke to him.

"Leave your country. Leave your people. Leave your father's household. Go to the land I will show you."

Wait — did you catch that? God didn't say WHERE. He didn't hand Abram a map. He didn't say, "Head north for 200 miles and take a left." He just said "Go. Trust me. I'll show you when you get there."

That is wild. Imagine your parents saying, "Pack your bags, we're moving." And you ask, "Where?" And they say, "We'll find out when we arrive." That takes serious trust.

But God also made Abram an incredible promise — one of the biggest promises in the entire Bible: "I will make you into a great nation. I will bless you and make your name great. And through you, ALL the peoples of the earth will be blessed."

All the peoples of the earth. That promise is huge. And spoiler alert — I'm the fulfillment of that promise. But we'll get there.

So what did Abram do? He packed up everything — his wife, his nephew, his servants, his animals, all of it — and left. He was seventy-five years old. Seventy-five! Most people are thinking about slowing down at that age. Abram was starting the biggest adventure of his life.

God led him to the land of Canaan. And when they arrived, God said, "This is it. This land is for your descendants." Abram built an altar right there and worshipped.

But here's the thing — there was a problem. A big one. Abram and Sarai had no children. They were already old. Way past the age of having kids. So how was God going to make him into a great nation with no son? It seemed impossible.

One night, God took Abram outside and said, "Look up. Count the stars — if you can." And on a clear night in the ancient world, with no city lights? There were millions. God said, "That's how many descendants you will have."

And here's the line that matters most: "Abram believed the Lord, and it was credited to him as righteousness."

He didn't have proof. He didn't have a child. He had nothing but a promise from God. And he believed it. And THAT faith — that trust — was enough.

Here's what I want you to take with you: faith doesn't mean you have all the answers. It means you trust the One who does. You might not see the full picture yet, but if God is leading, you're headed the right way.
