Let me tell you about a day that didn't stop. From sunrise to way past sunset — it was a day that showed exactly why I came.

It started at Simon Peter's house. We walked in after teaching at the synagogue, and Peter's mother-in-law was lying down with a burning fever. She was miserable. I walked over, took her hand, and lifted her up. Just like that — the fever LEFT. Gone. Instantly. And you know what she did? She didn't go lie back down. She got up and started serving everyone dinner! That's the kind of woman she was.

But here's where the day got CRAZY.

Word spread FAST. By sunset, it felt like the entire town showed up at the door. I'm not exaggerating — people were carrying the sick, leading the blind, supporting those who couldn't walk. The whole city was gathered outside that little house. And I healed them. One by one. Every disease. Every sickness. Every demon that had been tormenting them. I didn't turn a single person away.

But there's one moment I'll never forget.

A man with leprosy came to me. Now, you need to understand — lepers were completely isolated. Nobody came near them. Nobody touched them. They had to shout "Unclean! Unclean!" so people would stay away. They lived in total loneliness. It wasn't just a disease — it was a prison.

This man fell on his knees in front of me and said something that broke my heart and filled it at the same time: "If you are WILLING, you can make me clean."

Did you hear that? He didn't doubt my power. He knew I COULD heal him. His question was — would I WANT to?

I reached out my hand and TOUCHED him. Nobody had touched this man in who knows how long. And I said, "I AM willing. Be clean!"

Immediately — right there — the leprosy vanished. His skin was restored. He was whole again.

Here's what I need you to understand: I don't just have the POWER to help you. I WANT to. That's the part people miss. They wonder if God cares enough, if He's paying attention, if He actually wants to get involved in their problems. Let me settle that right now — I am WILLING. Whatever you're carrying, whatever you're struggling with, whatever feels broken — bring it to me. I am not just able. I am willing. Always.
