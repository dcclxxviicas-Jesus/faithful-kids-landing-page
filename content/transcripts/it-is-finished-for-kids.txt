At noon, the sky went black.

Not a cloud. Not an eclipse. The sun itself refused to shine. For three hours, darkness covered the entire land — from noon to three in the afternoon. People who had been mocking me went silent. The soldiers shifted nervously. Something was happening that no one could explain.

During those three hours, something heavier than the nails pressed down on me. The weight of every sin — every lie ever told, every act of cruelty, every broken promise, every hateful word from the beginning of time to the end of it — all of it was placed on me. Not my sin. Yours. The world's. All of it.

And for the first and only time in all of eternity, I felt something I had never felt before: separation from my Father.

I cried out in agony — "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"

That wasn't a question of doubt. It was a cry of real pain. My Father and I had been together forever — before the stars, before time, before anything existed. And in that moment, He turned His face away. Because He was looking at the sin on me, and His holiness couldn't look at it. That separation — that was the true cost. That was the real punishment. It was worse than the nails.

I said, "I am thirsty." They lifted a sponge of sour wine to my lips.

And then, with everything I had left, I said three words that changed the universe:

"It is finished."

Not "I am finished." Not "I give up." Not "It's over." The word I used was TETELESTAI — and it means "paid in full." The debt is settled. The work is complete. There is nothing left to add.

I bowed my head and gave up my spirit. Nobody took my life from me. I laid it down.

At that exact moment, the massive curtain inside the temple — the one that separated people from God's presence, sixty feet tall, thick as a man's hand — tore in two. Not from the bottom up, like a person would rip it. From the TOP down. God Himself ripped it open. The wall between you and God? Gone.

The earth shook. Rocks split apart. A Roman centurion — a hardened soldier who had watched hundreds of executions — stood at the foot of my cross, trembling, and said, "Surely this was the Son of God."

Here's what I need you to carry with you: when I said "It is finished," I meant it. Salvation is not something you have to earn. It's not something you have to complete. I completed it. Your debt is paid. The way to God is open.

All you have to do is walk through.
