EXILE & FAITH — EPISODE 8: JONAH — RUNNING FROM GOD
Based on Jonah 1–2 | Narrated by Jesus

Buckle up — because this story is wild. We've been talking about heroes who stood firm, who refused to bow, who prayed with windows open. But this next guy? He heard God's voice loud and clear — and SPRINTED the other way.

His name is Jonah. A prophet.

God gives him a mission: "Go to Nineveh and preach against it, because their wickedness has come before Me."

Nineveh was the capital of Assyria — the people who destroyed Northern Israel. Brutal, violent, terrifying. Israel's worst nightmare. And God wants Jonah to go HELP them? Give them a chance to repent?

Jonah's response? Nope.

He doesn't just say no — he runs. He finds a ship heading to Tarshish — the farthest point in the opposite direction — pays the fare, and goes below deck to sleep. He's literally trying to sail to the edge of the known world to escape God.

Spoiler alert: you can't outrun God.

God hurls a MASSIVE storm onto the sea. The ship is about to break apart. Waves crashing everywhere. Sailors are screaming, praying to every god they know. And Jonah? Fast asleep below deck. The captain shakes him awake: "How can you SLEEP? Get up and pray!"

The sailors cast lots to find out who caused this, and the lot falls on Jonah. He confesses: "I'm running from the Lord, the God who made the sea and the dry land." They're TERRIFIED. "What do we do?"

Jonah tells them: "Throw me into the sea, and it will calm down. This is my fault."

They try rowing harder first — but the storm only gets worse. Finally, they throw Jonah overboard. Instantly, the sea goes calm.

But Jonah doesn't drown. God sends a HUGE fish — and it swallows him whole. Three days and three nights in the belly of that fish. In the dark. In the muck. And from that impossible place, Jonah prays one of the most desperate prayers in the Bible: "I called out to the Lord, and He answered me. From the depths I cried for help, and You listened."

Then the fish vomits Jonah onto dry land.

Now here's something personal. I spent three days in a tomb — just like Jonah spent three days in that fish. That's not a coincidence. Jonah's story was pointing to Mine all along.

Here's your lesson — you can run from God, but you can't hide. And honestly? He's not chasing you to punish you. He's chasing you because He loves you too much to let you go.
