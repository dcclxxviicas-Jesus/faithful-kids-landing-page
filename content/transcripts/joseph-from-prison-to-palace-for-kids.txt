Welcome back for the grand finale of our Genesis series! Last time, we left Joseph sitting in an Egyptian prison — betrayed by his brothers, falsely accused, and forgotten. But God was about to do something nobody saw coming.

One night, Pharaoh — the most powerful person in the world — had two dreams that shook him to his core. In the first dream, seven fat, healthy cows came out of the Nile River. Then seven skinny, ugly cows came out and swallowed the fat ones whole. In the second dream, seven full heads of grain were swallowed up by seven thin, dried-out heads of grain.

Pharaoh woke up in a panic. He called every wise man and magician in Egypt, but nobody could explain the dreams. Nobody.

That's when the cupbearer — a man Joseph had helped in prison two full years earlier — finally remembered him. "Oh! There's this guy in prison who interpreted my dream perfectly..."

Joseph was pulled out of the dungeon, cleaned up, and brought before Pharaoh. And the first thing Joseph said? "I can't interpret dreams — but God can, and He will give you the answer." Right there, in his biggest moment, Joseph gave all the credit to God. That's character.

God revealed the meaning through Joseph: seven years of incredible abundance were coming to Egypt — harvests like they'd never seen. But after that? Seven years of devastating famine. Crops would fail. People would starve. Unless they prepared.

Joseph told Pharaoh, "Appoint someone to collect and store grain during the good years so Egypt can survive the bad years." Pharaoh looked at Joseph and said, "Since God has shown all this to you, there is no one wiser. YOU will be in charge."

Just like that — in a single day — Joseph went from prisoner to the second most powerful person in all of Egypt. From the dungeon to the palace. Only God can write a story like that.

The seven good years came, and Joseph stored massive amounts of grain. Then the famine hit — exactly as God said. It spread across the entire region, including Canaan, where Joseph's family lived. Jacob sent his sons to Egypt to buy grain.

And who was standing there when they arrived? The brother they had thrown in a pit and sold as a slave twenty years earlier. Joseph recognized them instantly. They had absolutely no idea who he was.

Joseph tested them. He wanted to see if they had changed. And they had. When Joseph put his youngest brother Benjamin in danger, the brothers stepped up to protect him. They offered themselves instead. They were different people.

Joseph couldn't hold it together anymore. He sent everyone else out of the room, broke down sobbing, and said, "I am Joseph! Is my father still alive?"

His brothers were frozen. Terrified. They thought, "This is it. He's going to destroy us."

But Joseph said something that echoes through all of history: "Don't be afraid. You intended to harm me — but God intended it for good, to accomplish what is now being done: the saving of many lives."

Read that again. "You intended to harm me, but God intended it for good."

Joseph forgave them. Completely. He brought his entire family to Egypt where they were safe, fed, and taken care of. And Jacob — the old father who thought his son was dead for twenty years — got to hold Joseph again.

And THAT, my friend, is the book of Genesis. From the creation of the world to a family reunited by forgiveness. Through every twist, every betrayal, every impossible situation — God was working.

Here's what I want you to carry with you: no matter what you're going through right now, God has a plan. What others mean for harm, He can turn for good. Your hardest chapter might be setting up your greatest purpose. Don't give up.

Thank you for walking through Genesis with me. It's been an honor to share these stories with you. This is just the beginning — there's so much more to come. I'll see you in the next series.

Until then — remember who you are. You are loved. You are known. And your story matters.
