I want to tell you about a letter that should have been the saddest letter ever written — but turned out to be the most joyful one in the entire Bible.

Paul — my apostle, the man who traveled the world telling people about me — was in prison. Not a nice, clean jail with a bed and a window. A Roman dungeon. Cold stone. Chains on his wrists. Guards day and night. He didn't know if he'd ever get out.

And from that dark, cramped cell, Paul picked up a pen and wrote a letter to the church in Philippi. And do you know what the theme of that letter was? Joy.

Not "I hope I get out of here." Not "This is the worst thing ever." Joy. The word "joy" or "rejoice" shows up more than a dozen times in this short letter.

Paul wrote: "Rejoice in the Lord always. I will say it again: rejoice!"

Always? Even in prison? Even when life is unfair? Even when things aren't going the way you planned?

Yes. Always.

Paul understood something most people miss. Joy and happiness are not the same thing. Happiness depends on what's happening around you. Good day? You're happy. Bad day? Miserable. It comes and goes like the weather.

But joy? Joy is an anchor. It doesn't depend on your circumstances. It depends on who you know. And Paul knew me. He knew I was with him in that cell. He knew my plan was bigger than his problems.

Paul also wrote this: "I have learned the secret of being content in every situation — whether well fed or hungry, whether living in plenty or in want."

You might know the next line. "I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me." Now here's something important — that verse doesn't mean you'll win every game, ace every test, or never struggle. It means that no matter what situation you're in — even the hard ones, even the painful ones — my strength is enough to carry you through it.

It's not "I can do all the fun things through Christ." It's "I can do all things" — including enduring hardship, facing loss, and keeping going when you feel like giving up.

So here's the lesson. Joy isn't something you find when everything is perfect. Joy is something you carry with you because I'm with you. And I am always with you — in the good days, the hard days, and everything in between.

Rejoice. I really do mean always.
