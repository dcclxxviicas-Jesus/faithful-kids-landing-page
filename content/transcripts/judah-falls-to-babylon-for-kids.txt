EXILE & FAITH — EPISODE 1: JUDAH FALLS TO BABYLON
Based on 2 Kings 24–25 | Narrated by Jesus

Hey — I need to tell you about one of the hardest days in the history of My people. And I'm not going to sugarcoat it, because you're old enough to understand. This one hurts. But stay with Me — because even in the darkest chapter, I was already writing the comeback.

Remember the Northern Kingdom — Israel? They fell to Assyria because they turned their backs on God. Well, the Southern Kingdom — Judah — watched that happen. They SAW it. And for a while, they tried to do better. But eventually... they didn't.

King after king after king ignored God's warnings. The prophets shouted, pleaded, even wept — but Judah wouldn't listen. So God allowed the consequences to come.

Enter Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon. The most powerful ruler on earth at the time. His army surrounded Jerusalem like a fist closing around a grape. They sieged the city until people were starving inside the walls. And then — they broke through.

Now here's the part that absolutely shattered everyone's heart. Solomon's Temple — the most magnificent building ever constructed — the place where God's presence dwelled, covered in gold, filled with sacred treasures — Nebuchadnezzar's soldiers tore it apart. They stripped the gold. They took the sacred vessels. The bronze pillars, the golden lampstands, the bowls, the altars — loaded onto carts and hauled to Babylon.

And the Ark of the Covenant? The very symbol of God's promise? It disappeared. To this day, no one knows exactly where it went.

Then they marched God's people out of their homeland in chains. Families torn apart. Children crying. Elders stumbling down dusty roads, looking back at smoke rising from the only home they'd ever known.

It looked like the END. Like God had given up. Like the story was over.

But listen to Me carefully — the story was NOT over. Not even close. Because even when everything falls apart, God doesn't lose track of His people. He was about to do something nobody expected — raise up heroes in the middle of enemy territory.

The worst chapter was setting up the greatest comeback you've ever seen.

And I should know — because comebacks? That's kind of My thing.
