Alright, buckle up because things are about to get INTENSE.

So Moses finally says yes to God. He packs up, heads back to Egypt, meets up with his brother Aaron, and together they march right into Pharaoh's palace. Picture this -- two guys who grew up as nobodies walking into the most powerful throne room on Earth and saying five words that would change history:

"Let my people go."

Can you feel the tension? The whole court goes silent. Pharaoh looks at them like they've lost their minds. And his response? Basically: "Who is this God of yours? I don't know Him, and I'm definitely NOT letting your people go."

But Pharaoh doesn't stop there. Oh no. He decides to PUNISH the Israelites for even asking. He tells the slave drivers: "Stop giving them straw to make bricks. Make them find their own straw, but they still have to make the SAME number of bricks." That's like your teacher doubling your homework and taking away your pencils. Impossible!

The Israelites are furious -- and not at Pharaoh. They're mad at MOSES. "Thanks a lot, Moses! You made everything WORSE!" And honestly? Moses is feeling it. He goes to God and says, "Why did you send me? Ever since I talked to Pharaoh, things have only gotten worse for your people!"

Have you ever tried to do the right thing and it totally backfired? You stood up for someone and got in trouble? You told the truth and people got mad? Yeah. Moses is living that right now.

But God doesn't panic. God says, "Now you'll see what I'm going to do to Pharaoh. I am the LORD. I made promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and I KEEP my promises. I will free my people with mighty acts of judgment."

Then comes the showdown. Moses and Aaron go back to Pharaoh, and Aaron throws down his staff. BOOM -- it turns into a snake! Pretty impressive, right? But Pharaoh's magicians do the same trick with their staffs. Snakes everywhere! Pharaoh smirks like, "See? Your God's nothing special."

But then -- Aaron's snake SWALLOWS all the other snakes. Gulp. Gone. Every single one.

Did that shake Pharaoh? Nope. The Bible says his heart was hardened. He refused to listen.

And that's the thing about stubbornness. When you see something true and STILL refuse to accept it, your heart gets a little harder each time. It's like putting on armor against the truth.

Here's what I need you to remember: doing the right thing doesn't always feel good right away. Sometimes it gets harder before it gets better. But God NEVER abandons His plan. And if He's asked you to do something, He'll see it through -- even when Pharaoh says no.

Especially when Pharaoh says no.

See you next time -- things are about to get REALLY wild!
