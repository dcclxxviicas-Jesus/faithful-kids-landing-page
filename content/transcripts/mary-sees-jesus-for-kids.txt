After everything that happened at the tomb, most people had run off — to tell the news, to figure out what was going on. But one person stayed. Mary Magdalene. And she was crying.

See, Mary had been one of my closest followers. I had set her free from terrible darkness — seven demons had tormented her, and I cast every one of them out. She had been with me through my ministry, through the cross, through it all. And now she stood outside the empty tomb, heartbroken, because she thought someone had stolen my body.

She bent down and looked inside the tomb. Two angels in white were sitting there — one where my head had been, one where my feet had been. They asked her, "Woman, why are you crying?"

She said, "They have taken my Lord away, and I do not know where they have put Him."

Then she turned around. And I was standing right there. Right in front of her. But she did not recognize me — not yet. Her eyes were blurred with tears. Her heart was heavy with grief.

I asked her, "Woman, why are you crying? Who is it you are looking for?"

She thought I was the gardener. She said, "Sir, if you have carried Him away, tell me where you have put Him, and I will get Him."

And then I said one word. Just one. Her name.

"Mary."

That was all it took. She KNEW my voice instantly. Her eyes flew open. "Rabboni!" she cried — that means "Teacher!" in her language. She must have grabbed onto me because I told her, "Do not hold on to me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father. But go to my brothers and tell them."

And she did. She ran to the disciples and declared: "I have seen the Lord!"

Now here is something beautiful I want you to notice. The first person I chose to appear to after rising from the dead was a woman — and in that culture, a woman's testimony was not even accepted in court. The world said her voice did not count. I said her voice would be the first to announce the greatest event in history.

I spoke her name and she knew me. And I want you to hear this: I know YOUR name too. I see you. I know you. You are not lost in the crowd to me. You never have been.

LESSON: Jesus knows you by name — personally, individually. You are never invisible to Him. He sees you, He knows you, and He calls you by name.
