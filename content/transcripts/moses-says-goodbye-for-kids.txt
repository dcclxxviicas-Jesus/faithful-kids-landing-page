Hey. Come sit with Me for this one. This story is different from the others. It's not about talking donkeys or giant grapes or snakes. It's about a man finishing his race. And honestly? It might be the most powerful episode yet.

Moses is a hundred and twenty years old. Let that sink in. One hundred and twenty. His eyes aren't dim. His strength hasn't left him. But he knows — he knows — that his time is almost up. He won't be crossing into the Promised Land. That consequence from striking the rock? It's still in effect. And Moses doesn't argue. He doesn't throw a tantrum. He accepts it with grace.

So what does Moses do with his final days? He gathers all of Israel together and gives them one last speech. And oh, what a speech it is.

He looks out at these people — many of them born in the wilderness, kids who grew up watching manna appear every morning — and he reminds them of everything. Everything God did in Egypt. Every miracle in the desert. Every time they failed, and every time God showed up anyway. He says, "Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid. For the Lord your God goes with you. He will never leave you or forsake you."

Can you hear the weight in those words? This man has walked with God for decades. He's seen the burning bush. He's stood before Pharaoh. He split a sea. And his final message to the next generation is: don't forget who God is, and don't be afraid.

Then Moses turns to Joshua — the same Joshua who was one of the two faithful spies — and passes the torch. "Be strong and courageous, Joshua. You will lead these people into the land God promised. And He will be with you."

After that, God calls Moses up Mount Nebo. Moses climbs that mountain, and from the top, God shows him the entire Promised Land. Jericho, the Jordan Valley, the hills stretching to the sea. Moses sees it all. Every inch of the land his people will inherit.

But he won't set foot in it.

Moses dies there, on that mountain. And here's the detail that wrecks me every time — God Himself buried Moses. No funeral home. No ceremony. The Creator of the universe personally laid His servant to rest. No one knows where that grave is to this day.

The Bible says there was never another prophet in Israel like Moses, who knew God face to face.

Here's what I want you to carry with you. Moses didn't get everything he hoped for. He never walked into that Promised Land. But he finished well. He was faithful to the very end. He poured into the next generation. He trusted God even when the answer was "not for you — but for them."

That's legacy. That's what it looks like to finish your race well.

You don't have to be perfect. Moses wasn't. But stay faithful. Keep going. Pour into the people around you. And trust Me — even when the story doesn't end the way you expected.

Your story isn't over yet. And I'm writing every chapter.
