PAUL'S ADVENTURES — EPISODE 2: PAUL AND BARNABAS SPLIT
Based on Acts 15:36-41
Series: Paul's Adventures | Episode 2 of 10
Target audience: Kids ages 7-15

Okay, this episode might surprise you. Because it's not about a miracle or a dramatic escape. It's about a fight. A real, heated argument — between two of the greatest missionaries who ever lived.

Paul and Barnabas had just finished their first incredible journey together. They'd faced sorcerers, angry mobs, and stoning. They were a legendary team. So naturally, Paul says, "Let's go back and visit every city where we preached. Let's see how the believers are doing."

Great idea. Barnabas is in. But then Barnabas says, "Let's bring John Mark with us."

And that's where it all falls apart.

See, John Mark had started the first journey with them — but halfway through, he quit. Packed up and went home. Maybe he was scared. Maybe the danger was too much. But he bailed when they needed him.

Paul says, "No way. He deserted us. He's not coming."

Barnabas pushes back. His name literally means "Son of Encouragement." That's who he is — he sees the potential in people. He's the one who vouched for Paul when everyone else was afraid of him. And now he wants to give John Mark a second chance.

But Paul won't budge. The Bible says their disagreement was so sharp, so intense, that they split up. These two men who had been through everything together — they go their separate ways.

Barnabas takes John Mark and sails to Cyprus. Paul chooses a new partner — Silas — and heads to Syria and Cilicia.

Now here's what I love about this story. Most people hear it and think, "That's terrible. They should have worked it out." And yeah — disagreements aren't fun. But look at what happened. Instead of one team going one direction, now there are TWO teams going in TWO directions. The mission didn't shrink — it DOUBLED.

And John Mark? That young man who quit? He grew up. He matured. He became so faithful that years later, Paul himself — the same Paul who refused to take him — wrote these words: "Get Mark and bring him with you, because he is useful to me in my ministry."

And oh — John Mark went on to write the Gospel of Mark. One of the four Gospels. The story of my life, written by the guy who once quit.

Here's the lesson: disagreements happen — even between great people of faith. But I can use conflict to multiply the mission. And I never give up on quitters. If you've ever walked away from something and felt like a failure — this story is for you. Get back up. Your best chapter hasn't been written yet.
