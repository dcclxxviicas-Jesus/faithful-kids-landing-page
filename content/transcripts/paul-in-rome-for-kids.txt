PAUL'S ADVENTURES — EPISODE 9: PAUL IN ROME
Based on Acts 28
Series: Paul's Adventures | Episode 9 of 10
Target audience: Kids ages 7-15

After everything — the shipwreck, the snake bite, fourteen days of hurricane — Paul finally made it to Rome. The capital of the entire known world. The city he'd been dreaming about reaching for years.

But here's the thing. He didn't arrive as a conquering hero. He arrived in chains. A prisoner. Escorted by Roman soldiers. Most people would look at that and say, "Well, that's a failure." But most people would be dead wrong.

When Paul got to Rome, something unexpected happened. The Roman authorities actually gave him special treatment. Instead of throwing him in a dungeon, they let him rent his own house. He had a soldier guarding him — chained to him, actually — but he could receive visitors. He could talk to anyone who showed up at his door.

And people showed up.

First, Paul invited the Jewish leaders in Rome to come hear his story. Some believed. Some didn't. But Paul wasn't picky. He welcomed everyone. For two full years, Paul sat in that rented house with a chain on his wrist, and he preached the Kingdom of God to every single person who walked through the door. Boldly. Without hindrance. That's how the book of Acts actually ends — with those words. "Boldly and without hindrance."

But wait — it gets even better. Remember those soldiers chained to him? They rotated shifts. Which means a new soldier was attached to Paul every few hours. They couldn't leave! They had to sit there and listen to him talk about me. Paul basically had a captive audience — literally! The gospel was spreading through the entire Roman imperial guard. The soldiers would go back to the barracks and tell the others what Paul said.

And here's what blows my mind about Paul. While chained to those soldiers, he wrote letters. Not just any letters. Some of the most powerful words ever put on paper. He wrote Ephesians — about the incredible riches of God's grace. Philippians — the letter of joy, written from prison. Colossians — about my supremacy over all things. And Philemon — a personal letter about forgiveness and second chances.

These letters? They've been read by billions of people. They changed theology, philosophy, and history itself. All written from a prison cell.

Here's what I want branded on your heart: your circumstances don't determine your impact. Paul was in chains, and he changed the world. Don't you dare let your limitations become your excuses. Where you are right now — that's your platform. Use it.
