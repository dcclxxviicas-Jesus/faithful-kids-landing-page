PAUL'S ADVENTURES — EPISODE 1: PAUL'S FIRST JOURNEY
Based on Acts 13-14
Series: Paul's Adventures | Episode 1 of 10
Target audience: Kids ages 7-15

Alright — it's adventure time. And I mean REAL adventure. Dangerous roads, angry mobs, false gods, and a man who just will not quit. Let's talk about Paul's very first missionary journey.

It starts in Antioch. The church is praying and fasting when the Holy Spirit speaks: "Set apart Barnabas and Saul for the work I've called them to." The church lays hands on them and sends them out. No GPS. No bodyguards. Just faith and a mission — go tell the world about me.

First stop — the island of Cyprus. They're preaching in synagogues when they run into trouble. A sorcerer named Bar-Jesus tries to turn the governor AWAY from the faith — whispering lies, twisting the truth, doing everything he can to block the message.

Paul looks him straight in the eye and says, "You son of the devil — will you never stop perverting the straight ways of the Lord?" And just like that — the sorcerer goes blind. The governor sees it happen, and he believes. Don't mess with my messengers.

They sail to Asia Minor — modern-day Turkey — preaching from town to town. In Lystra, Paul sees a man lame since birth, looks at him, and says, "Stand up on your feet!" The man JUMPS up and starts walking.

The crowd goes absolutely wild. But here's the problem — they think Paul and Barnabas are GODS. They start calling Barnabas "Zeus" and Paul "Hermes." A priest even brings bulls and wreaths to sacrifice to them!

Paul and Barnabas tear their clothes and rush into the crowd shouting, "What are you doing?! We are just humans like you! We're here to tell you about the LIVING God — the one who made the heavens, the earth, the sea, and everything in them!"

They barely stop the crowd from sacrificing to them. Barely.

But then — the mood shifts. Enemies from other cities show up and turn the people against Paul. The same crowd that wanted to worship him? They pick up stones and HURL them at him. They drag his body outside the city and leave him for dead.

The disciples gather around him, heartbroken. And then — Paul gets up. He stands to his feet, walks back INTO the city, and the very next day, he travels to the next town to keep preaching.

That is what unstoppable faith looks like. Following me is an adventure — exciting, dangerous, and absolutely worth it. Paul knew it. Barnabas knew it. And I want YOU to know it too.

The journey's just beginning.
