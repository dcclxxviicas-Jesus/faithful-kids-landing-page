THE EARLY CHURCH — EPISODE 7: PETER AND CORNELIUS
Based on Acts 10
Series: The Early Church | Episode 7 of 10
Target audience: Kids ages 7-15

Alright — this episode changes EVERYTHING. And I mean everything.

Up to this point, my followers were almost entirely Jewish. Most of them assumed my message was just for Jewish people.

They were about to find out how wrong they were.

There was a Roman centurion named Cornelius — a military officer commanding a hundred soldiers. He was a Gentile — not Jewish. But Cornelius loved God, prayed constantly, and gave generously to people in need.

One afternoon, an angel appeared to Cornelius and said, "God has heard your prayers. Send men to the city of Joppa and bring back a man named Simon Peter."

Cornelius sent his men immediately.

Now — here's where it gets really interesting.

While those men were traveling, Peter went up on a rooftop to pray. He fell into a trance and saw heaven open — a huge sheet being lowered down, filled with animals that Jewish law said were "unclean," forbidden to eat. Pigs. Reptiles. Strange birds.

Then a voice said: "Get up, Peter. Kill and eat."

Peter was horrified. "No way, Lord! I have never eaten anything impure or unclean!"

The voice came back: "Do not call anything impure that God has made clean."

This happened THREE times. Peter was completely confused. What did it mean?

Right then — knock, knock — Cornelius's men arrived at the door. The Holy Spirit told Peter, "Three men are looking for you. Go with them. Don't hesitate."

So Peter went to Cornelius's house — a Jewish man walking into a Gentile's home. Unheard of. But Peter stood in front of that room and said, "I now realize that God does not show favoritism. He accepts people from every nation who fear Him and do what is right."

Peter started preaching — and while he was STILL TALKING — the Holy Spirit fell on every Gentile in that room. Same power. Same gifts. Same Spirit that fell at Pentecost. The Jewish believers were absolutely stunned.

Peter said, "Can anyone keep these people from being baptized? They have received the Holy Spirit just as we have!" And he baptized them all.

Here's what I need you to know: God's love crosses every barrier. Every race. Every background. Every culture. No one is excluded from my family. If God has made someone clean — don't you dare call them unworthy.

Everyone belongs.
