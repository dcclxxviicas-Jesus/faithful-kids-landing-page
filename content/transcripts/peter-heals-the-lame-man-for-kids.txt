THE EARLY CHURCH — EPISODE 2: PETER HEALS THE LAME MAN
Based on Acts 3:1-16
Series: The Early Church | Episode 2 of 5
Target audience: Kids ages 7-15

After I went back to my Father, the apostles got to work. And one afternoon, something happened that changed everything.

Peter and John were heading to the temple for afternoon prayer. Just a normal day. But on the way in, right by the gate called Beautiful — great name, by the way — there was a man sitting on the ground. He'd been lame since the day he was born. Never taken a single step. Every day, his friends carried him to that same spot so he could beg for money. That was his life.

He saw Peter and John, held out his hand, and asked for money. Just another coin. Just another day.

But Peter stopped. Looked right at him. And said something the man did not expect.

"Look at us."

The man looked up, thinking he was about to get some cash. Instead, Peter said six words that changed his life forever:

"Silver or gold I do not have."

Imagine the man's face. Okay... so why are you talking to me?

"But what I do have, I give you."

And then Peter said it.

"In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth — WALK."

Peter reached down, grabbed the man's right hand, and pulled him up. And the second — the SECOND — his feet touched the ground, strength poured into his ankles and feet. Bones that had never worked suddenly worked. Muscles that had never fired suddenly fired.

The man didn't just stand up. He jumped. He LEAPED. He started walking and leaping and praising God — right there in the temple! This man who had never taken a step was now jumping around like a kid on Christmas morning.

People freaked out. They recognized him — "Wait, isn't that the beggar from the gate?!" They were completely astonished.

And Peter — my man Peter — looked at the stunned crowd and said: "Why are you staring at us like we did this? It wasn't our power. It wasn't our godliness. It was the name of Jesus. He's the one who made this man strong."

Peter didn't have money. He didn't have status or political power. But he had something better — my name. And that was more than enough.

You might feel like you don't have much to offer. But if you have me? You have something money can't buy.
