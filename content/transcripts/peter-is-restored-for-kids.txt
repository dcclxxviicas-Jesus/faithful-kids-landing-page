Let me tell you about a conversation that changed Peter's life — and it happened right after breakfast.

Remember Peter? The one who said he would NEVER deny me? The one who swore he would die for me? Well, the night I was arrested, Peter denied me three times. Three times someone asked if he knew me. Three times he said no. And after that third denial, a rooster crowed — and Peter remembered what I had told him. He went outside and wept bitterly. His heart was shattered.

Now here we are, days after I rose from the dead. We just had breakfast together on the shore — fish and bread over a charcoal fire. And I looked at Peter and asked him a question.

"Simon, son of John, do you love me more than these?"

Peter said, "Yes, Lord. You know I love you."

I said, "Feed my lambs."

Then I asked again. "Simon, son of John, do you love me?"

"Yes, Lord. You know I love you."

"Take care of my sheep."

Then I asked a third time. "Simon, do you love me?"

And this time, Peter was hurt. His voice broke. "Lord, you know everything — you know that I love you."

I said, "Feed my sheep."

Now here is what I want you to understand. I did not ask Peter three times to punish him. I asked three times because he had denied me three times. One question for each denial. One restoration for each failure. I was not rubbing it in — I was rebuilding him.

Every time Peter said "I love you," I gave him a job. Feed my lambs. Take care of my sheep. Feed my sheep. I was not just forgiving Peter — I was restoring him to PURPOSE. I was telling him: you are not done. You are not disqualified. I still have a plan for your life.

And then I told him something serious. I told him that one day, following me would cost him his life. And you know what I said after that?

"Follow me."

Not "hide." Not "play it safe." Follow me.

Here is the truth: Peter denied me three times. I restored him three times. I do not keep score. I restore. And I will do the same for you. Your worst day is not your final chapter. Your biggest failure is not your identity. When you come back to me, I do not just forgive you — I give you purpose again.

LESSON: Jesus does not just forgive — He restores you to purpose. Your failures do not disqualify you. When you return to Him, He rebuilds you and gives you a mission.
