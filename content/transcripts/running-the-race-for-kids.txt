Picture this. You're standing at the starting line of the biggest race of your life. Your heart is pounding. Your legs are ready. And when you look up into the stands — they're packed. Thousands of people, every single one on their feet, cheering for you.

Who are they? Abraham. Moses. David. Esther. Ruth. Daniel. Mary, my own mother. Every hero of faith who ever lived — all of them cheering you on. Hebrews calls them "a great cloud of witnesses."

And here's what the writer says next: "Therefore, since we are surrounded by such a great cloud of witnesses, let us throw off everything that hinders and the sin that so easily entangles. And let us run with perseverance the race marked out for us, fixing our eyes on Jesus, the author and perfecter of faith."

That's me. I'm not just in the stands — I'm at the finish line. I'm the one you're running toward.

Now let's talk about how to run this race well.

First — throw off what slows you down. Imagine running in a heavy winter coat, dragging a backpack full of rocks. That's what it's like when you hold onto bad habits, toxic friendships, constant distractions, fear, anger you won't let go of. Drop it. You can't run fast carrying stuff that was never meant to come with you.

Second — watch out for sin that entangles. Ever had your shoelaces come untied mid-run? You trip. You fall flat. That's what sin does — wraps around your ankles and pulls you down. Lying, jealousy, cheating, gossip — it tangles you up. Deal with it before it deals with you.

Third — run with perseverance. Paul said it in his letter to the Corinthians: "I do not run like someone running aimlessly. I discipline my body and keep it under control." This race isn't a sprint — it's a marathon. There will be days you're tired. Days you want to quit. Days it feels like you're not making any progress at all. Keep running anyway.

And finally — fix your eyes on me. Not on the runner next to you. Not on how far you still have to go. Not on how many times you've stumbled. Look at me. I endured the cross. I finished my race. And I will help you finish yours.

This is your race. No one else can run it for you. But you're not running alone. The stands are full, the finish line is real, and I'm waiting for you.

Keep running. Don't quit.
