TITLE: Taming the Tongue
SERIES: The Ending & the Beginning
EPISODE: 3
SCRIPTURE: James 3:1-12

I want you to think about the biggest ship you've ever seen. A massive vessel cutting through the ocean — thousands of tons of steel and cargo. Now picture the rudder underneath it. It's tiny compared to the ship. But that small rudder decides where the entire ship goes.

James looked at that and said: that's your tongue.

A small part of the body — but it steers your entire life.

He gave another picture too. A single match. One spark. Dropped in a dry forest, it can burn down thousands of acres. Just one tiny flame — and everything is destroyed.

"The tongue is a small part of the body," James wrote, "but it makes great boasts. Consider what a great forest is set on fire by such a small spark."

Your words have that kind of power. Every single day.

Think about the worst thing someone ever said to you. Can you still hear it? Maybe it was years ago — but it stuck. It burned. Now think about the best thing anyone ever said to you. The moment someone told you they believed in you, that you mattered, that you were loved. That stuck too, didn't it?

That's what James is talking about. The tongue has the power of life and death. And you get to choose which one you use.

But James noticed something strange — something that shouldn't be possible. He said, "With the tongue we praise God, and with it we curse human beings, who have been made in God's likeness." Fresh water and salt water don't flow from the same spring. A fig tree doesn't produce olives. But somehow, the same mouth that sings worship on Sunday tears someone apart on Monday.

That shouldn't be.

And in your world — right now — this matters more than ever. Words fly faster than they ever have. A text, a comment, a post — it's out there in seconds. Gossip spreads in group chats. Rumors travel through hallways. Someone's reputation can be destroyed before lunch. And once those words are out, you can't take them back.

James said no one can tame the tongue on their own. It's restless and full of poison. But here's what I want you to hear: with My help, you can master it. You can choose words that build instead of destroy, that heal instead of wound, that give life instead of taking it.

Before you speak, before you type, before you post — ask yourself: is this building someone up or tearing them down?

Your words are powerful. Use them wisely.
