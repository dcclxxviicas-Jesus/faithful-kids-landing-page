I had barely finished praying when I heard them coming.

Torches flickering through the olive trees. The clang of armor. The shuffle of hundreds of feet. A mob — Roman soldiers, temple guards, chief priests, elders — armed with swords and clubs, coming through the garden like an army marching to war.

And at the front of them all — Judas.

My disciple. My friend. A man who had eaten at my table, heard my teachings, watched me heal the sick and raise the dead. A man I had loved for three years. He had sold me for thirty pieces of silver — the price of a slave.

He walked straight up to me. He knew exactly where to find me because he had been here with me so many times before. The signal he had given them was this: "The one I kiss is the man. Arrest him."

Judas looked me in the eyes and said, "Greetings, Rabbi!" Then he kissed me on the cheek.

A kiss. The sign of friendship and respect. Turned into a weapon.

I looked at him — and I didn't look at him with hatred. I looked at him with heartbreak. "Friend," I said, "do what you came for."

I called him friend. Because even in that moment, even as his betrayal was being sealed, that's what he had been to me. And it destroyed me that he chose this.

The soldiers surged forward. Peter — impulsive, fiery Peter — pulled out a sword and swung it at the high priest's servant, a man named Malchus, and cut off his right ear. The garden erupted into chaos.

I said, "Put your sword away, Peter. Don't you realize that I could ask my Father right now and He would send twelve legions of angels?"

Twelve legions. Do you know what that is? Over seventy-two thousand angels. One angel had wiped out an entire Assyrian army overnight. I could have called seventy-two thousand. The soldiers wouldn't have stood a chance. None of them would have.

But I didn't call them.

Instead, I reached out and touched Malchus's ear — the ear Peter had just cut off — and healed it. My last miracle before the cross was an act of mercy toward one of the men who came to arrest me.

Then I said to the mob, "Am I some dangerous revolutionary that you come with swords and clubs? Every day I sat teaching in the temple courts and you didn't arrest me. But this is happening so that the Scriptures will be fulfilled."

And then... my disciples ran. Every single one of them. The men who had sworn they would die with me — they disappeared into the darkness.

I stood alone, surrounded by enemies, with the torchlight casting shadows across the garden.

But I need you to understand something, and I need you to never forget it: I was not a victim that night. I was not overpowered. I was not trapped. I could have ended it with a single word. Seventy-two thousand angels were waiting for my call.

I chose not to call them.

I chose the nails. I chose the cross. I chose the suffering. Because on the other side of all of it — was you.

I wasn't taken. I was given. And I was given willingly.

KEY SCRIPTURE: "Do you think I cannot call on my Father, and He will at once put at my disposal more than twelve legions of angels?" — Matthew 26:53

LESSON: Jesus wasn't a victim — He was willing. He could have stopped it at any point. He chose not to. He chose you.
