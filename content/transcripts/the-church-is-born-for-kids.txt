THE EARLY CHURCH — EPISODE 1: THE CHURCH IS BORN
Based on Acts 2:42-47
Series: The Early Church | Episode 1 of 5
Target audience: Kids ages 7-15

Hey — so you know how the Holy Spirit came down at Pentecost? Tongues of fire, people speaking in languages they'd never learned, three thousand people deciding to follow me in a single day?

Well, that was just the beginning.

After I went back to my Father, here's what happened next — and honestly? It's one of my favorite parts of the whole story.

Those three thousand new believers didn't just say "cool experience" and go home. No way. They stuck together. They formed something brand new — the very first church.

Now, when I say "church," erase whatever picture is in your head. There was no building. No steeple. No parking lot. No sign out front with a clever saying on it. The first church was... people. Just people — doing life together.

And here's what they did. They devoted themselves to four things. Write these down — they're that important.

First: the apostles' teaching. Peter, John, and the others would share everything I had taught them, and the people soaked it up like sponges.

Second: fellowship. That means real friendship. Not "hi, how are you, fine" friendship. Deep, I-know-your-struggles, I've-got-your-back friendship.

Third: breaking bread together. They ate meals in each other's homes — laughing, sharing, celebrating. Every meal felt like a party.

And fourth: prayer. They talked to my Father together — constantly.

But here's the part that really blows people's minds. They shared everything. If someone had a need — food, clothing, a place to stay — somebody else would sell what they had to meet that need. Nobody said, "Well, that's mine." They said, "What's mine is yours."

They ate together every single day with glad and sincere hearts, praising God. And the people around them noticed. You can't fake that kind of joy.

And here's what happened — every day, every single day, the Lord added to their number. More people kept coming because they saw something real. Something alive. Something they wanted to be part of.

This is what I dreamed of when I walked the earth. Not an institution. Not a religion. A family. A community that loves so radically, the whole world stops and stares.

Church isn't a place you go. It's a community you belong to. And you? You can start building that kind of community right where you are — today.
