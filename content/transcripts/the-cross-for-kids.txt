This is the hardest story I will ever tell you. But I need you to hear it, because this is why I came.

After the beating, they placed a heavy wooden cross on my back and made me walk through the streets of Jerusalem. My body was broken. The crowd that had waved palm branches five days earlier now lined the road to watch me suffer. Some were weeping. Most just stared.

I stumbled. My legs gave out. The soldiers grabbed a man from the crowd — a man named Simon, from a place called Cyrene. He was just passing through. He never planned to be part of this story. But they forced the cross onto his shoulders, and he carried it the rest of the way for me. Sometimes the person who helps you the most is someone you never expected.

We reached a hill called Golgotha — the Place of the Skull. And there, they nailed me to the cross. Iron spikes through my hands and feet. I felt everything. The wood. The weight. The tearing.

The soldiers took my clothes and gambled for them, rolling dice at the foot of my cross like it was a game. People walking by shouted insults. "He saved others — He can't save Himself!" The religious leaders mocked me. "If you're really the Son of God, come down from the cross!"

They didn't understand. I wasn't on the cross because I couldn't come down. I was on the cross because I wouldn't.

Two criminals hung on crosses beside me — one on my left, one on my right. One of them mocked me like the crowd. But the other one — he was different. He turned his head toward me and said, "Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom." I looked at him through the blood and the pain and said, "Today, you will be with me in paradise."

Even on the cross, it wasn't too late for him. It's never too late.

Then I looked down and saw my mother, Mary. She was standing there with John, one of my closest disciples. Her heart was shattering. I said to her, "Woman, behold your son." And to John, "Behold your mother." Even while dying, I made sure she would be cared for.

Here's what I need you to understand: every nail was a choice. Every moment on that cross, I could have called ten thousand angels. I could have stepped down and walked away. But I looked ahead through time and I saw your face — and I stayed.

Love that costs nothing isn't love. The cross was the price I paid for you.
