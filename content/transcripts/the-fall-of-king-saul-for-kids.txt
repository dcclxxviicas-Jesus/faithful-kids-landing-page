THE RISE OF KINGS — Episode 9: The Fall of King Saul
Based on 1 Samuel 28, 31 | Narrated by Jesus

Hey. This episode is different. This one is heavy. Today we're going to watch a king fall — and it's one of the saddest stories in the entire Bible.

Saul is in trouble. The Philistine army is gathering for a massive attack, and for the first time in a long time, Saul is terrified. He needs to hear from God. He tries praying — silence. He tries dreams — nothing. He asks the prophets — no answer. God has stopped speaking to Saul. And honestly? Saul stopped listening to God a long time ago.

So in his desperation, Saul does the WORST possible thing. He goes to a medium — a woman at Endor who claimed she could talk to the dead. This was something God had strictly FORBIDDEN. Saul himself had banned these practices from Israel! But now he's so desperate he disguises himself and goes at night, begging her to call up the spirit of the prophet Samuel.

And something terrifying happens. Samuel's spirit appears. But he doesn't bring comfort. He brings judgment. Samuel says, "Why have you disturbed me? God has turned away from you. Tomorrow, Israel will fall to the Philistines — and you and your sons will be with me."

Saul collapses on the ground. No strength. No hope. No way out.

The next day, everything Samuel said comes true. The battle on Mount Gilboa is a disaster. The Philistines overwhelm Israel. Jonathan — brave, loyal, noble Jonathan — is killed in the fighting. Saul's other sons fall too. Saul himself is badly wounded by archers, and rather than be captured by his enemies, he falls on his own sword.

The king who stood head and shoulders above everyone in Israel... is gone.

But here's what gets me. When David hears the news, he doesn't celebrate. He doesn't say, "Finally! My turn!" He WEEPS. He tears his clothes. He fasts. And then he writes one of the most beautiful laments ever recorded: "How the mighty have fallen! Tell it not in Gath... Saul and Jonathan — beloved and pleasant in their lives, in death they were not divided."

David mourned for the man who tried to kill him. He honored the king who hunted him. That tells you everything about David's heart.

And Saul? His story is a warning. He had EVERYTHING — height, strength, the crown, God's Spirit. But pride, jealousy, and disobedience stole it all. Step by step, choice by choice, he walked away from God until there was nothing left.

Don't let that be your story. Stay close. Stay humble. Stay faithful. See you next time.
