Have you ever tried to glue fake fruit onto a tree? Hot-glued a plastic apple onto a branch and hoped someone would think it was real? From far away, maybe it fools somebody — but up close? Everyone knows it's fake. It didn't grow there.

That's what it looks like when people try to fake being good without anything real happening on the inside.

Paul wrote to the churches in Galatia and described what a life connected to me actually looks like. He called it the fruit of the Spirit. And notice — he didn't say "fruits," plural. He said "fruit" — singular. One fruit. Because it all comes as one package. Think of it like an orange with nine segments. You don't get to pick three and throw out the rest.

So here are the nine parts of this one fruit.

Love — choosing to care about someone even when it's hard. Like sitting with the kid no one else talks to at lunch.

Joy — not just happiness when things go your way, but a deep-down gladness because you know God is with you no matter what.

Peace — being calm inside even when everything outside is chaos. Peace holds you steady.

Patience — waiting without freaking out. Like when your little sibling asks you the same question for the tenth time, and you answer kindly — again.

Kindness — going out of your way to make someone's day better. Holding the door. Sharing your snack. Saying something encouraging.

Goodness — doing the right thing even when nobody's watching. Returning the extra change. Telling the truth when lying would be easier.

Faithfulness — being someone people can count on. Showing up when you said you would. Keeping your promises.

Gentleness — being strong but not harsh. You can be tough and still be tender. Think of how carefully you'd hold a baby bird.

Self-control — the ability to stop yourself before you do something you'll regret. Not sending that mean text. Walking away from the argument.

Here's the secret — you don't manufacture these. You can't grit your teeth and force yourself to be patient or gentle. These grow naturally when you stay connected to me. I said it myself: "I am the vine, you are the branches. If you remain in me, you will bear much fruit."

Stay connected. Talk to me. Trust me. And over time, real fruit will grow — the kind that lasts.
