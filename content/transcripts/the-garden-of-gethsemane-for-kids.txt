I need to tell you about the hardest night of my life. And I'm going to be completely honest with you — because you deserve to know what it really cost.

After supper, we sang a hymn together and walked out into the night. Jerusalem was quiet now. The streets were mostly empty. We crossed the Kidron Valley and climbed the hill to a garden called Gethsemane — a grove of old, gnarled olive trees that I had gone to many times before to pray.

But this night was different.

I left most of the disciples near the entrance. "Sit here while I go over there and pray," I told them. Then I took Peter, James, and John — my three closest friends — deeper into the garden.

And that's when it hit me.

A wave of sorrow so heavy I could barely stand. I turned to them and said something I had never said before: "My soul is crushed with grief to the point of death. Stay here and keep watch with me."

I walked a little farther — just a stone's throw — and fell face-down on the ground. And I prayed the most honest prayer of my life.

"Father... if it is possible... let this cup pass from me."

Do you know what the cup was? It was everything that was about to happen. The betrayal. The arrest. The beating. The mocking. The nails. The cross. And something worse than all of that — carrying the sin of every human being who ever lived or ever would live. The full weight of all the world's darkness, loaded onto my shoulders.

I could see it all. And I was terrified.

I want you to hear that. I — Jesus, the Son of God — was terrified. My sweat fell like drops of blood to the ground. That's not a metaphor. The agony was so intense that my body was breaking down under the pressure.

I went back to Peter, James, and John. They were asleep. All three of them. "Could you not keep watch with me for one hour?" I asked Peter. I went back and prayed again. Same prayer. Same agony. Came back. Asleep again. A third time. Same thing.

I was alone. Completely alone in the darkest moment of my life.

But here's what I need you to understand. Listen closely. Each time I prayed, I didn't just say "let this cup pass from me." I said something else — the most important words I've ever spoken:

"Yet not my will, but Yours be done."

I was afraid. I was in agony. Every part of my human body and soul screamed to run. But I chose to stay. I chose obedience. I chose you.

Courage isn't the absence of fear. If I wasn't afraid, it wouldn't have taken courage. Courage is being terrified and choosing to do the right thing anyway. It's saying, "I don't want to do this — but I will, because it matters more than my comfort."

That night in the garden, I chose you over myself. And I would do it again.
