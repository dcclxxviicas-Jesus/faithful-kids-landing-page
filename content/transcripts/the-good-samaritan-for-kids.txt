THE TEACHINGS OF JESUS — Episode 4
"The Good Samaritan"
Based on Luke 10:25-37

A religious expert walked up to me one day trying to test me. He said, "Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?" I turned it back on him: "What does the law say?" He answered perfectly: "Love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, strength, and mind — and love your neighbor as yourself." I said, "You nailed it. Do that and you'll live."

But he wasn't satisfied. He wanted to justify himself. So he asked the question that changed everything: "And who is my neighbor?"

He wanted me to draw a line. He wanted me to say: your neighbor is the person who looks like you, talks like you, lives near you. Instead, I told him a story.

A man was traveling down the road from Jerusalem to Jericho — a dangerous, winding road through rocky desert. Bandits jumped him. They beat him up, stole everything he had, and left him half dead on the side of the road. Bleeding. Broken. Barely alive.

Along comes a priest. A religious leader! Surely HE would help. But the priest saw the man, crossed to the other side of the road, and kept walking. Gone.

Then a Levite came — another religious man. He walked up, looked at the beaten man, and kept right on going. Two religious guys. Two chances. Two failures.

And then came a Samaritan. Now here's what you need to know — Samaritans and Jews HATED each other. Absolute enemies. Generations of bad blood. This was the LAST person anyone expected to stop.

But he stopped. He knelt down. He bandaged the man's wounds, pouring oil and wine to clean them. He put the man on his own donkey, brought him to an inn, and took care of him through the night. The next morning, he paid the innkeeper and said: "Take care of him. Whatever else it costs, I'll pay when I come back."

Then I looked at that religious expert and asked: "Which of these three was a neighbor to the man who was attacked?"

He couldn't even say the word "Samaritan." He just said: "The one who showed mercy."

I said: "Go and do likewise."

Your neighbor isn't defined by address, race, or whether they're easy to love. Your neighbor is anyone in need — ESPECIALLY the person you'd least expect to help. Love doesn't draw lines. Love crosses them.

Who's lying on the side of YOUR road today?
