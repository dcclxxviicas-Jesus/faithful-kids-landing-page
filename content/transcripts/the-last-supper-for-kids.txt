The night I'm about to tell you about was the most important dinner in the history of the world. And it started with me on my knees.

It was Thursday evening. Passover. My disciples and I gathered in a borrowed upper room in Jerusalem. The city was buzzing with festival noise, but in that room, it was just us — thirteen men around a low table, oil lamps flickering, the smell of roasted lamb and unleavened bread filling the air.

They didn't know it was our last meal together.

But I did.

Before we ate, I stood up from the table. I took off my outer robe, wrapped a towel around my waist, poured water into a basin, and knelt down in front of my disciples. Then I started washing their feet.

Now, you need to understand — in my time, roads were dirt and dust and animal dung. Everyone wore sandals. Foot-washing was the job of the lowest servant in the household. It was humiliating work. And here I was — their Teacher, their Lord, the Son of God — doing it.

The room went silent. Nobody moved.

Then Peter. Always Peter. "Lord, are you going to wash MY feet?" I looked up at him and said, "You don't understand now what I'm doing, but someday you will." Peter shook his head. "You will NEVER wash my feet!" I said, "Unless I wash you, you have no part with me." Peter, being Peter, swung the other direction: "Then wash my hands and head too!"

I washed all twelve pairs of feet. Including Judas's. I washed the feet of the man who would betray me before the night was over. I knew exactly what he was about to do. And I knelt down and served him anyway.

Then we ate. And during that meal, I took bread, gave thanks, broke it, and said: "Take and eat. This is my body, which is broken for you. Do this in remembrance of me." Then I took the cup of wine: "Drink from it, all of you. This is my blood of the new covenant, poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins."

The room was heavy. Some of them didn't fully understand. But they could feel it — something enormous was happening.

I told them one of them would betray me. They were devastated. "Surely not I, Lord?" they each asked, one by one. Even Judas asked. I told him, "What you are about to do, do quickly." He left. Into the night.

I looked around that table at the men I loved most — knowing Peter would deny me three times before morning, knowing all of them would scatter and run. And I served them anyway. Every single one.

That's what real leadership looks like. It doesn't sit on a throne. It kneels with a towel. Love doesn't serve only the people who deserve it. Love serves even the ones who will fail you.
