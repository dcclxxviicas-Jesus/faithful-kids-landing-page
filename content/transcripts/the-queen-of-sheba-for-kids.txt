Alright — I've saved one of the most dramatic stories in Solomon's life for this episode. The day a powerful queen traveled over a thousand miles just to see if the rumors were true.

The Queen of Sheba had heard about Solomon. His wisdom. His wealth. His God. Reports had traveled down the trade routes and across the deserts all the way to her kingdom — likely in modern-day Yemen or Ethiopia, over 1,400 miles from Jerusalem. And she wasn't the type to just take someone's word for it. She needed to see for herself.

So she loaded up a caravan — and when I say caravan, I mean a MASSIVE one. Camels carrying spices so rare they were worth more than gold. Actual gold — tons of it. Precious stones. She rolled into Jerusalem like the superpower she was.

And then she tested Solomon. She came with hard questions — riddles, puzzles, deep philosophical questions designed to trip up anyone who was faking. She threw everything she had at him.

And Solomon answered every single one. Not one question was too hard for him. Not one.

But here's what really got to her — it wasn't just his answers. She saw the palace he built. She tasted the food on his table. She watched how his officials carried themselves. She observed how his servants were treated — and how they treated him. She saw the burnt offerings he made at the temple.

And the Bible says something incredible — "there was no more breath in her."

She was literally breathless. Speechless. Undone.

She turned to Solomon and said: "The report I heard in my own country about your achievements and your wisdom is true. But I didn't believe it until I came and saw with my own eyes. Indeed, not even half was told me. Your wisdom and prosperity far exceed the report I heard."

"Not even HALF was told me." The reality was so much greater than the reputation.

She gave Solomon 120 talents of gold — that's roughly four and a half TONS of gold. Plus massive quantities of spices and precious stones. And Solomon? He gave her everything she desired and asked for, plus gifts from his royal bounty.

Two powerful leaders. Mutual respect. Mutual generosity.

Here's what I want you to take from this: wisdom and godly character will speak for themselves. Solomon didn't send a marketing team to Sheba. He didn't run ads. He didn't try to prove himself. He simply lived faithfully — and his reputation traveled over a thousand miles on its own.

You don't have to prove yourself to anyone. You don't have to shout about how great you are. Just be faithful. Develop your character. Pursue wisdom. And let the fruit speak for itself.

Because when it's real, people will come from far away to see it.
