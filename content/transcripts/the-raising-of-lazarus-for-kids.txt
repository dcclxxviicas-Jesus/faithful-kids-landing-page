Hey kids. Sit close for this one. Because this story is personal. This one involves people I loved deeply — and a miracle that NOBODY saw coming.

Lazarus was one of my closest friends. He lived in Bethany with his two sisters, Mary and Martha. I'd eaten at their table. I'd laughed with them. They were family to me.

So when Lazarus got deathly sick, Mary and Martha sent an urgent message: "Lord, the one you LOVE is sick!" They expected me to drop everything and come running. And honestly? That's what everyone expected.

But I didn't go. Not right away. I WAITED. Two whole days. My disciples were confused. Mary and Martha were desperate. And Lazarus? Lazarus DIED.

By the time I arrived in Bethany, he had been in the tomb for FOUR days. The whole village was mourning. Martha came running out to meet me, tears on her face: "Lord, if you had been HERE, my brother would not have died."

And I looked at Martha and said something that echoes through all of history: "I am the resurrection and the life. Whoever believes in me will live, even though they die. Do you believe this?"

She said, "Yes, Lord. I believe."

Then Mary came. She fell at my feet, weeping. The friends who came with her were weeping. And the Bible records two of the most powerful words ever written about me:

Jesus wept.

I CRIED. Right there. In front of everyone. Not because I couldn't fix it — I was about to fix it in the biggest way possible. I cried because I FELT their pain. I cried because I love people THAT much.

Then I walked to the tomb. It was a cave with a stone rolled across the entrance. I said, "Take away the stone." Martha panicked — "Lord, he's been dead four days. There will be a terrible smell!" But they rolled it away.

I looked up to heaven, thanked my Father, and then I SHOUTED with a voice that shook the boundary between life and death:

"LAZARUS, COME OUT!"

And out he came. A dead man. WALKING. Still wrapped in grave clothes. ALIVE.

Here's what I want you to know: I waited on PURPOSE. Not because I didn't care. Because God's glory needed a BIGGER stage. If I had come earlier, it would have been a healing. But I came later — and it became a RESURRECTION.

God's timing isn't late. It's setting up something bigger than you can imagine.
