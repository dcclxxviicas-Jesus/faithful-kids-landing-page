PAUL'S ADVENTURES — EPISODE 6: THE RIOT IN EPHESUS
Based on Acts 19:23-41
Series: Paul's Adventures | Episode 6 of 10
Target audience: Kids ages 7-15

Okay — so Paul had been in Ephesus for almost three years, and things were going incredibly well. People were hearing the gospel, lives were being transformed, and the whole city was buzzing. Sick people were healed. Those caught up in dark magic were burning their spell books in public bonfires. The word of God was spreading like wildfire.

And that right there? That's when the trouble started.

See, Ephesus had a massive temple — one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World — dedicated to the goddess Artemis. And there was a guy named Demetrius who made a very comfortable living crafting little silver shrines of Artemis. Business was booming — until Paul showed up.

Because Paul kept telling everyone, "Gods made by human hands aren't gods at all." And people believed him. They stopped buying idols. Sales tanked. Demetrius was furious.

So he called a meeting of all the craftsmen and said, "Boys — this Paul is ruining everything. He's telling people our gods are fake. If this keeps up, the great temple of Artemis will mean nothing!"

That was all it took. The craftsmen went ballistic. They poured into the streets screaming, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!" The whole city erupted into chaos. A mob grabbed two of Paul's traveling companions — Gaius and Aristarchus — and dragged them into the massive open-air theater.

Paul wanted to go in and face the crowd himself. That's Paul for you — never backing down. But his friends literally held him back. "You'll get torn apart!"

Inside the theater, it was pure pandemonium. People were shouting different things. Most of them didn't even know why they were there! For two solid hours, thousands of voices chanted the same thing over and over: "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!"

Finally, the city clerk — basically the mayor — stood up and calmed everybody down. He said, "If Demetrius has a legal case, take it to court. But this riot? This is going to get us in trouble with Rome." And just like that, he dismissed the crowd.

Here's what I want you to see. When Paul was making the biggest difference, he got the biggest pushback — and it came from people whose wallets were threatened. That hasn't changed. When you stand for truth, expect resistance. Especially from people who profit from the way things are. Don't let the noise stop you. The riot didn't stop Paul, and it shouldn't stop you either.
