They dragged me through the streets of Jerusalem while the city slept.

First to Caiaphas, the high priest. His palace was lit up — torches blazing, guards everywhere. The whole council was already assembled. They'd been planning this for weeks. This wasn't a search for truth. It was a setup.

Witnesses came forward — liars, every one of them. Their stories didn't even match. One said I threatened to destroy the temple. Another twisted my words into something I never said. They stumbled over each other's lies. In any honest court, the case would have been thrown out in minutes.

But this wasn't an honest court.

Finally, Caiaphas stood up and looked me dead in the eyes. "Are you the Christ, the Son of the Blessed One?"

And I said it. "I am."

The room exploded. Caiaphas ripped his robes and screamed, "Blasphemy! We don't need any more witnesses!" They spat in my face. They blindfolded me and hit me with their fists. "Prophesy! Tell us who hit you!" they mocked. I felt every blow. I could have named every single one of them. But I stayed silent.

Then they took me to Pilate — the Roman governor. The religious leaders couldn't execute anyone without Rome's permission, so they needed him. Pilate questioned me privately. He looked troubled. He told the crowd, "I find no guilt in this man."

No guilt. He said it out loud. And it didn't matter.

The crowd screamed for my death. Pilate offered to release one prisoner — a tradition during Passover. He gave them a choice: me, or Barabbas. A murderer. A violent criminal.

They chose Barabbas.

Pilate took water and washed his hands in front of everyone. "I am innocent of this man's blood," he said. As if water could wash away a coward's choice.

Then the soldiers took over. They stripped me, put a scarlet robe on me, and twisted thorns into a crown. They shoved it onto my head and knelt in front of me, laughing. "Hail, King of the Jews!" They struck me with a reed. They spat on me again.

Here's what I need you to understand: they tried me in a courtroom, but I was judging them from the inside. I saw every heart. Every motive. Every lie.

Sometimes the system fails you. People in power make wrong choices. The crowd follows the loudest voice instead of the truest one. That happens today too — in schools, online, everywhere.

But the truth doesn't change just because people reject it. I was innocent that night. And no verdict on earth could change who I am.
