I was tired. Like, genuinely exhausted. I'd been traveling through Samaria, and around noon — the hottest part of the day — I sat down by a well near a town called Sychar. My disciples went into town to buy food. And that's when SHE showed up.

A Samaritan woman, coming to draw water. But here's the thing — nobody came to the well at noon. Women came early in the morning or in the cool of the evening, and they came in GROUPS. She came alone, in the blazing heat, because she was avoiding everyone. This woman was an outcast. The town had rejected her. She was carrying shame like a boulder on her back.

I looked at her and said, "Will you give me a drink?"

She was STUNNED. "You're a Jew," she said. "I'm a Samaritan. How can you ask ME for a drink?" See, Jews and Samaritans didn't talk to each other. There was deep hatred between our peoples — going back centuries. And on top of that, a Jewish man talking to a woman alone in public? Unheard of. Everything about this conversation broke the rules.

And I was just getting started.

I said, "If you knew the gift of God and who it is asking you for a drink, you would have asked HIM and He would have given you living water." She was confused. "Sir, you don't even have a bucket!" But I wasn't talking about well water. I told her, "Everyone who drinks this water will be thirsty again. But whoever drinks the water I give them will NEVER thirst. It will become a spring of water welling up to eternal life."

She wanted it. "Sir, give me this water!"

Then I said something that changed everything: "Go, call your husband." She said quietly, "I have no husband." And I told her, "You're right. You've had five husbands, and the man you're with now isn't your husband."

Her eyes went wide. I knew EVERYTHING about her. Every mistake. Every heartbreak. Every secret. And I was still sitting there, still talking to her, still offering her living water.

She dropped her water jar, ran back to town, and shouted, "Come, see a man who told me everything I ever did! Could this be the Messiah?" The whole town came out. Many believed.

This woman — rejected, ashamed, hiding — became the first evangelist in Samaria.

Here's the lesson: NOBODY is too far gone. Nobody is too messy, too broken, too "other" for me. I will cross every barrier, break every rule, and sit at your well in the middle of your worst day. You are not disqualified. You are LOVED.
