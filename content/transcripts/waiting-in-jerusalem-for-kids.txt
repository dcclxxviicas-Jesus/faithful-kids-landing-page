Let me tell you about one of the hardest things my followers ever had to do. It was not fighting a battle. It was not climbing a mountain. It was waiting.

After I ascended into heaven, the disciples walked back to Jerusalem — about a mile from the place where they watched me disappear into the clouds. I had given them a clear instruction: "Stay in Jerusalem. Wait for the gift the Father promised." I did not tell them exactly when. I did not give them a countdown clock. I just said WAIT.

And they did.

They went to an upper room — Peter, John, James, Andrew, Philip, Thomas, Bartholomew, Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, Simon the Zealot, and Judas son of James. All eleven of them. Plus the women who had followed me, including my mother Mary. And my brothers. About one hundred and twenty believers gathered together.

And what did they do while they waited? They PRAYED. Constantly. Together. They did not sit around complaining. They did not try to force something to happen. They did not make up their own plan. They prayed and they stayed united.

While they waited, Peter stood up and addressed something that had to be dealt with. Judas — the one who betrayed me — was gone. He had taken his own life, and his place among the twelve was empty. Peter said, "We need to find someone to take his place — someone who has been with us from the beginning and witnessed the resurrection."

They nominated two men: Joseph called Barsabbas, and Matthias. Then they prayed — again — and cast lots. Matthias was chosen to replace Judas, and the twelve were complete once more.

Ten days they waited. Ten days of praying, preparing, and trusting. They did not know a rushing wind was coming. They did not know tongues of fire were about to appear. They did not know the Holy Spirit was about to change EVERYTHING. All they knew was that I told them to wait — and they obeyed.

Here is what I need you to understand: waiting is not wasted time. Waiting is preparation. Sometimes I say "wait" not because I have forgotten you, but because I am getting something ready that is bigger than you can imagine. The answer is not "no." It is "not yet."

Obedient waiting is one of the hardest things you will ever do. But every single time, it is worth it.

LESSON: Sometimes God says "wait" — not "no," but "not yet." Obedient waiting is one of the hardest things to do, but it prepares you for what God has next. Prayer and patience are never wasted.
