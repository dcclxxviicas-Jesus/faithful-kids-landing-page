Hey kids! Buckle up, because this story has a plot twist that will blow your mind. It involves a dark lake, terrified disciples, and one very bold fisherman who did something NO human had ever done.

Right after I fed the five thousand, I told my disciples to get in the boat and cross the Sea of Galilee without me. I needed time alone to pray. So they sailed off, and I went up on a mountainside by myself.

Hours passed. It was now the middle of the night — somewhere between 3 and 6 in the morning. Pitch black. And my disciples were in TROUBLE. The wind was against them. Waves were battering the boat. They'd been rowing for HOURS and barely made any progress.

Then they saw something that made their blood run cold.

A figure. Walking toward them. ON TOP of the water.

They started SCREAMING. "It's a GHOST!" They were absolutely terrified.

But it was me. And I called out to them right away: "Take courage! It is I. Don't be afraid!"

Now here's where it gets INCREDIBLE. Peter — bold, impulsive, beautiful Peter — shouted back through the wind: "Lord, if it's really you, TELL ME TO COME TO YOU on the water!"

And I said one word: "Come."

Peter swung his legs over the side of that boat, put his feet on the water — and it HELD. He was WALKING. ON. WATER. Walking on the waves toward me. Can you IMAGINE?

But then — Peter looked down. He felt the wind. He saw the waves. And the moment his eyes shifted from me to the storm around him, fear rushed in. He started to SINK.

"LORD, SAVE ME!" he cried.

And IMMEDIATELY — not eventually, not after a lesson, not after he figured it out — IMMEDIATELY I reached out my hand and caught him. I pulled him up and said, "You of little faith, why did you doubt?"

We climbed into the boat together. The wind died down. And every single disciple fell to their knees and said, "Truly you are the Son of God."

Here's your lesson, and don't you EVER forget it: keep your eyes on ME, not on the waves. Peter didn't sink because the storm was too strong. He sank because he stopped looking at me. The moment you focus on the problem instead of the Problem-Solver — that's when you go under.

But even THEN — even when you sink — I'm right there to grab you. Every single time.
